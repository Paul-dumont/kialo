Discussion Name:
Should the police be defunded?

1. Thesis:
The police should be defunded.

  1.1. Pro:
  The police reinforce structural racism [1].

    1.1.1. Con:
    Defunding the police could encourage white supremacists to target marginalized people as a form of vigilantism [2], worsening racism in society.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      Vigilantism in the US is historically associated with anti-abolitionism and racial violence [3], meaning that defunding the police may actually harm those the movement aims to help.

        1.1.1.1.1. Con:
        The increased rate [4] of gun ownership in marginalized communities in America means that they may be able to defend themselves against any increase in racial violence.

        1.1.1.1.2. Pro:
        The people most likely to own guns [5] in the USA are white, Republican [6], and male: identity markers sometimes associated [7] with racial antagonism [8].

        1.1.1.1.3. Pro:
        Vigilantes have grown increasingly [9] emboldened [10] by the status quo; the police require more funding to curb their influence—not less.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      As white supremacists [11] are linked with American law enforcement [12], defunding the police could reduce the harm such groups can cause.

        1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
        A large number of off-duty police officers [13] took part in the January 6th riots at the US Capitol, in which five people died and white nationalist propaganda [14] was openly on display.

        1.1.1.2.2. Con:
        In the US, federal and local law enforcement have cooperated to crack down [15] on white supremacist gangs. Such actions weaken the links between extremists and law enforcement.

        1.1.1.2.3. Con:
        Better screening [16] techniques and background checks can be used to filter out [17] white supremacists and prevent them from being employed by law enforcement. Defunding the police isn't necessarily the solution.

      1.1.1.3. Pro:
      Some white supremacists support the idea of accelerationism [18] and want to increase civil disorder [19]. They may be more likely to target vulnerable groups if police presence is reduced.

        1.1.1.3.1. Pro:
        Curtailing the spread of white supremacy and its accelerationist ideals is necessary, especially as white supremacists seek to extend their reach [20] globally.

        1.1.1.3.2. Pro:
        Defunding the police at a time when white supremacists seek to fundamentally undermine [21] American political establishments is likely to be highly detrimental to public safety.

        1.1.1.3.3. Con:
        Due to active collusion [22] between regular police and white supremacist groups, defunding the police may help other federal agencies to more effectively counteract white supremacist movements.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    Policing in the US was established to maintain white supremacy [23].

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      Night watches and slave patrols [24] are considered to be the beginning of a racially directed system of law enforcement that secured capital for white settlers.

        1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
        The Boston police department [25], considered by many to be the first publicly funded police force, frequently thwarted the efforts of abolitionists by enforcing the racially-directed practice of fugitive capture [26].

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      Even if US policing was originally intended to maintain white supremacy, it can be racially unbiased now.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        Research indicates that police are more likely to exhibit both implict and explicit bias [27] than the general public.

        1.1.2.2.2. Pro:
        The Justice in Policing Act of 2020 [28], alongside similar pieces of legislation, proves that legislators are committed to improving police impartiality.

        1.1.2.2.3. Con:
        Globally, policing is an imperialist enterprise that is both influenced by and supportive of oppression [29]. In such a context, it is highly unlikely that American policing is bias-free.

      1.1.2.3. Pro:
      Often, the police's unspoken purpose [30] is to suppress Black communities by driving incarceration rates in for-profit prisons [31] and perpetuating profitable oppression [32].

        1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
        Several policy and enforcement strategies used to carry out the War on Drugs [33] served to reinforce structural racism in the United States.

        1.1.2.3.2. Con:
        Private prison contracts are regulated by federal and state authorities [34] that are independent of the police.

        1.1.2.3.3. Pro:
        Police enabled the destruction of, and participated in, the destruction [35] of Black Wall Street [36]. This defied law and order, and may have solely been carried out to harm Black communities.

      1.1.2.4. Con:
      The first American police departments [37] were set up to quell unrest between naturalized Americans (such as those of Dutch and English ancestry) and nascent immigrant populations (predominantly Irish and German) in Eastern cities.

        1.1.2.4.1. Con:
        Upon shifting from their slave patrol origins, the main objective of the earliest American police forces was to control Black people [38].

        1.1.2.4.2. Pro:
        Upon arriving in the United States, Irish Catholics were subjected to xenophobic discrimination and rhetoric from both the general public and nativist political movements, in a manner that was consistent [39] with the contemporary treatment of Black Americans (p.1 [40]).

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Police unfairly target [41] people of color and other minorities [42] by overpolicing [43] their communities (p. 3 [44]).

      1.1.3.1. Con:
      Minority communities are often underpoliced [45] with regards to violent crime and low clearance rates [46]. Defunding the police would entrench these issues, leaving these communities even more unsafe.

        1.1.3.1.1. Pro:
        Most Black Americans would prefer that the police maintain their current presence [47] in their neighborhoods, indicating that overpolicing is not considered to be an issue there.

        1.1.3.1.2. Pro:
        The mere presence [48] of police in hotspot areas can reduce the crime rate without increasing the arrest rate. Such a policy can only be implemented, however, if police funding is maintained.

        1.1.3.1.3. Con:
        Crime could be reduced by increasing the number and frequency of citizen patrols [49] in high-risk neighborhoods.

          1.1.3.1.3.1. Pro:
          In lieu of police presence, civilians have the power to perform a citizen's arrest [50] in order to protect the property and well-being of other citizens.

            1.1.3.1.3.1.1. Con:
            Sometimes people making a citizen's arrest end up being charged with a crime [51] themselves, suggesting that the average citizen is not a reasonable replacement [52] for a police officer.

            1.1.3.1.3.1.2. Con:
            Untrained citizens are likely to struggle to make an arrest, leading to the criminal escaping or worse [53]—harming someone.

            1.1.3.1.3.1.3. Pro:
            A successful citizen's arrest can prevent a dangerous suspect from fleeing [54] the scene of a crime, assisting police in the process of apprehending the suspect.

          1.1.3.1.3.2. Con:
          Arming citizens without training, and encouraging them to enforce laws onto others, may lead to harm [55].

          1.1.3.1.3.3. Pro:
          Community efforts are often more effective at maintaining order than external police intervention (p. 13 [56]).

      1.1.3.2. Pro:
      Overpolicing has led to disproportionate arrest rates [57] and an increased incidence of violence in Black communities.

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      Racist policing may stem from officers' unfamiliarity [58] with the communities they work in. Switching to a community-led model by defunding the police could alleviate these issues.

        1.1.3.3.1. Con:
        Given current levels of polarization [59] in the US, community-led policing could lead to decreased participation [60] between citizens and the police in densely populated areas.

        1.1.3.3.2. Con:
        Some communities have such poor relationships [61] with the police that it may be impossible to implement a community-led model. Nonetheless, these places need police to stay safe.

        1.1.3.3.3. Pro:
        There is precedent for the community-led model. A number of cities, like Albuquerque [62], Denver [63], and San Francisco [64], have developed programs to test the efficacy of using community leaders as first responders instead of the police.

        1.1.3.3.4. Pro:
        Community policing policy can significantly improve police-public interaction by increasing community trust [65] (p. 19894 [66]).

      1.1.3.4. Con:
      Statistically, Black and Latinx people commit a disproportionate [67] amount of crime, meaning they are aptly targeted by the police (p. 1 [68]).

        1.1.3.4.1. Con:
        Predictive policing that targets [69] "high-risk" individuals ultimately causes more harm in the long-term, including the degradation [70] of community trust.

        1.1.3.4.2. Con:
        Crime rates for Black communities are inflated [71] by charges for petty offenses, which often lock people in debt or long prison sentences [72] and deny them enfranchisement [73].

        1.1.3.4.3. Pro:
        Aggressive police strategies [74] are often used in predominantly Black communities due to the high rate of violent crime [75] in those areas.

          1.1.3.4.3.1. Con:
          The incidence of violent crime in predominantly Black areas is symptomatic of a criminal justice system that has failed to protect [76] these communties.

      1.1.3.5. Pro:
      Police brutality is a direct product of structural racism [77], and so disproportionately [78] harms Black people. The best means to reduce such harm is to defund the police.

        1.1.3.5.1. Pro:
        The juxtaposition between how unarmed Black people and white mass murderers are treated by police [79] demonstrates that the system is racist and must be defunded.

        1.1.3.5.2. Con:
        Procedural justice training [80] helps to reduce incidents of police brutality (p. 9815 [81]). Introducing such reforms can both improve policing and make justice more readily available.

        1.1.3.5.3. Con:
        If racism is structural [82], then it cannot change unless there is a large reorganization of society. Given that this is unlikely, police brutality can be expected to continue regardless of funding.

        1.1.3.5.4. Con:
        Reducing the police budget could encourage them to focus on fee-generating enforcement [83], ultimately leading to more policing in marginalized communities as has historically [84] been the case.

        1.1.3.5.5. Pro:
        White police officers are more likely [85] than the general public to believe that lethal acts of violence against Black people are isolated incidents. This makes it hard to change policing.

    1.1.4. Con:
    A minority [86] of police officers are racist towards others; most protect civilians without harming them.

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      Power imbalances and frequent opportunities for corruption make misconduct in policing inevitable (p. 9 [87]); only by removing such factors can corruption be tackled.

        1.1.4.1.1. Pro:
        Some laws are inherently racist or unfair, meaning that legislators cause police to enforce the law in a manner which could easily lead to corruption (p. 11 [88]).

        1.1.4.1.2. Con:
        The institution of policing is designed [89] to prevent corruption and the abuse of power. Therefore, individuals who act outside this framework are ultimately responsible for their own actions.

        1.1.4.1.3. Pro:
        The dehumanization [90] of people who have committed crimes makes them easier to mistreat and abuse. It is necessary to fundamentally reshape policing to avoid this.

      1.1.4.2. Pro:
      There are many minority police officers [91]; therefore, not all police officers in the US reinforce structural racism.

        1.1.4.2.1. Con:
        When assessing structural racism in law enforcement, it is more prudent to analyze policy than the demographics of those who enforce those policies.

          1.1.4.2.1.1. Con:
          Demographics are an important aspect of the internal working culture [92] of law enforcement departments. This can influence the creation of policy both directly and indirectly.

          1.1.4.2.1.2. Pro:
          By some metrics, regardless of race, all cops are embroiled [93] in an oppressive system.

            1.1.4.2.1.2.1. Pro:
            Even if only one police officer is actively racist in their conduct, the support they receive from their peers means that all police are complicit (pp. 10-11 [94]).

        1.1.4.2.2. Con:
        From 2007 to 2016, at least 332 US police departments became more white [95] relative to the demographics [96] of their jurisdiction, demonstrating that diversity has been decreasing.

        1.1.4.2.3. Pro:
        Nearly one-quarter [97] of police officers are non-white, a similar proportion [98] to the US as a whole. Minority officers are unlikely to willfully reinforce structural racism that would also harm them.

          1.1.4.2.3.1. Con:
          Internalized racism can lead marginalized communities to engage in their own disenfranchisement (pp. 384-5 [99]).

          1.1.4.2.3.2. Con:
          The overall share of Black officers matching national demographics is irrelevant if these officers are not assigned to police districts at the same local representative rate [100].

          1.1.4.2.3.3. Pro:
          In 2013, Black police officers comprised the same share [101] of local police officers as the overall US adult population.

      1.1.4.3. Pro:
      Corrupt cops are increasingly critiqued [102] by their peers, which may lead to more oversight with time. Changes to policing, like defunding, are likely to disrupt such progress.

        1.1.4.3.1. Con:
        Whistleblowers [103] in the police force may face severe retaliation [104], creating a culture of intimidation that prevents officers from reporting corruption or malpractice.

        1.1.4.3.2. Pro:
        The National Coalition of Law Enforcement Officers for Justice, Reform and Accountability is focused on fighting institutional racism [105], decreasing the number of dangerous police officers.

        1.1.4.3.3. Con:
        The current system of policing makes it hard to address the root cause of many issues, as the police often cover up [106] for one another to help hide [107] poor behavior.

          1.1.4.3.3.1. Pro:
          LA-area police forces have been accused of functioning like street gangs [108], carrying out extrajudicial murders and terrorizing citizens with relative impunity. Defunding such organizations would greatly disempower them.

  1.2. Con:
  Defunding the police could compromise the security of private citizens (p. 29 [109]).

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Half [110] of the US population believes that law and order should be protected above all else. As states achieve democratic legitimacy by obeying their voters, it would be wrong to ignore their preference.

      1.2.1.1. Con:
      Elected politicians frequently fail to fulfill [111] their campaign promises, suggesting that they are not concerned with obeying voters. Thus, it is feasible for politicians to still act to reduce harm by defunding the police.

        1.2.1.1.1. Con:
        A government that ignores the will of its voters rules without consent (p. 1 [112]). Consent is vital to treating people with dignity, and should not be ignored.

        1.2.1.1.2. Pro:
        Americans report very low rates of trust [113] in their politicians, demonstrating that their mandate is not necessarily bolstered by the faith of the people.

        1.2.1.1.3. Con:
        Low levels of political trust [114] can fuel populism (p. 263 [115]), making nations vulnerable to a number of negative outcomes. Thus, it is better that politicians not make a habit of breaking promises.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      Law and order can be achieved in many ways; there is no reason to believe that the police are the sole means through which crime rates can be lowered.

        1.2.1.2.1. Pro:
        Crime rates can be reduced through policies varying from increasing the cost of alcohol [116] to investing [117] in local infrastructure, neither of which necessitates police involvement.

        1.2.1.2.2. Con:
        While police are not the sole means of reducing crime rates, they are often involved in effective policies, e.g., those that increase deterrence [118].

      1.2.1.3. Pro:
      The fact that Americans still trust law enforcement [119] demonstrates that the police are still well-situated to deal with the majority of issues.

        1.2.1.3.1. Pro:
        The presence of uniformed police officers increases the perceived [120] security of citizens, demonstrating the extent to which Americans trust the police.

        1.2.1.3.2. Con:
        Americans trust [121] the service providers most likely to take on work if the police were defunded, e.g., firefighters, paramedics, and healthcare workers, far more than they trust the police.

    1.2.2. Pro:
    Without a sense of security [122], Americans are unlikely to be able to act freely. Thus, the police are vital in helping Americans live good lives.

      1.2.2.1. Con:
      Measures that are designed to increase the security of American citizens often place undue restrictions upon their freedom.

        1.2.2.1.1. Pro:
        The PATRIOT Act [123] of 2001 increased the federal government's surveillance [124] capabilities while restricting the privacy and civil liberties [125] of American citizens.

        1.2.2.1.2. Con:
        There is a necessary trade-off between freedom and security that limits the freedom of the individual to preserve the safety of the public, as seen with seatbelt laws (p. 237 [126]).

        1.2.2.1.3. Pro:
        Only a minor portion [127] of the American public favor restrictions on their freedom, making such measures illegitimate.

      1.2.2.2. Pro:
      Without a sense of security [128], people often become paranoid and experience a range of harms [129]. If the police can be used to mitigate this, then it is sensible to enact such policies.

        1.2.2.2.1. Pro:
        Most Americans tend to percieve crime rates as rising [130], but most do not consider this to be a major risk to global society (p. 89 [131]). As such, one may infer that Americans are confident in the police's ability to mitigate rising crime.

        1.2.2.2.2. Pro:
        Americans may feel that their paranoia is prudent [132], and, thus, be more reluctant to make changes that signal otherwise. Thus, defunding the police is unlikely to be a success.

        1.2.2.2.3. Con:
        Paranoia is often propagated [133] in America through the media and by politicians. If these actors were more responsible, it could cause people to detach and distance themselves from the police.

      1.2.2.3. Pro:
      Policing is often necessary to protect one's negative liberties [134]—ensuring people are unconstrained by violence. This then gives people the capacity to access positive freedoms.

        1.2.2.3.1. Pro:
        Police enforce state laws that protect [135] those providing abortion services [136]. The capacity to seek healthcare without threat of violence increases people's capacity for self-determination.

        1.2.2.3.2. Con:
        For many, freedom from harm or harassment [137] is counter to the presence [138] of police in their life. Thus, major reform is necessary for all to be truly autonomous.

    1.2.3. Con:
    Current policing policies legally justify cases of excessive police force [139]. Defunding the police would ensure that civilians are not subjected to extrajudicial violence by the state.

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      The term "lethal force" is subjective, meaning that it's difficult to ascertain what it actually entails. This gray area makes it difficult to train officers appropriately.

        1.2.3.1.1. Con:
        While somewhat dependent on context, lethal force, or deadly force [140], has a clear legal definition.

        1.2.3.1.2. Pro:
        Because the term is so subjective, investigations are needed to ascertain if the use of lethal force was justified [141], or if a police officer really used lethal force.

      1.2.3.2. Con:
      The number of incidents [142] of excessive police force is minuscule [143] compared to the overall population size.

        1.2.3.2.1. Con:
        Regardless of the actual number of people killed by the police annually, the threat and constant worry of such an event cause many people to dread interacting with the police (p. 1 [144]).

        1.2.3.2.2. Pro:
        A Black person is far more likely to be a victim of violence at the hands of another civilian [145] than the police [146] (p. 1 [147]). Thus, it is necessary to maintain existing police funding to tackle violent crime.

          1.2.3.2.2.1. Con:
          Approximately one in every 1,000 Black men in America will be killed by police (p. 16793 [148]); this is a staggeringly high number, which should be reduced regardless of how many Black people are killed by other methods.

        1.2.3.2.3. Con:
        In the eyes of many, the police are meant [149] to protect and serve the general public. They should be held to a higher standard than the public and avoid causing undue harm.

      1.2.3.3. Pro:
      Under the doctrine of qualified immunity [150], police officers are often immune from civil suits for violent misconduct. Defunding the police would make it harder to argue this defense in court.

        1.2.3.3.1. Con:
        Without the doctrine of qualified immunity, police officers wouldn't be able to perform their jobs without fear of baseless legal action [151].

        1.2.3.3.2. Con:
        US police departments across the country have paid out hundreds of millions of dollars [152] to victims of police misconduct cases, demonstrating the limitations of the qualified immunity defense.

        1.2.3.3.3. Pro:
        Qualified immunity often protects "truly egregious conduct by police [153]." Unless such a doctrine is removed, those harmed by the police are unlikely to find justice.

        1.2.3.3.4. Pro:
        Current statutes require prosecutors to ensure that there are high levels of replication [154] and, thus, legal precedent to successfully prove that qualified immunity is legally inapplicable in cases of violent misconduct.

        1.2.3.3.5. Con:
        Qualified immunity does not protect the police from criminal charges [155]; ordinary citizens can file such charges [156] against officers to pursue a stronger and more permanent form of justice, which would otherwise be inaccessible [157] through civil suits.

      1.2.3.4. Con:
      As police unions have both significant influence and power [158] to protect officers accused of using excessive force, efforts to defund the police should be focused on weakening the unions.

        1.2.3.4.1. Con:
        Individual [159] police officers are responsible for abusing their power, not the unions that protect them.

          1.2.3.4.1.1. Con:
          Police unions actively support [160] officers that act in a manner that is inconsistent with both their training and the law, making it very difficult to achieve justice for misconduct.

          1.2.3.4.1.2. Pro:
          The culture of many police unions has shifted from one of unilateral solidarity to a model of individual accountability [161], where members are held responsible for their actions.

          1.2.3.4.1.3. Con:
          Police unions abuse their power through collective bargaining tactics [162] that protect officers in cases of gross individual misconduct.

          1.2.3.4.1.4. Pro:
          Most police unions are legally required to offer equal protection [163] to all members, so unions cannot be held responsible for defending officers that abuse their power.

        1.2.3.4.2. Pro:
        Police unions actively interfere with policing reforms by lobbying [164] politicians to prevent the creation and adoption of legislation that could curtail their power.

        1.2.3.4.3. Pro:
        Police unions often allow [165] bad cops to continue working and climb the ranks of their respective precincts. This sets a harmful precedent that can facilitate more poor behavior.

      1.2.3.5. Pro:
      The police should be discouraged from using violence at any time as there is very little recourse for civilians to defend themselves.

        1.2.3.5.1. Con:
        As per District of Colombia v. Heller [166], civilians may possess weapons explicitly for the purpose of self-defense. This ruling could make it possible to push back against excessive police force.

        1.2.3.5.2. Pro:
        The US far surpasses [167] other nations in the number of civilians killed by police, both in whole terms and per capita; the annual rate of US police killings is triple [168] that of Canada.

        1.2.3.5.3. Con:
        Excessive force implies that an action goes beyond the guidelines of what is accepted. This is a problem caused by individual officers [169], not the policies themselves.

    1.2.4. Con:
    Defunding the police would actually make American citizens, residents, and visitors safer.

      1.2.4.1. Con:
      Defunding the police could make the police less able [170] to protect [171] themselves in dangerous situations.

        1.2.4.1.1. Pro:
        Due to the strength [172] of police unions, police departments with reduced funding might opt to minimize training expenditures [173] as opposed to firing officers, resulting in an under-trained force.

        1.2.4.1.2. Pro:
        Defunding the police could reduce the number of officers on payroll, forcing the remaining officers to work longer hours such that they are more susceptible [174] to injury or death.

        1.2.4.1.3. Pro:
        Protecting officers by maintaining their funding will also protect their children and families, given that officers who die on the job leave behind two children [175], on average.

        1.2.4.1.4. Con:
        Providing officers with adequate body armor is not particularly expensive [176], especially when compared to the amount spent [177] on arms annually.

        1.2.4.1.5. Con:
        As per the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Census of Fatal Occupational Injuries [178], several professions are significantly more dangerous than police work.

      1.2.4.2. Con:
      Police work is likely to decline with defunding due to either decreased pay [179] or reduced union funding resulting in increased arbitration losses [180]. This may make it harder to deal with crime adequately.

        1.2.4.2.1. Con:
        The median police salary in 2023 is $65,790 [181], which is higher than the median salary [182] for all Americans. Universally lowering this wage would discentivize highly qualified individuals from applying for police jobs, which could reduce the quality of police work.

        1.2.4.2.2. Pro:
        More than 240 million [183] emergency calls are made each year, many of which require immediate police response. Defunding the police could increase response times.

          1.2.4.2.2.1. Con:
          It is estimated that only one percent [184] of 911 calls report violent crimes in progress. These are the only ones that should be handled by the police.

          1.2.4.2.2.2. Pro:
          Long wait times [185] for emergency calls are already a major problem in many places in the United States. Due to staffing shortages, hold times [186] for 911 calls are generally increasing across the nation.

            1.2.4.2.2.2.1. Con:
            Public safety telecommunicators operate independently from the police service. As such, defunding the police would have no discernable impact on 911 hold times.

          1.2.4.2.2.3. Pro:
          Defunding the police could negatively impact firefighters [187] as they would lack adequate back-up when responding to calls.

        1.2.4.2.3. Con:
        As the majority of policing resources are spent on minor offenses that do not threaten public safety, current [188] high levels of police spending do not meaningfully benefit society.

        1.2.4.2.4. Con:
        Unlike other public sectors, there is little evidence [189] to suggest that employing more police officers produces a discernible improvement in service delivery (i.e. crime reduction). Reducing the number of police officers, therefore, could provide a sensible cost-benefit solution to help underfunded public services.

          1.2.4.2.4.1. Pro:
          Using police funding to increase the salaries of care workers, for example, could increase [190] job satisfaction and staff retention. Improving upon these metrics is likely to improve the stability and quality [191] of care services in the US.

          1.2.4.2.4.2. Con:
          Reducing funding will mean that there are fewer police officers and that those remaining will have to work more, exacerbating burnout [192] and low morale [193].

        1.2.4.2.5. Pro:
        Without policing, justice is often less feasible [194], as people commit more crimes and go unpunished.

          1.2.4.2.5.1. Pro:
          Defunding the police could mean that criminals perpetrate more crimes, as there will be less risk [195] of being caught. This creates a feedback loop that undermines the deterrent effect [196].

            1.2.4.2.5.1.1. Pro:
            Several US cities have reversed measures previously implemented to defund the police due to spikes in violent crime [197].

            1.2.4.2.5.1.2. Con:
            CCTV can deter [198] crime in specific contexts; this can act in place of the police if necessary.

          1.2.4.2.5.2. Con:
          Many small towns do not have their own police departments due to a lack of funding [199]; there is no increase in violent crime there.

      1.2.4.3. Pro:
      The police are becoming increasingly militarized [200], expanding their firepower and weaponry far beyond [201] the scope necessary to respond to standard crimes—often with poor oversight [202].

        1.2.4.3.1. Pro:
        Warrior policing [203] has increased the militarization of America’s police departments. It has directly contributed to a string of high-profile police shootings [204] of Black citizens.

          1.2.4.3.1.1. Pro:
          Warrior-style training fundamentally alters the psychology [205] of officers and consequently diminishes their capacity to effectively engage in community policing.

          1.2.4.3.1.2. Con:
          Warrior-style policing is necessary to effectively counteract the rising [206] level of violence against police in the US.

        1.2.4.3.2. Pro:
        According to investigations, militarizing the police hasn't resulted in a reduction of crime; instead, it has harmed police reputation (p. 9181 [207]).

          1.2.4.3.2.1. Con:
          Conversely, some political demographics [208] tend to support the increased militarization of the police. It may, in fact, increase their faith in the police.

          1.2.4.3.2.2. Pro:
          The reputational damage incurred by militarization affects the police's ability to effectively investigate crimes, particularly among alienated communities (p. 5 [209]).

        1.2.4.3.3. Con:
        The level of militarization of a police department is directly related to the criminal activity faced by the agency or community [210].

"The Robin Hood effect is a phenomenon most commonly used in discussions of income inequality. In a Robin Hood effect, income is redistributed so that economic inequality is reduced.
