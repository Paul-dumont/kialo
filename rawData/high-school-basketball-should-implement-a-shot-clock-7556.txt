Discussion Name:
High school basketball should implement a shot clock

1. Thesis:
High school basketball should implement a shot clock

  1.1. Pro:
  Implementation would prepare students what they have to do on the college level anyway.

    1.1.1. Con:
    While this might be a compelling reason for some, only 3.4% of high school basketball players go on to play at some level in the NCAA.Estimated probability of competing in college athletics [1]

  1.2. Pro:
  The implement of a shot clock at the high school level would give kids the challenge of understanding to run plays and work to together to limit the time wasted.

  1.3. Con:
  It would make students more competitive and therefore less peaceful.

  1.4. Pro:
  The necessity to at least take a shot within the shot clock time frame would speed up the pace of the game and avoid game-stalling tactics that take away from the game.

    1.4.1. Pro:
    In the 8-minute quarters of high school basketball it is often possible to burn the last 1-2 minutes of clock in a quarter on a single possession by just using a weave or other stalling tactic.

    1.4.2. Con:
    Recent data has shown that the shot clock does not improve pace or increase scoring.Numbers suggest high school shot clock doesn't necessarily lead to more scoring [2]

  1.5. Con:
  It would be somewhat expensive for schools to implement, as they would need additiions to existing scoreboard systems, as well as an additional individual at the table to manage the clock.

    1.5.1. Con:
    It would primarily be a one-time expense that most schools could afford if given enough lead time for implementation.

  1.6. Pro:
  A shot clock would help place more emphasis on the defensive aspects of the game, since preventing, defensively, a team on offense from shooting (or getting a good shot) would have an immediate reward.

  1.7. Con:
  It would take away from some teams the ability to run a methodical, deliberate half-court offense that emphasizes patience for the best shot opportunity.

    1.7.1. Con:
    There are college teams that still run methodical half-court offenses but are still able to function quite well within the limitations of a shot clock.  High school teams could make similar adjustments.
