Discussion Name:
Online learning is the future of education

1. Thesis:
Online learning is the future of education.

  1.1. Pro:
  Online learning can have benefits for mental health.

    1.1.1. Con:
    Isolation caused by online learning can drive towards anxiety and depression [1].

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      Online learning can connect the person with other students or teachers at any time, decreasing the isolation effect and creating a more sociable environment.

        1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
        Online communication tools such as video calls ( Skype ) help students feel less lonely while studying thanks to online face to face communication with other people.

      1.1.1.2. Pro:
      Online learning can inhance the already increasing loneliness in society by minimising the social aspects of study

    1.1.2. Pro:
    Online learning can be good for people suffering from anxiety [2].

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      Technology illiteracy [3] when it comes to online learning can increase mental anxiety.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      Online learning can be beneficial with students suffering from social anxiety [4].

    1.1.3. Con:
    A weak internet connection may become a source of stress for students which can negatively impact their learning progress [5].

    1.1.4. Con:
    Students may suffer from debilitating mental distress due to technological overconsumption that can harm their psychological health. [6]

  1.2. Con:
  Online learning can lead to overexposure to digital devices [7] which can be potentially harmful. (p.1)

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Overexposure to digital devices creates limitations on physical movement [8] which can have potential negative side effects.

    1.2.2. Con:
    Even if online learning requires digital devices, leisure time can still be spent away from digital devices.

    1.2.3. Con:
    Traditional learning also uses digital devices [9].

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      Traditional learning does not (and doesn't have to) use digital services to the same extent that online learning would.

    1.2.4. Pro:
    "Zoom fatigue" [10] and the "blue light" emitted by the screens can have negative consequences on behaviour and can drive towards a state of depression.

      1.2.4.1. Con:
      There are easy alternatives such as blue light glasses [11], to avoid the negative effects of blue light overexposure.

      1.2.4.2. Pro:
      "Zoom fatigue [12]" refers to the negative health effect that virtual interactions have on us.

    1.2.5. Pro:
    Online learning can promote a dependency to technology. [13]

    1.2.6. Pro:
    Online learning can promote a dependency to technology.

  1.3. Pro:
  Online learning is more efficient than traditional learning.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    If a global pandemic [14] strikes again, students are better prepared to continue their education with online learning.

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      Online and personalized learning [15] is considered to be the future for education in the wake of the COVID-19 pandemic.

      1.3.1.2. Con:
      Online classes during the first year of the COVID-19 pandemic, have shown that students are not psychologically prepared to continue with online learning [16].

    1.3.2. Con:
    Online learning compared to traditional learning lacks the development of interpersonal skills [17].

    1.3.3. Con:
    Traditional learning creates a group of like-minded people [18] and facilitates interaction in a more personal way.

    1.3.4. Pro:
    Online learning has shown a reduced need for funds in order to attend school [19].

      1.3.4.1. Pro:
      Online teaching can make the best teachers of the world accessible to every human being on earth.

    1.3.5. Pro:
    E-learning increases digital literacy [20], a necessary skill for the future.

      1.3.5.1. Pro:
      Technology is going to be a very important part of our future [21], therefore the future of education is based on the digital literacy [22] of the next generations.

      1.3.5.2. Con:
      Digital literacy is a pre-requisite for an effective e-learning environment [23]. (p.1)

      1.3.5.3. Pro:
      With online learning, it is easier for teachers to communicate, give instructions, and provide links to different resources.

        1.3.5.3.1. Con:
        Unless teachers master digital literacy [24] skills, they might struggle to provide resourceful links and digital instructions.

          1.3.5.3.1.1. Pro:
          Digital literacy [25] refers to "an individual's ability to find, evaluate, and compose clear information through writing and other media on various digital platforms" using technology.

          1.3.5.3.1.2. Con:
          Current educational teachings include digital literacy [26] to prepare future teachers to online learning.

          1.3.5.3.1.3. Con:
          Online learning by default teaches valuable computer skills necessary to our day and age

        1.3.5.3.2. Pro:
        Technology offers the possibility to integrate interactive tools such as screen sharing or the creation of polls within the classroom.

          1.3.5.3.2.1. Con:
          Digital sessions can be quite stressful for teachers [27].

          1.3.5.3.2.2. Con:
          By integrating too many interactive tools, sessions can be delayed due to technical issues [28].

          1.3.5.3.2.3. Pro:
          Video calls allow facilitators to mute attendees that are disturbing others or aren't engaged in the session, in order to create a better educational environment.

        1.3.5.3.3. Con:
        Some classes, like science labs, need access to hands-on resources to be effective.

          1.3.5.3.3.1. Pro:
          You can not learn team sports online.

        1.3.5.3.4. Pro:
        Teachers can provide a greater depth and breadth of resources [29] and information thanks to the online resources available with e-learning.

      1.3.5.4. Pro:
      E-learning offers online tutoring [30] if needed, and it is more reliable and hassle-free.

    1.3.6. Con:
    Online learning is subject to technical failures of whatever device/program is being used.

    1.3.7. Pro:
    Online learning opens the opportunity for collaborative learning [31].

      1.3.7.1. Pro:
      Collaborative learning has a positive impact on student's academic achievements [32] and the development of their social competencies.

      1.3.7.2. Con:
      Groups of learners based on different countries can face difficulties when collaborating as they might be in different time zones.

      1.3.7.3. Pro:
      Collaborative learning opens the opportunity to increase students' learning circles [33].

        1.3.7.3.1. Pro:
        Online learning has a global reach because learners can collaborate across different countries.

          1.3.7.3.1.1. Pro:
          Online learning can help students to learn different languages and cultures as they might interact with students all over the world.

          1.3.7.3.1.2. Con:
          Unless education is globalized [34], a global reach could also create many cultural differences.

        1.3.7.3.2. Con:
        Collaborative learning across different countries can become difficult and frustrating due to different time zones [35].

      1.3.7.4. Con:
      With online collaborative learning, it is harder to build meaningful relationships.

  1.4. Pro:
  With online learning, schedules are more flexible [36].

    1.4.1. Con:
    Flexible schedules can easily increase procrastination [37].

      1.4.1.1. Pro:
      With flexible schedules, it can be more difficult to develop self-accountability [38].

      1.4.1.2. Con:
      There are many different reasons why procrastination [39] might happen (such as lack of motivation), independently of the type of schedule.

    1.4.2. Con:
    A fixed schedule [40] it's more organized and efficient and requires less self-direction.

      1.4.2.1. Con:
      According to a recent study [41], adaptive or flexible scheduling outperformed fixed schedule at immediate and delayed tests of retention.

    1.4.3. Pro:
    Flexible schedules can improve students' quality of life.

      1.4.3.1. Con:
      Flexible schedules can also create a lack of routine [42] which can, in turn, increase anxiety levels.

      1.4.3.2. Pro:
      With flexible schedules fewer people need to commute.

        1.4.3.2.1. Con:
        Studying from home might not always improve the students' quality of life.

          1.4.3.2.1.1. Pro:
          Studying from home can increase the likelihood of suffering from social isolation [43].

          1.4.3.2.1.2. Con:
          Flexible schedules [44] don't imply studying from home. It refers to a"creative use of the time in the school day in an attempt to match the instructional time and format to the learning needs of students."

        1.4.3.2.2. Pro:
        There would be less traffic because flexible online schedules reduce the need to commute at specific times.

          1.4.3.2.2.1. Con:
          During the early parts of quarantine in the US, internet services experienced massive issues with lag [45].

      1.4.3.3. Pro:
      Flexible schedules allow students to sleep longer if needed.

    1.4.4. Con:
    The future of education is based on a responsive scheduling [46] instead of a flexible or fixed schedule.

      1.4.4.1. Pro:
      Responsive scheduling allows teachers to quickly adjust to the specific needs [47] of each student.

    1.4.5. Pro:
    E-learning environments encourage people to self-manage, self-direct, improve their planning skills, and to be more independent and resourceful.

      1.4.5.1. Pro:
      Schedules can be adjusted to each person's working mode.

      1.4.5.2. Con:
      E-learning can be challenging for students with attention-deficit and hyperactivity disorder(ADHD) [48].

      1.4.5.3. Con:
      If a user is habituated to face-to-face learning (F2F), it might be very challenging to jump into the online format.

        1.4.5.3.1. Con:
        The COVID-19 pandemic has proven that both students and teachers can quickly adapt to online education.

          1.4.5.3.1.1. Con:
          Even if some managed to adapt, the adaptation has been uneven [49].

          1.4.5.3.1.2. Pro:
          The access to information and communication technologies (ICT) tools were instrumental in quickly adapting to online teaching during COVID-19 school closures [50].

        1.4.5.3.2. Pro:
        There should be a training-transition period, to ensure that all users can adapt to online learning and that they can develop their self-direction and digital literacy skills.

          1.4.5.3.2.1. Pro:
          Digital literacy skills [51] are essential for students to progress with online education.

      1.4.5.4. Pro:
      E-learning environments make the learning process more efficient and attractive [52] thus increasing the self-discipline of students. (p.1)

    1.4.6. Pro:
    E-learning allows for asynchronous learning [53] which has many benefits.

      1.4.6.1. Pro:
      If e-learning is asynchronous [54] it allows students to learn when and where they prefer.

        1.4.6.1.1. Pro:
        Asynchronous learning can help single parents to study [55].

        1.4.6.1.2. Con:
        E-learning requires more self-discipline [56].

      1.4.6.2. Con:
      Asynchronous learning can also cause a lack of motivation [57].

  1.5. Con:
  Online learning can create a learning gap [58].

    1.5.1. Con:
    Online learning is filling the higher education gap [59].

      1.5.1.1. Pro:
      Online learning can help colleges provide education to refugees [60].

      1.5.1.2. Pro:
      There are many options to study free online college courses [61].

        1.5.1.2.1. Con:
        Free online college might not help future students [62] become workers because of the existence of a highly demanding market.

        1.5.1.2.2. Pro:
        Harvard University [63] offers free online courses.

        1.5.1.2.3. Pro:
        Some community colleges provide free courses for students to take [64]. [1] [65]

      1.5.1.3. Con:
      Even if online learning can help fill the higher education gap, online higher education certifications are not professionally recognized [66]. (p.5)

    1.5.2. Con:
    Online learning creates the opportunity for a more individualized instruction [67] which can help students with different needs and backgrounds.

    1.5.3. Pro:
    Online learning can increase the population's digital divide [68].

      1.5.3.1. Pro:
      Not all users have access to technological advances, which can become a barrier to education.

        1.5.3.1.1. Pro:
        According to the UNESCO, there were 826 million students around the world without computers [69] and 706 million without Internet access in 2020. (p.2)

          1.5.3.1.1.1. Pro:
          Online learning is ineffectual for countries who have slow internet connection. [70]

          1.5.3.1.1.2. Con:
          The fact that technology and Internet access is not globalized yet doesn't mean the online education might not be the future of education.

            1.5.3.1.1.2.1. Pro:
            Technology, internet and online environment are increasingly used in every single profession [71], therefore it would make sense to shift to online learning education.

            1.5.3.1.1.2.2. Con:
            If online learning becomes the future of education despite million of students not having access to it, inequalities would be reinforced.

          1.5.3.1.1.3. Pro:
          Students who do not have access to gadgets to utilize for learning objectives during online learning are affected.

            1.5.3.1.1.3.1. Pro:
            A survey done by Unicef in East Asia [72] found that most student phones are supplied by parents and regularly handed down from other persons. Multiple techniques have been devised for accessing devices and information in circumstances when children do not have their own devices, such as sharing their parents' phones to use WhatsApp for online learning.

        1.5.3.1.2. Con:
        Governments [73] are providing technology resources to more vulnerable students.

      1.5.3.2. Con:
      The digital divide will close over time because more students will have access to high-quality education materials on the public internet.

        1.5.3.2.1. Pro:
        Khan academy [74] already impacts educational outcomes [75] for those with access.

          1.5.3.2.1.1. Con:
          Some specific programs to close the digital divide could result in urban/wealthy education becoming the template to follow in other areas.

      1.5.3.3. Pro:
      In the U.S. it is estimated that 1 in 10 of the poorest children has little or no access [76] to technology for learning.

        1.5.3.3.1. Con:
        Providing internet [77] and technology for students (in most US states) [78] is significantly cheaper than maintaining larger schooling facilities for more in-person instruction.

    1.5.4. Pro:
    Not all age groups can benefit the same from online learning.

      1.5.4.1. Con:
      Online lessons can be adapted to different age groups.

      1.5.4.2. Pro:
      Children need face to face contact with other children in order to develop properly.

      1.5.4.3. Pro:
      While adult learners could benefit from the flexibility of learning, younger learners, need outdoor time to play and socialize.

        1.5.4.3.1. Con:
        Many elders have issues with modern technology [79], making e-courses difficult for them.

          1.5.4.3.1.1. Con:
          Many elders have lower compliance compared to youth [80], even regarding their own safety [81], anyways. They merely prefer to blame technology and complex interfaces [82].

    1.5.5. Con:
    The gap described in this article is that poorer nations cannot educate their citizens while wealthy countries can (because of COVID). A future with robust online education would actually close this gap.

  1.6. Con:
  Online learning contains many distractions and awful setbacks to students that may stop them from being determined to studying.

  1.7. Pro:
  Online learning allows to have more information about each student, which serves to have a large number of indicators that serve to make a more complete diagnosis, both of a student and of a group of students

  1.8. Con:
  With online learning, students will lack face-to-face interaction with teachers and other students, which will be detrimental to training the social skills they need in the future, as well as to decrease the efficiency of learning.

  1.9. Con:
  School is not just learning. It's also doing social activity and social life.

  1.10. Pro:
  Online learning offers unmatched flexibility, allowing learners to study at their own pace and schedule. It makes quality education accessible to people from any location, removing geographical barriers. It also provides a wide range of courses and resources, helping students upskill faster and stay industry-ready. Overall, it empowers continuous learning without disrupting personal or professional commitments.

  1.11. Con:
  Online learning is not a replacement of face-to-face learning.

    1.11.1. Con:
    Online learning is just another typology of learning/teaching, which could replace or support face-to-face learning.

    1.11.2. Pro:
    Teachers might struggle more when e-facilitating.

      1.11.2.1. Pro:
      Online learners can get easily distracted with online lessons.

      1.11.2.2. Pro:
      It is more difficult for teachers to check the quality and originality of the learners' work in order to avoid cheating.

        1.11.2.2.1. Con:
        There are online tools available to avoid plagiarism [83].

          1.11.2.2.1.1. Con:
          Plagiarism is not the only issue when it comes to cheating in online learning environments. That's why schools take extra precautions [84] against different methods of cheating.

          1.11.2.2.1.2. Pro:
          Technology has played an important role in decreasing plagiarism [85] at an academic level.

          1.11.2.2.1.3. Con:
          Sadly, these tools are not foolproof, as there are many students who know how to paraphrase in such a way to circumvent the tools.

        1.11.2.2.2. Pro:
        Students can easily access online information [86] during an exam without teachers being able to control it.

          1.11.2.2.2.1. Pro:
          No matter the typology of education, students tend to find ways of cheating.

            1.11.2.2.2.1.1. Pro:
            Sites like Peeranswer [87] can be accessed easily via phones.

              1.11.2.2.2.1.1.1. Pro:
              Peeranswer has had difficulty with search engines recently, making instructors having issues finding out if a student cheated.

                1.11.2.2.2.1.1.1.1. Con:
                If the question's answer was taken from another site like Brainly, the educator would instantly know.

                  1.11.2.2.2.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
                  Simply google searching a question to find the source is a great way to tell if a student did in fact cheat.

                  1.11.2.2.2.1.1.1.1.2. Con:
                  Paraphrasing; the act of rewording something to be somewhat original, is one of many tools in the cheater's toolbox, as it can circumvent this if done well enough.

                1.11.2.2.2.1.1.1.2. Pro:
                QuestionCove [88], a more reliable site, also does not have its questions appear on search engines.

              1.11.2.2.2.1.1.2. Con:
              In some educational environments, the sites are blacklisted on the school's network, most notably being Brainly.

                1.11.2.2.2.1.1.2.1. Con:
                These schools however often forget to ban the different regional variants of Brainly, such as eodev.com or nosdevoirs.fr, which can be problematic with the advent of site translation tools coming built-into browsers.

                1.11.2.2.2.1.1.2.2. Con:
                Phones often do not require connection to routers if the student is using mobile data.

            1.11.2.2.2.1.2. Pro:
            Cheaters have learned how to hide their tracks online, sometimes by getting answers wrong intentionally.

              1.11.2.2.2.1.2.1. Pro:
              It has often been known that getting a B or C on an assignment is going to be less noticeable than that of an A.

            1.11.2.2.2.1.3. Con:
            The fact that students will probably find a way of cheating shows that cheating is not an important factor to take into account when discussing about online learning.

          1.11.2.2.2.2. Con:
          There are many strategies to prevent cheating during online exams.

            1.11.2.2.2.2.1. Pro:
            By creating questions that require higher order thinking [89], students won't be able to answer with a simple web search, instead they will need to apply the skills of analysis, synthesis and evaluation.

              1.11.2.2.2.2.1.1. Con:
              These questions are often not used in programs such as Edgenuity [90] and Accelerate [91].

                1.11.2.2.2.2.1.1.1. Pro:
                Even when higher order thinking questions are used, they are automatically graded via an incredibly ineffective system.

                  1.11.2.2.2.2.1.1.1.1. Pro:
                  On Edgenuity, their grading system is built on keywords that are grouped together in a specific way, so if a student said "therefore, this action is bad" instead of "so, this action is morally unjust", they could lose points even though both sentences mean the exact same thing.

      1.11.2.3. Con:
      Teachers don't have free access to training programs, and it's difficult to level up skills very quickly.

      1.11.2.4. Con:
      Sometimes facilitators don't know how to efficiently manage e-crowds.

        1.11.2.4.1. Con:
        If online learning is the future, society will change dramatically since facilities will either start to employ digitally literate personnel or educate current employees on this subject.

        1.11.2.4.2. Pro:
        Sometimes teachers are not "fluent" at using tech tools.

          1.11.2.4.2.1. Pro:
          Many teachers are not used to virtual classroom or the use of technology in the classroom [92].

          1.11.2.4.2.2. Con:
          Facilitators will increase, in the future, their e-facilitation skills and they'll become better at designing meaningful e-learning experiences and facilitation plans.

            1.11.2.4.2.2.1. Pro:
            An increasing amount of research is being published to help teacher improve their skills to integrate technology in education [93].

            1.11.2.4.2.2.2. Con:
            The design of high-quality training programs will be needed in order to improve e-facilitators skills.
