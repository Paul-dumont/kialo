Discussion Name:
Was the 2020 UK recession inevitable even without the Covid-19 pandemic?

1. Thesis:
The 2020 UK recession was inevitable even without the Covid-19 pandemic.

  1.1. Con:
  The world economy was growing, and the UK would have been able to partake in that, resulting in an economic boom rather than a recession.

        1.1.1.1.1. Con:
        The UK has a much more developed, integrated, and efficiently run economy than North Korea.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      The UK's growth rate over the past several decades has largely reflected [2] the global trends of growth and decline.

      1.1.1.3. Pro:
      As an island nation, the UK is especially reliant on maritime trade [3], which had greatly slowed down during 2019.

      1.1.1.4. Con:
      Given the high levels of foreign direct investment [4] in the UK, a booming global economy would likely have a positive impact domestically.

    1.1.2. Con:
    The International Monetary Fund's assessment [5] in 2019 indicated a slowing down of growth globally.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      The slowdown [6] was largely the result of "trade barriers, renewed financial stress, and sharper-than-expected slowdowns in several major economies".

        1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
        The US-China trade war [7] played a major role in the slowdown of global GDP in 2019.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      Slower growth doesn't necessarily equate to a recession. The length and severity of the slowdown was unpredictable at the time.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Global GDP was at its highest level ever [8] in 2019, prior to the Covid-19 crisis.

      1.1.3.1. Con:
      The global economy grew at its slowest pace [9] in a decade in 2019, indicating that there were significant underlying problems.

      1.1.3.2. Pro:
      The stock of UK FDI invested abroad was £1.50 trillion [10] in 2019, up from £1.45 trillion in 2018, indicating that a growing global economy would provide rewards to UK investors.

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      The UK's largest trading partners [11] were relatively strong performers prior to the pandemic, including the US and Germany.

    1.1.4. Pro:
    The UK has several internationally recognized brands that would benefit from global prosperity.

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      The number of top 500 global corporation based in the UK was slipping [12] before the Covid-19 pandemic.

      1.1.4.2. Pro:
      Many UK companies are industry leaders [13] with large overseas markets.

    1.1.5. Pro:
    The City of London is one of the top financial centres in the world. The UK would have therefore benefited immensely from a growing global economy.

          1.1.5.2.1.2. Pro:
          London is fortunate to have good geography [19] for investors who want to capture both Asian and US markets.
