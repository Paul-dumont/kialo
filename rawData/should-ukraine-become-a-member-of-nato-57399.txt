Discussion Name:
Should Ukraine become a member of NATO?

1. Thesis:
Ukraine should become a member of NATO.

  1.1. Pro:
  Joining NATO would pull Ukraine further away from Russia’s sphere of influence [1].

    1.1.1. Con:
    It is unlikely that Russia will allow Ukraine to slip from its sphere of influence.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      Russia would have no choice but to allow Ukraine to make its own policy decisions or risk full scale war with NATO.

        1.1.1.1.1. Con:
        Russia could continue its efforts to destabilize [2] Ukraine through subversive means, rather than through a full scale war.

          1.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
          Russia has a long history of interfering [3] in democratic elections in countries that it wants to influence.

      1.1.1.2. Pro:
      Ukraine is an important country [4] within Europe that has close historic ties with Russia; therefore Putin doesn't want to lose Ukraine from its sphere of influence.

    1.1.2. Con:
    Ukraine is a historical place for Russian identity [5], so it might not be justified to pull Ukraine further away from Russia's influence.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      Many Ukrainians speak Russian [6] as either their native language or second language.

        1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
        The regions of Ukraine that Russia has annexed are majority Russian speakers [7].

        1.1.2.1.2. Con:
        Speaking the same language has no bearing on national sovereignty or the right of a nation to chose its own foreign policy.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      The regions of Donetsk and Luhansk [8] are against Ukrainian integration.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        The spokespeople for these regions are puppets [9] installed by Russia and do not necessarily reflect the will of the Ukrainian people living there.

      1.1.2.3. Con:
      Experts have dismissed Putin’s depiction of Russo-Ukrainian unity as political propaganda [10].

        1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
        Putin's depictions [11] of Ukrainian history are at odds with the historical facts.

        1.1.2.3.2. Con:
        This is an appeal to authority [12] ("argumentum ad verecundiam") fallacy. One can find "experts" with opposite views on this matter.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    NATO membership would draw Ukraine more firmly toward Europe, making it more likely that Ukraine could join the European Union.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      In a July 2022 survey, Ukrainians strongly supported [13] ascension into the EU.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      There is a significant portion [14] of Ukraine that does not want to get closer to Europe but to Russia.

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      The European Commision President Ursula von der Leyen has already stated that "Ukraine is one of our own and we want them in the EU [15]."

        1.1.3.3.1. Pro:
        The EU and Moldova, Georgia, and Ukraine are engaging in talks about EU ascension. [16]

  1.2. Con:
  NATO expansion is itself wrong, and so neither Ukraine nor any other country should join it.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    NATO expansion is destabilizing for Europe.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      Some prominent international relations scholars have argued [17] that NATO expansion has caused Putin to launch his invasion of Ukraine.

        1.2.1.1.1. Con:
        The vast majority of scholars, scientists, and academics have condemned [18] Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      NATO is stabilizing for those countries that are in it, which is why more and more countries are attempting to join [19].

      1.2.1.3. Pro:
      While security threats in Europe have largely come from terrorism in recent years, NATO has remained focused [20] on deterring Russia and spreading democratic values, which is destabilizing for the region.

        1.2.1.3.1. Con:
        Promoting democratic values do more to stabilize [21] a region in the long term than allowing authoritarian aggression to remain unchecked.

        1.2.1.3.2. Pro:
        Russia should be allowed to pursue its own political, cultural, and religious values without pressure to conform to European or democratic values.

          1.2.1.3.2.1. Pro:
          Putin has frequently espoused [22] the view that Russia is an anti-liberal society [23].

          1.2.1.3.2.2. Con:
          Every individual should be free to pursue their own value system, insofar as it does not hurt others. There are plenty of people inside Russia whose freedoms are suppressed [24] by the Putin regime.

            1.2.1.3.2.2.1. Pro:
            Liberal parties, movements, and ideas have been systematically suppressed [25] in Russia under Putin.

      1.2.1.4. Con:
      NATO is essentially the security iteration of the EU's political and economic institutionalization, which has provided the most peaceful and stable period [26] in European history.

      1.2.1.5. Pro:
      NATO bombed Yugoslavia in 1999 without UN Security Council approval – a violation of international law.

        1.2.1.5.1. Pro:
        Human Rights Watch estimated [27] that 489–528 civilians were killed, and there are concerns [28] that they acted recklessly.

    1.2.2. Con:
    NATO has been instrumental in preventing Soviet and Russian aggression since its inception.

      1.2.2.1. Con:
      NATO was not able to stop Putin's invasion of Georgia in 2008 [29] or his annexation of Crimea in 2014 [30].

        1.2.2.1.1. Con:
        Because these attacks, as well as the war in Ukraine, were against non-NATO members, the efficacy of NATO's ability to protect its own members is highlighted.

      1.2.2.2. Pro:
      NATO was responsible for preventing armed conflict [31] in Europe during the Cold War by acting as a deterrent.

      1.2.2.3. Pro:
      The purpose [32] of NATO originally was to defend Europe against Russian aggression for these exact reasons. If Ukraine had become a member, then this war likely wouldn’t have happened.

        1.2.2.3.1. Con:
        Putin has repeatedly denied [33] Ukraine's right to exist as a nation. Considering these erroneous and revisionist [34] views, it is likely he would have launched an invasion regardless of Ukraine's status.

        1.2.2.3.2. Pro:
        Russia's power has declined, causing Putin to adopt a cold war mentality [35] in which Russia wants to control former colonies and rebuild its lost empire.

    1.2.3. Pro:
    Russia has said it would interpret the alliance’s expansion as a direct threat [36].

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      Russia should not be allowed to dictate the defense policies of other countries.

        1.2.3.1.1. Pro:
        It is enshrined [37] in international law (which Russia has agreed to) that each country should be able to decide their own defense postures.

      1.2.3.2. Pro:
      Russia has consistently warned [38] for decades that NATO expansion eastward threatens its national security.

        1.2.3.2.1. Pro:
        NATO has frequently conducted war games [39] near the Russian border that are meant to simulate an attack from Russia. Russia is justified in being concerned about this.

          1.2.3.2.1.1. Con:
          Conversely, NATO is justified in being concerned about Russian aggression, insofar as Russia has launched a war of aggression [40] against Ukraine.

        1.2.3.2.2. Con:
        Despite these warnings, Russia has never stopped NATO from expanding [41] by force.

      1.2.3.3. Con:
      NATO is a defensive alliance [42] that has never threatened [43] the sovereignty of Russia.

        1.2.3.3.1. Con:
        But this was a threat to the power of Putin [44], who was ready to make any sacrifice to preserve it.

  1.3. Con:
  Russia has claimed that the West broke a promise it made in the 1990s not to expand NATO [45].

    1.3.1. Con:
    The situation has dramatically changed since 1990s since this non-binding promise that no conditions were set on.

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      The Soviet Union, to which the promise was given, no longer exists [46].

      1.3.1.2. Pro:
      The Russian Federation did not exist and had not started annexing regions of Ukraine at the time.

  1.4. Con:
  If Ukraine were to join NATO, most of Europe would be dragged into the war against Russia, due to Article 5 [47] of the Washington Treaty.

    1.4.1. Con:
    Knowing that there would be very little chance of winning a war against NATO, Putin might surrender or agree to negotiate peace.

      1.4.1.1. Con:
      If Putin feels his regime is at stake, he will be more likely to use nuclear weapons as a last ditch attempt to stay in power.

        1.4.1.1.1. Con:
        There is no scenario in which Putin uses nuclear weapons and is allowed to remain in power, or likely even survive.

        1.4.1.1.2. Pro:
        Putin may use nuclear weapons as an attempt to show domestic detractors that he is a strong leader [48].

      1.4.1.2. Pro:
      The domestic pressure [49] on Putin to stand down would be enormous, as elites and ordinary Russians would not want to lose more than they already have in a devastating war.

        1.4.1.2.1. Con:
        By crushing any dissent [50] against the war, Putin has shown he has little regard for the interests or desires of the Russian people.

        1.4.1.2.2. Pro:
        Putin has already become increasingly isolated [51] from his top advisors, many of whom have expressed discontent at the war.

      1.4.1.3. Pro:
      Russia is already struggling to defeat [52] Ukraine's relatively small military. They would have no choice but to surrender if they were faced with fighting all of NATO.

        1.4.1.3.1. Con:
        Russia could force conscription on its estimated two million military reserve personnel [53], which would allow them to continue fighting for much longer against more powerful forces.

          1.4.1.3.1.1. Con:
          Many Russian men have already fled the country [54] to avoid the limited mobilization in September 2022. It is likely that many more would flee if total mobilization was announced.

    1.4.2. Pro:
    A full scale war with Russia would be unpopular in NATO countries.

      1.4.2.1. Pro:
      Only 17% [55] of Americans say they should support Ukraine to the point that it initiates a full scale war with Russia.

      1.4.2.2. Con:
      Politicians would likely be able to spin the decision in such a way as to produce a rally around the flag effect [56], boosting support for the war.

        1.4.2.2.1. Pro:
        It is likely that a direct conflict with Russia would boost American military recruitment numbers, which are currently well below [57] their targets.

      1.4.2.3. Con:
      Europeans increasingly see Russia as a direct threat [58] to their own security. Popularity in Europe for a war against Russia would rise if it was considered self-defensive in nature.

    1.4.3. Pro:
    If Ukraine were allowed to join NATO, it would initiate World War Three.

      1.4.3.1. Pro:
      With the West engaged in a war with Russia, China could be more likely to launch an invasion of Taiwan.

        1.4.3.1.1. Pro:
        Feeling pressure from China, Taiwan has been boosting its defense posture [59] since the war in Ukraine.

          1.4.3.1.1.1. Con:
          This makes an invasion less likely, as China would face steeper costs, in terms of manpower and resources, if they invaded a well-defended island.

        1.4.3.1.2. Pro:
        China frequently conducts drills [60] to practice an invasion of Taiwan and has increased the number of incursions [61] into the island's airspace.

        1.4.3.1.3. Con:
        Concerns about a Chinese invasion of Taiwan are often overblown, as it is more likely that China will want to assume control over the island through non-military means [62].

          1.4.3.1.3.1. Pro:
          Beijing can use means such as economic coercion [63] to slowly bring Taiwan under its rule.

        1.4.3.1.4. Con:
        A full scale invasion of Taiwan would likely devastate the Chinese economy and ruin its relations with its neighbors, making an invasion less likely.

      1.4.3.2. Con:
      NATO forces have engaged in military action before without initiating World War III. There is no reason why such a war would start this time.

        1.4.3.2.1. Pro:
        NATO forces were engaged in Afghanistan for twenty years [64] without causing a wider war.

          1.4.3.2.1.1. Con:
          Unlike Russia, Afghanistan did not possess nuclear weapons [65].

        1.4.3.2.2. Con:
        NATO has never been engaged in a full scale war with a opponent as large as Russia.

      1.4.3.3. Pro:
      If Ukraine joins NATO it would mean that most of Europe would be forced to go to war with Russia thus we would enter WW3.

  1.5. Pro:
  Ukraine has had a working partnership [66] with NATO since 1991 [67]; therefore they should be allowed to join it.

    1.5.1. Pro:
    In a July 2022 survey, Ukrainians strongly supported [68] ascension into NATO.

      1.5.1.1. Con:
      This is likely only due to the immediacy of the ongoing war. If Ukrainians had always supported NATO membership, they would have prioritized it long before.

        1.5.1.1.1. Con:
        Ukraine support for joining NATO has been rising since 2014 [69], when Russia illegally annexed Crimea.

      1.5.1.2. Pro:
      Because Ukraine is a European democracy [70] that has wide public support for joining NATO, this should be sufficient grounds on which to at least begin processing their application to join.

    1.5.2. Con:
    A history of cooperation does not preclude membership in an organization.

      1.5.2.1. Pro:
      Despite close, long-term cooperation [71] with the EU, Switzerland is not a member.

      1.5.2.2. Con:
      It should preclude membership in this instance, as Ukraine faces an existential crisis due to Russian aggression, which NATO was designed to prevent [72].

    1.5.3. Con:
    Ukraine still hasn't fulfilled [73] the requirements needed to become a NATO member.

      1.5.3.1. Pro:
      The process [74] to becoming a NATO member is long and difficult, requiring the unanimous consent of all existing members.

        1.5.3.1.1. Pro:
        It is unlikely that member countries like Turkey would agree to NATO ascension for Ukraine, as they have tried to position themselves as a mediator [75] in the conflict.

      1.5.3.2. Pro:
      The criteria [76] for joining NATO would require Ukraine to dramatically strengthen its institutional capacities.

      1.5.3.3. Con:
      These requirements should be temporarily waived, given the existential crisis [77] that Ukraine faces.

    1.5.4. Pro:
    Dialogue and cooperation with NATO [78] started when an independent Ukraine joined the North Atlantic Cooperation Council (1991) and the Partnership for Peace programme in 1994.

      1.5.4.1. Con:
      NATO and Ukraine have had a complicated relationship [79] over the past several decades, which indicates neither NATO nor Ukraine are ready for membership.

        1.5.4.1.1. Pro:
        Even though Ukraine joined [80] NATO's enhanced opportunity partner interoperability program in 2020, this does not mean that NATO would give membership to Ukraine.

      1.5.4.2. Pro:
      Thirty years of cooperation indicates that the relationship has withstood multiple changes in administrations [81] and shifts in the international environment.

  1.6. Pro:
  Membership with NATO would significantly increase Ukraine’s international military backing.

    1.6.1. Pro:
    NATO's military strength is considerable, including 3.5 million service members [82] and the combined capabilities of 30 member states.

      1.6.1.1. Pro:
      NATO has already strengthened [83] its posture vis-a-vis Russia since the invasion of Ukraine.

      1.6.1.2. Pro:
      NATO troops have had a long history [84] of operational experience.

      1.6.1.3. Con:
      NATO is not able to defend [85] all of its members.

        1.6.1.3.1. Pro:
        Full scale NATO mobilization could take a long time, which Russia could capitalize on, given that they are already on wartime footing.

        1.6.1.3.2. Con:
        The combined forces of NATO are far stronger [86] than that of Russia.

          1.6.1.3.2.1. Con:
          Russia has the world's largest stockpile of nuclear weapons [87], which would render NATO's military useless in a full scale nuclear war.

        1.6.1.3.3. Con:
        If Ukraine joins NATO, it would also have to contribute money and military resources, which would improve the alliance.

          1.6.1.3.3.1. Pro:
          Ukraine's military is now battle tested and has an intimate knowledge of Russian military tactics. They would be a valuable asset to the NATO alliance.

            1.6.1.3.3.1.1. Con:
            Ukraine's military is also showing signs of fatigue [88] after the prolonged conflict.

          1.6.1.3.3.2. Con:
          Ukraine's economy has been devastated [89] by the war. They would not be able to contribute much financial support to the alliance for a long time.

    1.6.2. Con:
    Ukraine still has the option of becoming a neutral country [90].

      1.6.2.1. Con:
      Ukraine was already neutral before the war. This did not stop Russia from invading.

        1.6.2.1.1. Pro:
        On the 15th of March 2022, Ukraine's President Zelensky stated that Ukraine wouldn't be joining NATO, as it was one of the demands made by Russia before it invaded [91].

      1.6.2.2. Pro:
      By maintaining neutrality, Ukraine could act as a mediator between the West and Russia while preserving its own sovereignty.

        1.6.2.2.1. Con:
        It is impossible to act as a mediator on behalf of a regime that denies one's country's right to exist [92].

      1.6.2.3. Con:
      Ukraine is too strategically important to be a neutral country.

        1.6.2.3.1. Pro:
        Ukraine, and in particular the Crimean peninsula, are important as they are situated on the Black Sea [93].

          1.6.2.3.1.1. Pro:
          Russia's unprovoked and illegal annexation [94] of Crimea in 2014 illustrates why Ukraine cannot afford to be a neutral country.

        1.6.2.3.2. Con:
        Other neutral countries, such as Switzerland [95], are also strategically important but have been able to maintain their neutrality.

        1.6.2.3.3. Pro:
        Ukraine is one of the world's largest exporters [96] of staple grains.

    1.6.3. Pro:
    Because Ukraine is not a member [97] of the NATO alliance, NATO is not obligated [98] to launch an armed attack against Russia to protect Ukraine.

      1.6.3.1. Con:
      NATO member countries could decide on a national level to intervene in the conflict, regardless of Ukraine's NATO status.

        1.6.3.1.1. Pro:
        Several NATO members participated [99] in the 1991 Persian Gulf War to defend Kuwait's sovereignty from invasion by Iraq, despite Kuwait not being a NATO member.

      1.6.3.2. Pro:
      Ukraine's membership in NATO would make other member countries legally obligated [100] to intervene on their behalf.

    1.6.4. Con:
    Ukraine doesn't need to be a member of NATO to defend itself.

      1.6.4.1. Pro:
      Ukrainian forces effectively held back [101] Russian advances in 2022.

        1.6.4.1.1. Con:
        Russia still controlled [102] large portions of Ukraine's east, as well as the Crimean peninsula, at the end of 2022.

      1.6.4.2. Con:
      Having the full support of NATO will reduce the number of causalities [103] inflicted on Ukraine's limited number of soldiers.

        1.6.4.2.1. Con:
        If the war dramatically increases in scope, more soldiers would die overall, including more Ukrainian soldiers.

        1.6.4.2.2. Pro:
        Forcing Russia to fight on multiple fronts would draw their forces away from Ukraine, reducing further bloodshed and devastation there.

      1.6.4.3. Pro:
      Ukraine's armed forces are being supplied [104] by NATO countries with weapons, despite not being a member.

        1.6.4.3.1. Con:
        America, Ukraine's biggest arms supplier, could cease aid [105] in response to pressure from the Republican party to cut off support.

      1.6.4.4. Con:
      Russia has far more resources and manpower [106] than Ukraine. In a war of attrition, Russia is likely to win.

        1.6.4.4.1. Pro:
        -> See 1.4.1.3.1.
