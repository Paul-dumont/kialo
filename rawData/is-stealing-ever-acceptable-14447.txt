Discussion Name:
Is stealing ever acceptable?

1. Thesis:
Stealing is never acceptable.

  1.1. Con:
  Stealing can be a practical and fun way to get free stuff.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    For people that doesn't have much money stealing can give them acces to stuff they couldnt afford otherwise.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    Its easier to steal something than to work to earn money to buy it.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Stealing can give you a rush of adrenalin and exitement.

    1.1.4. Con:
    You can get caught and then you need to pay much more than if you had bought it.

    1.1.5. Con:
    You can get bad conscience.

    1.1.6. Con:
    Having fun at the expense of others is morally wrong.

  1.2. Con:
  Stealing is acceptable in cases where it is necessary.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    If it is necessary for people to combat immorality, then it is acceptable to steal in cases where the victim's ownership of the object is immoral.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      Stealing and freeing a slave from a slave owner would be acceptable.

        1.2.1.1.1. Con:
        Slavery is illegal [1] in virtually all modern societies. Reporting slaveowners to the authorities would be a much more reasonable way to help an enslaved person than stealing that person.

        1.2.1.1.2. Pro:
        The Underground Railroad [2] helped free slaves in the American South. Slave owners saw this as theft, but the people who worked for the Underground Railroad are now seen as heroes [3].

      1.2.1.2. Pro:
      Many museums contain artifacts stolen from their country of origin [4]. Stealing these artifacts back would be an acceptable way to correct this injustice.

        1.2.1.2.1. Pro:
        The Congolese activist Emery Mwazulu Diyabanza has taken artifacts from French museums [5] to draw attention to France's colonial legacy.

          1.2.1.2.1.1. Con:
          Diyabanza was not actually trying to steal the artifacts [6]. He was making a political statement about colonialism.

        1.2.1.2.2. Con:
        Many museums that house these items are safer homes [7] for the artifacts than their country of origin. Returning them may result in them being destroyed or damaged.

          1.2.1.2.2.1. Pro:
          The US [8] and Britain [9] returned stolen artefacts to Iraq throughout the 2010s when parts of the country were under the control of ISIL [10], who notoriously destroyed historical artifacts [11] in the territories they held.

      1.2.1.3. Con:
      Morality is subjective [12]. Judgements over whether possession of an object is immoral would vary significantly from person to person.

        1.2.1.3.1. Pro:
        The dispute over the Elgin Marbles [13], where Britain claims ownership of ancient sculptures purchased (or stolen) from Greece, demonstrates that the morality of ownership is contested even at the highest levels of world governance.

        1.2.1.3.2. Con:
        Some types of theft, such as wartime looting, have been condemned by international humanitarian law as war crimes [14]. This suggests that crimes like these are objectively wrong.

        1.2.1.3.3. Pro:
        Some anarchists argue that nobody should own private property. These people would consider all land ownership immoral (p. 327 [15]).

    1.2.2. Con:
    Most people have earned their wealth. It would be unjust to take this from them.

      1.2.2.1. Con:
      It is moral to steal money from those who earn their wealth by exploiting others.

        1.2.2.1.1. Pro:
        The legend of Robin Hood [16], one of the most enduring myths [17] ever created, promotes the idea of stealing from the immoral rich to give to the poor.

          1.2.2.1.1.1. Pro:
          The myth of Robin Hood increases in popularity during economic downturns [18], suggesting increased enthusiasm for stealing from the rich during times of financial hardship.

        1.2.2.1.2. Pro:
        Profits come from capitalists appropriating the surplus value [19] created by workers over their labor cost. Profits, therefore, require exploitation, reflected in the growing gap [20] between workers' wages and boardroom pay.

          1.2.2.1.2.1. Con:
          Warren Buffett, one of the world's richest people [21], has made sure strong business ethics [22] underpin his success. Not all wealthy people, therefore, earned their wealth through exploitation.

          1.2.2.1.2.2. Pro:
          Many companies steal labour from their employees (p. 1 [23]).

        1.2.2.1.3. Con:
        People deciding to rectify unjust ownership through theft may result in significant social disorder. People should lobby for changes to the law instead.

          1.2.2.1.3.1. Pro:
          Many billionaires also want to live in a fairer society [24]. Allying with these people, instead of stealing from them, will help lead to permanent structural change.

          1.2.2.1.3.2. Con:
          Absent any meaningful alternatives, theft is a perfectly legitimate way to rectify exploitation.

            1.2.2.1.3.2.1. Pro:
            In countries where it seems unlikely that any meaningful social reform will be enacted, it is legitimate for the poor to resort to theft.

            1.2.2.1.3.2.2. Con:
            Theft cannot actually rectify exploitation; it can only reduce its harms.

          1.2.2.1.3.3. Pro:
          Stealing from exploitative companies addresses only the symptoms of an unfair society, not its causes.

          1.2.2.1.3.4. Con:
          Influential people who have benefitted from an unjust system will not give up their position willingly. They often act as blockades against reforming the system [25], so it is justified to steal from them.

        1.2.2.1.4. Con:
        Attempts to correct social injustice must always take the moral high ground. Theft does not do this.

          1.2.2.1.4.1. Pro:
          Morally contentious attempts to correct injustices risk damaging the whole social justice movement.

            1.2.2.1.4.1.1. Pro:
            PETA's aggressive and divisive [26] campaigns for animal welfare may actually discourage veganism [27].

            1.2.2.1.4.1.2. Con:
            Controversial publicity stunts [28] can gain movements significant media attention.

              1.2.2.1.4.1.2.1. Con:
              Negative attention, which can turn people against movements [29], may be worse than no attention at all.

              1.2.2.1.4.1.2.2. Pro:
              Despite courting outrage over the commodification of national monuments, the Taco Liberty Bell marketing stunt [30] gained the fast-food chain millions of dollars of free advertising.

          1.2.2.1.4.2. Con:
          Some of the most pressing social injustices are so unjust that the alternative - addressing them piecemeal with campaigning and legislative change - is inappropriate [31].

      1.2.2.2. Pro:
      The majority of the wealthiest people in the world are self-made. [32]

        1.2.2.2.1. Pro:
        Many famous billionaires needed only a computer [33] to create their products, an increasingly common [34] and affordable [35] tool.

          1.2.2.2.1.1. Con:
          Computer poverty [36] is still a serious issue in many countries.

        1.2.2.2.2. Con:
        While many self-made millionaires come from financially humble backgrounds, most of them had educational and social advantages.

          1.2.2.2.2.1. Con:
          Many famous billionaires never completed a university degree [37].

          1.2.2.2.2.2. Pro:
          The self-made millionaire Jason Ford [38] attributes much of his success to a good education and the social benefits of being a white man, privileges that are unavailable to others.

          1.2.2.2.2.3. Pro:
          The highest geographical concentration of billionaires is in Europe and North America [39]. These continents contain many of the best-educated countries in the world [40] with comprehensive social safety nets [41].

      1.2.2.3. Pro:
      The majority [42] of people in the world are from poorer socioeconomic classes and have earned their wealth. Stealing from them is unjust.

    1.2.3. Con:
    Stealing with good intentions may be understandable, but it is not justifiable.

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      Generalized judgements on stealing obscure the reality that the morality of theft is heavily contested among philosophers [43].

        1.2.3.1.1. Con:
        Philosophers contesting theories of ethics are not helping people to live their everyday lives [44]. Society needs clear rules, and considering stealing to be universally wrong allows society to function.

      1.2.3.2. Pro:
      Being understanding about the reasons that someone committed a crime may result in implicit biases affecting how harshly they are treated (p. 82 [45]).

      1.2.3.3. Pro:
      Stealing is inherently evil because it uses another person as a means to an end [46], which makes it similar to other universally recognizable evils.

        1.2.3.3.1. Con:
        According to Kant, using others as a means to an end is justified [47] in certain circumstances.

          1.2.3.3.1.1. Pro:
          One of the fundamental institutions of modern society, employment [48], routinely uses people as a means to an end. This is not universally considered to be evil.

          1.2.3.3.1.2. Con:
          Kant suggests that there are cases where we can use others as a means, provided we still treat them as ends at the same time [49]. In theft cases, the victim has no agency and is therefore solely a means.

    1.2.4. Con:
    The most common items stolen [50] from stores are non-essential items.

      1.2.4.1. Pro:
      Products from famous brands [51] are more likely to be stolen from stores than essential items.

      1.2.4.2. Con:
      Food constituted over a quarter of stolen goods [52] in 2018, and baby formula [53] is one of the ten most frequently stolen items.

        1.2.4.2.1. Con:
        Baby formula is sometimes stolen by criminals looking to sell it on the black market or dilute it with heroin [54], not by desperate mothers.

        1.2.4.2.2. Pro:
        It is morally justified to steal food from a shop to feed a starving person, even if it causes financial harm to the shop.

          1.2.4.2.2.1. Con:
          Courts still convict people who steal to save lives.

            1.2.4.2.2.1.1. Pro:
            Starving people who steal from shops are often jailed [55].

            1.2.4.2.2.1.2. Con:
            Courts deal with laws, not ethics. That courts convict people for theft does not mean that all theft is unacceptable or unethical.

            1.2.4.2.2.1.3. Con:
            There is some precedent [56] for courts siding with those who steal out of need.

          1.2.4.2.2.2. Pro:
          Stealing for self-enrichment is immoral, whereas stealing for self-preservation is a human right.

          1.2.4.2.2.3. Con:
          It is acceptable to steal food only if the thief has no other way of obtaining it. Someone who can afford to buy food would be morally wrong to steal it.

          1.2.4.2.2.4. Con:
          If everyone was justified in stealing food whenever they needed to, few shops could stay in business.

            1.2.4.2.2.4.1. Con:
            This justification would likely encourage businesses to invest in reducing the need for theft, such as donating resources to impoverished communities.

          1.2.4.2.2.5. Pro:
          Food stolen from a shop may benefit the thief greatly [57] at a comparatively small cost to the shopkeeper.

      1.2.4.3. Pro:
      Most thieves steal items to sell them for profit [58] rather than for personal use.

    1.2.5. Pro:
    Stealing an animal in order to protect its well-being is justifiable.

      1.2.5.1. Pro:
      Animal rights activists [59] often steal animals from farmers and put them in better homes or set them free.

        1.2.5.1.1. Con:
        That animal rights activists steal animals does not necessarily mean that it is right.

        1.2.5.1.2. Con:
        Animal rights activists have been convicted [60] of theft for stealing livestock.

        1.2.5.1.3. Pro:
        Some cases of stealing maltreated animals, such as when animal rights activists stole a bull [61], can garner public sympathy for badly treated animals, and even change the law.

      1.2.5.2. Pro:
      Animals is living being that shouldn't be seen as property someone have a right to.

      1.2.5.3. Con:
      If an animal is being abused, the most appropriate action is to call an animal welfare organization [62] or law enforcement agency.

        1.2.5.3.1. Pro:
        Employees of animal welfare organizations are trained [63] to deal with mistreated animals and their owners. Vigilantism might cause more harm to the animal, its owner, or the vigilante.

        1.2.5.3.2. Con:
        If the animal is in imminent danger, there may not be time to make the necessary calls.

          1.2.5.3.2.1. Con:
          Stealing one animal may save that animal's life, but calling the authorities and having the criminals arrested may shut down a whole abuse ring.

            1.2.5.3.2.1.1. Con:
            Stealing an animal in imminent danger and contacting the authorities about an animal abuse ring are not mutually exclusive.

          1.2.5.3.2.2. Pro:
          A dog about to be released into a dog fight [64] cannot wait for the authorities to arrive.

        1.2.5.3.3. Con:
        The state [65] sometimes permits the maltreatment of animals; they cannot be relied upon to rescue legally mistreated animals.

          1.2.5.3.3.1. Pro:
          Many countries permit the factory farming [66] of livestock.

          1.2.5.3.3.2. Con:
          Over recent decades, many states have improved animal welfare on farms (p. 7 [67]).

    1.2.6. Pro:
    Some people have no choice but to steal to support themselves or their family.

      1.2.6.1. Con:
      If a thief is caught and arrested, their family will be left without their support.

        1.2.6.1.1. Con:
        Given that many forms of theft are not serious [68], some criminals will receive lighter punishments [69].

        1.2.6.1.2. Con:
        The thief's family may have been so desperate that the thief considered the risk of arrest worth taking.

          1.2.6.1.2.1. Pro:
          If somone has had their benefits removed by the state, stealing may be their only recourse [70] to avoid starvation.

          1.2.6.1.2.2. Con:
          Desperation clouds people’s judgment and makes it harder to appreciate the likelihood of adverse outcomes, such as getting arrested (p. 6 [71]).

            1.2.6.1.2.2.1. Con:
            Despite the judgement of the thief, they are only presented with two options. Steal and survive or don't steal and die. Even if they do appreciate the outcome, the worse outcome be not taking action and watching their family die.

        1.2.6.1.3. Pro:
        Even if a thief is not imprisoned, convictions will end up making it harder for them to find employment [72], continuing the cycle of poverty.

          1.2.6.1.3.1. Pro:
          The unemployment rate for ex-convicts is significantly higher [73] than the rate for the general public.

          1.2.6.1.3.2. Con:
          An increasing number [74] of employers are willing to hire those with misdemeanors.

            1.2.6.1.3.2.1. Pro:
            Large companies such as Amazon, Walmart, and McDonald's [75] have partnered with organizations helping ex-convicts find employment.

            1.2.6.1.3.2.2. Con:
            Greater awareness that many drug convictions [76] were racially motivated has encouraged some employers to employ people with misdemeanors. Convictions for theft, however, will probably be seen differently.

      1.2.6.2. Con:
      It is possible to respect the rights of both parties through trading rather than stealing.

        1.2.6.2.1. Con:
        Most large corporations that sell food and medicine do not work on a barter system.

        1.2.6.2.2. Con:
        If someone is desperate enough to steal to survive, they likely have little to trade in return for necessities.

        1.2.6.2.3. Pro:
        Trading has been the basis of human exchange for millennia [77].

      1.2.6.3. Pro:
      The most common crime that poor people commit is theft [78], and it is likely these crimes are committed specifically to afford products for survival.

      1.2.6.4. Pro:
      -> See 1.2.4.2.2.

      1.2.6.5. Pro:
      Only 3% [79] of shoplifters are professional thieves. Most people who steal are, therefore, probably only doing so to survive.

        1.2.6.5.1. Con:
        While only a tiny percentage of thieves may be professionals, that does not mean all other thieves are only stealing to survive. Amateur thieves may still be stealing luxury items, just not as a career.

        1.2.6.5.2. Pro:
        Few thieves can afford the items they steal. Most people who steal do so out of economic necessity (p. 177 [80]).

  1.3. Con:
  Some people cannot stop themselves from stealing.

    1.3.1. Con:
    Just because something is natural [81] or instinctive does not mean it is beneficial or acceptable in human society.

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      Humans are expected to suppress immoral or damaging natural urges such as sexual jealousy [82].

    1.3.2. Pro:
    Stealing is common, suggesting many people cannot resist the temptation to take something that is not theirs.

      1.3.2.1. Pro:
      At least one in five people have shoplifted [83]. It would be impractical to label all of these people as criminals.

      1.3.2.2. Con:
      Many crimes are common, but that does not mean that they are acceptable or that people can't help themselves from committing them.

      1.3.2.3. Pro:
      Stealing is common [84] in children, suggesting an innate human desire to steal.

        1.3.2.3.1. Con:
        Many children steal due to other issues at home [85] or peer pressure (p. 8 [86]), not because children are predisposed to be thieves.

          1.3.2.3.1.1. Pro:
          For many youths, stealing is a form of teenage rebellion [87] rather than a permanent lifestyle.

        1.3.2.3.2. Con:
        Most people who steal as children do not go on to steal as adults [88].

        1.3.2.3.3. Con:
        Teenagers often engage in risk-taking behaviour because the part of their brain responsible for understanding consequences is not well developed [89].

        1.3.2.3.4. Pro:
        According to the National Association for Shoplifting Prevention in the US, 79% [90] of criminal justice professionals consider shoplifting a gateway crime.

        1.3.2.3.5. Pro:
        One in five [91] adult shoplifters started stealing as teenagers.

    1.3.3. Pro:
    Some people suffer from kleptomania [92], which makes it difficult, if not impossible, for them to resist the urge to steal.

      1.3.3.1. Pro:
      Small thefts can develop into an addiction [93].

        1.3.3.1.1. Pro:
        Early thefts during childhood are strong predictors of future offending during adulthood (p. 1 [94]).

          1.3.3.1.1.1. Pro:
          Many antisocial behaviors in children indicate potential future criminality, possibly including theft (p. 2 [95]).

        1.3.3.1.2. Con:
        The majority of people arrested for shoplifting have never stolen before [96].

      1.3.3.2. Con:
      Impulse-control disorders like kleptomania are rarely considered a legitimate legal defence [97] for theft.

      1.3.3.3. Con:
      People suffering from kleptomania have a moral responsibility to seek help as soon as possible; it cannot be used to justify their behaviour.

      1.3.3.4. Pro:
      Kleptomania is an impulse-control disorder that often requires treatment to overcome (pp. 1-2 [98]).

      1.3.3.5. Pro:
      Kleptomania can be caused [99] by genetic factors or imbalances in the brain's opioid system.

        1.3.3.5.1. Pro:
        Kleptomaniacs often feel anxiety [100] that only the act of stealing can alleviate.

          1.3.3.5.1.1. Con:
          Many kleptomaniacs feel guilty and depressed [101] after stealing.

    1.3.4. Con:
    Even if a thief cannot stop themselves from stealing, that does not mean that their crime is acceptable or harmless (p. 4 [102]).

      1.3.4.1. Pro:
      Crimes should not be judged solely on intention, but also on the impacts that they have on society.

        1.3.4.1.1. Pro:
        If we were to forgive criminals for having good intentions, it is possible that many existing crimes would cease to be seen as crimes.

        1.3.4.1.2. Pro:
        Individuals are not the only victims of theft. The economy can suffer from theft as stolen valuables are often sold for a fraction of their price through non-official channels.

          1.3.4.1.2.1. Pro:
          Black markets [103] often sell things cheaper than regular market rates because they do not have to pay taxes [104] or various licensing fees.

            1.3.4.1.2.1.1. Pro:
            The illegal sale of goods damages society as it normalises criminality [105].

              1.3.4.1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
              The obvious and public sale of stolen goods may make crime seem acceptable [106] to normal society, leading to a broader erosion of public standards.

            1.3.4.1.2.1.2. Con:
            Some black market goods, such as firearms, are more expensive [107] than their legal counterparts.

            1.3.4.1.2.1.3. Con:
            Black markets can be associated with violence and organized crime [108], making them offputting for many consumers.

          1.3.4.1.2.2. Pro:
          The shadow economy, which includes the selling of stolen goods, costs governments vast sums of money. Some estimates suggest that it removes trillions of dollars [109] from the world economy.

          1.3.4.1.2.3. Con:
          The sale of stolen valuables in gray [110] or black [111] markets enriches poorer buyers, who could otherwise not afford those products.

            1.3.4.1.2.3.1. Con:
            Customers buying stolen goods have no legal protection [112] from unfair or unethical selling practices.

            1.3.4.1.2.3.2. Con:
            Poorer buyers do not need to resort to criminality to get discounts. They can legally buy stolen goods at reduced prices at police auctions [113].

            1.3.4.1.2.3.3. Pro:
            Stolen goods sell for approximately one-third to three-quarters of their retail price (p. 29 [114]), making them much more affordable for poorer buyers.

        1.3.4.1.3. Con:
        Some crimes can have a positive impact on certain sections of society. For example, theft benefits the security system industry, which contributes billions of dollars [115] to the global economy.

          1.3.4.1.3.1. Pro:
          Home security systems, primarily designed to prevent theft and break-ins, are expected to have a market value of 78.9 billion USD [116] by 2025.

      1.3.4.2. Con:
      A crime does not need to be harmless for it to be excusable and contextually acceptable [117].

      1.3.4.3. Con:
      It is pointless to punish people for behaviours they cannot control.

        1.3.4.3.1. Con:
        If a thief feels no remorse for their crime, they may commit it again. Society must be protected from such people.

    1.3.5. Pro:
    Nature does not recognize the existence of property. Theft is, therefore, natural.

      1.3.5.1. Pro:
      Human beings [118] are the only animals that classify things as belonging to them.

      1.3.5.2. Con:
      Territorial animals [119] are evidence that nature has a concept of ownership.

        1.3.5.2.1. Con:
        Animals can claim territories for variable amounts of time, with some animals only defending spaces for hours [120]. This suggests that these animals do not consider their ownership of these areas permanent.

        1.3.5.2.2. Pro:
        Some birds aggressively defend [121] their nests against incursions by other species. This implies a sense of ownership.

        1.3.5.2.3. Pro:
        Some animal rights activists and philosophers have called for an animal's territory to be legally defined as its property (p. 133 [122]).

          1.3.5.2.3.1. Con:
          Animal rights activists are people. That they want animals to be given property is only proof that animal rights activists, rather than the animals themselves, have a concept of property.

      1.3.5.3. Pro:
      Human beings have developed property rights over thousands of years [123].

  1.4. Pro:
  Stealing is harmful.

    1.4.1. Con:
    Often, the tangible benefits gained by the thief outweigh the costs to the victim.

      1.4.1.1. Pro:
      Some thefts are so minor that they have no meaningful financial impact on the victim.

        1.4.1.1.1. Pro:
        The shoplifting of small items does not involve significant losses for business and is therefore not morally significant.

          1.4.1.1.1.1. Pro:
          Shoplifting does not cause significant financial harm to massive corporations.

            1.4.1.1.1.1.1. Con:
            Some stores associated with large corporations are franchised out [124]. As such, stealing from these stores is akin to stealing from a small business.

            1.4.1.1.1.1.2. Pro:
            Shoplifters frequently target Walmart, but this only costs them an estimated 3 billion USD (1%) [125] of their profits.

              1.4.1.1.1.1.2.1. Con:
              Regardless of Walmart's profits, 3 billion USD is a lot of money. It is greater than the individual GDP of over thirty countries [126].

            1.4.1.1.1.1.3. Pro:
            Harsh shoplifting penalties are probably designed to discourage stealing [127] rather than reflective of the actual economic costs of theft.

            1.4.1.1.1.1.4. Con:
            Local branches are assessed in terms of their individual revenue-making ability [128]. Stealing from a local branch of a larger chain may result in that store being shut down.

              1.4.1.1.1.1.4.1. Pro:
              Significant losses from theft may cause the closure [129] of local branches, resulting in many people losing their jobs.

          1.4.1.1.1.2. Con:
          Small shops, which have less capacity to cope with losses from theft, are often targeted [130] by shoplifters and burglars.

            1.4.1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
            Big stores can usually afford security guards and anti-theft technology [131]. This makes it harder to steal from them.

            1.4.1.1.1.2.2. Pro:
            Small shops often have a small number of employees [132] in the shop at one time, making it harder to prevent theft.

            1.4.1.1.1.2.3. Con:
            A survey of shoplifting in Birmingham [133] found that the stores most frequently targeted belonged to large chains.

          1.4.1.1.1.3. Con:
          While low-value thefts may have negligible impact, the cumulative result of multiple thefts can force a store to close its doors [134].

            1.4.1.1.1.3.1. Pro:
            Some stores, such as dollar [135] stores, have business models based on selling low-value items. Legitimizing the theft of such items may cause them to go bankrupt.

        1.4.1.1.2. Con:
        If some thefts had a truly negligible impact or were otherwise acceptable, they would be permitted. That all theft is nearly always illegal shows that it is unacceptable.

          1.4.1.1.2.1. Con:
          The police no longer respond to shoplifting incidents in Britain if the losses are less than £200 [136]. Some store owners think this has effectively decriminalised shoplifting [137].

          1.4.1.1.2.2. Pro:
          Many jurisdictions will prosecute the theft of any item [138], meaning that even stealing a stick of gum is an offense.

        1.4.1.1.3. Con:
        Even if the victim is rich enough to cope with the theft, this should not justify stealing from them.

          1.4.1.1.3.1. Con:
          Since stealing from a rich person or company causes them minimal harm, it can be justified according to the harm principle [139].

        1.4.1.1.4. Pro:
        Not returning low-value borrowed items is often considered permissible.

          1.4.1.1.4.1. Con:
          Borrowing an object involves an intention to return it, and therefore does not constitute stealing.

            1.4.1.1.4.1.1. Con:
            Kleptomaniacs often return items they have stolen [140], but they are still considered thieves.

          1.4.1.1.4.2. Pro:
          Borrowed pens are rarely returned to their owners [141], but very few people consider this theft [142].

      1.4.1.2. Pro:
      In cases where the rightful owner is not using their property, it is more beneficial for others to use it, even if this is a form of theft.

        1.4.1.2.1. Pro:
        Some countries allow squatters [143] to become owners of the properties they live on if they have stayed there long enough.

          1.4.1.2.1.1. Pro:
          When squatters are granted property rights, this benefits them and ensures a more just and sustainable distribution of property [144].

          1.4.1.2.1.2. Con:
          In the UK, if the land's original owner objects to the squatter taking ownership, the transfer of ownership is automatically denied [145].

          1.4.1.2.1.3. Con:
          Squatters are not allowed to hide their use [146] of their claimed land from the original owner. Squatting, therefore, is not theft, as the original owners are entitled to know they are there and do not try to remove them.

        1.4.1.2.2. Con:
        People are never really not using a property. Land that the owner is not immediately exploiting is still an asset [147] they can use for financial gain.

      1.4.1.3. Con:
      The victim may not have enough money to offset the price of the theft, potentially pushing them into poverty.

        1.4.1.3.1. Pro:
        Residents in poorer neighbourhoods are significantly more likely [148] to be victims of burglary.

        1.4.1.3.2. Con:
        Victims of theft are often entitled to compensation [149].

        1.4.1.3.3. Pro:
        For people working in the gig economy with no workplace insurance [150], the theft of an essential item can remove their capacity to work.

          1.4.1.3.3.1. Pro:
          If a gig economy delivery driver [151] has their method of transportation stolen, they will be unable to work until they replace the item with their own money.

    1.4.2. Pro:
    Stealing can cause emotional harm to everyone involved.

      1.4.2.1. Con:
      Having no way to obtain everyday necessities is highly emotionally distressing [152]. Theft is one way to alleviate this distress.

        1.4.2.1.1. Pro:
        Some people need small items such as menstrual products [153]. The theft of these does not hurt a pharmacy as much as it helps the thief.

        1.4.2.1.2. Con:
        Shoplifting is unlikely to alleviate the emotional pain of being in poverty. At best, it would offer temporary relief.

      1.4.2.2. Pro:
      Stealing can cause emotional distress to the victims.

        1.4.2.2.1. Con:
        Petty theft and pickpocketing are unlikely to make anyone feel unsafe or traumatized.

          1.4.2.2.1.1. Pro:
          Petty theft and pickpocketing do not involve any physical altercation with the victim, and are therefore unlikely to leave a lasting impact on them.

          1.4.2.2.1.2. Con:
          Pickpocketing [154] has been linked to trauma.

        1.4.2.2.2. Pro:
        Some items have sentimental value to their owners far outweighing their monetary value. The theft of these items may have a considerable emotional impact on the owner.

          1.4.2.2.2.1. Con:
          Many items with sentimental value may not have enough monetary value for thieves to bother stealing them.

            1.4.2.2.2.1.1. Pro:
            For example, a child's teddy bear or someone's birthday card is unlikely to be stolen.

          1.4.2.2.2.2. Pro:
          Thieves often do not know the emotional value objects have for their owners, making it difficult for conscientious thieves to avoid taking them.

          1.4.2.2.2.3. Pro:
          High-value items, such as family jewellery, often hold great emotional significance [155] for their owners. These items are often specifically targeted by burglars [156].

        1.4.2.2.3. Pro:
        In the UK, 47% [157] of burglary victims feel unsafe in their own home afterwards and over a third subsequently suffer from sleeping problems.

        1.4.2.2.4. Con:
        In cases where thefts occur against major corporations or governments, emotional damage to individuals is much less likely.

          1.4.2.2.4.1. Con:
          Some types of corporate or state theft, such as the stealing of personal information through data breaches [158], can be emotionally damaging to the victims.

            1.4.2.2.4.1.1. Pro:
            The theft of personal data from the adultery website Ashley Madison resulted in several people taking their own lives [159].

            1.4.2.2.4.1.2. Con:
            Most people [160] don't even know they have been victims of a data breach, suggesting the level of harm is limited.

            1.4.2.2.4.1.3. Pro:
            31% of data breach victims later experience identity theft [161]. This type of crime can cause acute distress to the victims [162].

          1.4.2.2.4.2. Pro:
          Many large companies mistreat their workers [163]. These employees are unlikely to care if that company is then a victim of theft.

            1.4.2.2.4.2.1. Con:
            Workers will care if they lose their jobs when a store or company closes due to losses from theft [164].

        1.4.2.2.5. Pro:
        Mugging can lead to harmful psychological [165] impacts for the victims.

      1.4.2.3. Pro:
      Thieves are often traumatized by their own behaviour.

        1.4.2.3.1. Pro:
        Many thieves feel a lingering paranoia that they may be caught.

          1.4.2.3.1.1. Pro:
          -> See 1.3.3.5.1.1.

          1.4.2.3.1.2. Pro:
          Most thieves [166] are fugitives [167] from the law, and life for criminals on the run is often fraught [168] with paranoia and loneliness.

          1.4.2.3.1.3. Con:
          In the UK, the vast majority of small-scale thefts never come to the attention of the police [169], so most shoplifters know they will not be caught.

        1.4.2.3.2. Con:
        The shame of stealing may be less severe [170] than the suffering caused by starvation or desperation.

        1.4.2.3.3. Pro:
        Detained thieves, especially adolescents, are more likely to kill themselves than the general population (p. 1 [171]).

        1.4.2.3.4. Con:
        White-collar criminals [172], who are often involved in stealing from their employers, rarely experience feelings of guilt (p. 494 [173]).

    1.4.3. Pro:
    Stealing often turns violent.

      1.4.3.1. Con:
      Shoplifting, one of the most common [174] forms of theft, is rarely violent.

        1.4.3.1.1. Con:
        Many commonly stolen items are of low value [175]. In order to reap meaningful rewards, thieves need to re-offend frequently, leading to a higher likelihood of confrontation.

        1.4.3.1.2. Con:
        Many shoplifters steal to fund a drug habit [176], which can make them dangerous and unpredictable.

        1.4.3.1.3. Pro:
        Store employees are generally discouraged from detaining shoplifters [177] without proper training, reducing the likelihood of violence.

      1.4.3.2. Pro:
      Mugging [178], where an assailant forces someone to hand over their valuables, is based on a fear of violence.

        1.4.3.2.1. Pro:
        Muggers are almost always armed. Of the 243,600 robberies [179] in the US in 2020, in only 7,821 cases [180] were the assailants recorded as having no weapon.

        1.4.3.2.2. Con:
        Muggers are most likely to choose victims who they know are unlikely to escalate [181] the situation into violence.

          1.4.3.2.2.1. Con:
          Muggers often target older and other vulnerable people to avoid violence, which is particularly unethical.

            1.4.3.2.2.1.1. Pro:
            There are many [182] examples [183] of elderly victims suffering heart attacks or dying shortly after being mugged. This often means that mugging them is tantamount to grievous bodily harm, manslaughter, or murder.

          1.4.3.2.2.2. Pro:
          Violence is dangerous for muggers as well, so they have an incentive to avoid it as much as possible.

            1.4.3.2.2.2.1. Pro:
            Some muggers have targeted people who they thought were vulnerable, only for the mugger to be seriously injured by the intended victim [184].

            1.4.3.2.2.2.2. Con:
            Muggers can arm themselves and target people who look vulnerable [185]. By contrast, most victims are unsuspecting and unarmed.

        1.4.3.2.3. Pro:
        Muggers are often intoxicated when they rob someone, making them more likely to escalate the situation to violence or do something dangerous (p. 2 [186]).

      1.4.3.3. Pro:
      Thieves may bring weapons in case the people they are stealing from try to defend themselves.

        1.4.3.3.1. Pro:
        Research suggests that thieves are more likely to bring weapons when targeting establishments with security and guards [187].

        1.4.3.3.2. Con:
        The weapons effect [188] suggests that people are more likely to escalate violence if they see a weapon. Thieves may deliberately not arm themselves to prevent escalation.

        1.4.3.3.3. Pro:
        Convenience store robberies often involve resistance [189] from employees, resulting in high levels of violence during these incidents.

      1.4.3.4. Con:
      When theft turns violent, it is the violence that is unacceptable, not the theft.

        1.4.3.4.1. Con:
        If one action directly leads to another, worse action, then the first action is still morally wrong and should be punished as such.

          1.4.3.4.1.1. Con:
          By the same logic, it is arguable that it is not the theft itself that led to the violence, but the underlying conditions or economic needs that encouraged the theft.

          1.4.3.4.1.2. Pro:
          While drink driving itself is a relatively minor offense [190], drink driving that results in another person's death can be punished [191] by years in prison.

        1.4.3.4.2. Pro:
        In most jurisdictions, the length of sentences [192] for burglary increase significantly if the thief is armed, suggesting that the threat of violence is more serious than the theft.

      1.4.3.5. Pro:
      Large-scale robberies can result in fatal confrontations with the police.

        1.4.3.5.1. Con:
        Many large-scale robberies [193] are now conducted online, with no chance of people being physically harmed during the theft.

        1.4.3.5.2. Pro:
        Fourteen people, including two children, were killed in a shootout [194] between the police and bank robbers in Brazil in 2018.

        1.4.3.5.3. Con:
        It is a popular misconception [195] that many bank robberies end in violence. Very few shootouts happen during robberies, and most customers do not even realise that the bank is being robbed.

        1.4.3.5.4. Pro:
        In 1997, a bank robbery in Los Angeles ended in a gun battle [196] with the police, resulting in eighteen injuries and two fatalities.

    1.4.4. Con:
    Many harmful things are acceptable in certain circumstances [197]. Stealing is no different.

      1.4.4.1. Pro:
      It is acceptable [198] in many societies to binge drink [199], even though it is harmful to our health [200] and leads to higher levels of crime [201].

      1.4.4.2. Pro:
      It is socially acceptable to purchase and discard single-use plastic [202] items, even though this has a devastating long term impact [203] on the environment.

        1.4.4.2.1. Con:
        Single-use plastics have increasingly become socially unacceptable [204].

      1.4.4.3. Con:
      Stealing is different from other acceptable harms in that there is a clear and immediate victim.

        1.4.4.3.1. Con:
        Government-mandated quarantines have clear and identifiable victims - the forcibly quarantined - but they are still generally accepted (p. 1 [205], p. 1 [206]).

        1.4.4.3.2. Pro:
        In cases of theft, victims can often clearly quantify what they have lost in monetary terms instead of the more abstract consequences of other harms.

        1.4.4.3.3. Con:
        Killing another person is justified in cases of clear self-defence [207].

  1.5. Pro:
  Stealing is morally wrong and violates people's rights, regardless of justifications or circumstances.

    1.5.1. Con:
    There are times when the right to private property is outweighed by other rights.

      1.5.1.1. Pro:
      Public highways [208], where members of the public have a right to walk across private land, exist in many jurisdictions.

      1.5.1.2. Con:
      No one right in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights is more important [209] than others.

      1.5.1.3. Pro:
      The right to life outweighs the right to property.

        1.5.1.3.1. Pro:
        When an individual is illegally held at gunpoint, the victim stealing that weapon [210] is not considered a criminal act.

        1.5.1.3.2. Con:
        Homeless people often die from exposure [211] in countries with millions of vacant homes [212], suggesting that many states do not consider their right to life more important than other people's right to property.

    1.5.2. Pro:
    The right to own property is necessary to allow people to live free lives.

      1.5.2.1. Con:
      If property is necessary for freedom, it follows that those without property cannot be free, making theft a necessary part of self-determination.

        1.5.2.1.1. Pro:
        In 2020, the homeownership rate in the US was 65.8% [213], implying that 34.2% of the population was not free.

          1.5.2.1.1.1. Con:
          The legal definition of property [214] includes more than just home ownership.

        1.5.2.1.2. Con:
        People without property could acquire it through legitimate means rather than stealing it.

          1.5.2.1.2.1. Con:
          Even basic properties can be unaffordable [215] in many countries.

          1.5.2.1.2.2. Pro:
          Mechanisms exist to help people in poverty obtain property, such as the Right to Buy [216] scheme in the UK.

      1.5.2.2. Pro:
      Many political philosophers have argued that property ownership is a natural human right [217].

      1.5.2.3. Pro:
      Individual property rights [218] ensure that no person or entity can have total control over resources, guaranteeing the fair distribution of power between society and government.

    1.5.3. Pro:
    Victims of theft do not consent to lose their belongings. Non-consensual actions are always unacceptable.

      1.5.3.1. Con:
      A thief has not consented to the victim's right to own the item. Acting and defending something as yours is also non-consensual.

      1.5.3.2. Pro:
      Individual autonomy [219], whereby nothing happens to an individual that breaches their consent, should be considered the most critical concept in modern society.

        1.5.3.2.1. Con:
        Many things happen to people that they do not consent to, such as strip searches, or being arrested. The right to consent to all occurrences is rarely met in modern societies.

      1.5.3.3. Con:
      It is acceptable to violate a person's consent for the public good.

        1.5.3.3.1. Pro:
        A violent criminal may not consent to be arrested, but few argue that they should not be.

        1.5.3.3.2. Con:
        Very few forms of theft constitute a public good.

          1.5.3.3.2.1. Con:
          Eminent domain [220] allows a government to take private land for the public good. Some people consider this theft [221].

          1.5.3.3.2.2. Pro:
          One of the most common forms of theft, wage theft [222], takes from poor employees and gives to rich employers.

        1.5.3.3.3. Con:
        The only actor allowed to override consent is the state [223] because it is expected to make decisions in favor of its people.

        1.5.3.3.4. Pro:
        People do not directly consent to taxes [224], but few people would see this as a moral harm akin to stealing.

          1.5.3.3.4.1. Con:
          Many libertarians consider taxation to be theft [225].

      1.5.3.4. Con:
      If the owner of an item is unbothered by its theft and the thief benefits from it, the fact that the owner has not consented is unimportant.

        1.5.3.4.1. Pro:
        In one case where people had technically stolen discarded food from bins [226] outside a supermarket, the chief executive of the supermarket chain argued that the thieves should not be prosecuted.

        1.5.3.4.2. Con:
        If the owner is unbothered by someone taking their item, then this may amount to tacit consent [227], in which case it is not theft.

    1.5.4. Con:
    Criminal theft can be excused in specific contexts.

      1.5.4.1. Con:
      If a precedent were set for theft being acceptable in certain contexts, it would become much harder to have consistent social standards and a fair justice system.

      1.5.4.2. Pro:
      According to some versions of utilitarianism [228], stealing is justified when it produces more net good outcomes than bad.

        1.5.4.2.1. Con:
        Rule utilitarianism [229] suggests that a general rule prohibiting stealing in all cases would create more beneficial outcomes.

          1.5.4.2.1.1. Con:
          There are rules against stealing in most societies, but theft is still relatively common [230].

          1.5.4.2.1.2. Pro:
          A world without stealing would make people less anxious and suspicious of each other.

          1.5.4.2.1.3. Pro:
          A general rule against stealing would make life more predictable for all people.

          1.5.4.2.1.4. Con:
          The suffering of one person in need of food is greater than the suffering of many people worried about being a victim of theft. Society should prioritize the person in greatest need.

            1.5.4.2.1.4.1. Con:
            Even if the harm is minor for each individual, the fear of being a victim of theft across an entire society may constitute a much greater harm.

        1.5.4.2.2. Pro:
        If someone can prevent significant harm through stealing, they have a moral obligation to do so.

          1.5.4.2.2.1. Pro:
          -> See 1.5.1.3.1.

          1.5.4.2.2.2. Con:
          Cases of people taking dangerous weapons from assailants are not examples of stealing, but of self-defence.

            1.5.4.2.2.2.1. Con:
            In these cases, stealing and self-defence are not necessarily mutually exclusive.

        1.5.4.2.3. Pro:
        It is unclear why one person's right to property should outweigh the happiness of others.

          1.5.4.2.3.1. Con:
          A thief stealing to make themselves happy would deprive the victim of the happiness they derived from their possessions.

    1.5.5. Pro:
    The right to property is affirmed in national and international law.

      1.5.5.1. Pro:
      Article 17 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights recognizes the right to property (p. 5 [231]).

      1.5.5.2. Pro:
      The European Convention on Human Rights affirms that "every natural or legal person is entitled to the peaceful enjoyment of his possessions" (p. 33 [232]).

      1.5.5.3. Pro:
      The Fifth Amendment [233] of the US Constitution states that private property will not “be taken for public use without just compensation”.

        1.5.5.3.1. Con:
        Tens of thousands of Native Americans were forced off their lands in the 19th century [234]. The US government was the primary beneficiary of this policy.

      1.5.5.4. Con:
      Laws are not static. Changing norms and values result in the evolution of laws (p. 6 [235]).

      1.5.5.5. Con:
      That national and international laws support the right to private property does not mean the right should exist.

        1.5.5.5.1. Pro:
        Capitalist nations [236] developed these laws and conventions. It is unsurprising, therefore, that they support property rights.

        1.5.5.5.2. Con:
        That these laws and conventions have such broad support [237] shows how fundamental the principle of property is in human society.

      1.5.5.6. Con:
      Political systems like communism [238] reject private ownership of the means of production.

    1.5.6. Con:
    To own something is a violation of the rights of others as it denies people access to resources they may need or want.

      1.5.6.1. Pro:
      The concept of ownership resulted in Indigenous Americans being denied access to the land [239] required to maintain their lifestyle from the 18th century onwards.

      1.5.6.2. Con:
      Many resources cannot be accessed without specialist equipment and investment. No company would be willing to invest in accessing a resource (making it available to everyone) without ownership rights.

      1.5.6.3. Pro:
      The institution of property is already a form of theft [240]. Therefore, stealing property is legitimate.

        1.5.6.3.1. Pro:
        The anarchist Pierre-Joseph Proudhon, who first used the phrase "property is theft [241]", argued that landowners who profit from simply renting land are thieves [242].

        1.5.6.3.2. Pro:
        Government or corporate exploitation of natural resources is a form of theft from the local community.

          1.5.6.3.2.1. Pro:
          Some Indigenous Canadians do not have access to drinking water in lands where Nestlé extracts millions of litres of water [243].

          1.5.6.3.2.2. Pro:
          Scottish nationalists argue that the British exploitation of the North Sea oil fields [244] is theft of Scottish resources.

          1.5.6.3.2.3. Con:
          The exploitation of resources to the detriment of the local community may be wrong, but it does not prove that the concept of property is immoral.

        1.5.6.3.3. Con:
        -> See 1.2.2.1.3.

        1.5.6.3.4. Con:
        -> See 1.2.2.1.4.

      1.5.6.4. Con:
      Attempts to abolish the concept of private property have generally failed, suggesting that human societies cannot function without it.

        1.5.6.4.1. Pro:
        While the Soviet Union abolished land rights, they found it necessary to maintain some form of private property (p. 468 [245], p. 64 [246]).

        1.5.6.4.2. Pro:
        Studies have shown [247] that children as young as three display an understanding of property ownership, indicating that the concept is natural to human beings.

          1.5.6.4.2.1. Con:
          That a child does something is not proof that it is moral or helpful behaviour.

          1.5.6.4.2.2. Con:
          There is a difference between the concept of ownership people develop as children [248], and the strict definition of private property [249] within an economic system.

          1.5.6.4.2.3. Pro:
          Young children can be intensely possessive [250] of favoured toys or items, demonstrating a clear concept of possession.

        1.5.6.4.3. Con:
        The kibbutz [251] community in Israel was founded on socialist principles that abolished most forms of private property [252].

          1.5.6.4.3.1. Pro:
          Several socialist kibbutzim [253] still exist and thrive.

          1.5.6.4.3.2. Con:
          Many kibbutzim failed [254] because residents preferred [255] having possessions.

    1.5.7. Con:
    Some argue that the prosperity many wealthy countries currently benefit from was only made possible through historical theft [256].

      1.5.7.1. Con:
      Even if former empires are now wealthy due to exploitation, this does not make such practices ethical or justifiable.

      1.5.7.2. Con:
      Even if some wealthy countries benefitted from this theft, the countries that were victims of it were often devastated and may still suffer [257] long-term economic consequences.

      1.5.7.3. Pro:
      -> See 1.5.5.3.1.

      1.5.7.4. Pro:
      The UK, one of the world’s wealthiest countries [258], made much of its money from stealing resources from colonial territories [259].

      1.5.7.5. Con:
      Some states have paid reparations for historical damages, compensating for their theft.

        1.5.7.5.1. Pro:

        1.5.7.5.2. Con:
