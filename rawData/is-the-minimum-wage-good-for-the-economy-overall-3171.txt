Discussion Name:
Is the minimum wage good for the economy overall?

1. Thesis:
The minimum wage is good for the economy.

  1.1. Con:
  The minimum wage is unnecessary because the market determines a fair, equilibrium wage on its own.

    1.1.1. Con:
    Shocks to the market could result in the equilibrium wage becoming unfair. During the Coronavirus Pandemic, many jobs [1] have been lost and so people will be more desperate for jobs, making them willing to accept the lower wages that businesses would gladly offer to try and increase profits.

    1.1.2. Con:
    Without a minimum wage, there is an endless influx of inmigrants willing to work for very little. This endless influx of inmigrants prevents the equilibrium between demand and supply from happening.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    The minimum wage is an inefficient and unnatural tool to regulate labor costs

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      A study by UC Berkeley found [2] that the positive and negative effects of a minimum wage increase largely cancel each other out.

      1.1.3.2. Pro:
      In a high unemployment economy, an increase in minimum wage will create an even more inefficient labour market which will worsen the unemployment conditions.

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      The minimum wage creates inefficiencies (deadweight loss [3]), as it works the same way as a price floor.

    1.1.4. Con:
    The gap [4] between the highest pay rates and the lowest shows no sign of "fairness".

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      Fair is giving people the same opportunity [5], not outcome. Manipulating the economy by forcing people to pay some people more or other people less is unfair.

        1.1.4.1.1. Pro:
        An athlete running in a competition who finishes much slower than other competitors also has a gap between them and the best competitor. But that doesn't mean it was unfair.

        1.1.4.1.2. Con:
        'Fairness in opportunity' is unachievable [6]. Geography, access to education, family contacts, etc, all alter the 'starting line.'

          1.1.4.1.2.1. Con:
          "Fairness in opportunity" means that everyone plays by the same rules - equal opportunities.

      1.1.4.2. Con:
      If an employee generates high added value, it is fair to pay her more. Unfortunately a single factory worker does not add as much value economically as a CEO.

        1.1.4.2.1. Pro:
        If a person thinks he's perfectly able to do the work of a CEO, he's free to do so and start his own company. That's fair. The reason why people don't usually do that is because being a CEO is actually difficult, although necessary - thus more valuable.

      1.1.4.3. Pro:
      The free market economy is based on the idea of accumulation, therefore a true fairness cannot be reached by maintaining a capitalist economy.

    1.1.5. Con:
    This only applies on paper in a perfect competitive business scenario. When no local competitive option exists for the worker to move to, there is no incentive to pay a competitive wage.

      1.1.5.1. Pro:
      Large expenditures on long distance moves prevents global scale, or even national scale competition for low to middle income workers.

        1.1.5.1.1. Pro:
        The cost of long distance moves in the US can average [7] nearly $5000, which may be prohibitive to many low income families.

        1.1.5.1.2. Pro:
        Immigration laws prevent many unskilled workers from crossing national boundaries.

      1.1.5.2. Con:
      This fails to acknowledge that the same way employees need an employer, it is also true that employers need employees.

        1.1.5.2.1. Pro:
        An employer will be forced to pay higher wages if they can't find anyone willing to work for very low wages. A mandated minimum wage won't change this.

        1.1.5.2.2. Con:
        This is not true for unskilled workers during high unemployment times.

      1.1.5.3. Con:
      Workers can start their own business instead.

        1.1.5.3.1. Pro:
        In many countries [8], starting a new business is relatively easy, with many guides [9] available to help.

          1.1.5.3.1.1. Con:
          In many other countries, entrepreneurs face [10] a barrage of difficulties when starting a business.

        1.1.5.3.2. Con:
        Making a successful business requires a combination of luck, skill, intelligence, planning, and resources. It's not something that everyone has, and it's not something that everyone can get.

          1.1.5.3.2.1. Pro:
          In the US, about 20% of small businesses fail [11] in their first year, and only about a third survive after ten years.

    1.1.6. Pro:
    A minimum wage [12] is a dangerous response to the existence of poverty because it perverts the meaning and nature of work.

      1.1.6.1. Pro:
      The minimum wage implies that people should be paid disproportionately to what they produce, which can foster an entitlement [13] mindset

        1.1.6.1.1. Pro:
        Many Americans [14] see the danger of an entitlement mindset and are actively trying to combat the growing refusal to work.

      1.1.6.2. Pro:
      The minimum wage implies that the government should be involved with regulating the labor market, when in fact a free labor market [15] is better for the economy.

        1.1.6.2.1. Pro:
        Free labor markets are less susceptible [16] to politicians' agenda and lobbyist pressure.

        1.1.6.2.2. Con:
        Regulated labor markets provide [17] greater protection and benefits to workers.

      1.1.6.3. Pro:
      Minimum wage is unfair as the employee and the employer are owed nothing by the customer, even though prices may be placed on a menu of services, each transaction is a voluntary exchange.

      1.1.6.4. Pro:
      A minimum wage is a convention of a fiat currency, which is a convenience of the actual barter process taking place. A minimum wage gives money without the exchange of assets provided in a proper transaction.

      1.1.6.5. Con:
      Removing the primary incentive for work, reasonable compensation for choosing production over consumption, is a far greater threat to the meaning and nature of work.

    1.1.7. Pro:
    The standard of living has been increasing in the US since 1820 [18], over 100 years before the first minimum wage law [19] was passed.

      1.1.7.1. Pro:
      Minimum wage alone isn't sufficient justification for the standard of living increasing or decreasing, as there are multiple reasons for the standard of living to increase beyond economic causes.

        1.1.7.1.1. Pro:
        Although minimum wage can play a role, scientific advances in medicine, education, automation, expansion of legal rights and better social programs all increase the standard of living to varying degrees.

          1.1.7.1.1.1. Con:
          Access to the results of scientific advances is often dependent upon income. Without an adequate minimum wage, someone living in an advanced civilization may not be able to enjoy the 'available' benefits.

            1.1.7.1.1.1.1. Pro:
            Despite advances in women's hygiene, many low income women still suffer from "period poverty [20]".

        1.1.7.1.2. Con:
        The fact that things besides minimum wage can increase standard of living has no bearing on whether increasing minimum wage will increase the standard of living.

        1.1.7.1.3. Pro:
        Increases to income (let alone minimum wage) can not account for the technology leap [21] from from 1991 to the 2014 iPhone.

          1.1.7.1.3.1. Con:
          Given that the first minimum wage was established [22] in 1938, it's impossible to know exactly how much of an effect it had on an invention 66 years later.

          1.1.7.1.3.2. Con:
          The fact that this technological leap occurred during a time when minimum wages exist seems to undercut whatever point is trying to be made. iPhones were created [23] in a country where enough people made enough money to afford them, and were made by people who grew up, were educated, and worked in a country with a minimum wage and other similar programs.

            1.1.7.1.3.2.1. Con:
            iPhones are manufactured [24] in 43 different countries, many of which have lower wage standards than the US. Without this supply of cheap labor, the iPhone would be prohibitively expensive.

    1.1.8. Con:
    The market wage is a result of power structure, and is not necessarily the wage that benefits the economy the most when there is low employer-competition.

      1.1.8.1. Pro:
      An employer in a position of force, even if there is normal unemployment, will not only lower wages but will also lower worker security and can abuse their position in many other ways if the market is left unregulated. It is the role of the state to step in if there are abusive power structures and extreme working conditions.

          1.1.8.1.1.1. Pro:

          1.1.8.1.1.2. Pro:
          The US Department of Labor recovered [27] more than $330 million in back wages owed to workers in 2019

          1.1.8.1.1.3. Con:
          If employers are breaking the minimum wage laws, they should face the consequences, but having a minimum wage in the first place isn't the solution to this problem.

          1.1.8.1.1.4. Pro:
          In England [28], some companies hire self-employed delivery men to avoid the protection that the law imposes for employees. This caused extreme conditions that can lead to suicide, which is evidently not good for the economy.

        1.1.8.1.2. Con:
        There are many companies offering more payment than minimal wage. Companies willingly give more money to employers to motivate them to work better. Any decently educated company CEO knows that to earn more money you have to maximize profit, not to try reducing expenses.

          1.1.8.1.2.1. Pro:
          In 2018, Amazon [29], along with some other companies, increased their minimal wage, as they considered that it increases profit for the employer.

            1.1.8.1.2.1.1. Con:
            A government mandated minimum wage is not needed for private companies to pay their employees what they think is in the best interest of both employee and employer.

            1.1.8.1.2.1.2. Pro:
            Amazon, Target, and Walmart increasing the working wage increases the amount of money that the employees can spend in the economy and the amount of taxes that the government gets from those wages to spend on public works, education, etc. Both are good for the economy.

            1.1.8.1.2.1.3. Pro:
            Paying wages that are above the market rate, known within economics as efficiency wages [30], is an important motivating force for an existing employee base.

              1.1.8.1.2.1.3.1. Pro:
              This has shown [31] to increase labour productivity.

            1.1.8.1.2.1.4. Pro:
            A primary aim [32] for all business organisations is to add value and in the private sector this involves making a profit. If companies change their business models, it is to earn more profit; which is good for the economy as the company's profit can then be reinvested in the economy.

            1.1.8.1.2.1.5. Pro:
            In October 2018, Amazon [33] increased its minimum wage to $15 which allows employees to spend more and contributes to the economy.

              1.1.8.1.2.1.5.1. Pro:
              In the same time period, Amazon net income increased by 63% for the period October to December 2018 (as compared to the same quarter in 2017 [34]), also contributing more to the economy.

                1.1.8.1.2.1.5.1.1. Con:
                Reading into the Financial statement [35], the reason why they have such dramatic growth is not because of their direct selling operation (buying something directly from Amazon), but from third party vendors using their service. That amounted to half of all sales for Amazon.

                  1.1.8.1.2.1.5.1.1.1. Pro:
                  Those third party vendors do not count as employees of Amazon and hence they are not subject to the same wage increase but contribute more to Amazon's profit margin.

                1.1.8.1.2.1.5.1.2. Con:
                The $1 pay raise would only affect 350,000 (of which 100,000 are seasonal) [36] of it's work Force of 647,000 (Type in Employee) [37]. They are leveraging the wages in other parts of its worldwide workforce to offer this raise to its US staff.

                1.1.8.1.2.1.5.1.3. Con:
                As there is no publicly available breakdown of how many are low, entry level workers, the assumption that the $15 min wage applies to all employees is flawed, as most technology sector jobs are above minimum wage and do not have their salaries adjusted for the loss in purchasing power.

      1.1.8.2. Pro:
      Without regulation, owners of large businesses will continually undercut workers' wages regardless of harm to the replaceable workers and to smaller businesses in order to maximise their income.

        1.1.8.2.1. Con:
        -> See 1.1.8.1.2.

If they’re unable to enter the labor force, it’s harder to gain skills necessary for higher wages, thus perpetuating poverty.

  1.2. Con:
  Implementing a minimum wage increases inflation.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Minimum wage laws are cruel - by mandating an arbitrary threshold value to labor that, below that artificial point, labor is legally worthless and cannot be sold. If min-wage = $15, but I can only produce $12 in value, then my value is forced to $0 by law, and I am ineligible to legally earn any money at all. If my worth is magically inflated to equal $15 without actual value being created, the difference is inflation by definition, which is a form of regressive tax that hurts min wage earners.

    1.2.2. Con:
    A minimum wage is necessary because inflation occurs whether or not minimum wage increases.

      1.2.2.1. Pro:
      Inflation is essentially the result [38] of various market forces attempting to increase or maintain their relative value within the market. Without a minimum wage, or with a stagnant minimum wage, workers are unable to maintain the relative value of their work, which means that the relative value of their work decreases.

      1.2.2.2. Pro:
      From 1914 to 2020, the average inflation rate [39] in the US was 3.24%.

      1.2.2.3. Con:
      While some inflation can be good [40] for the economy, minimum wage increases can push it beyond what is healthy.

        1.2.2.3.1. Pro:
        Minimum wage allows workers to afford more necessities, and with the steady increase in price of valuable products (food, water, shelter, etc.), it reduces the amount of money that can circulate at any given time, thus encouraging inflation.

      1.2.2.4. Con:
      Many countries can go through prolonged periods of deflation [41].

        1.2.2.4.1. Pro:
        Japan's economy [42] has been in a state of protracted deflation since the 1990s.

      1.2.2.5. Pro:
      Most often, inflation occurs from the hoarding of resources by larger entities, for example, a company may increase the price of a product due to a shortage, but then the price would remain even after the shortage.

        1.2.2.5.1. Pro:
        Another example of inflation comes from the same source, a small group of people attempt to hoard most if not all of the wealth, thus forcing governments to bring more money into the system, and then causing the value of a singular dollar to drop immensely.

    1.2.3. Con:
    Every time there was a minimum wage increase between 1980 and 1991 in the US, there was a correlation with a decrease [43] in the annual inflation rate.

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      Correlation is not causation.

      1.2.3.2. Con:
      This seems to be demonstrably false using an Inflation [44] calculator and following the Federal Minimum wage [45] increase for each of the respective periods shown in the study.

    1.2.4. Pro:
    The rate at which savings are collected [46] of low income households are very low while they are very high for richer segments of society. This means that more money will be spent rather than saved and cause inflation.

      1.2.4.1. Con:
      This form of demand led inflation is typically not seen as an inflationary issue, because it implies that consumers are spending more and thus are better off financially.

    1.2.5. Pro:
    Corporations are likely to raise prices to make up for the increased cost of human capital.

      1.2.5.1. Con:
      If a company can't afford paying a minimum wage, it's likely not competitive in the first place. If the minimum wage eliminates uncompetitive market players, it's a positive thing from a macroeconomic perspective because it improves competition and market dynamicity. Only those higher prices will remain in the market which's sector was generally underpriced.

      1.2.5.2. Con:
      Not all costs are human capital costs and hence the rate at which corporations increase as a response to the rising cost of human capital is lower than the increased buying power of their employees.

    1.2.6. Pro:
    Minimum wage primarily distributes wealth from people making just over minimum wage, who do not receive wage increases when minimum wage goes up.

      1.2.6.1. Con:
      Inflation lessens everyone's buying power. If someone earns just over minimum wage, and minimum wage rises to match it, that person now has many more jobs to choose from, giving them more leverage over their employer.

        1.2.6.1.1. Con:
        This assumes that person who earns just over minimum wage is unable to choose lower paid job, which is nonsense.

        1.2.6.1.2. Con:
        If somebody earns just over minimum wage and minimum wage rises to match it, all products become more expensive and their wage probably rises just over new minimum wage again, so effectively nothing happened for them.

          1.2.6.1.2.1. Con:
          That assumes that raising wages will cause inflation at the same rate. This is not likely because human labor is just one of the contributing factors to inflation.

          1.2.6.1.2.2. Con:
          Inflation is the process by which money becomes devalued over time. Increasing wages is necessary for workers to maintain their relative position in the economy.

            1.2.6.1.2.2.1. Con:
            It is possible to increase wage without increasing minimum wage. Therefore minimum wage is not necessary for this.

            1.2.6.1.2.2.2. Con:
            Assumption is that worker loses its value (in inflating economy his wage is not increased), then with increased minimum wage this worker will either be unemployed or he will lose its value anyway, because even though his wage raised, costs of other products would increase more. Therefore his buying power would decrease with or without minimum wage same way (unless minimum wage makes him unemployed, in which case it decreases much more).

      1.2.6.2. Con:
      All wages should rise when there's inflation, but without a minimum wage, there's less reason to give a raise to the lowest-paid people, since they tend to have the least power.

        1.2.6.2.1. Con:
        Rising minimum wage only barely covers inflation caused by the rise of minimum wage, it doesn't cover other causes of inflation no matter how much you raise it.

          1.2.6.2.1.1. Con:
          This is inaccurate. The costs of goods and services are based on more than the wages involved, a ten percent rise in the cost of labor does not mean that the cost of the good will rise ten percent.

          1.2.6.2.1.2. Con:
          Inflation is driven by market competition. Different producers raise prices to increase, or maintain their position in the market relative to other goods or services. If all goods and services rise at the same rate, an equilibrium can be maintained. If one product or services stays the same price, it is, effectively, becoming cheaper. If labor prices don't rise, people are effectively getting paid less. Rising labor prices are necessary as long as inflation is occurring.

      1.2.6.3. Con:
      Eroded buying power of those just over minimum wage is offset by increased buying power of those making minimum wage, producing net economic benefit. Conceding that more people are making minimum wage than just over minimum wage, which is often the case.

        1.2.6.3.1. Pro:
        Minimum wage increases tend to increase consumer [47] spending [48] and overall economic growth which can only be attributed to more spending by those making minimum wage.

        1.2.6.3.2. Pro:
        The majority of people in the United States [49] are in the three lowest income tax brackets, of which the majority are minimum wage earners, given the maximum income [50] one is able to gain via full-time minimum wage.

      1.2.6.4. Pro:
      Minor inflation erodes their buying power, to slightly increase the wealth of people on minimum wage.

      1.2.6.5. Pro:
      People with real assets tend to lose the least, while those just over the poverty line lose the most.

  1.4. Con:
  Minimum wage laws harm the the least skilled workers by making it impossible for them to compete against more skilled workers, as it prevents them from working for less money.

    1.4.1. Pro:

      1.4.1.1. Pro:

      1.4.1.2. Con:
      Employers are almost always forced to demand higher productivity, regardless of employee wages. Businesses owners almost always desire more productivity from employees.

    1.4.2. Con:
    Minimum wage acts as a starting point for many; many skilled workers were at one point an unskilled worker, and in the current economic landscape, you need money to afford the education to become a skilled worker.

    1.4.3. Con:
    While minimum wage increases can result in a decrease in worker demand in some industries in the short term, it increases the amount of money going to the middle and lower class, which in turn increases the amount being spent, which results [53] in an increase in jobs in the mid to long term.

    1.4.4. Con:
    This argument is based upon the assumption that the typical unskilled worker is in competition with skilled workers, and that being able to work for less is keeping unskilled workers employed. However, the number of jobs available is related more to market demand for goods and services. As long as the amount of profit being made by a company exceeds the cost of the worker, the company will, in almost all cases, continue to employ enough people to keep up with the demand for their product.

      1.4.4.1. Con:
      This reply doesn't address the point of the post. It's not about companies making money, it's about how the minimum wage law hurts the least skilled. The post didn't say they wouldn't hire anyone, it said they would hire the most skilled of all available. Thus hurting the lesser skilled workers, who can only compete against better skilled workers by offering to work for less. Minimum wage laws outlaw their ability to do that.

        1.4.4.1.1. Con:
        It's always about the company making money. The pursuit of the best, most skilled worker for the money is a constant. The concern about the loss of low skill positions is only valid if the number of jobs available decreases. If the number of jobs available stay the same, then the company will continue trying to find the best, most skilled worker they can at the lowest price that they can, it just happens that the 'lowest price that they can' will have gone up.

      1.4.4.2. Con:
      It's reasonable and well explained assumption. You can choose quality or price. While skilled workers can earn more money, lesser skilled workers will be cheaper. By banning option to be cheaper, lesser skilled workers will be unemployed.

    1.4.5. Pro:
    Young workers routinely make-up about 50% of minimum wage earners 2017 [54] and 2016 [55]. As they age, workers negotiate better wages using skills, experience and education. The Bureau of Labor Stats shows the minimum wage is rarely used as a lifetime wage.

    1.4.6. Con:
    The state could offer training and support for unemployed people to increase their skills and increase their labor's market value so that they can find a job at the minimum wage.

      1.4.6.1. Pro:
      The US already has several of these programs at the state [56] and federal [57] level.

      1.4.6.2. Pro:
      Countries like Sweden are already offering [58] retraining programs to employees whose jobs will be lost to automation. This could be repurposed to those who have lost their job due to minimum wage increases.

      1.4.6.3. Con:
      Spending taxpayer money to improve the skills of society's least skilled is another form of unnatural wealth redistribution, as those with jobs would be the ones paying more taxes to fund the training. This should be avoided.

    1.4.7. Pro:
    The jobs [59] at the bottom rung of the opportunity ladder, allow people to climb to better opportunities.

    1.4.8. Con:
    With no minimum wage laws, there would be lower wages for the unskilled because those wages won't be regulated. There will also be lower wages for the skilled worker because the skilled worker will have to compete with the low wages of the unskilled workers.

      1.4.8.1. Pro:
