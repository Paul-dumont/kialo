Discussion Name:
Pro-Life vs Pro-Choice: Should Abortion be Legal?

1. Thesis:
Pregnant people should have the right to choose abortion.

  1.1. Con:
  Life begins at conception. Abortion, therefore, violates the foetus' rights to life and bodily autonomy.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Abortions pose several moral concerns about the treatment of the baby while in the womb, even amongst pro-choice advocates.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      Foetuses cannot feel pain until the third trimester (p. 947 [1]). In 2019 [2], 92.7% of US abortions were performed at 13 weeks or earlier.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      Abortions are a part of nature and, thus, sit outside of human constructions of morality.

        1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
        The Bruce effect [3] is a tendency in animals—mainly rodents—to terminate their pregnancies following exposure to the scent of an unfamiliar male.

          1.1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
          When a new male takes over a group of geladas [4] in the wild, the vast majority of females terminate their pregnancies [5].

          1.1.1.2.1.2. Con:
          The Bruce effect does not apply to humans. Rodents and other animals [6] also eat their babies if they cannot provide enough milk for them, something that humans do not do.

        1.1.1.2.2. Pro:
        Research suggests that pregnant female horses [7] might be able to make a subconscious decision, based on hormone levels, to terminate their pregnancies.

        1.1.1.2.3. Con:
        The contention that abortion is ethical or good because it occurs in nature is an appeal to nature [8]. Not all natural things are good. For example, infanticide is part of nature [9].

        1.1.1.2.4. Con:
        Many things happen in nature that would be unacceptable in human society, like female praying mantises eating the male [10] after mating to provide more nutrients to their eggs, or some spiders [11] dying for their offspring to have a life.

      1.1.1.3. Pro:
      Foetuses may feel pain [12] during a surgical abortion, which sometimes involves them being removed [13] piece by piece.

        1.1.1.3.1. Pro:
        A study concludes that, from the 15th week of gestation onward, “the foetus is extremely sensitive to painful stimuli", something that should preclude abortions from being carried out (p. 1031 [14]).

        1.1.1.3.2. Con:
        If causing someone to experience non-consensual pain is immoral, then it is similarly immoral to force someone to go through the pain of pregnancy and childbirth against their will.

        1.1.1.3.3. Con:
        There are many legitimate reasons [15] why people seek third trimester abortions, such as significant changes in their circumstances [16] or detection [17] of fetal anomalies and serious birth defects.

          1.1.1.3.3.1. Con:
          None of these reasons justify killing a living child that could survive outside the womb.

      1.1.1.4. Pro:
      Abortion does not give any due process [18] to the foetus, such as allowing it to confront an accuser [19], attend a hearing, or be judged by a jury of peers.

        1.1.1.4.1. Con:
        This conflates "humans" with "legal persons [20]". Only legal persons have de facto rights to due process. As foetuses are unborn they have no such rights or protections under common law.

          1.1.1.4.1.1. Pro:
          In US federal law, the concept of legal personhood is formalized by statute [21] to include "every infant member of the species homo sapiens who is born alive at any stage of development."

          1.1.1.4.1.2. Pro:
          It would be practically impossible to guarantee individual liberties before birth due to the high administrative costs and complex requirements such a policy would impose.

          1.1.1.4.1.3. Pro:
          Even if a foetus is considered human, it does not have a 'right to life' as it does not qualify for the status of 'personhood [22]' and, therefore, does not have legal rights.

            1.1.1.4.1.3.1. Pro:
            Until around 28-30 weeks [23] gestation, a foetus is not self-aware, conscious, or able to feel pain [24]. A woman should have the right to an abortion before such features of personhood [25] develop.

          1.1.1.4.1.4. Con:
          The fact that legal personhood laws that protect zygotes, embryos, and foetuses have had little success does not mean that they are wrong.

        1.1.1.4.2. Pro:
        The law does not necessarily always reflect what should be the case. Morally, one can still argue that a foetus should be recognised as a person regardless of the law.

          1.1.1.4.2.1. Pro:
          In Dred Scott v. Sandford [26], the US Supreme Court declared that Black people were 'not persons' and did not have the same rights as white people. While legal, this was certainly immoral.

          1.1.1.4.2.2. Pro:
          If laws were always moral, there wouldn't be any reason to demand changes in the law.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    The right to life [27] should be considered fundamental and absolute.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      Most people who are born do not wish to be killed; only 9.2% of people ever experience suicidal ideation (p. 1 [28]). Therefore, we can assume the foetus would not wish to die.

        1.1.2.1.1. Con:
        Measures of suicide can indicate how many people are willing to kill themselves, but it does not reflect how many foetuses would choose to be born or not.

        1.1.2.1.2. Con:
        The fetus would not wish not to die, and would have nothing to regret if it did not live to the point that it could reasonably be expected to hold such wishes.

        1.1.2.1.3. Pro:
        Governments make murder illegal as it violates the right to life of the victim. Given most people wish to live, the same should apply for abortion.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      The right to life is logically prior to other rights and should be prioritised accordingly.

        1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
        The right to life is a precondition for people to access their other rights and make any future choices. As abortion denies this to a foetus, it should be banned.

        1.1.2.2.2. Con:
        Governments do not do everything they can to ensure that people remain alive for as long as possible, which they should if the right to life is prior to other rights.

          1.1.2.2.2.1. Pro:
          Governments do not put all of their money into healthcare and medical research to allow people to live as long as possible.

          1.1.2.2.2.2. Pro:
          Governments do not make it a requirement of people to donate organs even though this would mean being able to save many people's lives.

          1.1.2.2.2.3. Con:
          Governments do not do many things that are good for their population. Such failures are no indication of what they should be doing.

          1.1.2.2.2.4. Con:
          The 'right to life' is not the same as 'the right to be kept alive for as long as possible'. It only means that a human has the right to live, and should not be killed by another human being.

      1.1.2.3. Con:
      States can and do condone the ending of life; there is no reason they should not extend this principle to abortion too.

        1.1.2.3.1. Con:
        A foetus has done nothing to warrant the removal of their rights. Therefore, states should not condone the ending of their life as occurs in other circumstances.

        1.1.2.3.2. Pro:
        Capital punishment is still legal [29] in a number of countries, and in a number of US states [30].

          1.1.2.3.2.1. Pro:
          The death penalty can be justified on the basis that it will save more lives [31] in the long run. The same could be said for abortion, in cases where the mother's life is at risk.

          1.1.2.3.2.2. Con:
          Capital punishment is an act of last resort, for example, when a prisoner is still a danger to society. It is not a statement on the right to life of the one being executed.

        1.1.2.3.3. Pro:
        It is not typically considered murder to "pull the plug" on someone who is brain dead, even if their heart is still beating (p. 103 [32]).

          1.1.2.3.3.1. Pro:
          A heartbeat is a mechanical function like any other and, though it can indicate life, it is not sufficient [33] to consider someone to be alive.

          1.1.2.3.3.2. Con:
          Pulling the plug on a brain dead person occurs when medical professionals agree there is very little to no chance at life [34]. The same is not always true of aborted foetuses.

          1.1.2.3.3.3. Con:
          In these cases, the assisted death of someone is performed with the intent to end suffering [35]. The same cannot be said of abortions.

      1.1.2.4. Con:
      As life [36] is net suffering, every birth is utility negative [37]. Abortion, therefore, decreases the total amount of suffering [38] in the world.

    1.1.3. Con:
    Even if a foetus is a life, it does not mean it has the same value as those who are born. Most people would likely opt to save a human child over fertilised eggs.

      1.1.3.1. Con:
      People might choose the child over fertilised eggs, not because the fertilised eggs lack value, but only because the child would feel pain while dying.

      1.1.3.2. Pro:
      This is largely because a born child possesses many of the qualities that give humans rights, like the ability to make decisions, interact with others, and learn, among others.

      1.1.3.3. Con:
      It might not have the same attributed moral value but it's still entitled to the human right of life. Most people would choose to save a baby over a very old person that doesn't mean the very old person isn't entitled to the same rights as the baby

      1.1.3.4. Con:
      Moral instincts do not necessarily correlate to correct moral decisions [39]. That most would choose to save a child over an embryo does not justify abortion.

    1.1.4. Con:
    Life begins at independent viability.

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      Future technology may be able to develop artificial wombs as an alternative to pregnancy (p. 751 [40]). This means what is 'viable' is partially dependent upon current technology.

        1.1.4.1.1. Con:
        Laws change as science and technology progresses (p. 222 [41]). We should make laws based on the capabilities of today's technology since we can't predict what will be possible in the future.

      1.1.4.2. Pro:
      Perception, a core component of life, requires a nervous system, which is not present in the zygote. The nervous system appears about 18 days after conception [42] and the zygote phase lasts only about four days [43].

      1.1.4.3. Pro:
      During pregnancy, the foetus is reliant [44] on its parent to maintain their life. This is no longer the case once they are born, making them independent and truly alive.

      1.1.4.4. Con:
      Even if a foetus does not look, walk, and talk like those outside of the womb, it has the potential to become a person. It is this potential that should be respected and preserved.

        1.1.4.4.1. Pro:
        Not all infants develop into adults. However, this does not justify labeling infants as non-persons, as is often the case with foetuses.

        1.1.4.4.2. Con:
        Like for "life", it depends where you put the cursor on where the "life potential" begins. A gamete (an egg or a spermatozoon) also has the potential to become a person and yet nobody (or maybe very extreme views) asks to preserve each gamete produced by humanity.

        1.1.4.4.3. Con:
        There is no guarantee that a foetus that remains in the womb will develop into a baby since, regrettably, miscarriages [45] occur.

          1.1.4.4.3.1. Con:
          The potential for something to happen unintentionally (e.g. miscarriage) does not mean it's ok to make it happen intentionally (e.g. abortion). It is as strange as to say: someone walking might stumble, that's why it's ok to push this person to make them stumble.

        1.1.4.4.4. Con:
        The potential to live cannot be used to justify abortion bans given because that assumes that bare life is morally significant, regardless of its length and quality.

          1.1.4.4.4.1. Con:
          It is uncommon for babies in modern societies to live short lives; it should not be assumed that any particular baby will undergo this.

          1.1.4.4.4.2. Pro:
          For example, we would not suggest that all foetuses that are likely to have short lives of suffering should be aborted.

    1.1.5. Pro:
    Human life begins at fertilisation [46].

      1.1.5.1. Con:
      If life starts at conception, all fertilised eggs, viable or otherwise, should be protected in the same way as a baby. Yet, this is not advocated [47] for by pro-life groups.

      1.1.5.2. Pro:
      Natural, developmental changes in the outward appearance of a living organism - even dramatic changes like with butterflies - do not produce a different kind of organism. According to some scientists [48], a zygote is a distinct, living, and whole human being.

      1.1.5.3. Con:
      Tadpoles are the beginning of the frog lifecycle. However, they are still not frogs, much as caterpillars are not butterflies, nor are blastocysts human.

  1.2. Pro:
  People have a right to bodily autonomy and should ultimately be able to make choices about what happens to their bodies.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    There are many reasons why a pregnant person may not be equipped to or want to raise a child. In these cases, it is important that they are able to have an abortion.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      Some people may not have the financial resources to go through a pregnancy, as it is very expensive [49] and time-consuming.

        1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
        Many people who get abortions are already parents [50], and want to provide the best care they can to their existing children, financially, emotionally, and physically.

          1.2.1.1.1.1. Pro:
          The drop in parents’ income and wealth around childbirth can have major [51] harmful consequences for children (p. 1 [52]), affecting their long-term socio-emotional, cognitive, and academic outcomes (p. 15 [53]).

          1.2.1.1.1.2. Con:
          After abortions, parents may attempt to replace the aborted child with another child. These children are more likely to face obsessive parenting styles (p. iii [54]), which can be detrimental to a child's development and behavior (p. 1 [55], p. 13 [56]).

          1.2.1.1.1.3. Con:
          Children may struggle to understand why their parents had an abortion if they find out; they could feel that their lives are disposable, or that they are a mere convenience.

            1.2.1.1.1.3.1. Pro:
            Children could experience feelings of guilt [57] about surviving when a possible sibling did not. Learning of their parent's abortion could be damage their psyche and harm the child-parent relationship.

            1.2.1.1.1.3.2. Con:
            Scientific studies have shown that parents opting against an abortion often results in negative developmental and socio-economic consequences for their existing children (p. 183 [58]).

        1.2.1.1.2. Pro:
        Pregnancy and motherhood have a substantial negative impact on one’s career (pp. 4-5 [59]).

          1.2.1.1.2.1. Pro:
          A study [60] at Cornell University found that being a mother is a clear disadvantage when applying for jobs. Mothers face a wage penalty of around $11,000 compared to their childless peers (p. 1316 [61]).

          1.2.1.1.2.2. Pro:
          Legal and accessible abortion contributes significantly [62] to increases in the number of women who become doctors and lawyers.

          1.2.1.1.2.3. Con:
          In most Western countries, there are legal measures [63] that allow women to retain their employment status, forbidding discrimination on the basis of reproductive capacity.

          1.2.1.1.2.4. Con:
          Patriarchal oppression, not motherhood, harms women's careers. It is possible [64] to employ and educate mothers while giving them ample maternity leave and equal pay.

        1.2.1.1.3. Con:
        Pregnancy and the growth in household size may trigger an increase in public program [65] eligibility and benefit levels among economically disadvantaged households.

          1.2.1.1.3.1. Pro:
          The Special Supplemental Nutritional Program for Women, Infants and Children (WIC [66]) is a federal assistance program that provides near-cash nutritional assistance to low-income pregnant women, breastfeeding women, and children under the age of five.

          1.2.1.1.3.2. Pro:
          Many [67] major [68] cash and near-cash [69] safety net programs in USA either restrict eligibility to households with children, or increase benefit levels when a new child is added to the assistance unit.

          1.2.1.1.3.3. Con:
          Even in most developed countries [70], the social assistance that new mothers are entitled to is rarely sufficient [71] on its own, especially if the mother was forced to give up her job.

          1.2.1.1.3.4. Con:
          Women who are denied abortions are 3.77 times more likely to be in poverty and 6.26 times more likely to be on public assistance six months afterwards compared to those who receive an abortion (p. 1290 [72]).

      1.2.1.2. Pro:
      A woman may not feel emotionally prepared to have a child, which could ultimately be damaging for both parties, if she then cannot procure an abortion.

        1.2.1.2.1. Con:
        Not being prepared to accept the consequences of one's decisions and actions is not an adequate justification for terminating a human life.

        1.2.1.2.2. Pro:
        Parenthood hastens marital decline, causing a steep [73] decline when compared to child-free couples (p. 1 [74]).

          1.2.1.2.2.1. Con:
          There is an increased risk for separation or divorce following an abortion (p. 4 [75]).

          1.2.1.2.2.2. Con:
          Anger [76] is a common post-abortion emotional response [77]. Therefore, couples may experience more conflict after an abortion.

        1.2.1.2.3. Pro:
        Some teen parents regret becoming pregnant, with this feeling being particularly exacerbated by poor mental health (p. 13 [78]).

      1.2.1.3. Pro:
      A person might feel that their home or neighborhood [79] does not provide a safe environment for a child and so seek an abortion.

        1.2.1.3.1. Pro:
        People in physically abusive relationships are likely to stay tethered [80] to their partners if they cannot access abortion services. This puts them and their children at an increased risk of violence.

          1.2.1.3.1.1. Con:
          Positive, healthy relationships also last longer [81] when abortion services are inaccessible. The benefits caused by healthy relationships lasting longer may outweigh the negative effects accrued from bad relationships enduring.

          1.2.1.3.1.2. Pro:
          Domestic violence is common [82] among women having abortions.

          1.2.1.3.1.3. Con:
          Governments are able to remove children from abusive families. This service – alongside adoption, imprisonment, and help from family members – saves countless children.

            1.2.1.3.1.3.1. Pro:
            For example, in the United States, the Child Protective Services [83] can remove children from dangerous environments.

            1.2.1.3.1.3.2. Con:
            The initial experience of being in an abusive household can give children lifelong trauma [84]. So too can the experience of being taken away by Child Protective Services.

            1.2.1.3.1.3.3. Con:
            It would be in the state's interest to have as few children taken away from their parents by Child Protective Services as possible to avoid significant increases in public spending for various social care services.

        1.2.1.3.2. Con:
        Optimal conditions for child-rearing are relatively rare. Children raised in unsafe environments can survive and thrive despite risk factors.

      1.2.1.4. Con:
      If a mother cannot raise her child, it is the moral and legal [85] duty of the government to look after the child as a citizen. By legalising abortion, the government washes its hands of this responsibility.

      1.2.1.5. Con:
      Many individuals' families [86] and communities can and do take on child-rearing responsibilities to help parents, reducing the need for abortion.

        1.2.1.5.1. Con:
        Given that 6 in 10 women [87] who had an abortion in the UK claimed their reason for doing so was childcare costs, it seems unlikely that families or communities are currently able to meaningfully reduce the burden of childcare.

        1.2.1.5.2. Con:
        Not everyone is able—or wants to—live with, or close to, their family. Given this, abortion may be the best way for them to avoid high childcare costs [88].

        1.2.1.5.3. Pro:
        In times of need, parents are often able to informally group together [89] and support each other to provide childcare.

        1.2.1.5.4. Pro:
        Parent and baby groups can help people strengthen [90] their community bonds and make it more likely that they have the support needed to raise a child, reducing the need to have an abortion.

    1.2.2. Con:
    The foetus is not part of one's body but a separate entity. Therefore, the pregnant person is not just making a decision about their body when they get an abortion.

      1.2.2.1. Con:
      If the child is a seperate entity to the pregant person, there exists no special moral obligation or relationship between them. They ought to be treated as strangers.

        1.2.2.1.1. Con:
        Unlike the relationship between a person and a stranger, a pregnant person typically makes a choice that leads to pregnancy, such that they have a direct relationship with and obligation to the foetus.

        1.2.2.1.2. Pro:
        Judith Jarvis Thomson [91] argues that people would be outraged if they found themselves plugged to an ailing violinist, obliged to keep them alive for nine months. In cases where biological parents do not choose to create life, the violinist in the thought experiment is equivalent to the foetus.

          1.2.2.1.2.1. Con:
          The violinist is a stranger, while in a pregnancy the foetus is the mother's own child. As the foetus is dependent on the mother to live, their relationship is not like that of two strangers.

            1.2.2.1.2.1.1. Pro:
            A mother who disregards her child's basic needs is guilty of child neglect [92], whereas someone who neglects a stranger begging for food is not guilty of a crime.

            1.2.2.1.2.1.2. Pro:
            A mother has duties [93] to her two-year old son, regardless of whether the child is "wanted". Therefore, it is plausible that the foetus being "unwanted" doesn't take away her duties towards it.

            1.2.2.1.2.1.3. Con:
            Just as you cannot force a mother to donate an organ or blood to her child, you cannot force her to use her body to carry a foetus, even if that foetus is technically under her care.

          1.2.2.1.2.2. Pro:
          Such an experiment demonstrates how abortion does not violate a foetus' right to life. Rather, abortion just deprives the foetus of the use [94] of the pregnant person's body.

      1.2.2.2. Pro:
      A mother and foetus are two separate and distinct human organisms and should be considered as such in order to protect the foetus.

        1.2.2.2.1. Con:
        The foetus is connected to the mother's body via the umbilical cord; it cannot sustain its own life. The foetus is not separate from her body but an extension of it.

        1.2.2.2.2. Pro:
        While similar due to inherited chromosomes and genetic traits, the DNA of a foetus [95] is not the same as its mother's.

          1.2.2.2.2.1. Con:
          It is possible [96] that a foetus is not genetically unique from its mother.

      1.2.2.3. Pro:
      If there is no consensus about whether the foetus is a person with protected rights, we should err on the side of caution and protect the foetus.

        1.2.2.3.1. Con:
        Whether or not to have a child is a personal decision. For any entity to intervene in this family matter is unwanted and intrusive action.

        1.2.2.3.2. Con:
        If no consensus on the need to protect foetuses exists, the decision should be given to individuals to decide.

        1.2.2.3.3. Pro:
        There is no consensus that foetuses do not deserve human rights. Given this lack of consensus, abortion should not be permissible.

    1.2.3. Pro:
    Decisions which largely impact an individual and their body should ultimately be made by the individual, rather than by the state.

      1.2.3.1. Pro:
      For example, states allow people to undertake many activities that may harm them, from extreme sports to smoking cigarettes.

        1.2.3.1.1. Con:
        The state also issues restrictions on these rights when risks are involved such as smoking or drinking before 21 or driving before 16. [97]

      1.2.3.2. Con:
      The state has the power to prevent actions that harm others, not to compel actions [98] to prevent harm. The two are not the same.

        1.2.3.2.1. Pro:
        The law cannot compel someone to jump in and save a drowning man, but it can intervene or punish someone who attempts to drown another person.

      1.2.3.3. Pro:
      Banning abortion betrays the role of government, which is to preserve individual liberty.

        1.2.3.3.1. Pro:
        Individual freedom is the basis of the US Constitution [99] and is stated as such in the Declaration of Independence [100]. This is also set out in the European Convention on Human Rights (p. 8 [101]).

        1.2.3.3.2. Con:
        The role of government is to create [102] well-functioning and sustainable environments where people can exercise their rights to the fullest extent. As such, where necessary, individual liberty can be partially sacrificed.

        1.2.3.3.3. Pro:
        By removing the ability of people to opt out of pregnancies they do not desire, they become controlled by their reproductive systems.

    1.2.4. Con:
    Bodily autonomy is not an unlimited or absolute right.

      1.2.4.1. Pro:
      In approximately 87% of casees, rape and maternal health are not cited as reasons for an abortion. Thus, very rarely should the bodily autonomy of the mother usurp that of the foetus (p. 113 [103]).

      1.2.4.2. Pro:
      The right to life should trump the right to bodily autonomy because the right to life is a precondition to accessing bodily autonomy.

        1.2.4.2.1. Con:
        Many wars have been fought and lives sacrificed for national and individual autonomy, and freedom. Thus, people commonly accept that preserving one’s liberty is worth more than life, be it their own or someone else’s.

        1.2.4.2.2. Pro:
        Bodily autonomy cannot be exercised unless one is alive and has the capacity to make choices. As such, bodily autonomy has a secondary importance to the guarantee of life.

      1.2.4.3. Con:
      Bodily autonomy—and freedom of choice more generally—are more important than the right to life.

        1.2.4.3.1. Pro:
        This is why many people are willing to risk death or grave injury to fight for their freedoms.

        1.2.4.3.2. Pro:
        Without the freedom to make choices, life would be meaningless for humans, as it is our decisions that allow us to craft our lives and assert what is valuable to us (p. 11 [104]).

        1.2.4.3.3. Con:
        States limit individuals' bodily autonomy [105] when such restrictions are better for society as a whole. This also holds in the case of banning abortion wherein the state can protect foetuses.

    1.2.5. Con:
    Men have a large stake in the health and life of a foetus. Legalising abortion allows this stake to be bypassed so it should be banned.

      1.2.5.1. Pro:
      Men can experience significant emotional harms from the decision to abort their child.

        1.2.5.1.1. Con:
        Emotional harm to one person is an insufficient justification for controlling the actions of another, especially when doing so would grossly invade their privacy.

        1.2.5.1.2. Con:
        While men may face emotional harms if their partner seeks an abortion, women face both emotional and physical harms when forced to carry a child to term.

        1.2.5.1.3. Pro:
        If a woman chooses to have an abortion, there can be emotional harms [106] to the man if he wanted the child, or if he believes that abortion is immoral.

      1.2.5.2. Pro:
      If a parent believes that their child's life is in danger, they have a right [107] to attempt to save it non-violently. This is true, even if such an intervention is very stressful for the pregnant partner.

      1.2.5.3. Con:
      Legalising abortion may allow the father's stake to be bypassed if he wishes for the child to be born, but prohibiting abortion would directly bypass his stake if he wishes [108] for the fetus to be aborted.

      1.2.5.4. Pro:
      It is not only mothers who are forced to take care of unwanted children.

        1.2.5.4.1. Con:
        In the US, only 50% of single parents [109] have a child support agreement with the other parent, which shows that it is difficult to enforce financial responsibility.

        1.2.5.4.2. Pro:
        Fathers often have to support their children until they are no longer a dependent [110] which emotionally burdens them.

      1.2.5.5. Con:
      A genetic relationship to a child does not grant a person any rights that would supersede a mother's right to bodily autonomy.

        1.2.5.5.1. Pro:
        Men seldom have to face the physical or emotional burden of carrying a child to term, and so cannot decide to put someone through the pains of pregnancy.

  1.3. Pro:
  Many people do not become pregnant by choice. It is wrong to force them to remain pregnant when they do not want to be.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    Some pregnancies are the results of acts of violence, like rape [111].

      1.3.1.1. Con:
      There are contraceptive methods, such as the morning-after pill, which can be used to guard against the possibility of becoming pregnant following cases of rape.

        1.3.1.1.1. Con:

This means that the status of person in law is granted to beings designated by the law as right-holders, even though on other occasions they can also be objects of rights or obligations held by other persons in law (as in case of, e.g. a corporation being a property of others or a child that the contract between its parents and a groove may relate to). In other words, being a person in law consists in the ability to enter a legal relation as a party and not only in being an object of a legal relation created by others."
