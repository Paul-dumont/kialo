Discussion Name:
Is global overpopulation a myth?

1. Thesis:
Global overpopulation is a myth.

  1.1. Con:
  The natural environment is undergoing a severe collapse due to human activity [1].

    1.1.1. Pro:
    While there is sufficient land area to produce more food and shelter for a larger population, the increased electricity output required would add tons of CO2 emissions [2] to the atmosphere.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      Society has not yet developed a reliable method for the recycling of lithium-ion batteries [3], despite their increased usage in the green technology sector.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      With sufficient efforts, energy production could be shifted to renewable, non polluting sources.

        1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
        Moving to renewable energy moves the consumption problem from hydrocarbon fuel chains to rare earth metals [4], something we are already drastically increasing consumption of year on year.

        1.1.1.2.2. Con:
        Wind and solar energy are not manipulable and do not meet [5] society's consumption requirements.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    If the environment were to collapse, it would be challenging to feed the human population.

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      There are many seed banks around the world [6] which contain the seeds of crucial food stocks in the event of an environmental collapse.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      Fish stocks are collapsing due to overfishing [7] to compensate for feeding the overpopulated planet.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        Fish farms [8] could be a way to supplement diminished wild fish stocks in the oceans.

        1.1.2.2.2. Pro:
        Overfishing is causing fishermen to catch fewer adult fish [9], which prevents juveniles from growing into adults and further diminishes fishing stocks.

      1.1.2.3. Pro:
      People might have to resort to cannibalism [10] without reliable food supplies.

        1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
        Throughout many tough periods in history, people have resorted to cannibalism [11] to survive. If food stocks were to run out, people would likely resort to cannibalism again.

        1.1.2.3.2. Pro:
        Despite the taboo nature of the subject, some widely practiced social customs [12] are inherently cannibalistic. It's perfectly logical to infer that, if necessary, more elements of the practice could become normalised in civil society.

        1.1.2.3.3. Con:
        The practice of cannibalism is opposed by most mainstream sytems of morality.

          1.1.2.3.3.1. Con:
          In some cultures, practices such as endocannibalism [13] are considered morally acceptable [14].

          1.1.2.3.3.2. Pro:
          Most cultures contain traditions that venerate the dead [15]. As such, the consumption of their flesh post-mortem is considered to be disrespectful, impious, and morally dubious.

        1.1.2.3.4. Con:
        Cannibalism is non-viable as a solution due its close association with the development of prion diseases [16], particularly Kuru [17].

          1.1.2.3.4.1. Pro:
          Cultural phenomena associated with cannibalistic societies, such as mortuary feasts, accelerate [18] or inform the pattern in which prion diseases spread in these communities.

          1.1.2.3.4.2. Con:
          Cannibalism might become a viable practice in the future [19].

      1.1.2.4. Con:
      Over the last 200 years, global hunger plummeted [20] as the human population exploded. [21]

        1.1.2.4.1. Con:
        The population exploded due to mechanical and chemical farming techniques [22]. Both will become much more difficult with oil and gas extraction peaks coming.

        1.1.2.4.2. Pro:
        In the 1900s [23], the average farmer could produce food for a family of five, today they can produce food for at least 100 people.

    1.1.3. Con:
    Reducing the population would not suddenly lead to everyone being more respectful of the environment.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      Behaviourial change is never guaranteed on an individual level, even if significant changes in demographic composition were proven to affect the behaviours of wider society.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      If authoritarian governments elected to kill people who disrespect the environment, especially the CEOs of companies that significantly pollute the environment, climate change would subside quickly.

        1.1.3.2.1. Con:
        Promoting the systemic slaughter of sentient human beings for the purposes of protecting the environment is a dangerous endorsement of ecofacism [24].

        1.1.3.2.2. Con:
        Genocide is a crime against humanity [25], regardless of any supposedly benevolent intention.

        1.1.3.2.3. Pro:
        People who significantly damage the environment contribute to deaths [26] associated with climate change and should be treated like murderers.

        1.1.3.2.4. Pro:
        Harsh punishments against people who damage the environment would deter others from taking part in similar activities (p. 4 [27]).

    Humanity is becoming increasingly capable of addressing environmental concerns through technology and innovation to ensure its survival.

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      Using the I = PAT [28] equation, the increased efficiency of technology does not sufficiently compensate for the growth of affluence and population size.

        1.1.4.1.1. Con:

        1.1.4.2.2. Pro:

        1.1.4.3.2. Con:

      1.1.4.4. Pro:
      With the current state of communication technology the global population could spread out evenly across the Earth's surface.

        1.1.4.4.2. Con:

          1.1.4.4.2.2. Con:

        1.1.4.4.3. Pro:
        Technological integation is much more prevalent in urbanized environments than rural settings, which attracts migration due to increased employment opportinuites in certain sectors.

          1.1.4.4.3.1. Pro:
          Many business and employment opportunities depend on the availability of high speed internet [43]; people move towards cities to avail themselves of those opportunities.

          1.1.4.4.3.2. Con:
          Cutting-edge communication techology, such as 5G, is much more readily available in urban settings because of its scaling limitations [44].

        1.1.4.4.4. Con:
        Widespread communication is expensive to develop, while the return on investment (ROI) significantly decreases [45] as the infrastructure moves further away from cities.

          1.1.5.4.3.3. Con:

1. Overview

The most widely used summary measure of inequality in the distribution of household income is the Gini coefficient.

The lower its value, the more equally household income is distributed.

The Gini coefficient is a measure of the way in which different groups of households receive differing shares of total household income.

For example, the bottom 5% of households might only have a 1% share of total household income. The bottom 10% of households might have a 3% share; the bottom 20% might have an 8% share, and so on.

The Gini coefficient is a measure of the overall extent to which these groupings of households, from the bottom of the income distribution upwards, receive less than an equal share of income."
