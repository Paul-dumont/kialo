Discussion Name:
Is working as an entrepreneur preferable to having a corporate job?

1. Thesis:
Working as an entrepreneur is preferable to having a corporate job.

  1.1. Con:
  Many people are more suited for corporate work than entrepreneurial.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Most people [1] dream of owning their own business, but only 6% make it. The other 94% has tried, failed, going back to employment status.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    Working as an entrepreneur requires one to have the discipline to establish and maintain steady work routines as opposed to being handed a set working routines that one is required to follow.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Being pushed into being an entrepreneur when one doesn't want or are able to will prevent people from reaching their potential as workers.

    1.1.4. Pro:
    Being an entrepreneur is extremely tough and requires you working much more than most normal jobs.

  1.2. Pro:
  Owning a business means unlimited financial earning potential

    1.2.1. Pro:
    As an entrepreneur, the earning potential is greater than working for a corporation.

      1.2.1.1. Con:
      The potential for loss is just as great as well.

        1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
        There is too much financial risk [2] involved with being an entrepreneur.

          1.2.1.1.1.1. Con:
          There is equally great potential for financial success.

          1.2.1.1.1.2. Pro:
          Most startups go bankrupt.

            1.2.1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
            Eight out of 10 new businesses fail [3] within the first 18 months.

            1.2.1.1.1.2.2. Pro:
            Eighty-two percent [4] of startups fail because of cash-flow issues.

            1.2.1.1.1.2.3. Pro:
            Start-ups era has ended, [5] therefore it makes no sense to become a start-up entrepreneur.

      1.2.1.2. Pro:
      Entrepreneurs earn7% [6] more than they would have in a corporate job.

    1.2.2. Con:
    Owning a business means competing against more established businesses, which means a lot more financial barriers.

      1.2.2.1. Pro:
      Bigger businesses can afford to offer lower prices on their products and services.

      1.2.2.2. Con:
      There is not always established businesses present in every location or niche

      1.2.2.3. Con:
      Established businesses are not necessarily competitive.

    1.2.3. Con:
    The potential is irrelevant. What is important are the expected earnings and these are meagre, especially if risk-adjusted, as most new businesses go bankrupt and just cost the entrepreneur money.

  1.3. Con:
  Owning a business means having full responsibility.

    1.3.1. Con:
    This responsibility can be empowering and allow people to enjoy their job more rather than having to work under someone else's decisions.

    1.3.2. Pro:
    A corporate job provides a stable pay check every month.

      1.3.2.1. Pro:
      -> See 1.2.1.1.1.

    1.3.3. Pro:
    When a entrepreneur's business fails, they are faced having to fire their loyal employees.

  1.4. Con:
  The more entrepreneurs there are, the fewer the number of job candidates there are to support the number of businesses created by them.

    1.4.1. Pro:
    Millennials [7] are increasingly choosing entrepreneurs over getting a traditional 9-to-5. As this progresses, less are entering already established businesses.

  1.5. Pro:
  Entrepreneurs can build something from the ground up that is truly their own.

    1.5.1. Con:
    Having something that is truly one's own doesn't matter as much on the consumer end, especially if the product's not sold or wanted.

    1.5.2. Con:
    It's easier to work for a corporation that is already started, than from the ground up.

    1.5.3. Pro:
    As an entrepreneur, one can take an idea that has already been done, and create it in their own unique style.

  1.6. Con:
  Entrepreneurship involves decisions and inherent features that make society worse off.

    1.6.1. Pro:
    Entrepreneurs, in the form of 'necessity entrepreneurship' [8], may not grow the GDP as quickly as they would as an employee in a business.

    1.6.2. Pro:
    The more self-employed a person is, the less money they contribute to the GDP (1 [9]).

    1.6.3. Pro:
    Monopolies can cause issues [10] for the whole economy.

  1.7. Con:
  Too many companies (from too many entrepreneurs) creates an inefficient industry that would be better off with a few great companies.

    1.7.1. Con:
    Healthy competition drives better products/services for customers

    1.7.2. Pro:
    The smaller companies get bought up [11] by larger companies anyway, so entrepreneurship really doesn't work in the end.

      1.7.2.1. Con:
      Without starting smaller companies for larger companies to grab, larger companies would not be as successful as they are today.

    1.7.3. Pro:
    Clients would have too much difficulty picking the right company to do business with if there are too many companies to choose from.

    1.7.4. Pro:
    It's more difficult to establish sustainable long-term systems [12] in businesses when there's too many entrepreneurs. This is because fewer people work on improving already established businesses and instead look to establish new ones.

  1.8. Pro:
  Being able to form and develop your own business, in your own way without the hindrance of meddling senior executives is how most of today's major corporations, such as the Ford Motor Company, Facebook etc., were started.

  1.9. Pro:
  Entrepreneurs have full decision-making capability for their business.

    1.9.1. Con:
    Have full decision making capacity puts a lot of stress [13] on the entrepreneur.

    1.9.2. Pro:
    The founder of a startup makes the final decision on all systems and structures of their business, ultimately allowing it to grow as the envision it.

    1.9.3. Pro:
    Entrepreneurs can define their own hours of work.

      1.9.3.1. Con:
      Entrepreneurs often struggle with work/life balance, many working more than 50 hours [14] a week.

        1.9.3.1.1. Pro:
        Work might be much more stressful.

          1.9.3.1.1.1. Pro:
          Stress can cause anxiety and depression. [15]

          1.9.3.1.1.2. Pro:
          Stress is counter-productive.

            1.9.3.1.1.2.1. Con:
            Some people might feel stress as an input to do better.

        1.9.3.1.2. Con:
        Although entrepreneurs may work more hours per week than employees, this larger amount of hours may feel more worthwhile than a lesser amount of hours worked as an employee of a business

      1.9.3.2. Pro:
      Many people go into entrepreneurship to become their own boss, essentially getting away from the standard 9-to-5 [16] work day.
