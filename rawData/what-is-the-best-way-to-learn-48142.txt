      1.32.3.1. Con:

    1.32.4. Pro:
    The information is generally more neutral and less biased than other sources.

      1.32.4.1. Pro:
      That the information presented is less biased is a stated goal of wikipedia, general goal of encyclopedias and intended ideal of all third sources.

        1.32.4.1.1. Con:
        This isn't the general goal of encyclopedias and intended goal of all third sources.

        1.32.4.1.2. Pro:
        Pillar #2 of Wikipedia's five pillars. [112]

      1.32.4.2. Pro:
      Ways of learning knowledge are better if they include checks on the honesty of the information. This claim is one of the goals that all the methods of learning are trying to accomplish. This claim is a criterion.

      1.32.4.3. Con:
      Wikipedia is nevertheless very biased when it comes to a few subjects which becomes clear when one reads just the lead of the Pentagon UFO videos [113] and compares it for example to third sources on the subject.

    1.32.5. Con:
    There aren't yet article-integrated quizzes that ask questions about key things one should have learned from the article. This helps to ensure that one understood or got things right after reading it. Additionally, one could use these to see whether one remembers things later on.

      1.32.5.1. Con:
      One could enter the article text into an AI and prompt it to generate questions about key facts.

      1.32.5.2. Con:
      Student could make cloze deletion flashcards right out of the article.

    1.32.6. Pro:
    It is more interactive thank books and allows easily going to related articles to learn more about related concepts via the wikilinks such as when one didn't understand a concept.

      1.32.6.1. Pro:
      The wikilink Article Previews hovercards allow seeing brief information about a concept by just hovering the mouse over the wikilink.

    1.32.7. Pro:
    Wikipedia is a reliable source of information. [114]

      1.32.7.1. Con:
      This is less the case for little read articles on Wikipedias that are not English Wikipedia.

    1.32.8. Con:
    Due to the notability policy [115] and related procedures, lots of things are not considered notable that you may wish to know.

    1.32.9. Pro:
    Often, it's also comprehensive than other sources – at least in terms of which aspects and angles it covers.

      1.32.9.1. Con:
      Wikipedia has notability and verifiability policies and guidelines. This means some perspectives get no mentions at all.

        1.32.9.1.1. Con:
        This is why it's only usually or often more comprehensive. There can be exceptions. However, those are rare, especially when considering the fraction of such where the perspectives that don't meet these policies are truly missing instead of better to at that moment leave out.

    1.32.10. Pro:
    It can be more up-to-date than other learning materials.

      1.32.10.1. Con:
      It's often only the third source though – first e.g. research is done, then published in academic journals, then other sources such as a systematic review or news articles pick it up, and often only then does it get published on Wikipedia.

        1.32.10.1.1. Con:
        This only applies to medical subjects [116] on English Wikipedia.

    1.32.11. Pro:
    Re-finding Wikipedia articles is remarkably easy even if one forgot how exactly the article was called. There are various tools and alike for that such as article categories and wikilinks from related articles (e.g. in the 'See also' section) and one can find it well by searching for things that are in its article text or by describing what one is looking for.

      1.32.11.1. Pro:
      One can always quickly look up things one has learned earlier – especially since one then knows where the given content can be found.

    1.32.12. Pro:
    Wikipedia is a great online educational resource.

      1.32.12.1. Con:
      There [117] have [118] been [119] large-scale campaigns or paid editing to modify important articles, usually for political or marketing purposes. It can be hard to detect and correct such.

        1.32.12.1.1. Con:
        These are usually quickly reverted or detected and adequately changed.

      1.32.12.2. Con:
      Since Wikipedia pages can be written by anyone [120], they are susceptible to bias and unreliable sourcing.

        1.32.12.2.1. Con:
        These are then reviewed, verified, and removed if found to be inaccurate or spurious.

        1.32.12.2.2. Con:
        Pages that can't be written anyone by default are also susceptible to that. The openness rather allows fixing any biases and problematic sourcing.

      1.32.12.3. Pro:
      -> See discussion #34335: Wikipedia is a reliable source of information.

      1.32.12.4. Pro:
      The website uses exclusively free and open source software which is better [121] and allows developers to help with extending it with scripts and code changes.

      1.32.12.5. Pro:
      Each article has transparent citations so for further research and can use these to find more detailed or possibly more reliable information. Wikipedia itself is not necessarily the source of information but a place to get a rough summary, an outline, and a few sources to get started.

      1.32.12.6. Con:
      For a few subjects or articles, small numbers of editors inappropriately stonewall constructive contributions or revert and delete content in conflict with Wikipedia policies such as neutrality. The gatekeeping can be problematic.

        1.32.12.6.1. Con:
        When it comes to deletions of articles, "EverybodyWiki" often still contains the deleted articles. Moreover, often they could be recreated at a later point when there are more reliable sources available.

        1.32.12.6.2. Con:
        Since Wikipedia is an open collaborative platform, such problems can be corrected if more people would participate.

      1.32.12.7. Pro:
      Wikipedia is a great source of freely accessible and mostly reliable information on a huge variety of topics.

        1.32.12.7.1. Pro:
        It is of relatively good quality and has extensive coverage of medial and health-related subjects [122] for which it is increasingly being used.

        1.32.12.7.2. Pro:
        Wikipedia currently has [123] more than 52 million articles in more than 300 languages.

          1.32.12.7.2.1. Con:
          Articles on Wikipedias other than English-language Wikipedia are [124] often missing, have much lower depth, or are out-of-date.

      1.32.12.8. Pro:
      The content is licensed under an open content CCBY-SA license that allows everyone to reuse and modify it.

      1.32.12.9. Con:
      Too few scientists and experts are contributing, leaving a large burden of work on volunteers, including relatively low-expertise people.

        1.32.12.9.1. Pro:
        -> See discussion #28756: Should scientists contribute to Wikipedia within their broader area of research?

        1.32.12.9.2. Pro:
        This problem often results in very outdated articles on scientific subjects.

      1.32.12.10. Con:
      Only the English-language Wikipedia [125] is fairly [126] up-to-date, high-quality, well-maintained, and [127] comprehensive [128].

      1.32.12.11. Con:
      It does not or barely feature interactive charts.

        1.32.12.11.1. Con:
        It may soon be possible to integrated OWID [129] charts and maps into Wikipedia articles using the OWID gadget [130] that is being tested in a few smaller Wikipedias as of 2024.
