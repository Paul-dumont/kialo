Discussion Name:
If Scientifically Possible, Should Humans Become Immortal?

1. Thesis:
If scientifically possible, humans should become immortal.

  1.1. Pro:
  Being immortal would result in more net happiness.

    1.1.1. Con:
    If there is an afterlife, nobody will experience it.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      "Life" after death is a religious concept, made by humans to comfort their fear of death [1] (p. 3).

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      Afterlife may not be pleasant, in which case it might be desirable to not experience it.

      1.1.1.3. Con:
      We don't know if there is an afterlife or not. If not, then all we likely face is oblivion.

        1.1.1.3.1. Pro:
        We certainly have the heat death of the universe [2] to contend with, so we'd eventually die anyway, so if there's an afterlife we'll eventually see it. If not, at least we'll have lived far longer than our normal lifespan before facing oblivion.

    1.1.2. Con:
    If we want immortality to happen because it results in more net happiness, then by the same standard, the mistreatment of others would be justified if the pleasure we receive outweighs the suffering of others.

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      We can use a different standard - happiness through one action, if it doesn't cause decreased happiness to others through that action remains a positive. An example would be having a laugh at someone's minor misfortune can produce more happiness to a group rather than the suffering of tripping up.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      That is almost certainly not the same standard.

        1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
        Most common forms of happiness-based consequentialism consider it because to cause mistreatment to others.

          1.1.2.2.1.1. Pro:
          Utilitarianism usually defines "utility" as something like "good feelings minus bad feelings".

      1.1.2.3. Con:
      This is merely an obtuse statement of the utility monster objection to utilitarianism; obtuse because it is psychologically unrealistic for "the pleasure we receive [to] outweigh the suffering of others", and the bland phrasing that omits the mention of a fantastical monster hides that.
