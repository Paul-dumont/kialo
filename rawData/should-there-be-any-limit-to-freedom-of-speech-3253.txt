Discussion Name:
Should there be any limit to freedom of speech?

1. Thesis:
There should be no limits on freedom of speech.

  1.1. Pro:
  Censorship can limit exposure to diverse ideas, fostering close-mindedness and intolerance.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Divergent ideas allow society to evolve through honest debate and ideological challenges.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      Many kinds of speech, such as extremist speech, are themselves narrow-minded [1]. Preventing such speech would promote a more pluralistic society.

        1.1.1.1.1. Con:
        It is challenging for ideas to develop complexity without criticism from opposing viewpoints, even when these ideas embrace pluralistic thought.

          1.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
          The adaptation and reform [2] of the free-market economy could be attributed to the global critique stemming from socialist perspectives.

          1.1.1.1.1.2. Con:
          In a society with certain speech restrictions, academic critique, as long as it avoids hateful rhetoric, can still exist. [3]

        1.1.1.1.2. Pro:
        In a boundlessly tolerant society, the capacity for tolerance can ultimately be undermined or eradicated by the intolerant (p. 543 [4]).

      1.1.1.2. Pro:
      Lack of exposure to diverse ideas can result in echo chambers [5], where individuals are exposed only to opinions and beliefs that reinforce their own biases.

        1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
        A lack of diverse ideas can lead to marginalized groups feeling socially isolated, which can make them vulnerable to extremist ideologies that offer a sense of community and belonging (p. 18 [6]).

        1.1.1.2.2. Con:
        Exposure to diverse ideas does not necessarily prevent echo chambers (p. 18 [7]) .

          1.1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
          People often seek out information that confirms their existing beliefs [8], even when they are exposed to opposing viewpoints.

          1.1.1.2.2.2. Con:
          Exposure to a variety of ideas and perspectives can lead to [9] the formation of smaller, localized communities with distinct beliefs and values, rather than a single dominant ideology or worldview.

        1.1.1.2.3. Con:
        Exposure to opposing viewpoints can lead to greater polarization, as it can cause people to become more entrenched in their existing beliefs [10].

        1.1.1.2.4. Pro:
        Echo chambers give people a false sense of reality, which affects their ability to interact with the rest of the world, leading to further seclusion and societal polarisation. [11]

      1.1.1.3. Pro:
      Divergent ideas and free expression, being democratic cornerstones [12], facilitate open dialogue and enable citizens to shape public policies.

        1.1.1.3.1. Pro:
        Enabling the expression of diverse ideas without censorship is crucial for fostering a representative democracy with multiple political parties.

          1.1.1.3.1.1. Pro:
          The existence of multiple robust political parties guarantees that citizens' views are aptly represented in parliament (p. 3 [13]).

          1.1.1.3.1.2. Con:
          Democratic representative systems [14] can inadvertently legitimize extremist parties by providing them with parliamentary representation.

            1.1.1.3.1.2.1. Pro:
            Germany's representative democracy inadvertently enabled far-right, anti-immigrant parliamentarians [15] to gain representation, thus potentially influencing policy.

            1.1.1.3.1.2.2. Con:
            A country's parliament should reflect all perspectives [16] including extreme ones, to ensure its governance aligns with the diverse will of its citizens.

        1.1.1.3.2. Con:
        In a defensive democracy [17], it is believed that it is necessary to limit some speech in order to protect and perpetuate a democratic government.

          1.1.1.3.2.1. Con:
          Progress is made through [18] the free expression of ideas.

      1.1.1.4. Con:
      Some divergent ideas might rather lead to devolution and a decline in freedom.

        1.1.1.4.1. Pro:
        Tyrannical and fascistic ideas should not be allowed because they rather take society backwards, and take away the freedom [19] that allow it to spread.

        1.1.1.4.2. Con:
        Without open debates, those ideas might remain unopposed, allowing them to be more appealing to people in society.

    1.1.2. Con:
    Many pluralistic and free-thinking societies enact censorship.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      Censorship in pluralistic democracies is typically aimed at protecting [20] fundamental rights and values.

        1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
        Limitations are often imposed on free speech in order to protect [21] people's rights to privacy.

          1.1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
          Many jurisdictions uphold the 'right to be forgotten [22],' requiring entities to refrain from publishing and to delete personal data of individuals instead of freely sharing it.

          1.1.2.1.1.2. Con:
          The right to privacy often facilitates free speech [23] instead of limiting it because, in order to practise true freedom of speech, particularly online, privacy, sometimes in the form of anonymity, is also required.

        1.1.2.1.2. Con:
        Limits on freedom of speech diminish our humanity because they suggest that people will be unable to make good decisions based on the information given to them.

          1.1.2.1.2.1. Con:
          People do not always possess complete or perfect information [24]; therefore, limitations on free speech take this reality into account.

          1.1.2.1.2.2. Pro:
          In a democracy, people should be able [25] to freely decide for themselves what is best for them.

          1.1.2.1.2.3. Pro:
          Limiting speech is akin to not trusting people to think and react critically.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      Limitations on freedom to express ideas are incompatible with pluralistic values.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        The recognition of rights must consider contextual factors, including their interaction with the rights of others, and therefore, rights are not absolute [26].

          1.1.2.2.1.1. Pro:
          Limitations on freedom of speech are similar to justifiable limitations on freedom of movement that prevent trespassing. [27]

          1.1.2.2.1.2. Con:
          Freedom of speech is a unique right [28] and must be treated differently from other rights.

        1.1.2.2.2. Pro:
        Free speech is a fundamental human right [29] recognised by pluralistic international human rights frameworks and the United Nations.

          1.1.2.2.2.1. Pro:
          Free speech is a fundamental right [30], as it is a necessary prerequisite for the realisation of other rights.

            1.1.2.2.2.1.1. Pro:
            Freedom of speech enables individuals to openly express and practise their religion, conscience, and thoughts [31] without fear of censorship or retaliation.

            1.1.2.2.2.1.2. Con:
            The protection of freedom of speech should be limited to the extent that it facilitates the realization of other rights, given its connection with them, but not beyond that extent.

          1.1.2.2.2.2. Con:
          Governments may limit [32] fundamental rights if they believe that such rights would pose a risk to public health or safety.

            1.1.2.2.2.2.1. Pro:
            Many governments imposed restrictions [33] on the free movement of people to curb the spread of COVID-19.

            1.1.2.2.2.2.2. Con:
            Limiting fundamental rights should only be done in exceptional circumstances [34] and be proportionate to the risk posed, as excessive restrictions could lead to the erosion of democracy and human rights.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Limiting freedom of speech may also limit the spread of ideas that could effect positive change in the world.

      1.1.3.1. Con:
      Harmful speech, if it can be distinguished from beneficial speech, can be limited, which would prevent any risks associated with limiting speech that is beneficial.

        1.1.3.1.1. Pro:
        There are clear differences between harmful ideas and beneficial ideas, which can help differentiate the speech—mainly whether that speech results in people being harmed.

        1.1.3.1.2. Con:
        Distinguishing between harmful and beneficial speech is not straightforward, as assessing the impact of speech before it is made can be challenging.

      1.1.3.2. Pro:
      Speech that is deemed beneficial in hindsight may be considered radical and harmful by current authorities, leading to its ban under the guise of harmful speech.

        1.1.3.2.1. Pro:
        Martin Luther King Jr.'s "I Have a Dream [35]" speech, which was controversial at the time, played a significant role in advancing the cause of civil rights in the United States.

        1.1.3.2.2. Pro:
        Nelson Mandela was jailed [36] for his involvement in anti-apartheid activism in South Africa.

        1.1.3.2.3. Con:
        Despite censorship, revolutionary speech with mass appeal usually prevails [37], making the risk of stifling progressive ideas relatively low.

    1.1.4. Con:
    Unrestricted freedom of speech can expose individuals to hateful ideologies, increasing the likelihood that they may be persuaded by them.

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      Hateful ideologies can be persuasive and emotionally appealing, especially when delivered charismatically.

        1.1.4.1.1. Con:
        The persuasiveness of hateful ideas diminishes when they are publicly confronted and deconstructed by facts and rational thought [38].

          1.1.4.1.1.1. Pro:
          Intolerance often stems from fear and unfamiliarity [39]; by shedding light on these unknown aspects, we can foster a more accepting society.

          1.1.4.1.1.2. Con:
          Media outlets often prioritize sensationalism over the presentation of factual information due to their inherent incentive structures [40].

          1.1.4.1.1.3. Con:
          Public confrontations and debates can foster a hostile environment, which is not conducive to constructive discussions.

        1.1.4.1.2. Pro:
        Controversial opinions frequently gain the most traction due to their sensationalism and persuasiveness.

          1.1.4.1.2.1. Pro:
          There's no guarantee that public discourse will be structured and balanced enough to prevent dangerous or misleading ideas from gaining momentum among larger groups of people.

          1.1.4.1.2.2. Pro:
          Hitler's compelling charisma succeeded in persuading [41] millions of Germans to endorse his anti-Semitic agenda and rhetoric.

          1.1.4.1.2.3. Con:
          Controversial views often spread widely due to the criticism they invite, resulting in dissemination not for their persuasiveness but for their contentious nature.

      1.1.4.2. Pro:
      Unrestricted freedom of speech can allow hate groups to spread their messages and recruit new members (p. 19 [42]).

        1.1.4.2.1. Pro:
        When these groups are allowed to operate openly without any restrictions, they can gain more visibility, making it easier for them to attract new followers and expand their influence.

        1.1.4.2.2. Con:
        Hate groups often recruit secretly [43] because of their illegal activities, so changes in censorship laws likely won't affect them.

      1.1.4.3. Con:
      Recognizing hateful speech is essential [44] for countering it, a task made difficult if it is censored and consequently driven underground.

        1.1.4.3.1. Pro:
        If people express racist views in public, it is easier for authorities to take legal action against them, such as through hate speech laws or anti-discrimination legislation.

          1.1.4.3.1.1. Con:
          Effective opposition becomes challenging when individuals self-censor [45] their contentious views due to the fear of legal consequences.

          1.1.4.3.1.2. Pro:
          For a charge to hold up in a court of law, physical evidence of a crime or harm is essential, such as a transcript of a speech or witnesses, as it provides a substantial basis for legal action.

          1.1.4.3.1.3. Con:
          Identification of racists does not necessarily lead to successful accountability.

            1.1.4.3.1.3.1. Pro:
            Many public figures have made racist comments without facing [46] substantial backlash.

            1.1.4.3.1.3.2. Con:
            Exposing racists often leads to [47] public backlash and accountability.

        1.1.4.3.2. Con:
        Censorship doesn't preclude the countering and challenging of hateful ideas.

          1.1.4.3.2.1. Con:
          Not everyone can be convinced through rational argumentation.

            1.1.4.3.2.1.1. Con:
            Even if not everyone can be convinced, all should be given access to information and the chance to re-evaluate their opinions.

            1.1.4.3.2.1.2. Pro:
            Individuals often respond more to emotional rhetoric than to logical reasoning [48]; thus, emotionally manipulative speech, despite lacking a logical basis, can profoundly influence a substantial population.

          1.1.4.3.2.2. Pro:
          Germany's anti-fascism rules [49] provide a good case study for banning offensive speech while retaining the ability to debunk their core ideas.

            1.1.4.3.2.2.1. Pro:
            Germany's case shows that hateful speech can be curtailed by using the ban as a means of reinforcing the faults and harms of those ideas.

            1.1.4.3.2.2.2. Con:
            Germany's anti-fascism laws are already authoritarian in nature [50], making them identical to the censorship and propaganda that make fascism undesirable.

      1.1.4.4. Con:
      Censoring hateful ideologies does not eradicate them; it merely reduces opportunities to engage and counteract such ideas.

        1.1.4.4.1. Pro:
        Individuals with entrenched hateful views are unlikely to alter their beliefs, even if public expression of them is restricted.

          1.1.4.4.1.1. Pro:
          People holding firm beliefs might not change them as it would mean accepting their past actions were wrong [51], which is hard.

          1.1.4.4.1.2. Con:
          Censorship limits the spread [52] and audience of hateful ideology, irrespective of its impact on those already harbouring such views.

        1.1.4.4.2. Con:
        Engaging with fanatics through debate popularizes [53] their views.

          1.1.4.4.2.1. Con:
          Deplatforming fanatics would popularise them more [54] by allowing them to claim victim status and weaponize concerns [55] about the erosion of free speech rights.

            1.1.4.4.2.1.1. Pro:
            Limiting or banning the expression of specific ideas might inadvertently legitimize them, framing them as symbols of resistance that individuals may embrace and enact in their daily lives.

            1.1.4.4.2.1.2. Con:
            A focus on censorship can divert the conversation away from the harmful ideas themselves, inadvertently limiting exposure to extremist ideologies among the public.

          1.1.4.4.2.2. Pro:
          The Nazi Party was thoroughly involved [56] in Germany's political activities, including participating in elections, which eventually facilitated its growth in popularity and influence.

            1.1.4.4.2.2.1. Pro:
            The extensive political liberties in the Weimar Republic enabled the Nazi Party to successfully influence public opinion [57] through strategic and sophisticated propaganda campaigns.

            1.1.4.4.2.2.2. Con:
            The longevity of Nazi rule was facilitated by the regime's political censorship, which might not have been achievable without such measures.

              1.1.4.4.2.2.2.1. Pro:
              The Nazi regime systematically executed a program that banned and incinerated [58] books penned by authors critical of their rule, manifesting their elaborate approach towards censorship.

              1.1.4.4.2.2.2.2. Con:
              Political censorship by the Nazis took place after [59] they were elected.

  1.2. Con:
  Limitless freedom of speech is impractical.

    1.2.1. Con:
    The lack of feasibility does not imply, in principle, that it is acceptable to limit free speech.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      The impact of free expression in the world, whether harmful or not, is less important than the fundamental recognition [60] that free speech should be without boundaries.

        1.2.1.1.1. Con:
        The impact of rights is important, as rights that allow harm to occur often face opposition. An example is the right to bear arms [61], which often faces opposition in the United States.

        1.2.1.1.2. Pro:
        It's crucial to build ideals and envision an optimal human state [62], using these foundations to shape a practical society, rather than determining what is practical first and then setting principles.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      A lack of feasibility indicates that an effort taken regardless would result in waste.

        1.2.1.2.1. Pro:
        Attempting to limit free speech would apply political capital [63], which could otherwise be used to resolve important public crises.

        1.2.1.2.2. Con:
        Even if an initial attempt fails, it is likely to spark discourse and a movement that could make future attempts more successful.

    1.2.2. Pro:
    There are legitimate reasons to keep some information out of the public domain, which means limiting the ability to talk about it or to publish it.

      1.2.2.1. Pro:
      It is justified to prevent the spread of misinformation [64] in the public sphere because it can inflict harm on numerous parties.

        1.2.2.1.1. Con:
        Identifying and eradicating misinformation can pose practical challenges.

          1.2.2.1.1.1. Pro:
          It might be more practical to label certain speech as 'controversial' or 'misinformation' instead of trying to eliminate it altogether, as this approach could prevent potential backlash [65] over perceived silencing.

          1.2.2.1.1.2. Con:
          Companies such as Twitter [66] and Facebook [67] have built new features to tackle misinformation specifically.

        1.2.2.1.2. Pro:
        Slander and defamation [68] can harm a person's reputation, potentially leading to negative consequences in their lives.

          1.2.2.1.2.1. Con:
          Defamation and slander mostly affect the rich and influential [69], while limitless free speech would greatly benefit all of society.

          1.2.2.1.2.2. Pro:
          Individuals have a right to protect their good name and business reputation [70] against falsehoods spread maliciously that cause them financial harm.

      1.2.2.2. Con:
      Allowing limitless freedom of speech would invalidate non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) [71], which are often used by the powerful to muzzle others.

        1.2.2.2.1. Pro:
        NDAs can conceal misconduct, such as harassment [72], allowing culprits to evade accountability by silencing victims.

        1.2.2.2.2. Pro:
        NDAs can prevent individuals from coming forward with information about unethical or illegal activities within an organization, stifling their ability to act as whistleblowers [73].

        1.2.2.2.3. Con:
        Most jurisdictions deem highly restrictive NDAs, especially those concealing unethical actions, as illegal [74].

        1.2.2.2.4. Con:
        NDAs protect sensitive information, uphold intellectual property, and enable secure innovation [75] and collaboration.

      1.2.2.3. Con:
      The conversations around free speech often do not centre around classified or protected information but rather controversial ideas.

        1.2.2.3.1. Con:
        Classified and protected information can often spark significant interest and controversy [76].

        1.2.2.3.2. Pro:
        The understanding that classified information will be declassified [77] in the future prevents the labeling of censorship when it comes to the concealment of state secrets.

      1.2.2.4. Pro:
      An author or creator must be allowed a monopoly over their creations [78], which might not be possible in the absence of speech restrictions.

        1.2.2.4.1. Con:
        Copyright protections, seen as unfair constraints [79] on freedom of speech, should not justify the censorship of individuals sharing art and knowledge.

        1.2.2.4.2. Pro:
        Limitless freedom of speech would prevent authors from censoring the unauthorized reproduction of their intellectual property [80].

      1.2.2.5. Pro:
      The preservation of state secrets [81] is crucial for both national and individual security, and thus, they should remain outside the public domain.

        1.2.2.5.1. Con:
        States unable to cloak themselves in secrecy would have more incentive to behave responsibly.

        1.2.2.5.2. Pro:
        The widespread agreement to criminalize the distribution of classified information [82] suggests an international consensus on restricting speech in such instances.

    1.2.3. Con:
    The practicality of unlimited freedom of speech arises from the difficulty of defining boundaries for speech limits and the absence of an objective measure to identify hate speech.

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      It is possible to have clear limits on speech based on what is unanimously or conventionally accepted to be inappropriate speech.

        1.2.3.1.1. Pro:
        There's near consensus that certain forms of speech, including hate speech and racial slurs, are unequivocally unacceptable (p. 9 [83])

          1.2.3.1.1.1. Pro:
          The cancel culture [84] movement has shown that even within the same culture, some speeches are collectively seen as offensive and therefore cancelled.

          1.2.3.1.1.2. Con:
          A significant segment of society holds a position that is critical [85] of political correctness.

            1.2.3.1.1.2.1. Pro:
            The belief that political correctness has gone too far is the majority view on both sides [86] of the political spectrum.

            1.2.3.1.1.2.2. Con:
            The use of language that is derogatory and incompatible with civilized discourse should be prohibited, irrespective of prevailing majority views.

              1.2.3.1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
              Allowing derogatory language can undermine efforts towards creating a more inclusive and equitable society.

              1.2.3.1.1.2.2.2. Con:
              Common social norms of courtesy [87] often limit the use of derogatory language, making formal regulation unnecessary.

            1.2.3.1.1.2.3. Pro:
            Conservative groups frequently dispute [88] what constitutes inappropriate speech.

              1.2.3.1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
              Politicians like Donald Trump have argued against political correctness, alleging it is a strategy to silence [89] conservative perspectives.

              1.2.3.1.1.2.3.2. Con:
              Conservative skepticism towards political correctness is understandable, as it is often perceived as a tool used by liberals [90] to suppress or marginalize conservative viewpoints.

          1.2.3.1.1.3. Pro:
          We already manage to balance individual freedoms while forbidding words that "by their very utterance, inflict injury" [91] or are obscenity. [92]

        1.2.3.1.2. Con:
        It is challenging to regulate instances where an individual may perceive offense from the use of particular language, even if the underlying message is factually accurate.

          1.2.3.1.2.1. Con:
          If an individual takes offence, it doesn't necessarily define what is appropriate speech, as most people may not find that speech offensive.

          1.2.3.1.2.2. Pro:
          Microaggressions [93], which can cause offense, pose a challenge in terms of defining clear boundaries due to their subtle and often unconscious nature.

          1.2.3.1.2.3. Pro:
          Differing sensitivities [94] mean someone will always find an opinion offensive, but that shouldn't necessitate its suppression.

      1.2.3.2. Pro:
      There is no universal definition [95] of hateful or offensive speech.

        1.2.3.2.1. Pro:
        Cultural diversity makes it impossible to establish universally accepted speech rules that fit all contexts and cultural groups.

          1.2.3.2.1.1. Con:
          It is not necessary to label every potentially offensive act in a universal definition, but rather to classify those acts as offensive which the majority perceives to be so.

          1.2.3.2.1.2. Pro:
          The perception of offense is subjective as it heavily depends on an individual's personal feelings, experiences, cultural background, beliefs, and values.

        1.2.3.2.2. Con:
        Political correctness may be difficult to define, but so are many social concepts such as freedom, morality, and obligation. However, it does not negate the importance of having limits on what is acceptable.

          1.2.3.2.2.1. Pro:
          Political correctness doesn't have inherent rigid criteria [96]; it's defined by societal consensus. It's society's responsibility to facilitate discussions to establish these criteria, a process that has historically shaped many norms and values.

          1.2.3.2.2.2. Con:
          In an extremely polarized society [97], reaching a universal consensus, similar to principles like the right to life and liberty, can be very challenging.

        1.2.3.2.3. Con:
        Many countries have instituted working definitions of hate speech [98].

          1.2.3.2.3.1. Pro:
          In 2016, the European Commission launched the Code of Conduct [99], a guideline to efficiently respond to the proliferation of racist and xenophobic hate speech online.

          1.2.3.2.3.2. Pro:
          In France, a law was passed in 1972, the Pleven Law [100], to combat racist speech. With this law, it became illegal to use language that was racially defamatory.

          1.2.3.2.3.3. Con:
          The definition of 'hate speech' may still vary depending on cultural, legal, and social norms.

          1.2.3.2.3.4. Pro:
          Most countries and entities typically characterize hate speech as communication that instigates or results in violence (p. 10 [101]).

    1.2.4. Pro:
    Legislation cannot govern individuals' thoughts.

      1.2.4.1. Con:
      While it may be impossible to legislate on what one thinks, people can be held legally liable for expressing hateful thoughts.

        1.2.4.1.1. Pro:
        Public expression of a thought enters it into the public discourse [102], which can be regulated by authorities without infringing on individual private freedoms.

        1.2.4.1.2. Con:
        Regulating or penalizing the expression of hateful thoughts in private settings is practically unfeasible.

          1.2.4.1.2.1. Pro:
          The right to privacy [103] protects private conversations.

          1.2.4.1.2.2. Con:
          In some cases, private conversations are subject to legal restrictions, such as laws prohibiting conspiracy to commit a crime [104].

      1.2.4.2. Pro:
      Legislation against what people say is difficult, if not impossible, as people's interpretation of something is not consistent.

        1.2.4.2.1. Con:
        Differences in individual interpretation of laws [105] shouldn't hinder the creation of legislation on crucial matters.

        1.2.4.2.2. Con:
        The existence of laws against slander and defamation indicates that it's possible to objectively determine the intent [106] behind someone's speech.

        1.2.4.2.3. Pro:
        In the U.S., legislating on free speech is doubly challenging due to the First Amendment protections enshrined in the Constitution and upheld by the Supreme Court [107].

          1.2.4.2.3.1. Con:
          The USA has some of the most liberal laws in the world and still has multiple legislations that limit freedom of speech [108].

          1.2.4.2.3.2. Pro:
          Modifying the U.S. Constitution is exceedingly complex [109], which further complicates the process of legislating free speech.

  1.3. Con:
  The censorship of harmful speech is necessary because it violates the rights of others.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    Some speech can cause psychological harm [110] to people who did not ask to be subjected to it, and should therefore be restricted by law.

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      Prioritizing the aggressor's freedom of speech over the victim's may result in mental trauma for the latter.

        1.3.1.1.1. Pro:
        Survivors of abuse, including sexual assault and molestation, need protection from contact with their perpetrators.

          1.3.1.1.1.1. Con:
          Research suggests that the most effective way to cope with fear is through exposure [111]. Avoiding fear can be more harmful than confronting it.

        1.3.1.1.2. Con:
        Not restricting an aggressor's freedom of speech does not equate to diminishing the importance of victims' voices and experiences.

          1.3.1.1.2.1. Con:
          Being allowed to express negative speech can lead to the development of sociopathy [112] in the perpetrator.

      1.3.1.2. Con:
      Any speech that can incite harm is just the vocalisation of an idea that can inspire harm; by censoring words, you prevent any reasoned response.

        1.3.1.2.1. Pro:
        When dangerous ideas go unchallenged, they can fester and inspire dangerous action.

        1.3.1.2.2. Con:
        Speech can influence one's emotions or actions and therefore is not merely vocalisation but could be a harmful action as well.

      1.3.1.3. Con:
      The potential for speech to cause psychological harm alone is not sufficient justification for its prohibition.

        1.3.1.3.1. Con:
        Some speech universally causes psychological harm, and is recognized as such.

          1.3.1.3.1.1. Pro:
          The universal harm caused by speeches that celebrate or endorse Nazi ideology [113] is widely recognized.

        1.3.1.3.2. Pro:
        Prohibiting speech due to potential psychological harm undermines individual autonomy, as individuals are capable of managing their emotional responses and seeking support if needed.

      1.3.1.4. Pro:
      Death threats, harassment, and online abuse can lead to [114] mental issues.

        1.3.1.4.1. Pro:
        Verbal abuse has been shown to lead [115] to depression, low self-esteem, self-harm, and a myriad of social and emotional disorders.

          1.3.1.4.1.1. Con:
          Pre-existing mental conditions [116] or predispositions cannot be disregarded as potential factors prior to harassment or bullying, and therefore, negative speech cannot be conclusively linked to mental health issues.

        1.3.1.4.2. Con:
        Without experiencing challenges, individuals may not develop the resilience required to navigate the complexities of the world.

          1.3.1.4.2.1. Pro:
          Being exposed to potentially dangerous phenomena enables one to build a tolerance.

          1.3.1.4.2.2. Pro:
          A society's laws should not be formed around the emotional state of its weakest members.

          1.3.1.4.2.3. Con:
          Having a "thick skin" does nothing to deter would-be abusers, as it places responsibility solely on the victims. [117]

          1.3.1.4.2.4. Con:
          Being harassed will never help someone do something or become strong.

    1.3.2. Pro:
    Speech that incites violence jeopardizes an individual's right to life [118] and therefore warrants restriction.

      1.3.2.1. Pro:
      Speech with a call to action [119] has the potential to threaten individuals' safety by inciting panic and causing chaos.

        1.3.2.1.1. Con:
        Generalising all calls to action as liable to legal action may result in an infringement on the freedom of speech, as calls to action are distinct from direct actions.

        1.3.2.1.2. Pro:
        Speech that provides practical steps and incentives for the audience to engage in acts of violence is tantamount to violence [120] itself.

          1.3.2.1.2.1. Pro:
          Persuading individuals to commit violent acts through the use of language can result in the actual occurrence of violence.

          1.3.2.1.2.2. Con:
          The indirect causal link between speech and violent action is too tenuous [121] to hold the speaker legally responsible unless there was direct solicitation or incitement.

            1.3.2.1.2.2.1. Pro:
            Individuals are more likely to act on violent ideas presented in speech if they already have a predisposition towards such actions and interpret the speech [122] in that manner.

            1.3.2.1.2.2.2. Con:
            Holding speakers legally responsible for their speech can serve as a deterrent and encourage them to be more mindful of the potential consequences of their words.

            1.3.2.1.2.2.3. Con:
            There is precedent for holding individuals civilly liable [123] for harm caused by their speech.

              1.3.2.1.2.2.3.1. Pro:
              The US Supreme Court ruled [124] that speech that supports violence is protected by the Free Speech Amendment only until the speech directly encourages people to take immediate action.

          1.3.2.1.2.3. Pro:
          Encouraging people to cause harm to others can be seen as a form of fraudulent agreement [125], and society has a precedent for restricting such acts.

      1.3.2.2. Con:
      Speech without intention or context is unlikely to incite violence and, therefore, should not be subject to prohibition.

        1.3.2.2.1. Pro:
        A speaker cannot control how a listener evaluates and receives [126] a speech.

        1.3.2.2.2. Con:
        Speech can induce fear or panic [127], which may result in violence or harm, even if it does not explicitly incite violence.

          1.3.2.2.2.1. Con:
          Yelling "fire" in a theatre is not a speech-related crime but rather a hazardous public action that can lead to criminal behaviour. While individuals have the right to express themselves, wild gestures and yelling can be dangerous.

          1.3.2.2.2.2. Pro:
          Shouting "fire!" in a densely populated area could incite [128] panic, chaos, and potentially a stampede that puts the safety of others at risk.

    1.3.3. Con:
    Political correctness [129] in modern society already ensures that discourse is safe and inclusive without the need for state censorship.

      1.3.3.1. Pro:
      The use of politically correct language [130] allows for the inclusion of marginalized groups that are frequently excluded from discourse due to the use of exclusionary language.

        1.3.3.1.1. Pro:
        Using inclusive and respectful language shows a willingness to listen to and understand the experiences and perspectives of marginalized groups.

        1.3.3.1.2. Con:
        Though offensive language may wound individual egos, managing such personal affronts is an individual responsibility; such offense does not inherently inflict societal harm.

        1.3.3.1.3. Con:
        Language alone cannot address societal injustices and may alienate those unfamiliar with the terminology, leading to disengagement from important discussions.

          1.3.3.1.3.1. Pro:
          Societal injustices are often intersectional [131] and require a multi-faceted approach beyond language changes.

          1.3.3.1.3.2. Con:
          The risk of being alienated due to a lack of familiarity with certain languages serves as an incentive to educate oneself and become more sensitive to the issues faced by marginalised communities.

        1.3.3.1.4. Pro:
        The pressure to be politically correct insulates marginalized groups from insensitive remarks and acts of bullying.

          1.3.3.1.4.1. Con:
          The obligation to adhere to political correctness does not inherently make individuals kinder or prevent non-racist or non-sexist forms of bullying.

          1.3.3.1.4.2. Pro:
          Bullying and offensive language could inflict mental distress on individuals, potentially leading to severe consequences such as suicide [132] in extreme scenarios.

          1.3.3.1.4.3. Pro:
          Women, frequently dismissed as emotional actors [133], are often excluded from meaningful discourse. Adherence to political correctness can mitigate this risk.

      1.3.3.2. Pro:
      A mindset that racial minorities are inferior discredits their ideas and hinders their participation in discourse.

        1.3.3.2.1. Con:
        Racism is not a prevalent mindset, and expressing such opinions leads to stigma and scorn for being a racist.

          1.3.3.2.1.1. Con:
          There exists a common perception that racism is a widespread societal norm [134].

        1.3.3.2.2. Pro:
        The culture of political correctness tends to stigmatize those expressing explicitly racist views.

      1.3.3.3. Con:
      Political correctness creates an avenue to attack [135] people for speaking freely without addressing the substance of their views.

        1.3.3.3.1. Pro:
        People may use accusations of political incorrectness as a way to attack or discredit individuals who express views they disagree with.

        1.3.3.3.2. Con:
        Attacks under the guise of free speech are unproductive and distract from the topic at hand. Political correctness dismisses them, enabling more meaningful conversations.

      1.3.3.4. Con:
      Political correctness can lead to self-censorship [136] due to the fear of causing offence, as perceptions of what is acceptable speech can vary widely.

        1.3.3.4.1. Con:
        Minority communities frequently initiate conversations aimed at enlightening [137] the broader population about the detrimental effects of specific slurs and phrases on marginalized groups, thereby clarifying acceptable language norms.

        1.3.3.4.2. Pro:
        Political correctness may impede the full participation [138] and open exchange of ideas within society, thereby limiting their potential for growth and meaningful discourse.

        1.3.3.4.3. Pro:
        Some people use politically correct terms as a form of gatekeeping [139] to bully or ban a speaker from online groups if the speaker uses a term without realising it's a slur.

        1.3.3.4.4. Con:
        Encouraging sensitivity when discussing sensitive topics can foster an inclusive environment that welcomes marginalized groups to participate in discourse.

    1.3.4. Con:
    Entitlement to protection against offense cannot [140] be claimed as a right.

      1.3.4.1. Pro:
      Protecting only universally accepted speech would violate fundamental rights; thus, to ensure freedom of speech [141], protecting offensive speech is necessary.

        1.3.4.1.1. Con:
        Protecting offensive speech might also contribute [142] to the abuse of the rights of others, and the state should never sponsor the oppression of others.

      1.3.4.2. Con:
      Offending others is a form of harm [143], and causing harm should be discouraged by law.

        1.3.4.2.1. Pro:
        Offending others can be seen as an ethical harm, as it violates the principle of treating others with respect and dignity.

        1.3.4.2.2. Con:
        Offence is subjectiv [144]e, and laws cannot disincentivize something without objective criteria.

      1.3.4.3. Pro:
      Individuals have a responsibility [145] to manage their own reactions to offensive speech, not to determine how others feel.

        1.3.4.3.1. Pro:
        Teaching people that how they feel about themselves is solely a product of external factors reduces their ability [146] to engage with challenging situations beyond their control.

      1.3.4.4. Con:
      Under certain circumstances, individuals may have a legitimate claim to protection from offensive speech, particularly in private spaces. [147]

        1.3.4.4.1. Pro:
        The existence of private spaces where people can hide from offensive content is essential to the existence of free speech in public.

  1.4. Pro:
  Government agencies are likely to abuse limitations on free speech for their own purposes if given such powers.

    1.4.1. Con:
    The existence of agencies and platforms with the power to regulate social discourse sets a precedent for its acceptability.

      1.4.1.1. Pro:
      Social media websites like Twitter [148], Facebook [149], and YouTube [150] have policies that limit what one can express.

        1.4.1.1.1. Con:
        Limitless freedom of speech is a feature on some online platforms. [151]

          1.4.1.1.1.1. Pro:
          There are many social media platforms, such as Parler [152], that do not restrict free speech.

          1.4.1.1.1.2. Con:
          The failure [153] of these platforms might indicate that limitless free speech is not a priority to most people.

        1.4.1.1.2. Con:
        In contrast to general laws in the broader society, people have the option to refuse to engage with private platforms that limit speech.

        1.4.1.1.3. Pro:
        These platforms prohibit the posting of content that harasses or bullies [154] individuals or groups.

        1.4.1.1.4. Pro:
        These platforms often restrict the posting of misinformation and false news [155] that could be deemed harmful to individuals or communities.

      1.4.1.2. Pro:
      Governments include regulatory bodies that supervise media and telecommunications, empowering them to govern disseminated content and setting a precedent for acceptable discourse regulation.

        1.4.1.2.1. Pro:
        In some jurisdictions, internet service providers (ISPs) [156] are required by law to block certain websites or types of content that are deemed illegal or harmful.

        1.4.1.2.2. Con:
        Media regulatory authorities are frequently government extensions [157] and, as such, may be vulnerable to the same incentives as the government to suppress opposing viewpoints.

      1.4.1.3. Con:
      The mere existence of agencies that exert control over discourse cannot serve as a justification for their existence.

        1.4.1.3.1. Pro:
        There are agencies in authoritarian countries [158] that clamp down on any dissenting speech, but this does not mean it would be a good idea to have the same in the liberal world.

        1.4.1.3.2. Con:
        The enduring presence of agencies that can limit public expression suggests a majority consensus on their utility.

    1.4.2. Con:
    A government's re-election incentive [159] will limit its ability to abuse its power.

      1.4.2.1. Pro:
      Concerned voters are likely to vote in elections [160] to oust a government that wields power against their wishes.

        1.4.2.1.1. Pro:
        The preservation of democracy and freedom are often high [161] on voter priority lists.

        1.4.2.1.2. Con:
        Abuse of democratic power might be ignored [162] by voters if other priorities, such as economic development, are assured.

      1.4.2.2. Con:
      A government prepared to exploit its power would probably be ready to manipulate electoral processes and outcomes in its favor.

        1.4.2.2.1. Con:
        Governments that do not exist to serve the will of the people, such as totalitarian [163] regimes, are illegitimate.

        1.4.2.2.2. Pro:
        Many authoritarian regimes around the world have manipulated elections [164] with impunity to protect their power.

      1.4.2.3. Pro:
      To avoid losing popularity with voters in the upcoming elections, the government has an incentive to promote media freedom.

        1.4.2.3.1. Pro:
        Journalists' speech is frequently safeguarded [165] from constraints, creating considerable challenges in implementing broad restrictions on the press.

        1.4.2.3.2. Con:
        As media suppression is on the rise [166] worldwide, restrictions on free speech may contribute to this trend.

      1.4.2.4. Con:
      The government's incentive to get re-elected would rather push it to limit public critique by curtailing free speech.

        1.4.2.4.1. Pro:
        Free speech allows citizens to tarnish the reputation of governments, which poses a threat to authoritarian regimes.

        1.4.2.4.2. Con:
        International watchdogs [167] often highlight censorship and opposition suppression in elections, guiding voters towards choices that uphold their freedom of expression.

    1.4.3. Pro:
    Governments may employ their authority to limit free speech as a means to consolidate or amass power.

      1.4.3.1. Con:

        1.4.3.1.1. Pro:
        Checks and balances within society would prevent abuse of power in the regulation of speech.

        1.4.3.1.2. Con:
        All uses of state power are coercive, including restrictions on speech; thus, an anarchist perspective [169] may better uphold ideals of freedom and liberty.

      1.4.3.2. Con:
      All governments have the authority to pass legislation and enforce the law, but despite this, not all governments slide into authoritarianism.

        1.4.3.2.1. Pro:
        There exist contemporary examples of countries, such as Canada [170], that restrict hate speech, demonstrating that no dictatorship ensues and that such limitations have a positive impact in general.

        1.4.3.2.2. Con:
        Permitting certain speech restrictions could set a precedent for further curbs, potentially eroding the principle of free speech and paving the way for worse forms of authoritarianism.

          1.4.3.2.2.1. Pro:
          Countries [171] where governments closely regulate speech have shown that limitations on free speech are dangerous.

          1.4.3.2.2.2. Con:
          It is a slippery slope [172] fallacy to assume that reasonable regulations on speech in themselves would lead to extreme outcomes.

        1.4.3.2.3. Pro:
        Censorship can diminish the legitimacy of democratic governance, which administrations aim to uphold.

          1.4.3.2.3.1. Con:
          Elected governments have been bestowed with the power to impose censorship by the consent of the people.

            1.4.3.2.3.1.1. Pro:
            Elected governments, being accountable to the people, can better balance the competing interests of free speech and other values, such as public safety and national security.

            1.4.3.2.3.1.2. Pro:
            Elected governments have a mandate [173] to act in the best interests of the people and are therefore better able to determine what types of speech are harmful and should be restricted.

            1.4.3.2.3.1.3. Con:
            Elected governments are often subject to legal constraints [174] that limit their ability to impose censorship.

            1.4.3.2.3.1.4. Con:
            In a democracy where the people still hold sway over the government, acceptable speech will continue to be judged according to the times.

          1.4.3.2.3.2. Pro:
          Censorship restricts the free flow of information [175] and ideas, which is a fundamental aspect of democracy.

        1.4.3.2.4. Con:
        Ethically, a government should not have the authority to suppress citizens' fundamental rights [176].

      1.4.3.3. Pro:
      Governments can use the term "hate speech" to criminalise opposition and undermine democratic freedoms [177].

        1.4.3.3.1. Pro:
