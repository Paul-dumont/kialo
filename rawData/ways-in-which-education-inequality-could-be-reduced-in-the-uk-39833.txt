Discussion Name:
Ways in which education inequality could be reduced in the UK

1. Question:
Ways in which education inequality could be reduced in the UK

  1.1. Thesis:
  Private schools have their own association groups with each other to help each other out. Match each UK school with another school: the match being made on the basis that each has expertise the other school struggles with. Promote the share of resources such as university application procedures, knowledge, and teacher exchanges.

  1.2. Thesis:
  Change linear exams to a modular system where students are assessed throughout the year

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Linear exams mean a student's grades can significantly be affected by mental health or a bad night's sleep

      1.2.1.1. Con:
      Some students are more likely to pick up the pace later down the line in terms of grades, and although they would have the same final grade at the end, they would be disadvantaged for this.

  1.3. Thesis:
  Increase public spending in mainstream education.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    Mainstream schools, particularly in poor inner-city areas, desperately need more budget to be able to support SEND [1] children.

    1.3.2. Pro:
    With more budget, public schools could afford to pay for more teaching assistants.

  1.4. Thesis:
  Due to capitalism, large companies who arrange tutoring, want to profit from money spent on it, and therefore this drives the price of tutoring up. Making it even more inaccessible to poorer students. Introduce a nationalised online tutoring arrangement service, undercutting these companies, to provide tutoring at a lower cost or take the profits from tutoring to provide free tutoring for poorer students.

    1.4.1. Con:
    The money these companies are profiting from comes from wealthy families. Having a higher price in the short term, reduces family wealth in comparison with poorer families.

    1.4.2. Con:
    Increasing the percentage of students with access to tutoring will exacerbate the inequality towards the poorest students who don't have funding

    1.4.3. Pro:
    Less money would then be in large companies, and now be distributed among multiple people, encouraging economic growth better.

  1.5. Thesis:
  Enforce programmes from year 7 to alter the poor gender balance in certain subjects to tackle gender inequality.

    1.5.1. Con:
    This might incentivize some schools to persuade students into taking certain subjects they might not be interested in.

    1.5.2. Con:
    Gender disparities in subject choice is the product of individual choice.

    1.5.3. Pro:
    Gender disparity in certain subjects is very high

  1.6. Thesis:
  Make it illegal for universities to provide offers for students until final grades are recieved

    1.6.1. Pro:
    State school students are more likely to be under predicted grades, which results in less offers from top universities. This policy would ensure they have offers proportionate to their real grades, reducing inequality among poorer students with their university placement.

    1.6.2. Con:
    Universities would now have to work over the summer

    1.6.3. Pro:
    Those who are predicted lower grades will have the option to overperform and apply for universities they otherwise may not have.
