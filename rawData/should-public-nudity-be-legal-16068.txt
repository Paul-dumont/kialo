Discussion Name:
Should public nudity be legal?

1. Thesis:
Public nudity should be legal.

  1.1. Pro:
  Public nudity is a valid and popular form of self-expression and thus should be recognized and protected by law.

    1.1.1. Con:
    Public nudity is immoral and largely taboo. Therefore, it should be illegal.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      Genitalia are an illicit, tempting [1], and inherently sexual part of one's body. As such, it is immoral to show them to others in non-sexual or non-private circumstances.

        1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
        Any sexual interaction, including non-consensual nudity, can be problematic without clearly-established consent.

          1.1.1.1.1.1. Con:
          Nudity is not an inherently sexual act and therefore does not require consent from others as sexual acts do.

        1.1.1.1.2. Con:
        Every part of the human body has a specific function. There are no 'bad' parts [2] of the body and, thus, there should be no social imperative to hide any of them.

          1.1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
          Human sexuality is an intrinsic part [3] of human nature. Therefore, it should be celebrated rather than banned or restricted.

            1.1.1.1.2.1.1. Con:
            Sexual behavior is not an intrinsic characteristic; it is a learned behavior [4].

        1.1.1.1.3. Con:
        Dozens of historic societies have considered genital exposure to be apotropaic [5]—displayed to fend off evil. Such displays are still common [6] in some parts of the world, especially in Asia [7].

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      Ethics and the law often diverge [8]. The morality of a given act has no bearing on whether it should be equal.

      1.1.1.3. Con:
      Social taboos regarding nudity have evolved over time. As such, it cannot be said that public nudity is immoral, nor can it be banned with this justification.

        1.1.1.3.1. Pro:
        Modern societies emphasize individualism in place of traditionalism. As a result, they are more tolerant towards different sexual lifestyles. Prohibition of public nudity is contrary to these ideals (p. 2 [9]).

        1.1.1.3.2. Con:
        All cultures have sexual taboos [10]. They serve an important role in society, developing sexuality and courtship.

        1.1.1.3.3. Pro:
        The revealing clothing used on Western beaches today would have been extremely offensive [11] just over a century ago.

        1.1.1.3.4. Pro:
        Until 100 years ago, Westerners were often shocked at the sight of bare male nipples [12] and showing bare legs was frowned upon.

        1.1.1.3.5. Con:
        The fact that social taboos change does not mean something currently taboo shouldn't be banned. If, later, public nudity becomes widely accepted, it can be legalized.

    1.1.2. Con:
    Every liberty comes with limitations. States, therefore, have the authority to regulate personal liberties, including nudity.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      Prohibiting public nudity is a legitimate way of regulating public spaces because it maintains social harmony, which is an important politico-legal principle (pp. 12-13 [13]).

        1.1.2.1.1. Con:
        Modern legal systems use ‘reflective equilibrium [14]’ - mutual compromise - to create coherent legal principles that adequately protect minority groups. Prohibiting minority groups from engaging in nudity does not satisfy this.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      A wide variety of countries and local authorities have laws regulating how people can dress beyond just banning public nudity.

        1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
        The penal code in Qatar [15] forbids and punishes topless sunbathing [16] and the wearing of revealing or indecent clothes, including transparent ones.

        1.1.2.2.2. Pro:
        In 2011, the New Jersey State Court ruled that "restrictions on the exposure of the female breast [17] are supported by the important governmental interest in safeguarding the public's moral sensibilities" (p. 1 [18]).

          1.1.2.2.2.1. Con:
          Although these restrictions remain in effect in New Jersey, women are allowed [19] to be topless in public in New York City, indicating that these laws are hardly seen as justified or necessary everywhere.

        1.1.2.2.3. Con:
        In Spain, nudity is allowed in all public areas with no prior authorization required [20].

        1.1.2.2.4. Con:
        The fact that many countries already have laws regulating clothing does not mean it is just or fair. Countries frequently have unjust laws that should be reversed.

          1.1.2.2.4.1. Pro:
          The laws of conservative countries should not dictate the those of other sovereign nations.

      1.1.2.3. Con:
      Even restrictitions on public nudity are justified, the banning of some forms of it is still unfair and unequal: if a man can take off his shirt in public, then a woman should be able to do the same.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Prohibiting public nudity restricts one's right to public self-expression and is a breach of civil liberties and runs counter to the legal principles that govern modern democratic societies.

      1.1.3.1. Con:
      Someone exposing themself to others could also be considered a breach of liberty as well as sexual harassment.

        1.1.3.1.1. Con:
        In the UK, non-confrontational, non-sexual public nudity was effectively decriminalized in 2003 [21]. This has increased nude events [22] without discernible adverse social consequences.

        1.1.3.1.2. Pro:
        The French penal code [23] declares that public nudity qualifies as the "deliberate sexual display in the sight of others in a place accessible to the public gaze" and is punishable by a fine.

        1.1.3.1.3. Con:
        Regular nudity does not necessarily mean one person forcing their sexual desires upon others. Therefore, public nudity will not necessarily constitute sexual harassment.

        1.1.3.1.4. Pro:
        When there is a lack of consent (p. 398 [24]), indecent exposure [25] is considered a crime in many countries.

          1.1.3.1.4.1. Con:
          The countries with the most robust democracies [26] are also those where nudity is most rapidly becoming accepted [27].

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      Laws should reflect the norms of their respective societies. Clothing has endured for millennia as an integral part [28] of most human societies. Therefore, it should be represented in law.

        1.1.3.2.1. Pro:
        Wearing some form of clothing [29] is almost universal among the historical cultures of the world, suggesting that there is a good reason to wear clothes.

          1.1.3.2.1.1. Pro:
          Nudity was historically practiced [30] by hunter-gatherer communities living in warm climates. This means there is a historical precedent for human beings opting for nudity in warmer climates.

          1.1.3.2.1.2. Pro:
          Human history has undergone a nearly global trend [31] from nudity towards modesty. This implies that this is a universal norm for humans.

            1.1.3.2.1.2.1. Con:
            The trend towards modesty in Western cultures from classical times to the Victorian era [32] went into reverse in the late 19th century [33] and has continued to increasingly reject modesty [34].

            1.1.3.2.1.2.2. Pro:
            Ancient Greek athletes competed in the nude [35] to show off their physique and even attended social events naked. Modern athletes, however, have always worn clothes.

          1.1.3.2.1.3. Con:
          Following tradition [36] is not an acceptable reason for implementing laws as some historical traditions are morally and ethically reprehensible (p. 9 [37]).

            1.1.3.2.1.3.1. Con:
            Humans are born in a nude state. Hence, it is our most natural state and therefore cannot be considered morally or ethically reprehensible.

        1.1.3.2.2. Con:
        Laws should reflect fundamental principles of justice, not the norms of a given society.

          1.1.3.2.2.1. Pro:
          All laws should be designed [38] to make sure that minority rights are protected against the cultural, moral, and political excesses of the majority.

          1.1.3.2.2.2. Con:
          Laws tend to reflect social customs [39] regardless of whether they are being crafted to do so. As such, changing them as customs change is necessary.

            1.1.3.2.2.2.1. Pro:
            Customs have an inertia that can rapidly make them become anachronisms [40]. Regardless of their actual utility, new ways to deal with the weather will be perceived as inadequate due to the familiarity of wearing clothing.

          1.1.3.2.2.3. Con:
          Social norms [41] are usually taken into consideration when governments make laws [42].

        1.1.3.2.3. Con:
        Modern societies [43] have normalized sexual and non-sexual nudity in fashion [44] and the arts, including films, television, and music videos [45]. As such, public nudity does not contradict dominant values.

          1.1.3.2.3.1. Pro:
          Most societies are generally accepting of nude art and statues in public. Allowing public nudity in designated areas would be no different.

            1.1.3.2.3.1.1. Pro:
            The naked human body has been depicted in European art for millennia, from ancient Greek and Roman sculptures to Renaissance nudes [46].

            1.1.3.2.3.1.2. Pro:
            Statues and frescoes [47] found at Pompeii frequently featured sexual acts and nudity, demonstrating the normality of public nudity at the time.

            1.1.3.2.3.1.3. Con:
            Depictions of nudity in modern society are generally frowned upon. Nearly all social media services [48] ban nudity; streaming platforms [49] have also followed suit.

          1.1.3.2.3.2. Con:
          Nude art is incapable of confronting or injuring others. In this sense, it is publicly acceptable but public nudity is not.

        1.1.3.2.4. Pro:
        Laws can and should adapt with changing moral codes (p. 4 [50]).

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      Applying the 'harm principle [51]', a person engaging in non-confrontational, non-sexual public nudity [52] does not do sufficient harm to others to justify legal repercussions.

        1.1.3.3.1. Pro:
        One theory defines [53] ‘harm’ as a wrong that sets back the interest of others. Therefore, the adverse consequences of prosecuting people for public nudity would likely be disproportionate [54] to any actual harm caused.

        1.1.3.3.2. Pro:
        On the ground of physical harm, it makes no sense to keep public nudity, which doesn't cause any harm, illegal when confirmed harmful substances, like alcohol, remain legal.

        1.1.3.3.3. Con:
        Legal injuries [55] often include emotional, economic, or reputational injuries as well as violations of privacy or property. Therefore, the emotional distress or discomfort caused by public nudity could be ground to ban it.

          1.1.3.3.3.1. Con:
          In the UK, public decency [56] laws apply only when a person’s behaviour is so obscene that it shocks a reasonable person. This is a high threshold that simple public nudity would not reach.

      1.1.3.4. Pro:
      Public nudity can be a religious practice. As such, it should be allowed and protected under the principle of religious freedom [57].

        1.1.3.4.1. Pro:
        In the early centuries of Christianity, the sacrament of baptism was practised nude [58].

          1.1.3.4.1.1. Con:
          These ceremonies, while nude, were not public [59] and often done in darkness.

            1.1.3.4.1.1.1. Con:
            For many years, these baptisms were practised in large enough social groups [60] to be considered public.

        1.1.3.4.2. Con:
        Some religious texts, including the Bible, promote a number of practices, including slavery [61], which are not protected by religious freedom or defended by religious followers today.

        1.1.3.4.3. Con:
        Religious rules on clothing are largely remnants of outdated social customs.

          1.1.3.4.3.1. Con:
          Many of those who oppose public nudity for religious reasons have openly admitted that there is no reason to ban public nudity from a secular point of view [62].

          1.1.3.4.3.2. Pro:
          Clothing traditions, which began in ancient Assyria [63] and Sumeria [64], persist across some modern cultures, particularly through the idea that respectable women should keep their head covered with a veil.

          1.1.3.4.3.3. Pro:
          The modesty stipulated by the Quran is solely based on arbitrary and discriminatory edicts [65].

          1.1.3.4.3.4. Con:
          Religious rules and norms remain influential [66] in the political systems and cultures of most countries. Therefore, these rules are far from outdated.

        1.1.3.4.4. Pro:
        Some historical religious sects [67] have embraced nudity and valorized the naked human body; the decriminalization of nudity could help re-establish some of their beliefs and eliminate nudity as a taboo for religions.

          1.1.3.4.4.1. Pro:
          Christian naturists [68] believe clothing is a sign of sin adopted by Adam and Eve after the Fall.

          1.1.3.4.4.2. Pro:
          According to the practice of Digambara [69], a sect of Jainism, the soul of a man can only attain liberation while completely naked.

          1.1.3.4.4.3. Con:
          Dualistic religions that separate body and soul [70] often depict the soul as virtuous and the body as unclean and in need of mortification. Accordingly, covering up one's body is an important part of the process of sanctification [71].

          1.1.3.4.4.4. Con:
          Scripture is predominantly opposed to public nudity; in both Genesis and Leviticus, “uncover[ing] one’s nakedness [72]” is considered shameful.

    1.1.4. Pro:
    Public nudity can be a form of personal or collective expression.

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      Clothing can already function aesthetically, allowing creativity and self-expression through outfits and accessories. Therefore, legalizing public nudity is not necessary for self-expression.

        1.1.4.1.1. Con:
        If individuals are able to wear as little clothing as they desire as a form of self-expression [73], they should, by the same principle, be allowed to wear no clothing at all.

          1.1.4.1.1.1. Pro:
          Many people in countries in Latin America actively seek tan lines; some Brazilian women even use electrical tape to perfect their tan lines [74] while tanning naturally under the sun.

            1.1.4.1.1.1.1. Con:
            There are several, more effective, alternatives for getting an all-over tan including personal backyards, tanning salons [75], and private nude beaches.

              1.1.4.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
              The majority of the Earth's population resides in areas where year-round tanning is not possible.

          1.1.4.1.1.2. Pro:
          Clothing can be used as a prelude to public nudity [76] or to accentuate it, as commonly used by strippers and burlesque performers [77], or during naturist fancy dress parties.

        1.1.4.1.2. Pro:
        A global and diverse fashion industry exists [78] to support different aesthetic preferences through clothing.

        1.1.4.1.3. Pro:
        Clothes represent a better, more inclusive and flexible form of self-expression, since one can choose to wear different articles of clothing.

          1.1.4.1.3.1. Con:
          Tattoos have become a widespread and popular way to express oneself [79] through their body, rather than their clothes.

          1.1.4.1.3.2. Pro:
          It is much harder to change the largely immutable characteristics of one's body than to experiment with different clothing.

        1.1.4.1.4. Con:
        The state should not decide what forms of self-expression or speech are 'sufficient' for its citizens.

      1.1.4.2. Pro:
      Regardless of effects, the choice to be nude should be left with each individual as to whether they wish to devalue their own private experience of nudity.

      1.1.4.3. Con:
      In Barnes v. Glen Theatre, Inc., 1991 [80], the US Supreme Court ruled that nude dancing is only marginally worth protecting as expressive conduct; nudity per se is not expressive conduct.

      1.1.4.4. Pro:
      Partial or complete nudity can be used to express [81] cultural values and identities [82].

  1.2. Con:
  The legalization of public nudity would cause social tensions and harm individuals and communities.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Legalizing public nudity would create and worsen cultural barriers and rifts between people around the world.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      Many Muslim women, due to their belief in Islamic practices, may be uncomfortable [83] with the idea of entering public spaces if there is a good chance of naked men and women being present.

        1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
        The idea that female nudity is empowering has been used to curb the religious liberties of Muslim women, as in the case [84] of the Burkini bans across France. Muslim women are doubly jeopardized [85] through both gender and religious discrimination.

        1.2.1.1.2. Con:
        Even though some countries frown on the legalization of same-sex marriage, it has been made legal in other countries. This same could be true of public nudity.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      Bans on public nudity could also deepen cultural barriers and isolate some communities and cultures [86] who embrace it.

      1.2.1.3. Pro:
      Legalizing nudity could decrease cross-cultural tourism.

        1.2.1.3.1. Con:
        Legalizing nudity could increase tourism. For many people, travel is a way of experiencing and understanding other cultures [87].

        1.2.1.3.2. Pro:
        If potential tourists become concerned about visiting a country in which public nudity is possible, this could damage the country's tourism industry thereby hurting its economy.

        1.2.1.3.3. Con:
        Legalizing public nudity would expand opportunities for nude tourism ventures.

          1.2.1.3.3.1. Con:
          There is little evidence that the 'niche' nude tourism sector is even profitable anymore; traditional nudist club memberships are in steep decline [88].

          1.2.1.3.3.2. Pro:
          Based on the economic principle of supply and demand [89], increasing the number of places one can be nude in public would increase the number of opportunities [90] tourists have to explore new destinations.

          1.2.1.3.3.3. Con:
          Generalizing nudity would decrease the 'niche' effect and appeal of nude tourism [91], and decrease its income.

    1.2.2. Pro:
    Public nudity would likely cause offense and discomfort to many people.

      1.2.2.1. Pro:
      Public nudity is not currently an acceptable or widespread social behavior; as such, people are likely to be uncomfortable seeing it.

        1.2.2.1.1. Con:
        Naturism is still a small market. Naturist businesses may grow and coordinate with each other as public nudity is normalized and popularized.

          1.2.2.1.1.1. Con:
          Many nudist businesses and restaurants have struggled financially [92] due to a lack of public interest despite positive media coverage [93].

            1.2.2.1.1.1.1. Pro:
            Established, traditional naturist clubs have been poor to adapt to changing expectations and trends, while contending with an aging membership base [94], rundown facilities, and a generally bureaucratic attitude [95] towards innovation and change.

            1.2.2.1.1.1.2. Con:
            In 2023, a nude vegan dinner party [96] series began in New York City with successful events leading to additional outings [97] in Los Angeles, demonstrating that new nudist businesses can thrive in modern times.

            1.2.2.1.1.1.3. Con:
            Many businesses are interested in branching out [98] into nudism; a wide variety of high-profile businesses offer nudist events [99].

          1.2.2.1.1.2. Pro:
          A number of public activities have have garnered widespread public interest, including naked bike rides [100] and nude hiking [101], embracing nudity as the central part of their attraction.

          1.2.2.1.1.3. Con:
          With normalized public nudity, the popularity of naturist clubs may decrease, thereby reducing the benefits they might have provided.

          1.2.2.1.1.4. Pro:
          In Florida, where nudism is commonplace, nude tourists and local naturists contribute more than US$7 billion [102] annually to the state economy.

        1.2.2.1.2. Con:
        The pre-existing social consequences will be sufficient to prevent excessive, uncomfortable public nudity.

          1.2.2.1.2.1. Con:
          There are people who don't care about social stigma. Only legal and criminal sanctions can prevent them from inciting a negative response in people.

        1.2.2.1.3. Pro:
        The popularity of nudism is fading as the baby boomer generation ages (p. i [103]); millennials and younger generations are less inclined [104] to practice nudism.

        1.2.2.1.4. Pro:
        Even existing forms of legal public nudity are still rare. Nearly half of all women have difficulty finding places to breastfeed and more than 10% [105] are confronted while breastfeeding.

      1.2.2.2. Con:
      Taking offense may be a learned behavior from a society that wears clothes exclusively, where public nudity is often sexual or threatening. This can be unlearned.

        1.2.2.2.1. Pro:
        Experiments aimed at helping people unlearn negative emotions associated with nudity and to decouple nude behaviour with shame have been [106] successful.

        1.2.2.2.2. Pro:
        Taking offense may lead people to consider why they are offended, and if that reaction is justified, prompting greater reflection on nudity more generally.

        1.2.2.2.3. Con:
        Public nudity is often meant to cause offense and punishment. In both historic and modern times, it has been used as a punishment meant to warn others of the consequences for wrong-doing.

          1.2.2.2.3.1. Pro:
          In 2009, three women in Pakistan were stripped naked and humiliated [107]; the vigilante justice was supported by the police and later, upheld by the courts.

          1.2.2.2.3.2. Pro:
          Naked walks of shame were frequently used as a punishment for adultery [108] in medieval Europe.

          1.2.2.2.3.3. Con:
          The practice of nude executions in Europe drastically decreased in popularity [109] by the 19th century.

            1.2.2.2.3.3.1. Con:
            Naked public humiliation was used as a punishment for a variety of offenses [110] in Europe, even up until the late 1970s and early 1980s.

            1.2.2.2.3.3.2. Con:
            Some photographic evidence demonstrates that humiliating nude executions [111] continued to be carried out in Europe until at least the mid-1940s.

            1.2.2.2.3.3.3. Pro:
            When sensibilities and proper etiquette became more mainstream alongside human rights, humiliating nude executions fell out of favor [112], especially in the UK [113].

          1.2.2.2.3.4. Con:
          The shock of nudity can be used towards progressive ends. Historically, protesters [114] have used intentional nudity [115] to add emphasis to their cause.

            1.2.2.2.3.4.1. Pro:
            Those who wish to shock others with their nudity may begin purposefully targeting places where nudity remains socially unacceptable.

            1.2.2.2.3.4.2. Pro:
            Feminists commonly use nudity to draw attention to causes they feel would otherwise be overlooked or ignored [116] by society at large.

              1.2.2.2.3.4.2.1. Pro:
              Vulva activism [117], a feminist movement, aims to end the stigma attached to genitalia typically associated with women. Their viewpoint is that the vulva is not immoral or shameful and should be proudly displayed by anyone who so desires.

            1.2.2.2.3.4.3. Con:
            Guessing whether public nudity will continue to have shock value after legalization is not possible to ascertain. Consequently, the decision to legalize it should not be influenced by this.

            1.2.2.2.3.4.4. Con:
            Protesters use nudity due to its shock factor [118] to attract the media and public attention. If public nudity were legalized, then this shock value would decrease.

      1.2.2.3. Pro:
      People who do not want to see nudity might become more reluctant to attend public events.

        1.2.2.3.1. Pro:
        Regardless of legality, many people have gymnophobia [119], a fear of nudity and being nude. Widespread public nudity could exacerbate these fears, triggering mental health issues.

          1.2.2.3.1.1. Pro:
          One major component of gymnophobia is the fear of seeing others nude; normalized public nudity could prevent gymnophobes from ever leaving the house [120] due to the crippling fear of running into someone in the nude.

          1.2.2.3.1.2. Con:
          Gymnophobia can be treated with therapy [121]. There is no need to prohibit public nudity based on the treatable fears of a select few.

        1.2.2.3.2. Con:
        Many people already avoid certain social events due to worries about public behaviors, like widespread alcohol use. This has not created significant social problems.

        1.2.2.3.3. Con:
        The number of people who would stop attending social and political events because of public nudity is impossible to estimate.

      1.2.2.4. Pro:
      There already exist pre-defined spaces [122] where social nudity is acceptable, allowing everyone's opinions and sensibilities to be protected.

        1.2.2.4.1. Con:
        There are countries, like Pakistan, where public nudity is not legal anywhere [123], even in pre-defined places.

        1.2.2.4.2. Con:
        The general public is opposed to public nudity even at pre-defined places such as private nude events [124], demonstrating that some people want their sensibilities protected even when not invited or attending.

        1.2.2.4.3. Pro:
        A number of music and cultural festivals allow, and even encourage [125], nudity; for the most part, large numbers of attendees from around the world, including those who prefer to stay clothed, remain unbothered [126].

        1.2.2.4.4. Pro:
        It is already legal for nudists to express themselves on designated public nude beaches [127].

    1.2.3. Con:
    Those who feel uncomfortable would be under no obligation to also go nude or interact with nude spaces. As such, these tensions and harms will be minor.

      1.2.3.1. Pro:
      Many private or indoor locations would likely ban nudity on their premises.

        1.2.3.1.1. Pro:
        Venues with poor lighting, like theaters, clubs, and cinemas, would likely prohibit nudity given that clients cannot clearly see what others around them are doing.

        1.2.3.1.2. Con:
        Many public locations unable to ban nudity and would likely face backlash or non-compliance.

          1.2.3.1.2.1. Con:
          In the US, many shops and public spaces successfully ban guns to create a feeling of safety among their clients. The same rule could be used for public nudity.

          1.2.3.1.2.2. Pro:
          Shop employees may not be able or willing to confront and throw out nude customers.

        1.2.3.1.3. Pro:
        Many upscale venues request customers dress in certain ways to sustain its aesthetic and culture.

          1.2.3.1.3.1. Pro:
          Venues and events where there are likely to be many children typically take up a larger number of safeguarding rules.

      1.2.3.2. Pro:
      People uncomfortable with nudity can opt out of events that are likely to have large amounts of nudity.

      1.2.3.3. Con:
      Even just a brief interaction with a nude person can be distressing [128] or act as a 'trigger' in some cases.

    1.2.4. Con:
    Governments should not ban behaviors just because they may cause offense to some individuals.

      1.2.4.1. Pro:
      Popular distaste does not give governments the right to ban nudity. The same logic could justify bans against piercings, colored hair, tattoos, religious garb, and more.

        1.2.4.1.1. Pro:
        There are plenty of behaviors which cause offense that are not banned.

          1.2.4.1.1.1. Pro:
          Speech can often cause offense, but is mostly protected nonetheless.

          1.2.4.1.1.2. Pro:
          Very few of the offensive behaviors outlined in the Ten Commandments [129] are prohibited by law.

            1.2.4.1.1.2.1. Con:
            As the Bible is not considered the foremost authority on the law, it should not be consulted when discussing the legality of public nudity.

          1.2.4.1.1.3. Con:
          Freedom of speech remains regulated by hate speech laws, laws against character defamation, and even laws against making bomb jokes at airports.

            1.2.4.1.1.3.1. Con:
            Such restrictions protect against inciting violence, intimidation and reputational damage. Nudity in the majority of social settings poses no comparable risks.

        1.2.4.1.2. Con:
        Many countries do ban religious garb for similar reasons, meaning this logic is not contradictory.

          1.2.4.1.2.1. Pro:
          France has banned various forms of religious face covering [130] because the wearing of religious garb violates the country's avowed republicanism.

          1.2.4.1.2.2. Con:
          These laws are often unpopular and face vociferous opposition [131]. They are not a useful example for other laws.

      1.2.4.2. Pro:
      Nudity doesn't fit the traditional definition of a social harm [132].

        1.2.4.2.1. Con:
        Some forms of harm, such as those associated with nudity, are more ambiguous [133] and therefore hard to legislate against.

        1.2.4.2.2. Pro:
        Arrests, fines, and prison sentences are grossly disproportionate penalties for a behavior that is merely socially unacceptable.

      1.2.4.3. Con:
      The role of the state is to ensure that all of its citizens can live comfortably. If public nudity impedes general comfort, it can and should be banned.

        1.2.4.3.1. Con:
        In some situations, widespread public nudity could make people feel safe in the knowledge that no one has any concealed weapons, increasing their comfort.
