Discussion Name:
Is freedom of sexuality inducing harm?

1. Thesis:
Freedom of sexuality is inducing harm.

  1.1. Con:
  Greater freedom of sexuality normalises diverse sexual identities.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    It is important for people to feel free to pursue their sexual desires.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      Many spiritual and philosophical traditions argue that the pursuit of sexual desires leads to vice and ruin.

        1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
        Christianity has a long and strong tradition of warning against the excessive pursuit of sexual desires (see Matt. 5:27-28 [1] amongst many others).

        1.1.1.1.2. Pro:
        Buddhism is based on the idea that desire is what creates suffering [2] and binds sentient beings to endless reincarnation.

          1.1.1.1.2.1. Con:
          Buddhism talks about desire in general, not specifically sexual desire.

        1.1.1.1.3. Con:
        In several religous traditions the pursuit of sexual desire is considered morally acceptable.

          1.1.1.1.3.1. Pro:
          Many religions have historically engaged in some form of 'sacred prostitution' [3].

          1.1.1.1.3.2. Con:
          Lust is considered a cardinal sin [4] in the Catholic religious doctrine.

          1.1.1.1.3.3. Pro:
          Ritualized sex is a component of several spiritual movements, such as Hindu or Buddhist tantra [5].

        1.1.1.1.4. Con:
        Contemporary philosphical movements such as sex-positive feminism view sexual emancipation as a means of achieving both liberation and empowerment.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      Even if it is important for people to feel free to pursue their sexual desires, this does not negate the point that this freedom can induce harm.

      1.1.1.3. Pro:
      Acting out one's sexual desires in a consensual and legal way is an overlooked mode of self-expression.

      1.1.1.4. Con:
      Sexual taboos [6] - the antithesis of freedom of sexuality - are vital for building sexual desire because sexual attraction stems from a sense of breaking the rules.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    Diversity of sexuality increases tolerance towards minorities.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      Allowing for greater freedom of sexuality helps fight discrimination against LGBT people [7].

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      Socities that promote unfettered sexual freedom tacitly endorse the fetishization [8] of women and minorities.

        1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
        Freedom of sexuality increases gender inequality.

          1.1.2.2.1.1. Pro:
          Freedom of sexuality forces women [9] to take more responsibility than men as they are usually left to raise the children alone when unintended pregnancies occur.

            1.1.2.2.1.1.1. Con:
            Both a man and woman can choose not to raise a child. They may instead put the child up for adoption.

              1.1.2.2.1.1.1.1. Con:
              Men usually leave the women to raise the children alone as they are the ones who get pregnant in the first place.

            1.1.2.2.1.1.2. Con:
            In many countries, women do not have to take on the responsibilitiy of raising children. Instead, when pregnant, many women can choose [10] to have an abortion.

            1.1.2.2.1.1.3. Con:
            The freedom of sexuality and sexual education reduces unwanted pregnancies significantly. Western and more sexually free countries have less unwanted pregnancies than some others: https://www.guttmacher.org/fact-sheet/induced-abortion-worldwide [11]

    1.1.3. Con:
    Some sexual orientations and practices, such as paedophilia, are objectively harmful and should not be normalized by civil society.

      1.1.3.1. Con:
      Moral nihilism [12] purports that nothing, inclusive of sexual orientation, is objectively wrong or harmful.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      Increasing freedom of sexuality in society does not constitute the acceptance of non-consenual sexual activity or other harmful sexual practices.

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      Society should not condone or normalize paraphillias [13], as they fundamentally rely on the absence of consent to achieve sexual gratification.

    1.1.4. Con:
    There is not necessarily a causation between liberalizing the right to sexual freedom and the acceptance of diverse or niche sexual orientations in society.

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      When the public overwhelmingly vote to liberalize sexual freedoms/rights via direct democracy [14], it's reasonable to infer that the society is broadly tolerant.

      1.1.4.2. Pro:
      Those who oppose the liberalization of sexual freedoms may become more hardened in their beliefs as the social acceptance of sexual minorities increases.

  1.2. Pro:
  Successful societies tend to exhibit culturally enforced monogamy.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Societies which allow freedom of sexuality generally have higher rates of abortion, domestic violence, divorce [15] and single motherhood, when compared to societies which do not.

      1.2.1.1. Con:
      Among our close hominid relatives [16], Bonobo hypersexuality [17] correlates with a remarkable drop in violence, infanticide, rape, and tribal warfare.

        1.2.1.1.1. Con:
        The correlation between hypersexuality and lack of violence does not imply that the former causes the latter. It could be that ape species that are already less violent have more time for sex and become hypersexual.

      1.2.1.2. Pro:
      Polygamous societies [18] tend to be far more violent [19] than societies with culturally enforced monogamy.

        1.2.1.2.1. Con:
        All those societies are also very patriarchal, and only allow men multiple wives, but not vice versa. The reason for increased violence in those countries is patriarchy, i.e. sexual power imbalance, which is precisely the opposite of sexual freedom.

          1.2.1.2.1.1. Con:
          Those societies allow both monogamy and polygamy. Thus it's more sexual freedom than only monogamy.

          1.2.1.2.1.2. Con:
          There are biological tendencies that give rise to these societies in lieu of culturally enforced monogamy (e.g Gorillas, Chimps etc.).

          1.2.1.2.1.3. Con:
          Polyandry is rare [20] in nature, and mostly occurs in species that do sexual role-reversal.

      1.2.1.3. Pro:
      Societies which allow freedom of sexuality [21] have higher abortion rates than societies which do not [22].

        1.2.1.3.1. Con:
        -> See discussion #5637: Pregnant people should have the right to choose abortion.

      1.2.1.4. Con:
      High divorce rates are not an indicator that freedom of sexuality is inherently harmful: divorce merely provides a pathway for those in unhappy relationships to leave their partners.

        1.2.1.4.1. Pro:
        Millenials are arguably among the most sexually free of all the generations, but drive a decreasing divorce rate [23].

          1.2.1.4.1.1. Con:
          Millenials might have lower divorce rates but they are still much higher than the countries which do not allow freedom of sexuality.

          1.2.1.4.1.2. Con:
          Online dating, not sexual freedom, is the reason millennials more easily find compatible parters ideal for lasting marriages.

        1.2.1.4.2. Con:
        Divorce has a negative economic [24] and social [25] impact on the affected family as well as the society as a whole.

          1.2.1.4.2.1. Con:
          Regarding the social impact, as the parent claim's source admits, a number of factors can actually mean divorce has a positive social impact on the affected family, and it is better to leave an unhappy relationship [26] than stay in it just for the children.

        1.2.1.4.3. Pro:
        Divorce provides a legal mechanism for victims of economic abuse to establish conditions [27] for financial renumeration and/or independence from the abuser.

      1.2.1.5. Con:
      Sexual emancipation, and the resulting breakdown of stigma and shame surrounding sex, creates more open conditions for victims of domestic violence to report perpetrators to law enforcement.

        1.2.1.5.1. Con:
        Domestic violence is illegal in some of the countries which aren't sexually free [28] and those countries have lower domestic violence rates than those which allow freedom of sexuality.

        1.2.1.5.2. Con:
        Limited sexual freedom in a society doesn't mean that they treat women unequally. As these limitations apply to both men and women equally.

        1.2.1.5.3. Pro:
        Countries with Sharia law [29] only only allows sex after marriage, and may allow domestic violence by interpretation of Sharia law or by followers favouring men's rights over women.

        1.2.1.5.4. Con:
        This is not necessarily the case: in England, a 2018 report found that only 28% [30] of women using community-based domestic violence services reported such abuse to the police, with even fewer seeing criminal sanctions or a criminal case against the perpetrator.

    1.2.2. Con:
    Even if the most successful societies tend to be monogamous, this does not prove that societies which have greater freedom of sexuality are harmful.

    1.2.3. Con:
    Monogamy and sexual freedom are not mutually exclusive.

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      Culturally enforced monogamy is by definition less free than a society that allows its populace to freely choose between monogamy and polygamy.

      1.2.3.2. Pro:
      Humans are evolutionarily hardwired to pursue monogamy, thus many people today consider themselves sexually free while exhibiting monogamous preferences.

        1.2.3.2.1. Con:
        Only 17% [31] of human cultures are strictly monogamous.

      1.2.3.3. Pro:
      The practice of ethical non-monogamy [32] (such as open relationships) combines elements of traditional monogamy and polygamy.

      1.2.3.4. Con:
      By definition, sexual freedom and monogamy are incompatible concepts.

    1.2.4. Pro:
    Freedom to cheat and break off relationships without repercussions leads to great emotional pain.

      1.2.4.1. Con:
      Infidelity is a direct consequence [33] of culturally enforced monogamy.

      1.2.4.2. Pro:
      Infidelity has been closely linked [34] to the development or exacerbation of mental health conditions such as post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD).

    1.2.5. Pro:
    Monogamy provides the basis for stable family units, which are essential for a stable societal structure.

      1.2.5.1. Con:
      Familes are no longer solely constructed to be hetero-normative structures, such as the nuclear family [35].

      1.2.5.2. Pro:
      Monogamous families have been shown to be the most stable and safe environment for raising children [36].

      1.2.5.3. Pro:
      From a functionalist view, the structure of monogamous families compliments [37] the demands of modern industrial socieity.

    1.2.6. Con:
    States and governments that actively repress the right of individuals to freely pursue their sexual orientation cause more harm than those who do not.

      1.2.6.1. Con:
      Harm is subjective.

        1.2.6.1.1. Pro:
        Harm caused by freedom of sexuality is an illusion shared by those opposed to it.

          1.2.6.1.1.1. Pro:
          People with an ideology that opposes freedom of sexuality, feel threatened by the freedom of those who live it.

        1.2.6.1.2. Con:
        Harm can often be measurable and observable, such as the infliction of physical injury on a non-consenting party.

        1.2.6.1.3. Con:
        Harm must be viewed as an objective reality in order to uphold the rule of law and order in any society.

      1.2.6.2. Pro:
      Culturally enforced monogamy reaffirms the patriarchal power structures affixed to normative social institutions, such as traditional marriage [38].

      1.2.6.3. Pro:
      Laws that specifically restrict the freedoms of sexual minorities violate their rights.

        1.2.6.3.1. Pro:
        Laws which promote freedom of sexuality such as those laws which legalise same-sex marriage [39] can help individuals live more fulfilling lives.

          1.2.6.3.1.1. Con:
          Studies show that marriage only makes you happier temporarily. [40]

          1.2.6.3.1.2. Pro:
          Whether some gay people choose to get married or not, they will have the freedom to do so and not feel like second class citizens.

        1.2.6.3.2. Pro:
        Any state that restricts what two consenting adults do with their bodies is actively harming the agency of those individuals.

          1.2.6.3.2.1. Pro:
          The right to self-define and express sexuality is protected [41] by international human rights laws and conventions.

      1.2.6.4. Con:
      States that are theocratic or traditionally conservative choose to limit sexual freedoms in order to maintain their cultural heritage and traditional values, not to cause harm.

        1.2.6.4.1. Pro:
        Sexual freedom is not binary, but if a culture forces you to abide by one sexual configration such as in Islamic states, clearly that is less sexual freedom than in one where no such restriction exists.

  1.3. Pro:
  Highly sexualized societies increase social pressure [42] on individuals and, as such, normalize undesirable or risky patterns of behaviour.

    1.3.1. Con:
    Greater normalization of sexual orientations that fall within the asexual spectrum [43] could alleviate the social pressure on these groups to conform to an increasingly sexualized society.

    1.3.2. Con:
    Improving the quality and accessibility of sex education should actively counteract the likelihood of pressure-susceptible groups, such as adolescents, engaging in risky sexual behaviour.

      1.3.2.1. Pro:
      Research has shown that access to comprehensive sex education reduces [44] the rate of teenage pregancies.

      1.3.2.2. Con:
      Certain forms of sexual education, such as abstinence education [45], are wholly ineffective.

        1.3.2.2.1. Pro:
        Classrom-based sex education is largely ineffectual [46] across most indicators.

      1.3.2.3. Pro:
      Raising awareness of the dangers of sexually transmitted diseases (STDs) has been associated with lower rates of fatality [47] amongst certain demographics.

      1.3.2.4. Con:
      Irrespective of the level of sexual education, individuals may still choose to refuse to change their approach to risky sexual behaviour.

    1.3.3. Pro:
    The commodification of sex reduces people to sexual objects, thus creating an unhealthy culture.

      1.3.3.1. Con:
      The commodification of sex tends to occur whether societies are sexually liberal or not.

        1.3.3.1.1. Con:
        Sexually liberal societies are less likely to practice more harmful methods of sexual commodification such as arranged marriage [48].

        1.3.3.1.2. Pro:
        Irrespective of their political alignment, capitalist societies will always commodify and sexualize women in the pursuit of profit.

      1.3.3.2. Pro:
      Freedom of sexuality encourages sexual objectification which degrades human dignity.

        1.3.3.2.1. Pro:
        Women are usually sexually objectified more than men, thereby perpetuating the patriarchal oppression of women [49]. (p. 309)

      1.3.3.3. Con:
      Utilizing sexuality in marketing is a separate issue from freedom of sexual expression.

      1.3.3.4. Pro:
      Commodifying sexual imagery, primarily through pornography, has unintentionally led to addictions [50] to sexual release and compulsive behaviors.

        1.3.3.4.1. Con:
        Commodifying alcohol arguably exacerbates alcohol addiction [51] for some people, but this is not an argument for prohibiting alcohol or reducing people's freedom to consume it.

      1.3.3.5. Con:
      Sexual desire is arguably a personifying desire, not an objectifying desire. This is because sexual desire is ordinarily directed towards people, not inanimate objects.

        1.3.3.5.1. Pro:
        Selling sex appeal does not necessarily reduce people to sexual objects; a person can be appreciated for their sex appeal as well as their kindness, humour, or intellect depending on the context.

      1.3.3.6. Pro:
      Advertisers use sex because it tends to attract the attention [52] of the audience.

    1.3.4. Pro:
    When sexuality is discussed openly and sex becomes a focal point of society, people feel pressured [53] to engage in sexual activity.

      1.3.4.1. Con:
      Even in sexually repressed societies there are pressures to engage in sex (e.g teen girls being married to elder men) that are far worse.

        1.3.4.1.1. Pro:
        In some countries, the taboo around sex outside of marriage is so great that women have to marry [54] their rapist.

      1.3.4.2. Con:
      Open discussions about sex are not necessarily positive. Thus, as a result of these discussions, instead of feeling pressured to engage in sexual activity, many people may see the reasons not to engage in sexual activity.

        1.3.4.2.1. Pro:
        Openly discussing sex may lead to more conversations about pregnancy and sexually-transmitted diseases. Such conversations may in turn serve to discourage sexual activity.

      1.3.4.3. Pro:
      Conservative attitudes towards sex, such as celibacy [55], are often highly stigmatized in sexually tolerant societies.

        1.3.4.3.1. Con:
        Increased openess and sexual freedom in society actively counteracts the formation and dissemination of stigmas or taboos.

        1.3.4.3.2. Pro:
        This, in turn, has the potential to contribute to the ostracization of social groups who choose to abstain from sex on the basis of a religious or moral belief.

      1.3.4.4. Pro:
      Social pressure is a significant factor in the sexual behaviours of adolescents, leading to a higher incidence of teenage pregnancy (p. 9) [56].

  1.4. Con:
  Sexual activity provides numerous social and health benefits to individuals.

    1.4.1. Pro:
    Sex induces a rich neurochemical cascade [57] that promotes wellbeing and bonding [58], thus casual sex acts as a social lubricant.

      1.4.1.1. Con:
      If sex induces wellbeing and bonding, the null hypothesis must be that this goes for all sex, not just the casual variant.

        1.4.1.1.1. Con:
        Casual sex would broaden the dissemination of this wellbeing and bonding.

        1.4.1.1.2. Con:
        Sexually liberal societies are differentiated by sexual frequency, not by having sex.

      1.4.1.2. Pro:
      Oxytocin [59] plays a crucial role in social cohesion and trust.

      1.4.1.3. Con:
      Alcohol can also act as a social lubricant [60]. This does not prevent something from also being harmful.

    1.4.2. Pro:
    Increased freedom of sexuality in society should positively affect human health, behaviour, and happiness.

      1.4.2.1. Pro:
      Research suggests that if your sexual orientation is accepted by society you will be happier and more satisified [61] with your life.

      1.4.2.2. Con:
      Many things lead to happiness, this does not mean it should be allowed to occur unchecked.

        1.4.2.2.1. Pro:
        Taking drugs may lead to happines for me. One should not then be allowed to use this logic to justify zero legal, moral and social restrictions to drug-taking.

        1.4.2.2.2. Con:
        Absent some sort of harm (other than the sort people consensually enjoy in sexual contexts), there is no reason to "check" it.

      1.4.2.3. Pro:
      Sex is highly pleasurable.

        1.4.2.3.1. Pro:
        Freedom of sexuality can encourage people to explore and improve their sexual pleasure, thus heightening sexual satisfaction.

          1.4.2.3.1.1. Pro:
          A sense of sexual freedom likely makes it easier to try new things [62], which is shown to improve sex.

        1.4.2.3.2. Pro:
        During the build up period toward sexual activity, the tissue in human genitals fill up with blood, increasing the sentitivity [63] of these areas.

        1.4.2.3.3. Pro:
        Sex causes increased production of feel-good chemicals such as dopamine [64] or oxytocin [65].

        1.4.2.3.4. Con:
        Asexual people tend to not find sex pleasurable [66].

        1.4.2.3.5. Con:
        Some people may find sex painful. [67]

          1.4.2.3.5.1. Con:
          Sexual freedom inherently creates more opportunity to deal with those issues that make sex painful.

        1.4.2.3.6. Con:
        Some women are not experiencing pleasure while having sex. [68]

          1.4.2.3.6.1. Con:
          Many women who do not experience pleasure during sex can implement solutions to increase their pleasure, or to help their partner(s) better provide them with pleasure.

      1.4.2.4. Con:
      The more readily available sex [69] is, the less satisfying it is because there is no real build up of desire.

        1.4.2.4.1. Con:
        The source for this claim lacks scientific credentials.

        1.4.2.4.2. Con:
        82% [70] of men say they feel glad they hooked up the following morning, indicating that they were not left sexually unsatisfied.

        1.4.2.4.3. Pro:
        50% [71] of women report having negative reactions after having casual sex with someone.

    1.4.3. Con:
    Chastity has also been closely asscoiated with psycho-social benefits.

    1.4.4. Con:
    Freedom of sexuality means people having to be sexually responsible [72], but being sexually responsible is a burden.

      1.4.4.1. Con:
      Freedom, in its truest sense, means that responsibility is optional. As such, sexual responsibility is not necessarily a burden, but rather a personal choice.

        1.4.4.1.1. Pro:
        Freedom, by definition, is not compatible with being forced into anything.

        1.4.4.1.2. Pro:
        Taking responsibility is empowering; it will make you more confident [73].

        1.4.4.1.3. Con:
        In some sexually liberated societies, intentionally transmitting STD's to an unwitting partner can be considered a criminal offence. [74]

        1.4.4.1.4. Pro:
        If people understand the responsibility with which they are purportedly burdened, they will likely take fewer risks, reducing more actualized and serious harms.

      1.4.4.2. Pro:
      Freedom of sexuality increases the spread of sexually transmitted diseases [75].

        1.4.4.2.1. Con:
        Sexually transmitted diseases can largely be avoided by having safe sex [76].

        1.4.4.2.2. Con:
        It is safe to assume that adults agreeing to have sex are aware of potential health risks.

        1.4.4.2.3. Con:
        By the same argument, freedom of movement increases car accidents.

          1.4.4.2.3.1. Con:
          There are strict rules in place to mitigate that issue.

      1.4.4.3. Con:
      The absence of freedom does not guarantee an absence of responsibility.

        1.4.4.3.1. Pro:
        Many Germans were conscripted [77] to serve in the Wehrmacht against their will, but they are still considered responsible for their actions during the Second World War.

      1.4.4.4. Pro:
      Freedom generally means individuals have choice. Choosing takes up mental energy and is thus a burden.

        1.4.4.4.1. Con:
        Several studies show that the feeling of control [78] is one of the most important psychological factors for health and wellbeing.

        1.4.4.4.2. Pro:
        Research [79] shows that the burden of choice actually exists.

          1.4.4.4.2.1. Con:
          The research and ideas this article refers to, pertains to human psychology in a specific context, none of which could be used to univeralize claims against sexual freedom.

          1.4.4.4.2.2. Con:
          Nonetheless, that freedom of choice is particularly important in terms of sexual choices because freedom of sexual choice allows one to escape pleasure-less situations that cannot otherwise be improved upon or sexual partners who turn inattentive or abusive.

        1.4.4.4.3. Pro:
        If one is responsible for a choice, regret and anxiety may occur in the decision-making process.

          1.4.4.4.3.1. Con:
          This is simply a myopic focus on a pessimistic chain of events. You could also be responsible for a choice, learn, realize what you need to improve, grow, become happier.

          1.4.4.4.3.2. Con:
          If you are responsible for a choice, then there is a likelihood that you will succeed; so there is happiness, which is uplifting.

          1.4.4.4.3.3. Con:
          This claim contradicts mainstream science which suggests that asense of control lowers anxiety and depressive symptoms [80]. [2] [81]

        1.4.4.4.4. Con:
        In terms of sexual choice, not having the freedom of choice would be worse, as it could mean not being able to choose away from stubbornly pleasureless, inattentive, or even abusive partners.

  1.5. Con:
  It is freedom-without-responsibility which induces harm not freedom as such.
