Discussion Name:
Should there be equal pay for elite sports athletes?

1. Thesis:
There should be equal pay for elite sports athletes.

  1.1. Pro:
  In regular jobs, governments protect workers such that women and men receive equal pay for equal work [1]. Sports work is no different.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Employers have a legal duty under the laws such as the Equality Act 2010 [2] to ensure that men and women receive equal pay for equal work. This should extend to sports as well.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      These laws are often contingent on women being employed by the same employer [3] as the male workers. Seeing as female elite players tend not to have the same employer as their male counterparts, the pay gap is legal.

    1.1.2. Con:
    The government implementing a policy does not equate to that policy being a good or desirable policy.

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      A policy that is made in an effort to appropriately value talent regardless of gender should be considered a desirable policy.

        1.1.2.1.1. Con:
        There is no policy capable of valuing talent without discrimination better than the free market system.

          1.1.2.1.1.1. Con:
          The free market system is discriminatory, especially when it comes to preventing racism [4]. This means that the talent of certain races will be undervalued in many cases.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    In Australia, both the men's team and the women's team were competing in the World Cup. The women's team vastly outperformed the men's team, yet without equal pay protection, the men's team made 8x [5] the amount of the, more successful, women's team.

      1.1.3.1. Con:
      If countries/ clubs were paying their women's teams equally to their men's teams, it would create an incentive for them to ask competition organizers to invest more equally into the prize pools for both men and women's leagues.

        1.1.3.1.1. Pro:
        In Australia, those paying the women's team equally to men would now have a strong reason to criticize the fact that the male league offers much more money to performers, given they are spending equally on their players but not receiving equally (based on performance, rather than gender alone).

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      The performance of the women's team is not comparable to the men's, because they weren't playing against the same opposing teams.

    1.1.4. Con:
    The level of competition and physical intensity tends to be higher in male sports. Thus, the work being completed by men and the work being completed by women are not equal.

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      In Grand Slam tennis compeitions men play best of 5 sets while women play best of 3 sets.

        1.1.4.1.1. Con:
        Regulations dictate the disparity in the number of sets played by men and women at Grand Slams. Many are calling for women to also play five sets at grand slams. [6]

      1.1.4.2. Pro:
      In a study between elite male and female professional footballers, researchers found that when looking at distance covered at a high intensity, there is anywhere between a 30-200% performance difference. [7]

        1.1.4.2.1. Con:
        Even if we accept that there are performance differences between men and women, this could be explained by women having less opportunities to develop their skills [8].

      1.1.4.3. Con:
      Proportionate to physical ability, the amount of effort and intensity would be roughly equal for men and women.

    1.1.5. Con:
    Male players are competing for larger prize pools [9] than female counterparts for their respective sports clubs. Thus, they are not engaging in equal work.

      1.1.5.1. Con:
      One study [10] shows that 35 out of 44 sports offer men and women equal prize money.

    1.1.6. Con:
    To make the work equal there should be no women's sport or men's sport, there should be one sport where people compete equally. If a woman or man can perform then they should be paid equally.

    1.1.7. Con:
    In terms of elite sports, you get paid based on how much revenue you will likely generate.

    1.1.8. Pro:
    There has already been a move towards sports paying sportswomen and men similar wages.

      1.1.8.1. Pro:
      -> See 1.1.5.1.

      1.1.8.2. Pro:
      In sports such as netball [11], women tend to be better paid than men.

      1.1.8.3. Pro:
      In tennis, elite female tennis players' earnings are relatively similar [12] to their male counterparts.

  1.2. Con:
  It would be difficult to enforce equal pay.

    1.2.1. Con:
    Difficulty in enforcing equal pay does not (of itself) make equal pay a non-worthwhile policy to pursue. At worst it would be enforced in some situations.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      Many club owners and teams may believe in the philosophy of equal pay and be happy to comply with equal pay regulation.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      The amount of effort required to pursue this equal pay regulation would limit the amount of time and energy policymakers have for other changes which could be more fully achieved.

      1.2.1.3. Con:
      If the equal pay is poorly enforced, but causes harms to individual clubs or female athletes, then the benefits would not justify the collateral damage.

        1.2.1.3.1. Pro:
        Looking for equal pay despite the disparity between male and female sports currently could create backlash if female athletes are seen as 'greedy' by the general public.

        1.2.1.3.2. Pro:
        Enforcing equal pay would kill off women's sports teams.

          1.2.1.3.2.1. Pro:
          If enforcing equal pay is financially unsustainable, it would increase the chance of female sports teams going out of business.

          1.2.1.3.2.2. Pro:
          Sports teams are commercial enterprises. Enforcing equal pay would increase costs and threaten profitability.

            1.2.1.3.2.2.1. Pro:
            Equal pay will result in costs increasing radically.

              1.2.1.3.2.2.1.1. Pro:
              Male Premier league footballers are paid over £50,000 a week on average. [13] Women playing in the FA Women's Super League receive an average £26,752 a year [14].

    1.2.2. Con:
    Even if enforcing equal pay is difficult, that does not mean there should not be equal pay.

    1.2.3. Pro:
    The owners of clubs could simply lay off their female sports teams to be able to pay competitive salaries to the members of their (far more profitable) male sports teams.

    1.2.4. Pro:
    Owners could split their company into two companies, having one for male and another for females, thus circumventing rules which demand equal pay within a company.

    1.2.5. Pro:
    Companies could pay both male and female athletes minimum wage plus a bonus proportional to viewership, thus allowing unequal take home money despite identical employment contracts.

    1.2.6. Pro:
    Big teams can just move to countries which don't have the equal pay laws. Other stadiums/leagues are very likely to accommodate them as they are extremely profitable.

  1.3. Pro:
  There is no reason not to have equal pay. To claim otherwise is just discriminatory and nothing else.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    The constitution of almost all countries guarantee the right to equality and equal treatment.

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      The 14th Amendment [15] to the Constitution of the United States guarantees all citizens the right to equality and equal treatment.

      1.3.1.2. Pro:
      Non-discrimination is an integral part of the principle of equality [16] protected under the Australian Constitution [17].

      1.3.1.3. Con:
      Unequal outcomes does not mean there were unequal opportunities or treatment. It can simply mean unequal performance.

        1.3.1.3.1. Con:
        In many cases, unequal outcomes are a result of unequal treatment.

          1.3.1.3.1.1. Pro:
          It is likely that those who are treated in a negative manner [18] do not perform well.

    1.3.2. Con:
    This is a dogmatic logical fallacy, assuming there are no viable alternative reasons as to why equal pay might not occur between genders in elite sports beyond sexism/discrimination.

    1.3.3. Pro:
    A gender should not justify a pay grade for professional athletes, female or male. Instead rather dedication, hard work and the skill an individual puts towards a sport.

  1.4. Con:
  Female sports have less viewers and therefore less money is earned. So less pay is justified.

    1.4.1. Pro:
    Earning in the sports industry is heavily influenced [19] by the popularity of the sports. Seeing as most women's sports struggle to generate comparable levels of interest, the pay gap is fair.

      1.4.1.1. Con:
      This is not the fault of female players, but rather the individual sporting bodies that fail to invest [20] sufficient funding into marketing and promotion opportunities of female sports in order to boost its popularity.

        1.4.1.1.1. Con:
        The reason for the lower popularity of female sports is likely because of the lower standard of competitiveness.

    1.4.2. Con:
    There is far less women's sport shown on tv or in big stadiums. Without changing the level of investment in the sport, this will never change.

      1.4.2.1. Con:
      Investing large sums of money into something does not mean that it will be successful.

        1.4.2.1.1. Pro:
        If this were true, then every business with high starting capital would be a success.

        1.4.2.1.2. Pro:
        Many companies with significant funding have failed [21].

      1.4.2.2. Con:
      The lack of investment in the female sports market when compared to the male sports market is because investors are not as confident in the female sports market.

        1.4.2.2.1. Pro:
        Investors decide whether to invest in opportunities based on their perception of risk and return [22] (p.1).

      1.4.2.3. Con:
      Even if it is true that to increase viewership of women's sports, investment must be increased, we should not compel individuals to invest in women's sports teams.

        1.4.2.3.1. Pro:
        Individuals have a right to use their capital how they see fit.

        1.4.2.3.2. Pro:
        It would be unreasonable to introduce legislation that forces those who wish to invest in men's sport, to invest in women's sport instead.

      1.4.2.4. Pro:
      Many argue that the level of viewers and tv time depends on the games being successfully marketed [23]. Significantly less marketing is given to women's sports teams.

        1.4.2.4.1. Pro:
        They argue that the root cause [24] of the unequal pay between male and female sports people stems from this marketing budget disparity and that it is a central issue fuelling the gender pay gap in sports.

      1.4.2.5. Pro:
      The amount of coverage of women's sport does not reflect the increased demand [25] and interest in watching women's games across sports fans.

      1.4.2.6. Pro:
      One of the biggest causes [26] of the increase in sports salaries (especially for men) has been the billions of dollars spent on live sports coverage causing their salaries to increase as a result.

      1.4.2.7. Pro:
      TV viewer levels are a key factor [27] in determining which players are offered sponsorship deals. In this way, the unequal investment in visibility on tv for female sports makes them unable to attract sponsorship deals.

    1.4.3. Con:
    In tennis, players are paid roughly equal wages [28] across genders, suggesting that there can be equal viewers and equal wages earned in male and female sports. It is not a fair assumption that female sports always attract lower viewership and that lower wages are therefore justified.

      1.4.3.1. Pro:
      This is incomparable due to the fact that tennis is a lot less popular, where female matches are watched more frequently in comparison to the male games.

    1.4.4. Con:
    The women's football team in the USA hit the same viewing ratings [29] as the male team during the World Cup.

      1.4.4.1. Pro:
      In the United States, viewership of the 2019 Women's World Cup final (involving the US women's team) was 22% higher than the 2018 men's final. [30]

      1.4.4.2. Pro:
      This is because it is a different competition, against a country more prominant with football interest.

      1.4.4.3. Pro:
      According to research, the USA women's soccer team's games have generated more revenue [31] than the men's team for the past 3 years.

    1.4.5. Pro:
    Those running sports clubs and organizations need fans and ticket sales and tv deals in order to make money to continue to fund their club/ team. Paying a team above the amount they generate for the organization would be financially damaging to the clubs.

      1.4.5.1. Pro:
      If this means that sports clubs are unable to sustain themselves, this may threaten the very existence of female sports clubs.

    1.4.6. Pro:
    Female sports represent only 4% [32] of all sports coverage on tv.

      1.4.6.1. Pro:
      This is despite the fact that women make up 40% [33] of all athletes.

      1.4.6.2. Con:
      Yet the coverage of women that is shown receives clear signs (high viewer numbers [34]) that the women's coverage is desired and valued by the public.

        1.4.6.2.1. Pro:
        The demand exists for more coverage, which would undermine the argument that women do not deserve equal pay on the basis of the unequal viewers they receive.

          1.4.6.2.1.1. Con:
          Although there is a demand for more coverage than is the norm for women's football today, that doesn't mean that the demand for women's football is the same as the demand for men's football. Thus, it does not follow that women should receive equal pay because of equal demand.

    1.4.7. Pro:
    In regular jobs, pay is often proportional to the level of stress or responsibility that a worker has. By having larger audiences and more on-air coverage, the job of male sports stars contains more responsibility, visibility and stress than those of their female counterparts.

      1.4.7.1. Pro:
      It is only fair that the pay received reflects the nature of the job being completed by the athlete, including the responsibilities expected of them as a public figure based on their audience size.

  1.5. Pro:
  It encourages younger girls to aspire and strive to reach elite levels of sport.

    1.5.1. Con:
    It is wrong to encourage young girls to pursue sports merely because of financial incentives.

      1.5.1.1. Pro:
      Money only increases well-being up until [35] a certain point. This point can easily be reached in other careers.

      1.5.1.2. Pro:
      Prioritising financial concerns over alternative things is likely to encourage consumerism, which can have a number of problematic effects. [36]

    1.5.2. Pro:
    Having young girls pursuing sporting goals would help in promoting healthy lifestyles and role models to the next generation of women.

      1.5.2.1. Con:
      The next generation of women can have healthy lifestyles [37] without entering elite levels of sport.

    1.5.3. Pro:
    The gender pay gap in sport sends a very negative message to young girls about their value relative to men, or about the place for women within sport, and the value placed on female sporting achievement.

      1.5.3.1. Pro:
      The fact there is such a gap in earning between men and women in elite sports makes it almost impossible [38] for girls to believe that they can secure a livelihood from their athletic practice regardless of how hard they try.

      1.5.3.2. Pro:
      It perpetuates [39] the idea that female sports are ''not as good to watch''.

        1.5.3.2.1. Con:
        There is nothing wrong with making a generalisation about female sporting competitions being less entertaining to watch when compared to male sporting competitions given that the level of competitiveness is higher in most male competitions.

          1.5.3.2.1.1. Pro:
          There is an average 10-12% performance gap [40] between elite male athletes and elite female athletes.

    1.5.4. Pro:
    Elite female athletes are widely [41] seen [42] as inspirational [43] for women and girls and show off the joy and achievement possible in the world of sport.

  1.6. Con:
  -> See 1.2.1.3.2.

  1.7. Con:
  Men compete in sports at a higher level than women. The pay gap reflects this disparity.

    1.7.1. Con:
    Women still compete in female leagues, so the recognition is for a fair success among equals.

    1.7.2. Pro:
    -> See 1.5.3.2.1.1.

    1.7.3. Pro:
    Since males are generally physiologically stronger, this does not necessarily reflect fair recognition / reward for those that reach true, absolute pinnacle of strength, skill, athleticism.

    1.7.4. Con:
    Any competition is limited to displaying the pinnacle of strength/ skill/ athleticism relative to the other athletes competing.

      1.7.4.1. Pro:
      We would not pay athletes less if the former world champion was unable to compete due to injury, despite it arguably allowing athletes to win without being the true pinnacle of athleticism in their sport.

      1.7.4.2. Pro:
      Similarly, women's sports are about reaching the pinnacle of skill, strength and athleticism within the constraints of female physiology. This does not make the reaching of this potential any less valuable than doing so in male sports.

        1.7.4.2.1. Con:
        If the constraints of female physiology mean that not many people find the sport entertaining then this would likely be reflected in the commercial standing on the sport and thus legitimately result in lower wages for participants within that sport.

    1.7.5. Con:
    Many jobs require physical strength (e.g. lifting in bar work, stock rooms). But while guidelines recognise gender strength differences, it remains illegal [44] to assume that women would be unable to carry out the same job as a man.

      1.7.5.1. Con:
      It may well be illegal to presume that on average, a woman is unlikley to be able to complete a physically demanding job in as efficient a manner than man. This does not make the statement any less true [45].

      1.7.5.2. Con:
      Even if we can't assume this, if a woman gets a job and is unable to perform to a given standard, they will likely be removed from their position.

      1.7.5.3. Pro:
      This suggests that we do not consider it acceptable to base the rate of pay on the physical limitations of gender.

        1.7.5.3.1. Con:
        It is however legal [46] to specify requirements for a job. This means that athletes could be legally classed into different categories for pay based on their strength, effectively creating a different rate of pay for men and women.

  1.8. Pro:
  Equal pay could create opportunities for increased sponsorship and funding of women's sports.

    1.8.1. Pro:
    If clubs are increasing the amount they spend on women's sports (through equal pay), this creates a bigger incentive to increase revenue, e.g. promoting women's games to get sponsorship, in order to get a greater return on this investment.

      1.8.1.1. Pro:
      Efforts to increase revenue would likely involve more promotion of women's matches to increase attendance (revenue) which in turn creates sponsorship opportunities for the women's events and teams.

      1.8.1.2. Con:
      Clubs will just find other areas to cut costs from, which may result in these athletes having access to a poorer quality of equipment and resources.

      1.8.1.3. Pro:
      This can lead to increased promotion of match attendance in order to create sales from tickets.

      1.8.1.4. Pro:
      This could lead to more televising of women's sports, increasing access to watching women's sport coverage.
