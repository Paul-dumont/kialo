Discussion Name:
Is Premarital Sex Immoral?

1. Thesis:
Premarital sex is immoral.

  1.1. Pro:
  Religion condemns premarital sex.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    The Quran condemns premarital sex.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      Quran 24:31 [1] condemns premarital sex by telling women "not expose their beauty except to their husbands [and close family members]."

      1.1.1.2. Pro:
      Quran 24:33 [2] says, "And let those who do not find the means to marry abstain..."

      1.1.1.3. Con:
      Most people are not Muslim and are therefore not obligated to adhere to the Islamic principles of the Quran.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    The Bible condemns premarital sex.

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      The Song of Songs [3] permits premarital sex.

        1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
        The Song of Songs approvingly describes the premarital sexual activity of the lovers in the poem.

          1.1.2.1.1.1. Con:
          In Song of Songs 4:8-12 [4], 5:1 [5] the woman is called "my bride," implying that the couple is married.

            1.1.2.1.1.1.1. Con:
            The woman being called "my bride" is figurative, not literal.

              1.1.2.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
              The woman is also called "my sister", but this is obviously figurative (Song of Solomon 4:9 [6]).

          1.1.2.1.1.2. Pro:
          The lovers appear to be meeting in secret, which shouldn't happen if they are married.

            1.1.2.1.1.2.1. Pro:
            One of the lovers is described "in the clefts of the rock, in the secret places of the stairs" (Song of Solomon 2:14 [7]).

          1.1.2.1.1.3. Con:
          The lovers already appear to share a house, suggesting they are married.

            1.1.2.1.1.3.1. Con:
            The woman wants to bring the man "to [her] mother’s house" (Song of Songs 3:4 [8], 8:2 [9]), implying that they are not married yet.

            1.1.2.1.1.3.2. Pro:
            In Song of Solomon 1:17 [10] the woman refers to "our house."

              1.1.2.1.1.3.2.1. Con:
              "Our house" probably refers to the house the couple shared at that particular time, not to a single house they consistently shared.

                1.1.2.1.1.3.2.1.1. Pro:
                In Song of Solomon 1:16 [11] the woman refers to "our couch." It doesn't make sense to conclude from this that the lovers consistently shared a single couch, but that they happened to share the couch at that time.

            1.1.2.1.1.3.3. Con:
            If the couple was cohabiting, they could have shared a house without being married.

            1.1.2.1.1.3.4. Con:
            In Song of Solomon 1:4 [12] we find that the lovers had sex in the king's chambers, not in their own house (where it would have happened if they were married).

          1.1.2.1.1.4. Pro:
          In Song of Solomon 3:1-4 [13] the woman is in bed but her lover is not in bed with her, and she had to search through the city to find him. This suggests they are unmarried because if they were married, surely he would have been in the same bed or at least house as her at night.

            1.1.2.1.1.4.1. Con:
            The lover could have been away late at night for some reason.

          1.1.2.1.1.5. Pro:
          According to Song of Songs 1:6 [14], the woman is still under the control of her brothers, suggesting she is unmarried.

        1.1.2.1.2. Con:
        The Song of Songs says "Daughters of Jerusalem, I charge you: Do not arouse or awaken love until it so desires" (Song of Songs 8:4 [15]). This is a reference to not engaging in sexual activity before marriage.

          1.1.2.1.2.1. Con:
          "Until it so desires" is vague and could refer to when someone reaches the age of consent instead of marriage.

          1.1.2.1.2.2. Con:
          This could be a warning not to disturb lovers while they are occupied.

            1.1.2.1.2.2.1. Pro:
            This interpretation fits better with the passage's overall tone of celebrating passion than the "no premarital sex" interpretation.

          1.1.2.1.2.3. Con:
          This could be a warning not to try to force true love or [16] seek it in the wrong relationships.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      There are various verses in the New Testament which state that sexual immorality is a sin [17]. Many have inferred sexual immorality to include fornication.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        The article [18] doesn't explain why the authors of the lexicons included "premarital sex" in the definition of "sexual immorality," so for all we know this could be an example of translation bias.

          1.1.2.2.1.1. Con:
          Some Bible verses imply that "premarital sex" is included in the definition of the Greek word "porneia," or "sexual immorality."

            1.1.2.2.1.1.1. Pro:
            1 Corinthians 7:1-9 [19] says that unmarried people can get married so they can avoid "sexual immorality" and "burning with passion." If premarital sex was permissible Paul would have recommended that the unmarried have premarital sex, not that they get married.

              1.1.2.2.1.1.1.1. Con:
              It is true that Paul recommends the unmarried believers in his audience get married, but it does not follow that he says this because sex before marriage is a sin. There are other reasons he could have told them to get married.

                1.1.2.2.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
                Paul didn't mention premarital sex because at the time there was no unproblematic way for Christians to engage in it.

                  1.1.2.2.1.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
                  Premarital sex with a slave would have been problematic due to it being exploitative, similar to a boss-employee relationship.

                  1.1.2.2.1.1.1.1.1.2. Pro:
                  Premarital sex with a free woman would [20] have been considered illegal [21] at the time and would have left the seducer vulnerable to being sued.

                    1.1.2.2.1.1.1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
                    In Romans 13:1-2 [22] Paul advises Christians to obey laws (except when they mandate sin).

                  1.1.2.2.1.1.1.1.1.3. Pro:
                  Concubinage would not have been considered a notable alternative to marriage for several reasons.

                    1.1.2.2.1.1.1.1.1.3.1. Pro:
                    Concubinage was not commonly practiced by freeborns [23].

                    1.1.2.2.1.1.1.1.1.3.2. Pro:
                    Concubinage would not have been considered different enough from marriage to mention separately.

                      1.1.2.2.1.1.1.1.1.3.2.1. Pro:
                      The only difference between marriage and concubinage was [24] that in marriage the wife was raised to the husband's status.

                1.1.2.2.1.1.1.1.2. Pro:
                Paul says he gives his advice "because sexual immorality is occurring [25]," which is likely a reference to the prostitution in 1 Corinthians 6:12-20 [26]. He probably told the unmarried believers to get married because marriage is the most effective way to ward off the temptation to sleep with prostitutes.

                  1.1.2.2.1.1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
                  Soliciting a prostitute would have been sinful because prostitutes at the time were [27] considered "devotees of Aphrodite", and as such sleeping with one would have been participation in idolatry.

                  1.1.2.2.1.1.1.1.2.2. Pro:
                  Unmarried couples tend [28] to [29] cheat more [30] than married couples, so a marriage is better than a premarital relationship at preventing sex outside the relationship.

                    1.1.2.2.1.1.1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
                    Married people have more sex [31], so it makes sense that marriage would be the best way to deal with unwelcome sexual desires, even if premarital sex was also an option.

                  1.1.2.2.1.1.1.1.2.3. Pro:
                  Unmarried unions are [32] more likely to break up than marriages, so a premarital relationship is more likely to not fix the problem Paul was trying to address permanently.

            1.1.2.2.1.1.2. Pro:
            Hebrews 13:4 [33] implies that premarital sex is sexual immorality: "Marriage should be honored by all, and the marriage bed kept pure, for God will judge the adulterer and all the sexually immoral."

              1.1.2.2.1.1.2.1. Con:
              Interpreting "sexually immoral" in this context to mean "incestuous," "pedophile," or some other behavior makes sense of the passage and its context. There is no reason to favor a "premarital sex" interpretation over these others.

              1.1.2.2.1.1.2.2. Con:
              It is possible that "the adulterer and all the sexually immoral" is an example of Hendiadys [34], or the expression of a single idea using two words connected by "and" ("all the sexually immoral" is one word in the Greek [35]). If this is true, "sexually immoral" would be a synonym for "adultery" in this context, not "premarital sex."

            1.1.2.2.1.1.3. Con:
            In Matthew 5:32 [36] (also 19:9 [37]), "porneia" is given as a reason why a couple may divorce. However, "premarital sex" doesn't make any sense in this context.

              1.1.2.2.1.1.3.1. Pro:
              If premarital sex is a justification for divorce in Matthew 5:32, this would contradict Deuteronomy 22:28-29 [38], where a man who seduces or rapes (depending on the interpretation) a virgin must marry her and is specifically forbidden from divorcing her.

              1.1.2.2.1.1.3.2. Pro:
              The "porneia" exception in Matthew 5:32 and 19:9 point back to Deuteronomy 24:1 [39], where a man is permitted to divorce his wife if he "found some indecency in her." The word [40] for "indecency" is also used in the Leviticus 18 [41] sexual prohibitions and thus probably refers to those. However, premarital sex is not mentioned in Leviticus 18.

            1.1.2.2.1.1.4. Pro:
            In John 8:41 [42] the Jews say, "We were not born of sexual immorality. We have one Father—even God." Here "sexual immorality" means premarital sex.

              1.1.2.2.1.1.4.1. Con:
              Here the Jewish disciples are trying to say that they are spiritual offspring of God and Israel, not Satan and Israel. However, within this metaphor the relationship between God and Israel is often portrayed as a marriage covenant (e.g. Ezekiel 16:8 [43]), so any spiritual offspring between Satan and Israel would be the product of adultery, not premarital sex. Thus, "sexual immorality" here means adultery, not premarital sex.

      1.1.2.3. Con:
      Some Bible stories only make sense if premarital sex is not viewed as a sin.

        1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
        After Isaac claims that Rebekah is his sister, not his wife, to prevent people from killing him for Rebekah, Abimelek complains, "What is this you have done to us? One of the men might well have slept with your wife, and you would have brought guilt upon us." (Genesis 26:10 [44]). This complaint only makes sense if a man sleeping with Rebekah if she had not been married would not have "brought guilt."

        1.1.2.3.2. Con:
        In 1 Samuel 2:22-23 [45], Eli's sons commit wicked deeds. For instance, "they slept with the women who served at the entrance to the tent of meeting." Here Eli's sons are condemned for engaging in premarital sexual activity.

          1.1.2.3.2.1. Con:
          In 1 Samuel 4:19 [46] we learn that at least one of Eli's sons was married. If the sons were married, their sexual activity with the women at the tent of meeting would be adultery, not premarital sex, and thus not relevant to the debate.

          1.1.2.3.2.2. Con:
          The women at the tent of meeting were probably married, which would make the actions of the sons of Eli adultery, not premarital sex.

            1.1.2.3.2.2.1. Pro:
            Yoma 9b [47] says that the women at the tent of meeting had husbands.

        1.1.2.3.3. Pro:
        Ruth and Boaz probably had premarital sex [48] on the threshing floor (Ruth 3 [49]), yet the whole episode is viewed positively by the Bible.

          1.1.2.3.3.1. Pro:
          In Ruth 3:14 [50] Boaz says, "And he said, Let it not be known that a woman came into the floor." This suggests they had sex and didn't want others to know.

          1.1.2.3.3.2. Pro:
          Ruth 3:3, 7 [51] suggests that Ruth got Boaz somewhat intoxicated.

          1.1.2.3.3.3. Pro:
          When it says that Ruth uncovered Boaz's feet (Ruth 3:7 [52]), and lay down at his feet (Ruth 3:8 [53]), feet is a euphemism for "genitals."

            1.1.2.3.3.3.1. Pro:
            Elsewhere in the Bible, "feet" is a euphemism for "genitals."

              1.1.2.3.3.3.1.1. Pro:
              In Isaiah 7:20 [54], "feet" is a euphemism for "genitals."

              1.1.2.3.3.3.1.2. Pro:
              In Deuteronomy 28:57 [55], "feet" is a euphemism for "genitals."

            1.1.2.3.3.3.2. Con:
            The word literally means "feet" in this case, and simply refers to Ruth uncovering Boaz's literal feet to get them cold and wake him up.

              1.1.2.3.3.3.2.1. Pro:
              Wearing socks (i.e. covering feet) can help people fall asleep faster [56], so it makes sense that uncovering a person's feet may help wake them up.

              1.1.2.3.3.3.2.2. Con:
              Uncovering a person's feet is a strange way to wake someone up. Plausibly, Ruth would have used some other method instead, such as nudging Boaz gently while telling him to get up.

          1.1.2.3.3.4. Con:
          If Ruth and Boaz did have sex, this would have been a consummation of their marriage [57], not premarital sex.

            1.1.2.3.3.4.1. Con:
            Ruth and Boaz marry in Ruth 4:13 [58], not in Ruth 3.

        1.1.2.3.4. Pro:
        In Judges 16:1 [59] Samson has sex with a prostitute, but he apparently keeps the strength he got from his Nazirite vow (Judges 13:5 [60]). He only loses his strength when he lets his hair get cut (Judges 16:19 [61]). If Samson's sex with the prostitute was a sin, he wouldn't have gotten to keep his superhuman strength.

        1.1.2.3.5. Con:
        In Genesis 38:24 [62], after Judah found out that his daughter-in-law Tamar was guilty of prostitution, he said, "Bring her out and have her burned to death!" This means that there was a prohibition on prostitution and probably premarital sex in general.

          1.1.2.3.5.1. Con:
          Tamar was understood to be the daughter of Shem, a priest [63]. This is important because there is a specific prohibition against the daughters of priests being prostitutes (Leviticus 21:9 [64]) that Tamar likely violated. Thus, this passage can be explained without assuming that a general prohibition on prostitution exists.

            1.1.2.3.5.1.1. Pro:
            The punishments in Leviticus 21:9 and Genesis 38:24 both consist of burning and use the same verb, saraph [65], to describe this. This strengthens the link between the passages.

        1.1.2.3.6. Con:
        In John 4:18 [66] Jesus tells a woman, "... you have had five husbands, and the man you now have is not your husband ..." The implication is that her living with a man that is not her husband is sinful.

          1.1.2.3.6.1. Con:
          It is likely that the woman was guilty of adultery, not premarital sex. She probably (unlawfully?) divorced her previous five husbands and began sleeping with another man, which Jesus considered a form of adultery (Mark 10:12 [67]).

        1.1.2.3.7. Pro:
        In 2 Corinthians 11:2 [68] Paul tells his audience metaphorically that "... I betrothed you to one husband, to present you as a pure virgin to Christ." This assumes that betrothal deters a woman from sleeping with anyone other than her future husband, but this is only true if premarital sex outside of betrothal is acceptable (otherwise betrothal would do nothing to change the woman's obligations).

      1.1.2.4. Con:
      The Bible was written at a time when sex education, condoms, and prophylaxis for STDs were non-existent. In modern society, where the pleasures of sex can be enjoyed without the associated risks, the relevant Bible verses can be ignored.

        1.1.2.4.1. Pro:
        Today, we can [69] cure half of the most common STDs.

        1.1.2.4.2. Con:
        Abortions were being practiced at least 4000 years ago [70], indicating that people at the time had some knowledge and control of reproductive issues.

      1.1.2.5. Pro:
      The Old Testament Law condemns premarital sex.

        1.1.2.5.1. Con:
        The Old Testament condemns many things - ranging from eating shrimps [71] to not shaving [72] the sides of one's beard. Given that these laws are no longer followed in modern society, premarital sex laws should also be considered obsolete.

        1.1.2.5.2. Pro:
        According to Biblical law, premarital sex is punishable by the death penalty.

          1.1.2.5.2.1. Pro:
          In Deuteronomy 22:13-21 [73], the punishment for a woman not being a virgin at the time of marriage is being stoned to death, which means that premarital sex is a sin.

            1.1.2.5.2.1.1. Con:
            The woman was not stoned to death for having premarital sex. We know this for several reasons. Thus, the woman must have been stoned for a different reason such as fraud or deception.

              1.1.2.5.2.1.1.1. Pro:
              After all, in Exodus 22:16-17 [74] we see that the worst penalty for someone caught in the act of premarital sex is a fine and forced marriage. It makes no sense for finding out about premarital sex through indirect evidence to carry a much more severe and incompatible penalty than being caught in the act.

              1.1.2.5.2.1.1.2. Pro:
              Leviticus 21:14 [75] is a commandment specifically saying that a priest should only marry a virgin, but this law would not make much sense if a woman could be stoned to death just for not being a virgin at the time of marriage.

            1.1.2.5.2.1.2. Pro:
            Unatoned, unpardoned premarital sex, regardless of when it took place or the intention behind it, did require [76] the death penalty.

            1.1.2.5.2.1.3. Con:
            The scope of this law must be restricted to women who lose their virginity by cheating during the betrothal period, not to any woman who attempts to marry without being a virgin.

              1.1.2.5.2.1.3.1. Pro:
              Otherwise, a widow could get stoned to death for attempting to remarry, even though elsewhere the Bible is clear that widows are allowed to remarry (1 Corinthians 7:8-9 [77]).

              1.1.2.5.2.1.3.2. Pro:
              If the woman was not a virgin at the time of betrothal, the future husband would have found out because his family would not have had to pay the bride-price for virgins (Exodus 22:16-17 [78]). Since the husband was surprised, his family must have paid this bride-price, which would imply the woman was a virgin at the time of betrothal.

                1.1.2.5.2.1.3.2.1. Pro:
                The bride-price was paid at the beginning of the betrothal period [79], while consummation happened at the end.

        1.1.2.5.3. Pro:
        In Exodus 22:16-17 [80] and its parallel Deuteronomy 22:28-29 [81], a man who has sex with an unmarried woman is forced to marry her without the possibility of divorce, and if the father refuses to marry her the lover has to pay "the bride price for virgins." Since a punishment is given for premarital sex, it must be a sin.

          1.1.2.5.3.1. Con:
          The man is punished, but not specifically for premarital sex. He is punished for cheating the father out of the "bride price for virgins" the father would have received had he married off the daughter while she was still a virgin. This is why the lover has to pay the bride price and possibly marry the daughter: to make up for the father's economic loss.

            1.1.2.5.3.1.1. Pro:
            Economic sins in the Old Testament are typically punished through fines (Exodus 22:1-15 [82], Leviticus 6:1-7 [83], Proverbs 6:30-31 [84]), whereas sexual sins are typically punished with the death penalty, exile, or dying childless (Leviticus 20:10-21 [85], Leviticus 21:9 [86], Deuteronomy 22:22 [87]). Since the man was punished with a fine rather than the death penalty or exile, he probably committed an economic sin and not a sexual one.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Many religions regard sex as the ultimate expression of love that ought to be enjoyed and expressed within the emotional, psychological and physical security of commitment through marriage.

      1.1.3.1. Con:
      That something is supposedly best enjoyed in a certain way does not make not enjoying it in that way immoral.

        1.1.3.1.1. Pro:
        That a piece of music is best enjoyed with a certain sound system doesn't make listening to it with a worse sound system immoral.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      Sex, just like eating or sleeping, is a fundamental human desire and therefore cannot be immoral.

        1.1.3.2.1. Con:
        Even if sex is a fundamental human desire, this does not make all expressions of sexuality moral.

          1.1.3.2.1.1. Pro:
          Rape is immoral.

            1.1.3.2.1.1.1. Con:
            Rape involves sexual activity in which one person did not consent to that activity. By contrast, premarital sex is activity to which all participants are enthusiastically consenting.

            1.1.3.2.1.1.2. Pro:
            Rape can still occur among married couples [88].

          1.1.3.2.1.2. Pro:
          Incest is immoral.

            1.1.3.2.1.2.1. Con:
            It is legal to marry your cousin in some states [89], which means that you can have sex with your spouse (cousin).

        1.1.3.2.2. Pro:
        Marriage is a societal construct while sex is an innate human drive. It has no meaning beyond legal reasons for those who are not religious or belong to a religion where it is not important.

        1.1.3.2.3. Con:
        Having a fundamental human desire does not equate to it being moral or justified. Evil actions are fundamental humans desires, but are morally unacceptable.

      1.1.3.3. Con:
      This is an opinion or a belief. It is not an argument with evidence sufficiently strong to counter lived experience of connectedness, intimacy, security and so on of people who are not married, some of whom may have expressed commitment in other ways.

      1.1.3.4. Pro:
      Waiting until marriage to have sex shows a commitment to marriage that increases the integrity of the intimacy shared between two people.

      1.1.3.5. Pro:
      Sex is a vulnerable act for many. Having sex within a secure relationship can protect against negative consequences for such people.

    1.1.4. Con:
    Since God created us, the urge to have sex is innate within us. Acting on that urge should not be immoral.

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      This argument could be applied to any urge, but it does not follow that acting on any urge is moral because they exist.

      1.1.4.2. Pro:
      The rabbinic community acknowledges [90] the human desire for sex. It is thus considered a natural urge rather than an immoral act.

    1.1.5. Con:
    It does not follow that something is immoral because it is condemned in a religious text. Religious texts do not dictate morality.

      1.1.5.1. Con:
      Religious texts do dictate morality for the majority of humanity [91] who are religious.

        1.1.5.1.1. Con:
        The third largest [92] percentage of the human population, behind Christianity and Islam, don't follow the precepts of any religion.

      1.1.5.2. Pro:
      Religious texts are not accurate representations of the moral code expected by God.

        1.1.5.2.1. Pro:
        It is impossible to accurately represent the expectations of an omnipotent, eternal, all powerful being to the human mind.

      1.1.5.3. Pro:
      The Bible is not a relevant source for deriving moral truths.

        1.1.5.3.1. Pro:
        Many laws in the Bible are no longer considered applicable (such as laws concerning diet, purity, and sacrifice, among many others). The laws concerning premarital sex should or may be considered likewise no longer applicable.

          1.1.5.3.1.1. Pro:
          The Old Testament prohibits eating pork, shellfish, and dairy with meat, yet such rules are widely disobeyed.

            1.1.5.3.1.1.1. Con:
            There are reasons for not following the Old Testament dietary prohibitions that do not apply to other Biblical laws.

              1.1.5.3.1.1.1.1. Pro:
              In Mark 7:19 [93], Jesus "declared all foods clean."

                1.1.5.3.1.1.1.1.1. Con:
                Those words are missing from early manuscripts [94] and translations.

                  1.1.5.3.1.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
                  The King James Version of Mark 7:19 [95] is missing the phrase "declared all foods clean."

                  1.1.5.3.1.1.1.1.1.2. Pro:
                  The Codex Vaticanus and Codex Sinaiticus [96] are missing the phrase "declared all foods clean" in Mark 7:19.

              1.1.5.3.1.1.1.2. Pro:
              In Acts 10 [97] Peter has a vision where he "saw heaven opened and something descending like a great linen sheet being let down by its four corners on the earth, and in it were all sorts of four-footed animals and reptiles of the earth and birds of heaven." When Peter refused to eat the animals because they violated Old Testament dietary laws, God said "Stop calling defiled the things God has cleansed."

          1.1.5.3.1.2. Pro:
          The Old Testament prohibits working on the Sabbath (Saturday), yet most or all people do some sort of activity on Saturday which the Old Testament designates as work.

          1.1.5.3.1.3. Pro:
          The Old Testament prohibits lending money at interest (usury) [98], yet the modern economy is founded on the principle of lending money at interest.

          1.1.5.3.1.4. Pro:
          The Old Testament prohibits wearing garments of mixed wool and linen [99]. Yet, many people do wear such garments of mixed fiber.

        1.1.5.3.2. Pro:
        This assumes that one accepts Christian scripture as authoritative. Yet, many do not accept it as such and there are reasons not to do so.

          1.1.5.3.2.1. Pro:
          Christian scriptures do not reflect the knowledge and values of a post-Enlightenment world.

  1.2. Con:
  Premarital sex can offer greater health benefits than premarital abstinence.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Premarital sex allows people to ensure that they are sexually compatible before marriage, which is important to a healthy sex life.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      People that have committed to abstinence their whole lives may have difficulty adjusting to marriage life, which could negatively impact their sex lives.

        1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
        Sexual incompatibility can lead to divorce [100]. As such, not having premarital sex could increase the risk of marital failure.

        1.2.1.1.2. Pro:
        An expectation to perform sexually may manifest as pressure, which can negatively impact [101] one's sexual experience.

        1.2.1.1.3. Con:
        A commitment to abstinence is a conscious and considered choice, as such individuals are prepared for the change that comes with marriage.

        1.2.1.1.4. Pro:
        Sexual dysfunction conditions are more likely in people [102] who do not have sex until later in life.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      Premarital sex can reduce the ability for someone to romantically bond in the future.

        1.2.1.2.1. Pro:
        Bonding chemicals [103] in the brain such as oxytocin and vasopressin released by sex can lead to people bonding to previous partners rather than their current partner, which can impair future romantic relationships.

          1.2.1.2.1.1. Con:
          These chemicals are also released by nonsexual activities such as hugging. Therefore, if this were a sound argument against premarital sex it would also be a sound argument against premarital hugging.

          1.5.4.1.1.1. Con:
          Contraceptives are not 100% effective.

        In some countries [200], women can choose to abort the fetus.

          1.5.4.1.2.1. Pro:

      1.5.4.2. Con:
      Premarital sex acts that are nonprocreative cannot lead to unplanned pregnancies.

      1.5.4.3. Con:
      Marital sex may also lead to unplanned and unwanted pregnancies.

        1.5.4.3.1. Con:
        If an unplanned pregnancy happens in the context of a marriage, there is a much lower risk of any one partner leaving or withdrawing support, as the two partners are legally bound.

          Divorces and abandonment can still occur.

  1.6. Pro:
  Some philosophies argue that premarital sex is immoral.

    1.6.1. Pro:
    Natural law theory [202] demonstrates that premarital sex is immoral.

      1.6.1.1. Con:
      Morality derived from "natural law[s]" and conditions – i.e. human nature – would be an argument for rather than against premarital sex since it is characteristically encoded [203] in human [204] physiology to seek and enjoy sex also for non-reproductive [205] purposes [206] such as mental well-being, during old non-reproductive age, joy, social circle development motivator or reward function more broadly, and as motivator to get to know more potential reproduction partners well.

      1.6.1.2. Pro:
      The perverted faculty argument [207] of natural law theory successfully demonstrates that premarital sex is immoral.

        1.6.1.2.1. Pro:
        The basic idea is that the purpose of sexual activity is procreation within the context of marriage, and any use of sex outside of that frustrates its purpose and is immoral.

          1.6.1.2.1.1. Con:
          It is not immoral to use a thing for something other than its intended purpose.

            1.6.1.2.1.1.1. Con:
            Contraception and extramarital sex do not merely use sex for something "other than" its end, but "contrary to" its end.

              1.6.1.2.1.1.1.1. Con:
              Contraception and premarital sex are not "contrary to" sex's possible end of procreation within marriage because procreation within marriage can still be performed later even if these acts are engaged in.

                1.6.1.2.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
                To be contrary to the end of procreation would involve counteracting procreation, namely eliminating life. Sex with contraceptives does not do that, hence it is not contrary to the end of procreation. It simply does not result in procreation, like almost all other acts.

              1.6.1.2.1.1.1.2. Con:
              Even using a faculty for something "contrary to" its end is not necessarily wrong.

                1.6.1.2.1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
                Using the mouth to spit out food is a use that is contrary to its end of providing nourishment, but this use is not immoral.

                1.6.1.2.1.1.1.2.2. Pro:
                Making art projects [208] out of egg cartons is clearly contrary to the purpose of egg cartons (storing and transporting eggs), yet this use is not immoral or bad.

            1.6.1.2.1.1.2. Pro:
            It is not immoral to use one's ears to hold up their glasses.

            1.6.1.2.1.1.3. Pro:
            Using sheets to make a rope to escape from a burning building is not immoral, even though the sheets were made to be used for bed coverings, rather than as rope.

              1.6.1.2.1.1.3.1. Con:
              Here, the action is justified because it is needed to save one's own life. However, this is not the case with contraceptives.

                1.6.1.2.1.1.3.1.1. Con:
                It follows that going against the purpose of something is not universally wrong in every case. Thus natural law theory is not a complete ethical system, whereas competing moral systems are.

            1.6.1.2.1.1.4. Pro:
            Using a wooden chair as firewood if one is snowed in is not immoral, even though a chair is meant to be used as something to sit upon.

          1.6.1.2.1.2. Con:
          This would imply that any form of sex which cannot lead to procreation is likewise immoral. However, that seems absurd.

            1.6.1.2.1.2.1. Pro:
            Oral sex cannot lead to procreation, yet it is not immoral.

            1.6.1.2.1.2.2. Pro:
            Anal sex cannot lead to procreation, yet it is not immoral.

            1.6.1.2.1.2.3. Pro:
            Sex with an infertile partner cannot lead to procreation, yet it is not immoral.

            1.6.1.2.1.2.4. Pro:
            Sex with contraceptives is very unlikely to lead to procreation, yet such sex is not immoral.

            1.6.1.2.1.2.5. Con:
            The proponent of natural law theory would not find opposition to contraception, oral sex, and anal sex absurd, but only counterintuitive to modern sensibilities.

      1.6.1.3. Con:
      There are good reasons not to accept natural law theory, or to prefer alternative moral theories.

        1.6.1.3.1. Pro:
        Natural law theory [209] rejects all forms of masturbation, homosexual sex, and contraception [210]. Thus, if one thinks these things are morally permissible, one would reject natural law theory.

        1.6.1.3.2. Pro:
        There are no moral facts (morality isn't objective) and so Natural Law theory cannot be right.

      1.6.1.4. Pro:
      New Natural Law Theory [211] successfully argues for the impermissibility of premarital sex.

        1.6.1.4.1. Pro:
        Finnis (p. 15-16 [212]) argues that regarding premarital sex as permissible undermines the marital good.

          1.6.1.4.1.1. Con:
          There is no reason why one should base their moral system on the arguments of Finnis over other moral systems that allow premarital sex.

      1.6.1.5. Con:
      It is unfair to compare natural law with a law as artificial as marriage.

        1.6.1.5.1. Pro:
        Marriage is a human invention. Animals do not marry. Humans did not marry until the last several thousand years [213], before which humans procreated perfectly fine wholly apart from marriage. Thus, the "perverted faculty" argument cannot show that pre-/exra-marital sex is immoral.

          1.6.1.5.1.1. Con:
          Even if animals do not have official marriages, many are monogamous [214], which is the closest they can get to marriage.

            1.6.1.5.1.1.1. Con:
            Premarital sex is often monogamous [215].

            1.6.1.5.1.1.2. Con:
            Simply being monogamous is not the same as marriage as humans practice it.

              1.6.1.5.1.1.2.1. Pro:
              Marriage carries specific legal weight and benefits in human society.

                1.6.1.5.1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
                Marriage creates a contract between the people who agree to it.

              1.6.1.5.1.1.2.2. Pro:
              Many cohabiting human couples are monogamous but not married.

        1.6.1.5.2. Pro:
        Marriage is an artificial social-construct; sexual intercourse is natural. Trying to make human nature conform to artificial rules without clear benefits is, itself, immoral.

    1.6.2. Con:
    Premarital sex is also supported by various philosophical systems.

      1.6.2.1. Pro:
      Under hedonistic utilitarianism [216], good is defined in terms of the greatest pleasure/happiness for the greatest number. As consensual premarital sex brings people happiness, it can be said to be moral.

        1.6.2.1.1. Con:
        Under utilitarianism, unplanned children, emotional distress, disease, and other consequences of premarital sex (plus the loss of deep connection a single partner can give) all detract from the greatest pleasure/ happiness sum.

          1.6.2.1.1.1. Con:
          Many or all of these potential undesired outcomes may be avoided by appropriate safeguards such as contraceptives and good communication.

        1.6.2.1.2. Pro:
        Many who marry have a better quality of life, having already learned from premarital relationships. If morality is about having a life of goodness, and premarital sex can contribute to that, it cannot be considered immoral.
