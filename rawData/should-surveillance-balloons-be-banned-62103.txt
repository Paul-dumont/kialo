Discussion Name:
Should surveillance balloons be banned?

1. Thesis:
Surveillance balloons should be banned.

  1.1. Con:
  Surveillance balloons are important for scientific research.

    1.1.1. Con:
    Many countries are developing [1] tools for satellites, such as the UK's new millimetre wave technology, that have the potential to replace surveillance balloons as tools for scientific research.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      Millimeter wave technology can expand the resolution of satellite images [2], so balloons may not be needed for high-resolution imaging.

        1.1.1.1.1. Con:
        Comparable technology can be applied in balloons as well; balloons will always have a greater capacity for high-resolution images than satellites because they are significantly closer to the Earth [3].

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      Balloons will continue to be preferred over satellites for a number of applications, such as magnetosphere studies, because satellites move too fast [4] to study some phenomena.

      1.1.1.3. Con:
      Surveillance balloons can serve some of the same purposes as satellites at a fraction of the cost [5].

        1.1.1.3.1. Pro:
        The launch of a single satellite can cost up to $300 million [6].

        1.1.1.3.2. Pro:
        A simple high-altitude balloon can be launched for as little as a few hundred dollars [7].

        1.1.1.3.3. Con:
        Satellites can remain in orbit for up to 15 years [8], so they offer an advantage for long-term applications.

    1.1.2. Con:
    Scientific advances should not come at the expense of potential danger to the public.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      In the same way that we would not allow scientists to unleash a potentially deadly chemical in a populated area in order to test its effects, we should not allow scientists to utilise dangerous surveillance technology.

        1.1.2.1.1. Con:
        The effects of surveillance balloons are not inherently harmful in the way that deadly chemicals are. Many of their uses are innocuous.

        1.1.2.1.2. Pro:
        It is irresponsible to deploy potentially harmful technology without first investigating and understanding its downstream effects.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      The relative benefits and risks of research balloons are difficult to quantify; balloon experiments have made significant contributions [9] to our understanding of high-altitude physiology, space radiation, atmosphere, and climate.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Surveillance balloons can be used for scientific research, such as weather monitoring.

      1.1.3.1. Con:
      There are significant differences [10] between surveillance balloons and other forms of high-altitude research balloons, such as weather balloons.

        1.1.3.1.1. Pro:
        It would be possible to ban high-altitude balloons used only for surveillance while allowing the continued operation of research balloons.

        1.1.3.1.2. Con:
        The difference between many research balloons and surveillance balloons is only in their intended use. Imaging and communication technology is common to both types of balloons, and can easily be used for either purpose.

        1.1.3.1.3. Pro:
        Weather balloons are small, with a volume of 100 cubic feet [11], and carry relatively simple devices [12] to measure atmospheric conditions; surveillance balloons have a volume of 64,000 to 400,000 cubic feet and carry advanced equipment with military capabilities [13].

      1.1.3.2. Pro:
      The World Meteorological Association maintains the Global Observing System [14], which utilizes over 1,000 balloons to provide all nations with useful information about weather and climate.

        1.1.3.2.1. Pro:
        The data gathered by weather balloons allows for more accurate climate modelling [15], which is essential to the continued well-being of humanity.

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      Many research universities use surveillance balloons to conduct studies on climate change (p. 1 [16]).

      1.1.3.4. Pro:
      Surveillance balloons could contribute real-time disease outbreak data, allowing scientists and doctors to respond to epidemics more quickly and efficiently.

        1.1.3.4.1. Con:
        Personal health records are private information and subject to a number of privacy laws, such as HIPAA [17].

          1.1.3.4.1.1. Con:
          Patient information can be used by authorized entities when it is sufficiently anonymized and serves a legitimate public health or research purpose [18].

        1.1.3.4.2. Pro:
        Geospatial outbreak mapping [19] can help scientists to better understand the mechanisms and patterns of disease spread.

      1.1.3.5. Con:
      Countries have taken advantage of the widespread existence and tolerance of science and weather balloons as cover for intrusive foreign surveillance [20] programs.

    1.1.4. Con:
    Banning them makes it easier to detect and track more interesting or genuine UFOs [21].

    1.1.5. Pro:
    Surveillance balloons are used to gain an unobstructed view of space [22] and to test other high-altitude equipment.

      1.1.5.1. Pro:
      Balloons offer a much better view of space than telescopes [23] on the ground due to the lack of dense atmosphere at high altitudes, and they cost less than orbital telescopes.

        1.1.5.1.1. Pro:
        NASA's ASTHROS [24] project uses a large high-altitude balloon, mirror, and telescope to examine stellar feedback in wavelengths that cannot penetrate Earth's atmosphere.

      1.1.5.2. Pro:
      Balloons have been used extensively to develop and test the electronics used in cubesats and nanosats [25] before deploying them on space missions (p. 2 [26]).

        1.1.5.2.1. Pro:
        Cubesats and nanosats encourage innovation [27] and enable thousands of amateurs and independent investigators to perform space research.

      1.1.5.3. Con:
      Space exploration should not be a priority because it does not offer appreciable benefits [28] to humanity.

  1.2. Con:
  Surveillance balloons are useful for disaster response.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Surveillance balloons can be used to provide internet access to remote areas that are otherwise difficult to reach, which can be useful for emergencies.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      Projects such as Google's Loon [29] aim to provide internet connectivity to areas with poor connectivity.

        1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
        Such technology could also be deployed as part of a disaster response, for example, if internet connection went down because of a storm.

        1.2.1.1.2. Con:
        Loon was ultimately discontinued [30] by Google for being too costly.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      Surveillance balloons may not be reliable or sustainable for providing internet access to remote areas due to susceptibility to weather and the need for constant maintenance.

      1.2.1.3. Con:
      Providing internet access may not be the most pressing need in emergency situations, such as natural disasters or conflicts.

        1.2.1.3.1. Con:
        Internet access is considered an essential resource in emergency situations as it enables the coordination of responders (p. 1 [31]).

    1.2.2. Con:
    Drones [32] can do many of the same things that balloons do in terms of disaster relief.

      1.2.2.1. Pro:
      Drones are significantly more maneuverable [33] than balloons.

        1.2.2.1.1. Con:
        Chinese-made spy balloons have been reported to score highly [34] on maneuverability.

        1.2.2.1.2. Pro:
        Drones can easily fly in and around damaged structures [35], delivering supplies, locating survivors, and detecting gas leaks and other hazards.

      1.2.2.2. Con:
      Drones can be very costly [36].

        1.2.2.2.1. Con:
        Drones that are normally used for disaster mapping and response are not as costly [37] as large drones.

        1.2.2.2.2. Pro:
        The software and hardware needed to analyze drone-collected data can also be costly [38].

      1.2.2.3. Con:
      The negative reputation of drones could hinder their effective use in disaster relief efforts.

        1.2.2.3.1. Pro:
        The acceptance of disaster-relief drones in conflict zones may be low [39] due to their previous and current active use in conflicts.

          1.2.2.3.1.1. Pro:
          Many parts of the world strongly associate drones with military surveillance and attacks from weaponized unmanned combat aerial vehicles (UCAV [40]).

        1.2.2.3.2. Con:
        Combat drones are visibly distinguishable from smaller delivery drones, making it unlikely for people to confuse the two.

        1.2.2.3.3. Con:
        The same could be said of national military forces, yet many armies contribute significantly to disaster relief [41] around the world.

      1.2.2.4. Pro:
      Drone technology is more advanced [42] than balloon technology.

        1.2.2.4.1. Pro:
        Recent drone prototypes [43] exhibit significant advances in both autonomous AI navigation and increased carrying capacity.

        1.2.2.4.2. Con:
        Some balloon technologies, such as high-altitude balloons, have advanced significantly in recent years and are being used for a variety of applications.

          1.2.2.4.2.1. Pro:
          Modern surveillance balloons have integrated artificial intelligence (AI) [44] to adjust altitude in response to changes in weather conditions.

          1.2.2.4.2.2. Con:
          The market for drone technology is projected to continue expanding rapidly [45], which is likely to drive further innovation.

      1.2.2.5. Con:
      Using balloons for high-altitude and stationary tasks would free up rescue drones to perform tasks for which they are better suited.

    1.2.3. Pro:
    Surveillance balloons have been used to rescue people trapped beneath rubble [46] following a disaster by tracing their cell phones.

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      Analytical mapping technology [47] can do the same thing, without the use of a balloon.

      1.2.3.2. Pro:
      Larger balloons can also be used for emergency evacuations [48] of people from disaster-stricken areas.

        1.2.3.2.1. Con:
        The balloons typically used for surveillance are ill suited for evacuating humans as they are generally operate at altitudes [49] where humans cannot survive [50] without significant support systems.

        1.2.3.2.2. Con:
        Surveillance balloons cannot descend quickly and generally initiate free fall then deploy a parachute [51] in order to land.

      1.2.3.3. Con:
      Surveillance balloons cannot be deployed quickly, and timely deployment is crucial during disaster rescue.

        1.2.3.3.1. Pro:
        It normally takes more than two hours [52] for stratospheric balloons, such as surveillance balloons, to ascend to operational altitude.

        1.2.3.3.2. Con:
        Balloons do not [53] need to be fully inflated to commence their flight.

        1.2.3.3.3. Con:
        A number of balloon capabilities remain useful during the longer relief and recovery phases [54] of natural disaster response.

        1.2.3.3.4. Pro:
        Search and rescue operations must act extraordinarily quickly in the wake of a natural disaster in order to save as many lives as possible; in particular, the first 72 hours after a disaster are crucial (p. 784 [55]).

          1.2.3.3.4.1. Con:
          Surveillance balloons can easily be deployed and provide essential support during the first 72 hours after a disaster.

      1.2.3.4. Pro:
      Surveillance balloons can make use of a wide range of technology, such as thermal imaging [56], that is useful in locating disaster survivors.

    1.2.4. Con:
    It may be impractical to use surveillance balloons for disaster relief.

      1.2.4.1. Con:
      During Hurricane Ian, which struck Florida in 2022, the US National Weather Service launched balloons [57] every six hours in order to capture data on the storm's wind speed and direction.

        1.2.4.1.1. Pro:
        Weather balloons across the US were launched during the storm, gathering atmospheric data to aid in the development of more accurate models [58].

        1.2.4.1.2. Con:
        Balloons are of limited value in the immediate area of a storm because they are easily blown off course by high winds.

          1.2.4.1.2.1. Con:
          Surveillance balloons can make use of weather patterns and change altitude [59] to find a current that will take them in the desired direction.

          1.2.4.1.2.2. Con:
          Surveillance balloons can also be fitted with propellers [60] to enable more precise steering.

          1.2.4.1.2.3. Pro:
          Wind speed [61] in the stratosphere can exceed 275 miles per hour.

      1.2.4.2. Pro:
      Weather conditions can affect the ability of surveillance balloons to function properly, making them unreliable in times of disaster.

      1.2.4.3. Pro:
      Surveillance balloons are not fast-moving [62].

        1.2.4.3.1. Pro:
        The speed at which surveillance balloons move depends on the wind speed and direction [63], and they are typically used for stationary applications.

      1.2.4.4. Con:
      While surveillance balloons are not well suited for all relief tasks, they offer a number of useful capabilities.

  1.3. Pro:
  Surveillance balloons violate an individual citizen's fundamental rights.

    1.3.1. Con:
    Surveillance balloons can also be used to protect individual citizens' fundamental rights, for example, protecting their right to life [64] by stopping crime.

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      Surveillance balloons can be used to monitor problem areas [65], helping police to combat violent crime.

      1.3.1.2. Con:
      Crime rates are linked to economic disparity (p. 14 [66]); thus, monitoring areas with high crime would unfairly infringe on the privacy of the economically disadvantaged.

    1.3.2. Pro:
    Surveillance balloons could be used by authoritarian governments to spy on their own citizens.

      1.3.2.1. Con:
      Many modern states regularly spy on their own citizens for law enforcement purposes [67]; this is not the exclusive province of authoritarian states.

      1.3.2.2. Con:
      Surveillance balloons do not equip states with any additional surveillance capabilities beyond other methods of spying.

        1.3.2.2.1. Pro:
        Spy satellites [68] currently orbiting the earth enable authoritarian regimes to spy on its people without the need for surveillance balloons.

        1.3.2.2.2. Pro:
        Authoritarian regimes have numerous other methods to surveil their population, such as monitoring the internet [69]. It is unclear that a ban on balloons would do much to impact their surveillance capabilities.

          1.3.2.2.2.1. Pro:
          Authoritarian states often encourage [70] neighbours to spy on each other.

        1.3.2.2.3. Con:
        The NSA has employed a blimp known as the "Hover Hammer [71]" fitted with equipment capable of intercepting wireless data transmissions and calls.

      1.3.2.3. Pro:
      China [72] uses balloons equipped with high-definition and infrared cameras in order to monitor public gatherings.

      1.3.2.4. Pro:
      The Republic of Korea recently intercepted [73] a spy balloon belonging to the Democratic People's Republic of Korea (DPRK) that had drifted into their airspace, suggesting that the DPRK may be utilising surveillance balloons domestically.

    1.3.3. Con:
    While surveillance balloons can be used in ways that violate citizen rights, this is a result of using them inappropriately rather than an inherent effect of the balloons themselves.

      1.3.3.1. Pro:
      Many common things, such as cars and knives, can be used in an inappropriate way that results in harm, but this is not a sufficient reason for banning them.

      1.3.3.2. Con:
      Surveillance, and particularly the far-reaching and technologically advanced surveillance of which balloons are capable, carries inherent risks to society and others that are disproportionate to the benefits available to most users.

    1.3.4. Pro:
    Surveillance balloons can invade a person's right to privacy.

      1.3.4.1. Pro:
      Surveillance balloons can be equipped with high-resolution cameras that can capture images and videos of people and activities on the ground.

        1.3.4.1.1. Pro:
        Some surveillance balloons are already capable of producing images with a resolution of 15 cm from high altitude [74], and manufacturers expect to further improve these capabilities.

        1.3.4.1.2. Con:
        Some online services, such as Google Maps [75], already routinely collect images of homes and passersby using vans. Images taken from high altitude are no more intrusive than these images.

      1.3.4.2. Pro:
      Surveillance balloons can be used to intercept private correspondence and communications [76].

      1.3.4.3. Pro:
      Privacy is a fundamentally important [77] right because it allows human beings to create boundaries against unwanted intrusion into our lives, allowing us a modicum of control over the way we are perceived by others and thus the capacity to create our own identities. Surveillance balloons therefore intrude on one's ability to construct one's identity in the way they see fit.

        1.3.4.3.1. Pro:
        The right to privacy is included in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (Article 12) and the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (Article 17) (p. 4 [78]).

        1.3.4.3.2. Con:
        The government intrudes on this right all the time. The question is whether the circumstances of the intrusion are legitimate, and in some cases surveillance balloons' usage can be.

      1.3.4.4. Pro:
      Surveillance is only ethical [79] if there exists reasonable, publicly available accountability mechanisms for those approving the surveillance.

        1.3.4.4.1. Pro:
        Most government surveillance, including the use of surveillance balloons, is very opaque and unaccountable.

        1.3.4.4.2. Con:
        National security interests [80] preclude the possibility of fully transparent government surveillance programs.

        1.3.4.4.3. Pro:
        US courts have in recent years ruled [81] in favour of the government in freedom of information cases related to state surveillance, citing state secrets privilege to deny the release of information. It is likely they would do the same for information related to surveillance balloon use.

      1.3.4.5. Con:
      Using a surveillance balloon to monitor public space is ethically no different from a CCTV camera to do the same, which is ethically no different [82] than someone observing a public street from the window of an apartment.

        1.3.4.5.1. Con:
        A CCTV camera is one part of a wider surveillance infrastructure, which is what makes it ethically distinct from an individual observer.

        1.3.4.5.2. Pro:
        It is not an invasion of privacy to monitor or film things that occur in outdoor, public spaces [83].

          1.3.4.5.2.1. Con:
          Surveillance balloons can also monitor private spaces, such as an individual's private property.

      1.3.4.6. Con:
      The right to privacy must be balanced with other competing rights, such as the right to safety.

        1.3.4.6.1. Pro:
        Because surveillance balloons can be used to prevent things like crime or terrorism, we ought not ban them over concerns about the right to privacy.

        1.3.4.6.2. Con:
        Privacy forms the basis of many other freedoms [84]; free society is impossible without adequate protection of privacy.

  1.4. Con:
  There is no need to ban surveillance balloons, as they can be sufficiently controlled by regulation.

    1.4.1. Pro:
    The US government has recently called for stricter regulations [85] on unpiloted balloon flights.

      1.4.1.1. Pro:
      The near space [86] region, where surveillance balloons operate, is poorly defined in international law and lacks a comprehensive legal framework.

        1.4.1.1.1. Con:
        Outer space, and therefore the point at which a nation's airspace ends, is demarcated by the Kármán Line [87].

        1.4.1.1.2. Pro:
        Increased activity in the upper atmosphere has raised new jurisdictional questions regarding access, security, and environmental concerns (p. 2 [88]).

      1.4.1.2. Con:
      Such calls for regulation have only appeared in connection with foreign balloons; the US government has not called for stricter regulations on its own surveillance balloons.

        1.4.1.2.1. Con:
        Any regulation that is globally agreed upon would also apply to the US.

        1.4.1.2.2. Pro:
        Surveillance balloons from foreign states are more likely to pose a security threat since military-grade surveillance equipment is prohibitively expensive for most individual operators.

      1.4.1.3. Pro:
      The US government has announced the establishment of an interagency team to study [89] the security risks posed by unidentified, unmanned aerial objects and to suggest policy.

        1.4.1.3.1. Con:
        Such investigation may yet result in a recommended full or partial ban on surveillance balloons.

        1.4.1.3.2. Pro:
        The origins of several recent high-altitude objects over the US remain unknown [90].

      1.4.1.4. Con:
      A ban is more strict than any other form of regulation.

    1.4.2. Con:
    In general, enforcing a regulation on something costs the state more than an outright ban.

      1.4.2.1. Pro:
      Regulating high-altitude balloons would require government resources devoted to licensing operators.

        1.4.2.1.1. Con:
        The cost of licenses would offset the cost of such administration.

      1.4.2.2. Pro:
      Regulating surveillance balloons would require personnel and procedures for registering balloons and scheduling and logging flights.

        1.4.2.2.1. Con:
        The Federal Aerospace Administration (FAA) already requires the registration [91] of weather and research balloons that reach the altitudes used by commercial aircraft.

          1.4.2.2.1.1. Pro:
          FAA regulations [92] require flight planning, monitoring, and regular reporting of unpiloted balloons.

      1.4.2.3. Con:
      Banning surveillance balloons would entail significant additional law enforcement spending to identify surveillance balloons among all the other balloons in use and to apprehend and prosecute those who break the ban.

        1.4.2.3.1. Pro:
        Police departments currently lack the technology to locate high-altitude balloons of any type, much less identify which balloons are being used for surveillance purposes.

          1.4.2.3.1.1. Pro:
          Surveillance balloons are difficult to detect [93].

            1.4.2.3.1.1.1. Pro:
            US military radar systems have a hard time detecting [94] surveillance balloons, as they are not like the hot, hard, and fast objects the systems typically look to detect.

            1.4.2.3.1.1.2. Con:
            Radar systems can easily spot most surveillance balloons [95], but detecting them has not been a priority.

    1.4.3. Con:
    Effective regulation of surveillance balloons is unlikely to be feasible.

      1.4.3.1. Pro:
      Given the great number of balloons already used for other purposes [96], identifying which balloons are intended for surveillance would be near impossible.

        1.4.3.1.1. Pro:
        A great deal of the technology used in surveillance balloons can equally serve other purposes [97].

      1.4.3.2. Con:
      The purchase of materials used to construct surveillance balloons could be regulated.

        1.4.3.2.1. Con:
        It is easy for civilians to construct spy balloons [98] with readily available materials, making regulation infeasible.

        1.4.3.2.2. Pro:
        Surveillance equipment capable of monitoring the surface of the Earth from high altitude is expensive, and purchases could be limited to those with legitimate need.

      1.4.3.3. Con:
      Limitations can be placed on surveillance technology itself, such as enforcing a limitation of the maximum resolution [99] of cameras.

        1.4.3.3.1. Con:
        Regulators often struggle to keep up with the increasing pace of technological innovation (pp. 563, 567–568 [100]).

      1.4.3.4. Pro:
      Although surveillance balloons do show up using radar, distinguishing between the vast number of smaller objects [101] shown by such scanning is essentially impossible.

        1.4.3.4.1. Pro:
        Smaller surveillance balloons are indistinguishable on radar from birds and other airborne objects.

    1.4.4. Con:
    The surveillance balloons most likely to pose a threat to the privacy of citizens are those operated by the government, which is unlikely to effectively monitor and regulate itself.

      1.4.4.1. Pro:
      Domestic surveillance programs suffer from a chronic lack of oversight [102].

        1.4.4.1.1. Pro:
        Intelligence agencies cannot effectively oversee themselves [103].

      1.4.4.2. Con:
      Surveillance performed by large data companies, such as Google, would have great potential for abuse and infringement of privacy.

      1.4.4.3. Pro:
      -> See 1.3.4.4.3.

    1.4.5. Pro:
    There is insufficient justification for restricting the freedom of citizens by a ban on surveillance balloons since operating a surveillance balloon does not inherently infringe on the rights of others.

      1.4.5.1. Pro:
      Freedoms and rights should only be curtailed in cases where an act actually limits the rights of another or otherwise harms them.

        1.4.5.1.1. Pro:
        While it is possible to use surveillance balloons in a harmful way, their use is not inherently harmful.

        1.4.5.1.2. Con:
        It is better to prevent harm in the first place than to address it after it has already occurred.

      1.4.5.2. Con:
      The potential for surveillance balloons to infringe on individual rights to privacy and personal safety are great enough to warrant a ban.

    1.4.6. Pro:
    Foreign unauthorized surveillance balloons are already banned [104]. In the U.S., Article 8 of the Chicago Convention on International Civil Aviation states that [105] aircraft, at least human-made aircraft, flown without pilots must obtain permission from the country below and must be controlled.

    1.4.7. Pro:
    A ban on surveillance balloons would likely negatively impact many balloon hobbyists [106], who do not deserve to see their hobby be banned.

      1.4.7.1. Con:
      Balloon hobbyists are not adequately able to protect others from injury if a high-altitude balloon fails or crashes.

        1.4.7.1.1. Con:
        Most amateur hobbyists design [107] balloons with safety features in mind, in order to mitigate risk to people on the ground.

        1.4.7.1.2. Pro:
        Amateur hobbyist balloons are unlikely to have essential safety features due to their inexpensive nature [108].

      1.4.7.2. Con:
      Balloon hobbyists should not be entrusted with the extensive surveillance capabilities of a high-altitude surveillance balloon.

      1.4.7.3. Pro:
      Many hobbyists and amateur scientists participate in amateur radio high-altitude ballooning (ARHAB [109]), creating spacecraft models and using high-altitude balloons to test them in conditions similar to space.

      1.4.7.4. Pro:
      Amateur radio operators [110] often run experiments using high-altitude balloons.

      1.4.7.5. Pro:
      The SpaceGrant program operates BalloonSat [111] projects that allow engineering students to test their design skills using high-altitude balloon launches.

        1.4.7.5.1. Pro:
        Such programs offer an essential learning opportunity for students entering aerospace engineering.

        1.4.7.5.2. Con:
        A ban on surveillance balloons is likely to apply to foreign balloons, rather than local balloon flights that have obtained appropriate permissions to fly.

  1.5. Pro:
  Surveillance balloons are a health and safety threat.

    1.5.1. Con:
    Surveillance balloons are operated safely, following strict regulations and guidelines [112] to ensure public safety.

      1.5.1.1. Pro:
      In early 2023, US senators introduced legislation [113] which would mandate tracking systems on high-altitude balloons.

        1.5.1.1.1. Pro:
        Such legislation, if implemented, would help the military differentiate between friendly assets, civilian balloons, and potential threats.

      1.5.1.2. Pro:
      Surveillance balloons, like other aircraft, are subject to international aviation law. [114]

      1.5.1.3. Con:
      While professional operators may follow guidelines and regulations, amateur operators remain subject to the limitations of inadequate knowledge and experience.

        1.5.1.3.1. Con:
        The expertise of amateur balloon operators is expected to improve over time.

        1.5.1.3.2. Con:
        Manned balloon pilots must obtain a flying license [115] from the FAA prior to flying.

        1.5.1.3.3. Con:
        Unmanned balloon operators are legally obligated [116] to track and report the location of their balloons every two hours.

        1.5.1.3.4. Pro:
        Amateur balloon operators may lack formal training and be unaware of safety regulations and guidelines, unlike their professional counterparts.

        1.5.1.3.5. Pro:
        Amateurs' limited understanding of the potential consequences of their actions may lead them to take risks and push the limits of their equipment, thereby increasing the likelihood of accidents or incidents.

    1.5.2. Con:
    Traditional aerial surveillance methods, such as helicopters or drones, pose a greater safety risk.

      1.5.2.1. Pro:
      A number of people have been injured [117] by drones.

        1.5.2.1.1. Pro:
        The higher incidence of civilian access and demand [118] for drones increases the probability of drone-related injuries.

        1.5.2.1.2. Con:
        Drones used by the public are subject to strict regulations [119] aimed at minimizing the occurrence of injuries.

      1.5.2.2. Pro:
      Helicopters are associated with a much higher crash rate [120] than other types of aircraft.

        1.5.2.2.1. Pro:
        There is evidence to suggest helicopter fatalities are increasing [121] each year.

        1.5.2.2.2. Con:
        Despite being more risky than commercial airlines, helicopter flights are not overly dangerous; for example, they are substantially safer than driving a car [122].

      1.5.2.3. Con:
      As surveillance balloons become more widespread, it is likely the risks associated with them will also increase in prevalence.

      1.5.2.4. Con:
      Drones and helicopters are more reliable than balloons as they are not weather dependent and can safely navigate away from populated areas during emergencies.

    1.5.3. Pro:
    Surveillance balloons can contribute to pollution [123], as they contain non-biodegradable components, such as latex, lithium batteries, and solar panels, that can have harmful effects on the environment and wildlife.

      1.5.3.1. Pro:
      Lithium batteries contain toxic metals [124], such as cobalt, which can leach out of landfills and contaminate water supplies.

        1.5.3.1.1. Pro:
        In parts of Argentina, locals claim [125] that lithium operations have contaminated streams used by both animals and humans.

        1.5.3.1.2. Con:
        Lithium batteries are more environmentally friendly [126] than other types of batteries or fossil fuels.

      1.5.3.2. Pro:
