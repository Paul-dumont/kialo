      The quality of user experience on Parler is not dependent on the size of the userbase.

        It is unlikely that the average user would directly interact with millions of other users on the platform. Even if a user interacts with a hundred users a day and uses social media each day, this would still not amount to 20 million users (the total number of Parler users).         The number of users is important for users with commercial interests on the platform.

        1.1.2.3.3. Con:
        Politicians and thought leaders on the platform benefit from a larger userbase.

        1.1.2.3.4. Pro:
        If userbase was a crucial factor, Twitter and Facebook would have failed as they initially had significantly less users than the social media platforms that existed before them.

      1.1.2.4. Pro:
      Parler has seen a decrease [16] in sign-ups and traffic in recent months.

        1.1.2.4.1. Pro:

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      The existence of Parler ensures political representation on social media.

        1.1.3.2.1. Pro:

        1.1.3.2.2. Pro:
        The existence of platforms such as Parler ensure that those on the right are given a platform to share their views on social media.

        1.1.3.2.3. Con:

      Parler does not use algorithms [28] to make recommendations to users.

      Parler's user interface is almost indistinguishable [29] from Twitter's.

          X's growth rate is expected to decline [79] over time.

        1.3.2.2.2. Con:
        The content on X is heavily commercialized and popularity is relatively dependent [80] on paid boosting, paid verification, and proactive marketing, degrading the quality of content on the platform according to the concept of 'enshittification [81]'.

        1.3.2.2.3. Con:
        Unlike on Kialo, a substantial fraction [82] of those users largely or only create low-quality content such as deliberate disinformation [83] or are [84] bots [85].

        1.3.2.2.4. Pro:
        X's large user-base as well as its existence for 5 years longer [86] than Kialo means X is a larger repository of information.

        X's lack of effective content moderation means a large percentage [87] of its content could be misinformation.

          X introduced Community Notes [88] that enables users to collectively attach a note to misinformation that is popular and notify people who liked the tweet earlier.

          1.3.2.2.5.2. Con:
          On Kialo, claims sometimes make up false claims without a source or with a flawed source only to be corrected in the claims below if it all which readers may not read.

          On Kialo, claims have to pass the reviewers of a debate [89] and subsequently can be further scrutinized by reviewed comments.

          Kialo aims to be a hub for civilized debate where shouting and rudeness aren't allowed and reports [90] indicate [91] it has been successful in these goals.

      Discussion on Kialo is better structured and organized than on X.

          1.3.2.3.1.1. Pro:
          Kialo is better for debate because unlike on Twitter, debates are structured (organized into branches, impact voting, per claim discussion & revision, etc), not repetitive (usually one debate per question and the ability to link/mirror branches), one can build up on earlier debates [92] (instead of starting from scratch or being limited in debate size/complexity/depth), and so on.

        A Wikidata item was created for Kial [93]o argument maps that, if they reached sufficient quality, are then attached to Wikidata items of the discussed subject integrating the structured maps into the larger structured Web [94] where Wikidata [95] is the biggest platform [96].

        1.3.2.3.3. Con:
        Many argument maps on Kialo have sub-debates or interesting major aspects that are not immediately apparent from just the debate question and thus often remain unused and undiscovered.

          1.3.2.3.3.1. Pro:
          While Kialo has functionalities that allow for a modular approach where sub-debates can be separate debates such as linking other debates, these are rarely used.

          1.3.2.3.3.2. Pro:
          The sub-debates can not be specified and marked as a "sub-debate" and are neither simple to find nor well-discoverable when browsing the site.

          1.3.2.3.3.3. Pro:
          There could be branches that are broader than about the narrow subject. For example, a debate only about veganism [97] could have a large branch about enviro [98]–health [99] benefits of reducing meat consumption which is not specific to veganism.

          1.3.2.3.3.4. Pro:
          There can be branches about broader philosophical frameworks and aspects because they are relevant to some claims in a debate or are an aspect to most of the debate. For example, a debate about the value of human life vs animal life [100] may also extensively debate how humans are different from other animals and what is valued in humans.

        1.3.2.3.4. Con:
        There are no clear indicators of debate quality regarding depth, sourcing, completeness, neutrality, outdatedness, accuracy, and so on.

          1.3.2.3.4.1. Pro:

          1.3.2.3.6.1. Con:
          Debates that have been stale for a while are not very discoverable. The personalized lists like the "Recommended" tab are not extensively used, there is no feed view of new arguments across debates, and debates quickly fall off from the four Explore tabs.
