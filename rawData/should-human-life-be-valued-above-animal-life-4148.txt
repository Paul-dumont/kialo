Discussion Name:
Should Human Life Be Valued Above Animal Life?

1. Thesis:
Human life should be valued above animal life in the case that only one can be preserved.

  1.1. Pro:
  Humans are more intelligent than animals. A value system according to brain complexity and social intelligence is the most reasonable system of valuation we have.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Some forms of uniquenesses are more valuable than others. In the case of human intelligence being magnitudes greater than animal intelligence, one may suggest that this uniqueness is far superior to the unique ability of electric eels to discharge electricity, for example.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      The value or superiority of an adaptation depends upon one's situation and environment.

        1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
        If a significant gamma-ray burst and stripped off parts of the atmosphere, tardigrades would survive, while [1] humans would not.

        1.1.1.1.2. Pro:
        There are many things animals can do that humans can't, like seeing ultraviolet light, detecting electromagnetic field, sensing smells and sounds from very large distances and more.

          1.1.1.1.2.1. Con:
          As the original claim argued, such capabilities are not as valuable as intelligence or cognitive depth.

        1.1.1.1.3. Con:
        Relatively advanced cognitive abilities or complex abstract thinking and intelligence are usually advantageous in all situations and environments in terms of surviving and advancing since they allow for more flexibility and intelligent behavior. Human cognitive abilities were essential for [2] human adaptation and survival.

        1.1.1.1.4. Con:
        It is a false assumption that the superiority of an adaptation depends on its evolutionary merit as in the evolution of the fittest. The value and superiority also depends on other, partly abstract, considerations.

    1.1.2. Con:
    Intelligence is not the only metric to measure superiority on Earth, which benefits from the connected behaviors/actions of all species working in tandem with each other.

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      The fact that humans are by far the most dominant species on Earth indicates that intelligence is the parameter most important to determining superiority.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      If we value life based on intelligence, this would mean bacteria is unimportant. Yet, without the bacteria living inside us [3], humans would die.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        Bacteria would still be valued as a result of their value to humans and other animals.

      1.1.2.3. Pro:
      What counts as intelligence is a highly disputed concept.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Assessing the value of different things is a uniquely human capability, therefore the human point of view determines what is valuable and should naturally preserve humanity.

      1.1.3.1. Con:
      Not all sentient humans are the arbitrators of value, yet all sentient humans matter morally. So, one can matter morally without passing judgement on other species.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      If value is a human construct, humans will naturally value themselves to the exclusion of others, even if that is not the correct judgement.

        1.1.3.2.1. Con:
        If value is a human construct, then there cannot be a criterion of value that transcends humans.

        1.1.3.2.2. Con:
        The claim does not argue value(s) are a human construct, it argues that the practice of assessing the value of different things seems to be uniquely human.

      1.1.3.3. Con:
      "Assessing value" is an inadequate description of the uniquely capability of humans. A better description would be that humans recognize moral responsibility.

      1.1.3.4. Con:
      The fact that the human point of view determines what is valuable does not imply that it should naturally prioritize humanity. This is anthropocentrism [4].

        1.1.3.4.1. Pro:
        Morality should be assessed from an universal perspective, rather than an anthropocentric one.

          1.1.3.4.1.1. Con:
          A universal conception of morality is difficult to ascertain, given our inability to truly understand the perspectives of flora, fauna, and inanimate objects.

    1.1.4. Pro:
    Sentiocentrism [5] is a good moral system: when forced to choose, it is better to prefer the more intelligent creature.

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      There are human beings who are less intelligent. However, it would be wrong to see them as less valued or worthy than others. Animals should be no different.

        1.1.4.1.1. Pro:
        Following this reasoning, it would be better to eat certain disabled humans or babies, than certain animals. From this, it is clear that value should not be dependent on intelligence or brain complexity.

          1.1.4.1.1.1. Con:
          Humans feel a strong sense of empathy for other humans, especially those close to them. Eating a baby causes harm to the mother and father, and to their family, friends and neighbours. Eating a chimpanzee, by contrast, does not have the same impact.

            1.1.4.1.1.1.1. Con:
            The parent claim is not advocating for eating babies. The parent claim is pointing out that the reasoning in the claim above it is faulty.

            1.1.4.1.1.1.2. Con:
            This does not consider situations where the baby's parents are not alive anymore or have lost all contact to the baby earlier. More importantly, that it can't have a similar impact for chimpanzees is not backed up, especially when such cases are considered.

          1.1.4.1.1.2. Con:
          It would not be better to eat babies than certain animals even given that value is dependant on intelligence, because we have ethical rules, social conventions, and legal laws against murder and cannibalism. Making an exception to those rules for one group that are claimed not to count would weaken them, so one should avoid doing so.

            1.1.4.1.1.2.1. Con:
            -> See 1.1.4.1.1.1.1.

          1.1.4.1.1.3. Con:
          Babies have the potential to become much more conscious, mentally complex and intelligent. That likelihood and potential is a key element that needs to be considered.

        1.1.4.1.2. Con:
        The term 'mentalism' covers the exact sort of prejudice where humans with lower IQs are deemed less worthy by society and discriminated against. Sometimes humans are deemed less valued or worthy by society.

          1.1.4.1.2.1. Pro:
          IQ scores are not [6] good measures [7] of intelligence [8] or qualities of cognitive functioning [9].

          1.1.4.1.2.2. Con:
          Society rewards intelligent people by having them do more important jobs and giving them higher salaries, but it doesn't value their lives more than other human beings.

            1.1.4.1.2.2.1. Pro:
            For example, it is not more immoral to kill a very intelligent person than it is to kill a less intelligent person. No legal system in the world gives a higher prison sentence to someone who kills highly intelligent people.

            1.1.4.1.2.2.2. Con:
            It is, however, generally considered less immoral to kill an animal than it is to kill a human. Hunting is legal in many places.

        1.1.4.1.3. Con:
        Some may indeed consider it the more ethical choice to save people who do not suffer from certain disabilities if they were forced to choose since for instance more life with good quality in terms of DALYs [10] would be saved. Nevertheless, that does not mean that it would then also be more ethical to save certain animals over disabled humans if one was forced to choose.

        1.1.4.1.4. Con:
        This is merely a statement but not an argument – it does not explain why that would be wrong when in which ways but only claims that "would be wrong".

        1.1.4.1.5. Con:
        Even the least intelligent functional humans are far smarter than any other animal. It would be reasonable to value humans above animals on these grounds, even less intelligent humans.

    1.1.5. Pro:
    Nowadays, established practice consists of giving a gradual range of rights to other animals, based on their emotional and intellectual skills, as well as closeness to humans. So, in evaluating issues like this, one should remember that it doesn't have to be same rights versus none.

    1.1.6. Pro:
    Humans are the only species that can appreciate the beauty of the entire biosphere.

      1.1.6.1. Pro:
      Our species is the only one capable of building technology beyond simple tools, and of understanding abstract concepts and planning non-instinctually (rather than instinctive pseudo-planning behaviors such as bears going into hibernation).

        1.1.6.1.1. Con:
        Animals may understand abstract concepts.

          1.1.6.1.1.1. Pro:
          Behavioral analysis has shown that non-human animals understand abstract concepts such as monetary value systems and make identical choices [11] (both rational and irrational) to humans in similar situations.

          1.1.6.1.1.2. Con:
          Animals can be trained to place value on things in their immediate environment, but they are incapable of understanding abstract concepts that do not pertain to their surroundings, as is the case with ethics and morality.

            1.1.6.1.1.2.1. Con:
            Humans also cannot understand abstract ideas without memory of objects and concepts that have been in their presence at some point (usually introduced by another human). This is a commonality of humans and non-human animals.

              1.1.6.1.1.2.1.1. Con:
              Humans have the unique ability to formulate entirely new ideas and abstract concepts—this ability lies at the bedrock of human civilization. Without it, we’d likely still be living in caves.

              1.1.6.1.1.2.1.2. Pro:
              The purpose of schools is to create an environment where humans pass on knowledge by introducing facts, concepts, and critical thinking skills so that young humans can use these pre-introduced ideas to form novel ideas of their own.

                1.1.6.1.1.2.1.2.1. Pro:
                Feral [12] human children demonstrate abstraction abilities on par with those of non-human animals. This is because they have not been enculturated/trained to by humans.

          1.1.6.1.1.3. Pro:
          Some non-human animals have been shown to have object permanence [13], and the social complexity to  differentiate ethnic groups by language [14], and remember their distant friends and relatives despite spending most of their lives apart [15]. We have no proof that they cannot maintain abstract ideas at a distance.

            1.1.6.1.1.3.1. Con:
            Yet these are all things that have existed in their immediate presence at some point. It’s a matter of memory, not comprehension of abstract ideas.

          1.1.6.1.1.4. Pro:
          Koko the gorilla makes art and has opinions [16] about her art.

        1.1.6.1.2. Pro:
        The existence of entire species across the globe is an abstract concept, and the value placed on humans or animals on this global scale is a uniquely human ability.

      1.1.6.2. Pro:
      Beauty is a purely subjective [17] human concept and may take many forms depending on social and cultural conditioning. Beauty does not exist outside of human experience.

      1.1.6.3. Con:
      Not all humans appreciate the beauty of the biosphere - some wilfully harm it.

        1.1.6.3.1. Con:
        Harm is a relative construct. Humans shape their environment to fit the human race better.

          1.1.6.3.1.1. Con:
          In the vast majority of cases, harm to living things is objectively demonstrable.

        1.1.6.3.2. Con:
        While some humans do harm the biosphere, this does not remove our ability as a species to appreciate the biosphere's beauty.
        
        One can both recognize beauty and be willing to destroy it for personal gain.

      1.1.6.4. Con:
      If we want to appreciate the beauty of the entire biosphere, we need to value animal life over human life sometimes in order to preserve that rich and diverse biosphere.

      1.1.6.5. Con:
      Humans cannot directly experience the extreme deep sea, which means their relative appreciation for that part of the biosphere is less than that of the animals who inhabit it.

        1.1.6.5.1. Con:
        The lack of direct experience does not represent an inherent inability to appreciate this part of the biosphere. In the future, technology might enable humans to travel the deep sea.

        1.1.6.5.2. Con:
        Humans do frequently write [18] and think about the beauty and otherness of the deep ocean, demonstrating their appreciation.

      1.1.6.6. Con:
      There is no evidence that animals do not appreciate the beauty of Earth. It is possible that they simply appreciate it in a different way than humans do.

        1.1.6.6.1. Pro:
        Animals have evolved [19] an appreciation for beauty by choosing an aesthetically pleasing mate as humans [20] have, but express it differently. The same is possible for appreciating the beauty for other things necessary for survival, such as the Earth.

    1.1.9. Con:

      1.1.9.1. Pro:

        1.1.9.1.1. Pro:

"Without the actions of pollinators, agricultural economies, our food supply, and surrounding landscapes would collapse.

Birds, bats, bees, butterflies, beetles, and other small mammals that pollinate plants are responsible for bringing us one out of every three bites of food. They also sustain our ecosystems and produce our natural resources by helping plants reproduce.

Pollinating animals travel from plant to plant carrying pollen on their bodies in a vital interaction that allows the transfer of genetic material critical to the reproductive system of most flowering plants – the very plants that

 bring us countless fruits, vegetables, and nuts,
 ½ of the world’s oils, fibers and raw materials;
 prevent soil erosion,
 and increase carbon sequestration"
