Discussion Name:
Is banning books inherently wrong?

1. Thesis:
Banning or removing access to books is inherently wrong.

  1.1. Con:
  If it is not wrong for editors to "kill" book projects before release for economical reasons. It is also not wrong for societies to ban released books for economical reasons.

    1.1.1. Con:
    Removing or not releasing books for reasons around economics or quality are not controversial, it is the banning of books for ideological reasons that is the issue. Forbidding the distribution of "Das Kapital" is not the same as refusing to publish a book bound in sharkskin and gold thread.

    1.1.2. Con:
    When an editor chooses to not publish a book, they do not prohibit or make it ilegal to access its content, whereas banning it does, therefore banning a book continues to be wrong.

    1.1.3. Con:
    There is no reason for a society to ever ban a book for economical reasons, as society as a whole cannot experience economic loss from a book that is the private property of the author or publisher.

    1.1.4. Pro:
    Publishers already function as an oligopoly. There are only a few large publishing companies. To get a book widely distributed or read, one must submit it to them and have it read and approved by them.

  1.2. Pro:
  Banning books is instrumentalising ignorance, and ignorance is inherently wrong.

    1.2.1. Con:
    Not necessarily so. Some books spread ignorance [1].

    1.2.2. Pro:
    Human intelligence and ability to learn and pass on information is key to what make us human and has allowed us to achieve the good things we have.

    1.2.3. Pro:
    Learning more allows us to make better decisions.

    1.2.4. Con:
    It cannot be said that *all* knowledge is right and useful, therefore, it cannot be said that *all* ignorance is wrong.

      1.2.4.1. Pro:
      Ignorance is useful in some instances. [2] Knowledge of an audience impacts performance (anxiety). When asked to describe why they liked a particular painting or image --- people, forced to analyze their reasons, later reported less satisfaction with the painting. [3] Some categories of knowledge come with trade-offs and ignorance can, in some cases, enhance experience.

        1.2.4.1.1. Pro:
        Were humans not ignorant, at least temporarily, of the amount of bacteria on the human body [4], human reproduction would've likely declined well below replacement rates many millennia ago leading to human extinction. Were one fully aware at all times of the flourishing, feasting and breeding creatures alive on (and in) a lover's flesh at all moments, one would certainly find the lover less attractive.

          1.2.4.1.1.1. Pro:
          One begins to speculate on how much of the lover is "The Lover" and how much is actually the microbiome. 2 to 6 pounds of microorganisms on a 200 pound adult. [5]

          1.2.4.1.1.2. Pro:
          "Knowledge" and "ignorance" are, to some extent, influenced by the amount and types of bacteria in a particular human [6] rather than the books we've read. Assuming behavior as one measurable side effect of knowledge, that is.

          1.2.4.1.1.3. Con:
          Doctors and microbiologists are aware of the amount of bacteria present in the human body, but still willing to reproduce.

          1.2.4.1.1.4. Con:
          Disgust is an evolutionary mechanism [7] that was used to protect us as we evolved. Humans have evolved past the point of needing to rely on it. We now know, intellectually, which things contain pathogens, and can discern under what circumstances something disgusting is safe to touch--which is a benefit. We pass on this knowledge through books. Banning books would once again make us ignorant and take away this benefit.

            1.2.4.1.1.4.1. Con:
            People can find the same information contained in books, through other mediums, such as the internet.

      1.2.4.2. Pro:
      Since full knowledge incorporates experience [8] in many cases, full knowledge of what it is like to kill someone is best left to the imagination and speculation in most cases ( defense of family, nation ...etc. might be defended as exceptions here ).

        1.2.4.2.1. Pro:
        The world would be better off if all humans were ignorant of how to construct WMDs.

      1.2.4.3. Pro:
      Books that contain knowledge regarding the creation of weapons and the perpetration of violence are not right or useful.

        1.2.4.3.1. Pro:
        Banning is inherently wrong for everything - because it will not change the demand, it just opens doors wide open for the black market.

          1.2.4.3.1.1. Pro:
          Banning something can have the unintended consequence of making it more appealing or more widely cherished. If the information is wrong, it is more effective to write and publish a succinct rebuttal to the book showing how it is wrong, and letting readers decide for themselves.

        1.2.4.3.2. Pro:
        Even books praising immorality are not instructions to be immoral. This is because of the existence of free will.

        1.2.4.3.3. Con:
        Even an opinion is often seen as the initiation of violence, but we can't ban opinions, otherwise we wouldn't be doing this.

          1.2.4.3.3.1. Con:
          Many countries have laws [9] which prevent people from expressing the opinion that the Holocaust never happened.

        1.2.4.3.4. Con:
        Weapons are not inherently evil. Books that contain knowledge on combat or the the creation of weapons could be used to fight against an oppressor.

        1.2.4.3.5. Pro:
        Some books, like the Anarchist Cookbook [10], contain objectively dangerous information, and by allowing it into public knowledge we make the world a more dangerous place to live.

          1.2.4.3.5.1. Con:
          The knowledge can be used to find a cure/response to the possible harm.

            1.2.4.3.5.1.1. Pro:
            One could read the Anarchist Cookbook to understand how a bomb was made in order to know how best to defuse a bomb.

          1.2.4.3.5.2. Con:
          Chemistry books aren't banned despite them having information that could harm people if used with malicious intent.

            1.2.4.3.5.2.1. Con:
            One provides easy-access to the dangerous information. The information found in chemistry books relates to dangerous chemicals which are not easy to create, access, or isolate (like sodium nitrate). The Anarchist Cookbook discusses how to use common, easy-access chemicals (like bleach) to make destructive items.

              1.2.4.3.5.2.1.1. Con:
              As said above, chemistry books can show how one can isolate a substance. If one wants to know if these chemicals can be found in normal household objects, one merely needs to look it up on the internet.

            1.2.4.3.5.2.2. Con:
            The intent of a chemistry book is to teach children about the world they live in. The intent of the Anarchist Cookbook is to teach people to make destructive/disruptive devices.

              1.2.4.3.5.2.2.1. Con:
              Intent on the author's side has little relevance as in both cases the same task can be accomplished.

          1.2.4.3.5.3. Con:
          The banning of a book is used as an oversimplified scapegoat where a book/s can be blamed and made responsible for an act of violence and/or a subjectively immoral act, instead of putting the responsibility on our societies, cultures or governments.

            1.2.4.3.5.3.1. Pro:
            Addressing societal issues would lead to a much more complicated and nuanced conversation about acts of violence, which is more realistic than funneling the 'fault' into a book or set of books - or even a specific author [11].

          1.2.4.3.5.4. Con:
          By the same token, we would have to ban everyting that contains the potential of danger. Which would mean we'd have to ban everything, since all things can potentially be used to endanger others.

          1.2.4.3.5.5. Con:
          Books don't kill people; people kill people.

            1.2.4.3.5.5.1. Pro:
            -> See 1.2.4.1.1.4.1.

      1.2.4.4. Con:
      Ignorance prevents individuals from being able to make informed decisions.

        1.2.4.4.1. Pro:
        By definition, one has to have all of the knowledge related to a topic in order to make an informed decision. A book ban disallows such decision-making by removing some information.

    1.2.5. Pro:
    Book banning can risk valuable history being lost forever.

    1.2.6. Pro:
    Banning access to books is similar to prohibiting people to know the truth.

    1.2.7. Con:
    It could be argued that in certain circumstances, blissful ignorance [12] is a positive and morally correct thing.

  1.3. Pro:
  banning literature leads to limiting critical thinking, the ability to approach scenarios with different perspectives as well as erasing general knowledge.

  1.4. Pro:
  Book banning is a tool of authoritarian institutions.

    1.4.1. Pro:
    The Nazis banned authors who wrote things that disagreed with their politics or for ethnic reasons [13]. Clearly they intended this as a way to suppress criticism and to help stay in power.

    1.4.2. Pro:
    The People's Republic of China has banned books [14] to suppress ideas that differed from the officially sanctioned ideas.

    1.4.3. Pro:
    For 400 years the Index Librorum Prohibitorum [15] was the Catholic Church's official list of books they wanted suppressed in order to eliminate heretic ideas.

      1.4.3.1. Pro:
      No one has the right to tell you what you can and can't read, unless you're a kid and your parents say so.

    1.4.4. Pro:
    The Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC) has actively lobbied for a global ban on what it perceives as anti-Islamic blasphemy [16].

    1.4.5. Pro:
    Book banning can be used as a form of manipulation to rally communities(e.g. religious groups)against people/ideas which are construed as threatening to the values,rules and identities of the individuals within those communities.Emotions of anger,injustice,righteousness & even hatred are channeled against the book/s,which becomes 'symbolic'.This creates a strong sense of solidarity amongst the members which leaders/influencers of such communities can use as a source of power.Ingroups & Outgroups [17]

    1.4.6. Pro:
    When books are banned for political reasons, the author's freedom and life can also be at risk. pen-international.org [18]

    1.4.7. Con:
    In Germany, book banning was undertaken, not by the Nazi authorities, but by student organizations [19]. This demonstrates that the collective sentiments of the masses are more relevant than those of authorities.

    1.4.8. Con:
    What some label authoritarianism may in fact be a good thing.

      1.4.8.1. Pro:
      The Dunning-Kruger effect [20] demonstrates that people uniformly believe they are more knowledgeable on any topic than they really are. The guidance from a real expert is often dismissed as merely "authoritarian demands" because the non-experts believe their position is more valid than it really is.

        1.4.8.1.1. Con:
        The Dunning-Kruger effect also means that government authorities would not be knowledgeable about which books are good or bad. Their expertise is in government, not literature.

        1.4.8.1.2. Pro:
        Experts, like doctors, are a type of authority we trust with our physical health. Trusting a trained authority to govern the land or for the ideas we put in our head, AKA "mental health", is no different.

          1.4.8.1.2.1. Con:
          Authoritarian rulers can rarely be trusted to make good decisions for others. An authoritarian generally makes decisions based on what is good for themself.

          1.4.8.1.2.2. Pro:
          DIY surgery is not appealing to anyone, yet DIY evaluation of ideas is strangely popular among the non-expert.

    1.4.9. Con:
    Many books never get checked out [21] anyway, why not reduce this down to a core set of vetted and approved items from a centralized group of selectors.

      1.4.9.1. Con:
      It's not that people are too busy to make decisions, it's that they don't have enough choice. Low voter turnout in the US may be due to having a 2-party system. Multi-party systems like the UK have had voter turn-out as high as 83.9% [22], while US turn-out in the US has not gone over 65% [23].

      1.4.9.2. Con:
      -> See 1.1.4.

    1.4.10. Con:
    Having a centralized authority reduce the signal to noise ratio for you allows you to make choices easily. Too much choice is tyranny. [24]

  1.5. Pro:
  Book banning limits free speech.

    1.5.1. Pro:
    Your thoughts and opinions are informed by your knowledge. Removing any particular book or media limits the amount of general knowledge you can have and therefore limits your thoughts and freedom thereof.

    1.5.2. Con:
    Removing books from schools and libraries does not mean the public is totally unable to access them.

      1.5.2.1. Con:
      Schools, libraries and universities are the first places of books circulation.

      1.5.2.2. Pro:
      All institutions curate their collections. Small libraries do not have room on their shelves for every book, and some works are considered more important than others. So curating knowledge must happen to some degree in society.

        1.5.2.2.1. Pro:
        Librarians remove "books full of inaccurate, irrelevant and often offensive information" in a process known as weeding [25].

          1.5.2.2.1.1. Con:
          Weeding is not the same as banning. Weeded books are quite often sold and donated [26] for use by others.

          1.5.2.2.1.2. Con:
          Weeding is not intended to censor information. It is not a challenge to free speech.

  1.6. Con:
  Children should not be allowed to read or view books that are inappropriate for their age.

    1.6.1. Con:
    Parents can better guarantee their kid's book choice is appropriate than with e-books since the internet, nowadays, is known to have many inappropriate books.

    1.6.2. Pro:
    Books with graphic nudity, like Maus [27], are not appropriate for younger readers. Banning these books protects them from topics that should be introduced at the discretion of their parents.

    1.6.3. Con:
    Some parents and teachers think the Harry Potter books should be banned, but Harry Potter should not be banned as it provides lessons on moral values, and facilitates creativity.

    1.6.4. Pro:
    Some subject matter, such as sexually explicit content, is not appropriate for some environments, such as a public school environment where minors would be exposed to the content.

      1.6.4.1. Pro:
      The most frequently banned book in public schools in 2022 was Gender Queer by Maia Kobabe [28]. The reason was due to sexually explicit graphic illustrations of an underage character performing oral sex on an erect penis, and of an illustration of a naked Greek deity caressing the manhood of a naked boy that wasn't yet a man. Many who generally considered themselves to be against restricting access to books agreed its content was not appropriate for a public school setting [29].

    1.6.5. Con:
    Parents have the ultimate decision as to whether or not a book is inappropriate for their child to read. A book that one person or parent might find inappropriate for their child might not be found inappropriate by another parent for their child. Parents are who should monitor what their child is reading, not society deciding for them.

    1.6.6. Con:
    This is not banning books. This is categorizing according to maturity. This is good as a general guideline but not a hard rule.

  1.7. Con:
  Banning books would lead to a reduction in the number of paper books, which would help the environment.

    1.7.1. Pro:
    The poisonous processes of the book-making industry (bleaching paper, binding glue, factories and shipping that put out carbon dioxide) outweigh the benefits of paper books being produced.

      1.7.1.1. Con:
      If we really want to stop the harmful impact from the “book industry,” we would be better served by regulating the billions of dollars made in profits by the textbook industry, [30] rather than limiting the intellectual development of our democracy.

    1.7.2. Con:
    Banning paper books is different from removing access to books.

    1.7.3. Con:
    Banning paper books would lead to the dependency on technological devices that can result to a tremendous impact on the environment.

    1.7.4. Con:
    Trees are a renewable resource. [31] Deforestation which emits high levels of C02 is more associated with land clearance, not domestic production of paper.

      1.7.4.1. Con:
      -> See 1.7.1.

    1.7.5. Pro:
    The book industry in America produced 695,400 tons [32] of book paper in 2014, and the percentage of recycled paper used declined from 22% in 2012 to 12% in 2014.

      1.7.5.1. Con:
      Recycling paper considering books isn't a good thing, it lowers the need of trees that are fixing carbon dioxide.

    1.7.6. Con:
    Ebook sales have continued to rise [33], and the technology is likely to remain viable and popular for the foreseeable future, thus making a ban on paper books ineffective.

      1.7.6.1. Con:
      Printed book sales are up 18 percent [34] indicating that there is still an interest in paper books.

    1.7.7. Con:
    Paper books are primarily carbon. Anything persistent made from carbon helps to "capture" carbon from the atmosphere. So printing more books could help reduce the amount of carbon produced overall which might -- in a small way -- help the environment.

      1.7.7.1. Con:
      Carbon sequestration is an active process done by living trees. Thus, leaving a tree living is more environmentally friendly than cutting it down to make paper.

        1.7.7.1.1. Con:
        The tree has to die at some point. After which the carbon it has taken up gets eaten by microbes [35] and goes back to CO2 over a long time. Whereas a tree turned into wood or paper, less CO2 is returned to the air.

      1.7.7.2. Con:
      -> See 1.7.1.

      1.7.7.3. Pro:
      When trees die, something has to happen to the carbon in them. Either it goes into the air, to the ground, or is used by humans for wood or paper.

  1.8. Con:
  Not all books add value, and there is no value in keeping absolutely everything, so there is benefit in removing and discarding some books.

    1.8.1. Con:
    There's no need to ban or remove access to the books then.

      1.8.1.1. Pro:
      In our modern day and age, this is a function the market facilitates. Books that are not sold, or searched online will be forgotten and removed from collective memory automatically.

      1.8.1.2. Pro:
      We already have sources such as book reviews which serve the purpose of informing readers so that they may self-filter bad/useless books from good/useful books.

    1.8.2. Pro:
    The First Council of Nicaea [36] met in in CE 435 to decide what would be in the bible, and what would be left out. Leaving a book out was equivalent to banning it ... the books became effectively unavailable. This sort of choice is unavoidable in any society.

      1.8.2.1. Con:
      The Council of Nicaea was an effort to determine which texts would be canonically accepted, since some accounts disagreed with eachother. While this was important for Christian canon, it could be argued that religious texts are not objectively valuable, so this sort of filtering was a waste of time, equivalent to arguing over whether Hermes or Prometheus is the keeper of fire.

      1.8.2.2. Pro:
      Determining which selected texts of a number will be preserved is essentially the same as deciding which texts will be ignored, forgotten, or banned. If it was possible to save everything, then there is no point in selecting certain ones to be saved.

    1.8.3. Pro:
    Removing books that contain "information" we objectively know to be inaccurate can reduce misinformation and promote knowledge-growth.

      1.8.3.1. Pro:
      Book containing verifiable pseudoscience [37] are harmful not just to the knowledge of the public, but also the health of the public.

        1.8.3.1.1. Con:
        Part of determining truth in science has to do with determining what is false. Scientists can approach or approximate "truth" by taking the mean value of false data points.

        1.8.3.1.2. Con:
        There is a concept is known as "Via Negativa" in Apophatic Theology. One learns what is true (or divine) by identifying what is false (profane). To remove the false is to deny access to truth.

          1.8.3.1.2.1. Con:
          Pseudoscience, by definition, misleads people using facts in a disingenuous way. This conflates the true and the false and makes it more difficult for people to tell them apart. It works against Via Negativa.

            1.8.3.1.2.1.1. Con:
            One may also be lead astray by genuine misunderstanding of generally accepted facts. Within the context of open conversation these problems can be illuminated. Books passively sitting on shelves allow for both illumination and delusion. We risk one for the other.

          1.8.3.1.2.2. Con:
          This is hyperbolic. The books being removed are by no means removing ALL the sources of negative information. Stamping out every mention of a particular idea would indeed be an epic ban. However, banning a book, or even the complete works of an author, will not by any means stamp out everything about a particular idea.

      1.8.3.2. Con:
      The banning of a book inherently limits the growth of knowledge. Banning a book means it is removed from the shelves or not published for the purpose of it not being read by the public.

        1.8.3.2.1. Con:
        The Bible says: [38] "The words of the wise are like goads, their collected sayings like firmly embedded nails—given by one shepherd. Be warned, my son, of anything in addition to them. Of making many books there is no end, and much study wearies the body." There are endless books, and one cannot read all of them. Removing non-information or junk information does not decrease the share of relatively infinite knowledge.

        1.8.3.2.2. Con:
        Time spent reading factually incorrect information would be better used reading and discussing true information on the topic. Banning incorrect information has the potential to promote (rather than limit) growth of knowledge.

          1.8.3.2.2.1. Con:
          Information available on any non-trivial subject is on a spectrum of high-probability or low-probability based on available information and the biases of the people/researchers/scientists in a given context at that time. Providing only 100% true information is not possible and gatekeepers are likewise incapable of unbiased and perfect cognition.

          1.8.3.2.2.2. Pro:
          School librarians in Australia recently instituted a two-week ban on Harry Potter books [39] in order to get children to read other books, because students "were reading the series over and over at the expense of other books”.

      1.8.3.3. Pro:
      -> See 1.5.2.2.

      1.8.3.4. Pro:
      The Piltdown Man [40] was an anthropological hoax. Fragments from various current species (a human skull, an orangutan jaw, and chimpanzee teeth) were combined and said to be the remains of an early human. Anthropology books published from its "discovery" in 1913 until its exposure as a hoax in 1953 contained erroneous information about human evolution. It would be appropriate for an anthropology teacher to request that such books be banned from their university or teaching curriculum.

        1.8.3.4.1. Con:
        Piltdown man, not being saddled with religious concepts or a following, would not need a *ban* to remove him from textbooks. Just evidence. He would be removed without ceremony and replaced with current relevant information to students. No teacher would need to fight to get him out of textbooks or protest. Evidence would suffice.

        1.8.3.4.2. Pro:
        Piltdown man is NOT understood best by reading the original paper, but rather by reading a retrospective that explains why it was wrong. This is a general principle: any book containing false information can be better explained by a book covering the reason it was false.

      1.8.3.5. Pro:
      In Germany, it is illegal to publish/distribute Holocaust denial literature [41]. Such a ban is a good example of how reducing misinformation can be positive.

      1.8.3.6. Con:
      Information objectively known to be inaccurate and harmful [42] is a part of many religious scriptures. Yet, these are not banned.

      1.8.3.7. Pro:
      Library of Congress -- the US authority that assigns a shelf-location number to nearly every book printed in the US -- created a shelf-location number for Holocaust denial books (D804.355) that places them alongside books about the Holocaust. They are sandwiched between books about world politics during the Holocaust and first-hand memoirs [43], making it difficult to distinguish [44] their claims as hoax rather than fact. It would be better if such books were banned.

      1.8.3.8. Con:
      This argument form has been used to justify the banning of books, both non-fiction and fiction by numerous governments [45] in the past. The argument says, in essence "any material which is not congruent with our version of objective reality [46] should be hidden from public view". Objective truth, in this version, is a social construct.

        1.8.3.8.1. Con:
        Governments may twist truth or sentiment to justify their wishes. This does not change the fact that removing inaccurate information can be good.

          1.8.3.8.1.1. Pro:
          -> See 1.8.3.5.

        1.8.3.8.2. Pro:
        -> See 1.4.

      1.8.3.9. Con:
      Books which contain "inaccurate information" can still be valuable.

        1.8.3.9.1. Pro:
        Out of date, controversial and inaccurate books can be studied to see how knowledge and opinions have changed over time.

          1.8.3.9.1.1. Con:
          Inaccurate knowledge doesn't necessarily set a historic precedent. Books can be both new and grossly misleading, discussing information which isn't widely accepted or considered valuable at the time of publication.

        1.8.3.9.2. Pro:
        Public libraries stock books on creationism [47], conspiracy theories, and cryptid sightings. 001.9 Controversial knowledge in the Dewey Decimal System. [48] They keep these books because they are valuable despite containing "inaccurate information".

        1.8.3.9.3. Pro:
        Fiction books are valuable and useful.

        1.8.3.9.4. Pro:
        All information has a place, and even factually false books can teach us about peoples thought and feeling. It is up to the reader to find whatever is worthwhile in a book.

    1.8.4. Pro:
    Banning books occurs everyday. Libraries have only so much room, and so librarians must be selective in what goes into them. Therefore, someone is always restricting access to some books.

  1.9. Pro:
  Banning books has a negative effect on critical thinking.

    1.9.1. Pro:
    Banning books means the authority doesn't trust in its peoples' critical faculties.

    1.9.2. Con:
    It is not possible for every human to assess the value of every book. There has to be some collective action to filter worthwhile books from the detritus. Not a matter of trust, but rather the reality of a finite amount of time available to do such critical reading.

      1.9.2.1. Con:
      -> See 1.8.1.2.

      1.9.2.2. Con:
      According to google [49] there are around 130 million unique books in the world. The overwhelming majority of these books are not banned. Thus the notion that certain 'unnecessary' books should be banned is out of touch with reality.

      1.9.2.3. Con:
      This is now done by publishing companies. One can chose to trust such companies and neglect what they don't offer. Banning is totally different as it forbids any potential reader to access to a book. There is no way to define who decides what is to be banned in a consensual way (and therefore some will be coerced into this), nor any way to define who will keep those "banners" in check [50]...

    1.9.3. Pro:
    On that front, allowing a public to have more knowledge both creates risk and prevents it by making it common knowledge that something is both dangerous and how to nullify that danger. Banning something and making it harder for the public to get a hold of it but allowing those with lower morals to access such information then makes it a danger to those who have a moral obligation, duty, or ground to prevent these things from happening. All literature are words, and words that offend are lessons.

    1.9.4. Con:
