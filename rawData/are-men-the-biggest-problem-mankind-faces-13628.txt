Discussion Name:
Are Men the Biggest Problem Mankind Faces?

1. Thesis:
Men are the biggest problem mankind faces.

  1.1. Con:
  Mankind faces bigger problems than men [1].

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Religion is the biggest problem mankind faces currently since certain interpretations fuel intolerance, discrimination, and impede the creation of inclusive societies.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      Religion leads to uneducated ignorance, which can cause societal division and hatred.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      Religion is not the biggest threat since it will eventually disappear, and so will the need to conform to such strictures.

        1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
        In many countries the practice of religion has drastically reduced over time [2]. Each generation is less religious than the former, this will continue until religion is only practiced by minorities in the world.

        1.1.1.2.2. Con:
        Part of religion’s appeal is that it offers a feel of security in an uncertain world. Religion won't disappear [3] because sources of insecurity will always exist.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    The rapid advancement of technology is considered one of the biggest problems mankind is facing nowadays.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      Technology enables the creation and dissemination of misinformation, deepfakes, and manipulated content [4]

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      The increasing interconnectedness of digital systems makes societies vulnerable to cyberattacks [5].

      1.1.2.3. Con:
      Technology contributes to medical breakthroughs, improved diagnostics, and more effective treatments, leading to enhanced healthcare and longer life expectancy. [6]

      1.1.2.4. Con:
      Automation and technological advancements contribute to improved living standards, from smart home technologies [7] to advancements in transportation and infrastructure.

      1.1.2.5. Con:
      Technological advancement can be and overall tends to be a great thing for mankind – the issues are with issues which particular technologies are developed to which form, when and how they are adopted and regulated [8].

        1.1.2.5.1. Pro:
        It is not the rapid advancement of technology but rather problems that are linked to the shape of it [9], such as specific risks of specific technologies [10] under current conditions (such as geopolitical rivalry and profit market systems).

    1.1.3. Con:
    The roots of all of the other bigger problems that mankind faces can still be found in men.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      Addressing the root causes of major global issues requires acknowledging and rectifying the historical and systemic patriarchal imbalances in decision-making to achieve more inclusive and equitable representation. [11]

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      Blaming men of all societal problems is unfair and inaccurate since it doesn't even take into account how intersectionality [12] affects inequalities.

        1.1.3.2.1. Pro:
        Blaming discourse [13] doesn't lead to any improvement since it categorizes women as responsible for being victims and men as perpetrators of the violence.

        1.1.3.2.2. Con:
        Intersectionality [14] studies how systems of oppression, domination, or discrimination impact on individuals. Patriarchy still remains one of the strongest societal systems of oppression. [15]

    1.1.4. Con:
    The patriarchal system [16] can be regarded as one of the most significant challenges humanity confronts given its implication in fostering inequalities.

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      Patriarchy is intricately woven into diverse social, political, and economic structures [17], influencing societal norms, power dynamics, and gender roles.

      1.1.4.2. Con:
      Patriarchy is slowly losing strength [18], however, capitalism is currently destroying the Earth and widening inequalities. [19]

  1.2. Pro:
  Throughout history, men have held positions of power leading to decisions that have negatively impacted societies.

    1.2.1. Con:
    Historical narratives often highlight negative impacts without acknowledging the positive contributions of men in leadership roles.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      There are numerous examples of positive contributions by male leaders.

        1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
        Nelson Mandela [20] played a key role in ending apartheid in South Africa and fostering reconciliation among the country's diverse communities and worked towards building a more inclusive and democratic nation.

        1.2.1.1.2. Pro:
        Mahatma Gandhi [21], a leader in India's struggle for independence, advocated for nonviolent civil disobedience. His leadership played a pivotal role in India gaining independence from British rule.

        1.2.1.1.3. Pro:
        Martin Luther King Jr. [22], was a prominent leader in the American Civil Rights Movement who advocated for racial equality and justice through nonviolent means. His efforts led to significant legislative changes and improvements in civil rights.

        1.2.1.1.4. Con:
        Male leaders have been responsible for inflicting the greatest harm on humanity.

          1.2.1.1.4.1. Pro:
          Adolf Hitler [23] orchestrated the Holocaust, leading to the genocide of six million Jews and millions of others and initiating World War II, resulting in widespread devastation, loss of life, and suffering.

          1.2.1.1.4.2. Pro:
          Pol Pot's regime [24] was responsible for the Cambodian Genocide, resulting in the deaths of an estimated 1.7 million people due to execution, forced labor, and starvation.

          1.2.1.1.4.3. Con:
          Queen Mary I of England is often referred to as "Bloody Mary" [25] due to her persecution of protestants.

          1.2.1.1.4.4. Con:
          A study [26] found that European "polities led by queens engaged in war more than polities led by kings", indicating that, at least, gender is a misguided or irrelevant subject or variable to consider in power when social stability and peace is concerned.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      Male leaders, often with complex legacies, should be assessed through historical events to appreciate the multifaceted negative consequences of their contributions to society.

    1.2.2. Con:
    Making men responsible for societal failures is a result of selection bias [27]. As women are starting to be better represented in positions of power, this will eventually change.

      1.2.2.1. Con:
      The selection bias affects negatively women, since people tend to report that women are less qualified for leadership than their male counterparts based on perceptions of how women think and process issues [28].

      1.2.2.2. Pro:
      According to a study, male leaders tend to be more blamed for negative outcomes [29] than female leaders.

    1.2.3. Pro:
    Men hold a disproportionate share of political and economic power [30]. This imbalance can contribute to policies and practices that perpetuate inequality and marginalize certain groups.

      1.2.3.1. Pro:
      According to the 2023 Global Gender Report by the World Economic Forum, men account on average for 71.7% of Vice-President (VP) roles and 74.6% of C-suite positions worldwide. [31]

      1.2.3.2. Con:
      Structural inequality [32] is not only the result of gender inequality but also racism. White older men [33] are the only ones holding a disproportionate share of political and economic power.

  1.3. Pro:
  Men have historically played a significant role in industrialization [34] and resource exploitation [35], contributing to environmental degradation and climate change.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    Men played a central role in the expansion of coal mining, a key driver of the Industrial Revolution.

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      The extraction and burning of coal for energy, a job performed by men [36], became a fundamental aspect of industrial processes, powering factories, steam engines, and transportation. [37]

      1.3.1.2. Con:
      Women also participated in coal mining. [38]

    1.3.2. Con:
    Women have just as much causally contributed to environmental problems [39] and climate change.

      1.3.2.1. Pro:
      Women are quite directly behind large demands for products and services linked to environmental issues.

    1.3.3. Con:
    Where women haven't directly played a role they played indirect roles such as expecting mainly men to work [40] (resulting in them playing larger roles in that), having financial status as a major factor in partner decisions [41], and expecting patterns of large consumption (such as having a personal house [42] and car) before having children.

    1.3.4. Con:
    Overpopulation is a greater threat to climate change [43] than men.

      1.3.4.1. Pro:
      Overpopulation results in higher consumption which contributes to ecological degradation, heightened conflicts, and an elevated risk of large-scale disasters such as pandemics. [44]

      1.3.4.2. Con:
      Men and patriarchy [45] are more to blame for overpopulation than women. Therefore, overpopulation and men are strongly connected.

  1.4. Pro:
  Toxic masculinity [46] promotes aggressive behaviors as a display of dominance which contributes to conflict and harm.

    1.4.1. Con:
    Traditionally, women have raised men [47]. As a result, women have a major part to play in making the types of men that are causing problems for society.

      1.4.1.1. Pro:
      There is also a prevalence of single parent families, where the vast majority [48] are female-headed.

        1.4.1.1.1. Con:
        Mothers are considered to be the first defense against toxic masculinity. [49]

        1.4.1.1.2. Pro:
        The same could be said about single fathers [50].

      1.4.1.2. Pro:
      There's a raising trend of "Tradwives" [51] referring to traditional women that reinforce gendered stereotypes in their households.

      1.4.1.3. Con:
      It is misogynistic to suggest that women are at fault for the actions of men. [52]

        1.4.1.3.1. Con:
        It has been studied that narcissistic mothers raise narcissistic sons. [53]

        1.4.1.3.2. Pro:
        Arguably, young men [54] are influenced by their peers and older men in social settings that are not accessible not women (especially mothers).

      1.4.1.4. Con:
      Children in most modern societies are still raised by both parents. [55]

    1.4.2. Pro:
    Traditional masculinity promotes reactive and proactive aggression behaviors by enabling poor emotional regulation and limited impulse control [56].

      1.4.2.1. Pro:
      Urban violence and gang cultures [57] have explicitly centered men and masculinity as key factors driving such violence.

      1.4.2.2. Con:
      Stereotypes of traditional masculinity also affect men negatively, for example a study [58] found that men who cried at work were perceived as less competent than women who cried.

  1.5. Con:
  Blaming an entire gender oversimplifies complex societal issues and undermines the importance of individual accountability. [59]

    1.5.1. Con:
    Men tend to resist evolving attitudes [60] which impedes the efforts to create more inclusive and diverse societies.

      1.5.1.1. Con:
      Many men are finding themselves lonely, depressed, anxious and directionless because past models of masculinity feel unreachable or socially unacceptable but new ones have yet to crystallize. [61]

      1.5.1.2. Pro:
      The creation and reinforcement of the 'manosphere [62]' shows the reactionary behavior of a large community of men.

      1.5.1.3. Pro:
      Education will never be available to all people, so there has to be a cultural shift before change can prevail.

        1.5.1.3.1. Con:
        The access to education is increasing all around the world. [63] We can be optimistic, and believe that it will eventually be available to all people.

        1.5.1.3.2. Pro:
        Education is one of the most effective pathways towards gender equity [64] and sustainable change.

    1.5.2. Pro:
    It is wrong to attribute the ills of society to specifically men.

      1.5.2.1. Pro:
      As the hashtags #NotAllMen [65] supports, we can't talk about 'men' in general: each man is different, and some don't have the flaws that are often attributed to men.

      1.5.2.2. Con:
      It could be argued that the successes of society have historically been attributed to men [66], therefore, its ills should also be attributed to them.

      1.5.2.3. Pro:
      Oversimplifying social challenges by attributing them solely to gender neglects the impact of other factors such as race, class, and cultural background [67].

  1.6. Con:
  Acknowledging the positive contributions of men throughout history [68] is crucial for understanding the progress humanity has made.

    1.6.1. Pro:
    Men actively participate in the workforce, making substantial contributions to economic growth through entrepreneurship, skilled labor, and leadership roles.

      1.6.1.1. Pro:
      In the high-risk realm of entrepreneurship and venture-capital-backed startups, women are still significantly outnumbered by men [69].

      1.6.1.2. Con:
      Men have advantage since there are biases against women. According to the UN [70], half of people worldwide still believe men make better political leaders than women, and more than 40% believe men make better business executives than women.

    1.6.2. Pro:
    Men are actively engaged in community service, volunteering, and philanthropy, contributing to the well-being and development of local communities.

      1.6.2.1. Con:
      The 2018 Global Trends in Giving Report indicated that 65% of the donor community was female. [71]

        1.6.2.1.1. Pro:
        A generational study with a gender focus found out that older women donated 89% more than men of the same age. [72]

        1.6.2.1.2. Con:
        This study still shows that 35% of donors are men.

      1.6.2.2. Pro:
      Most of the philanthropists that are positively changing the world in 2023 are males. [73]

        1.6.2.2.1. Con:
        According to studies, women exhibit greater levels of charity compared to men, particularly in terms of altruistic and prosocial behaviour [74].

        1.6.2.2.2. Pro:
        The three greatest philanthropists of all time [75] in terms of money donated are males: Jamsetji Tata [76], Bill Gates [77] and Warren Buffet [78].

    1.6.3. Con:
    Men have been able to contribute positively throughout history thanks to institutionalized sexism. [79]

      1.6.3.1. Pro:
      Society is androcentric [80], which means that there is a propensity to center society around men and men's needs, priorities, and values and successes.

      1.6.3.2. Con:
      Regardless of how men came into those roles, their contributions can't be denied.

        1.6.3.2.1. Pro:
        Men tend to take more risks than women [81], which means that they're more likely to take jobs that are more strenuous as well as more dangerous.

        1.6.3.2.2. Con:
        It could be argued that without the existence of institutionalized sexism, women could have also contributed positively throughout history.

"Androcentrism is the evaluation of individuals and cultures based on male perspectives, standards, and values. The term refers to a male-centered worldview which does not necessarily present explicitly negative views of women and girls, but positions men and boys as representative of the human condition or experience and women and girls as diverging from the human condition. It is a complex, subtle, and often unacknowledged form of sexism, existing on a continuum which includes misogyny and patriarchal attitudes, but it is also informed by patriarchal cultures in which men are granted more power and influence, and thus the right to evaluate and interpret individuals and cultures. Androcentrism exists in all fields of study and cultural expressions, including the arts, sciences, medicine, law, fine arts, and media."
