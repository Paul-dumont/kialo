Discussion Name:
No one should earn ten times more than a nurse

1. Thesis:
No one should earn ten times more than a nurse.

  1.1. Pro:
  As we have experienced during the COVID-19 pandemic, nursing is a fundamental and critical profession for our society to function.

    1.1.1. Con:
    "Fundamental" and "critical" should not be the properties that determine wage.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      "Essential" workers [1] included everyone from doctors to retail store employees, yet differences in skill levels would not justify paying them the same amount of money.

        1.1.1.1.1. Con:
        The fact that there are other considerations to make, doesn't mean that "fundamental" and "critical" don't belong in the equation.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    There is scientific evidence to suggest that the COVID-19 pandemic is only the first of many [2] large-scale disease outbreaks to affect human beings. Poor wages may disincentivize nursing a career choice, leading to shortages.

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      Nurses have long suffered low wages, and this has not changed the enrolment rate significantly. Nurses are evidently driven by something other than a desire for money.

    1.1.3. Con:
    The fundamental and critical nature of the nursing profession should determine only the wages of nurses, not the upper limit of other professionals.

  1.2. Pro:
  Nursing involves a lot of hard and difficult work.

    1.2.1. Con:
    Pay is not always contingent on the difficulty level of a job.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      Women do a vast majority of unpaid labor [3], which is emotionally and physically challenging, yet they are never paid for domestic labor.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      Just because pay is not contingent on difficulty level does not mean that this is right. Pay norms should be revised to reflect difficulty levels.

        1.2.1.2.1. Con:
        This is not possible, because there is no objective standard for measuring difficulty.

      1.2.1.3. Pro:
      CEOs often earn significantly more [4] than their employees, even when their performance does not justify their pay.

        1.2.1.3.1. Con:
        CEOs have often suffered the toil and hardship for much longer than their business subordinates. This incites a deserved pay increase.

    1.2.2. Pro:
    Nursing requires a high level of tolerance for things that are usually considered disgusting and repulsive [5], such as body fluids and emissions.

      1.2.2.1. Con:
      This can’t change the income of the nursing profession as it has no bearing on the overall income of a hospital.

      1.2.2.2. Con:
      Nurses accept this risk when choosing the nursing path. It is not an unexpected occurrence.

  1.3. Con:
  Wages should be determined by market demand, which has determined that there are those who deserve a much higher income than what nurses earn.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    In 2018, Forbes estimated Jeff Bezos' fortune to be $112 billion, which is more than 100 000 times as much as the average yearly salary of a nurse in the US.

    1.3.2. Con:
    Services like healthcare cannot be left to the free market because, unlike most businesses, the work nurses do has life-and-death implications for millions of people worldwide.

    1.3.3. Con:
    Free markets lead to monopolies and are not always fair.

    1.3.4. Con:
    People clearly want nurses, and there is a global shortage of them-- yet they earn far less [6] than, for example, a hedge fund manager [7], whose services are wanted by fewer.

    1.3.5. Con:
    The market can only be a real indicator of what people want, if people earn more or less the same.

  1.4. Con:
  It would be harmful to the economy if it were made to follow this prescription.

    1.4.1. Con:
    high-income individuals have a smaller propensity to spend, and a higher propensity to save - a more equal distribution would lead to more spending, stimulating the economy and making it more dynamic

    1.4.2. Pro:
    If implemented as a matter of policy, the most straightforward means would be a wage cap set at 10x a nurse's wage. This is a price control, and most economists agree [8] that price controls are harmful.

      1.4.2.1. Con:
      Price controls apply to goods and services, not to wages. Therefore, it is unlikely that wage limits would, in any way, have a significant impact on the economy.

        1.4.2.1.1. Con:
        There is no difference between "paying a wage" instead of "purchasing a service" that is substantial enough to make the laws of supply and demand disappear.

          1.4.2.1.1.1. Con:
          In the case of secured jobs, employees continue to earn a way even though they may not be providing a service, to the expected quantity and quality level. This differentiates them from service providers.

      1.4.2.2. Con:
      There are other (possibly less straightforward) means, like unionization, and different kinds of taxes, that could steer us in this direction.

      1.4.2.3. Con:
      Introducing wage caps would reduce income inequality [9], which is bad for the economy.

    1.4.3. Con:
    Income tax is typically progressive [10], meaning those earning more would end up paying more taxes, thereby increasing government revenue.

      1.4.3.1. Con:
      The wealthiest sections of society often pay very little in taxes [11], by invoking tax breaks, benefits, and loopholes.

  1.5. Con:
  Nursing is a safe career choice; people who make riskier choices should be rewarded.

    1.5.1. Pro:
    Nursing is relatively recession-proof [12], as people will continue to need healthcare, regardless of changes in the economy.

    1.5.2. Pro:
    Nursing is among the most respected professions [13] in the world. Unlike other less socially acceptable professions, nurses take few social risks.

    1.5.3. Pro:
    The global nursing shortage [14] implies that nurses will always be in demand. This means nursing is less competitive than other professions.

    1.5.4. Con:
    Nurses are more likely to catch diseases [15] since they spend most of their time with patients. This makes the choice to enter this profession full of risks.

      1.5.4.1. Pro:
      By the most conservative of estimates, approximately 1,500 nurses [16] lost their lives to COVID-19.

    1.5.5. Con:
    Wartime nurses [17] risk their lives to provide healthcare to injured and dying soldiers.

  1.6. Pro:
  Reducing disparity in pay across sectors may reduce income inequality [18], which has a number of advantages.

    1.6.1. Con:
    Reducing income disparity across sectors has a number of disadvantages, especially the inability to induce anyone to perform more difficult jobs.

    1.6.2. Pro:
    Reducing disparity in pay may make nursing a more attractive career option to those considering more-lucrative alternatives.

    1.6.3. Con:
    Reducing pay disparity across sectors is not commonly regarded as a means to reduce income inequality.

      1.6.3.1. Pro:
      To reduce income inequality, the minimum wage [19] should be increased to keep up with inflation. As nurses earn well above this limit [20], using their pay as a criterion for determining pay in other sectors is unlikely to have any impact.

  1.7. Con:
  Some other professions require longer education than nursing, and practitioners of those professions should be rewarded for that.

    1.7.1. Pro:
    It can take a maximum of four years [21] for a person to become a registered nurse, but it can take up to 14 years [22] for a person to become a doctor. This justifies doctors being paid more than nurses.

    1.7.2. Con:
    Practitioners who invest more time in education and preparation can be rewarded with higher pay without breaching a 10x limit.

  1.8. Pro:
  Studies show [23] that, the more you earn, the less money matters for your happiness, showing little or no effect beyond the average wage for a nurse (~85k/year in the US).

    1.8.1. Pro:
    For many, nursing is a calling [24] rather than a profession. Thus, income is likely to have a much lower impact on the happiness of nurses than on those from less-noble professions.

    1.8.2. Con:
    Marginal income may matter less, on a statistical basis, but it still matters enough to be a very substantial motivation.

    1.8.3. Con:
    Whether a higher income makes a person happy is irrelevant. Instead, the worthiness of a higher income should be determined based on the nature of work.
