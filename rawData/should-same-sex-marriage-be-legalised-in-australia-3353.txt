Discussion Name:
Should same-sex marriage be legalised in Australia?

1. Thesis:
Should same-sex marriage be legalised in Australia?

  1.1. Pro:
  The legalisation of same-sex marriage makes society fairer.

    1.1.1. Con:
    Legalising same-sex marriage without changing the social structures that fundamentally privilege some relationships over others does little to enhance fairness [1].

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      Promoting stability and permanence in relationships by allowing more people to get married is a social good.

        1.1.1.1.1. Con:
        A legal marriage does not equate to a stable and permanant relationship, nor is it the only means through which to achieve one.

          1.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
          Numerous legal marriages ultimately result in divorce. [2]

          1.1.1.1.1.2. Con:
          Legal marriage often comes with legal and societal commitments that can enhance stability and permanence by providing a framework for shared responsibilities and protections.

        1.1.1.1.2. Pro:
        Giving same-sex couples rights through marriage supports their ability [3] to form strong interpersonal connections.

      1.1.1.2. Pro:
      Society can informally isolate and oppress [4] same-sex couples from social settings despite the legalisation of same-sex marriages.

        1.1.1.2.1. Con:
        Same-sex couples from countries where same-sex marriage is illegal report higher levels of discrimination. [5]

        1.1.1.2.2. Pro:
        Same-sex couples from Netherlands, where same-sex marriage has been legal for almost a decade, continue to report [6] discrimination.

        1.1.1.2.3. Pro:
        Many same-sex couples have reported increased backlash [7] after legalisation of same-sex marriage.

      1.1.1.3. Con:
      The legalization of same-sex marriage allows couples to avail of state aid [8] in the battle against discrimination by being able to report unequal treatment as crimes to legal authorities.

      1.1.1.4. Pro:
      Legalising same-sex marriage without changing society privileges those who best fit hegemonic ideals [9] and encourages homonormativity [10], instead of breaking down any social barriers.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    The equality brought about by legalising same-sex marriage would be in line with human rights [11].

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      Same-sex marriage legislation is not necessary to provide legal protections to same-sex relationships. Same-sex couples can have civil unions which serve the same legal function.

        1.1.2.1.1. Con:
        Civil unions often lack the same societal recognition and benefits as marriage, which can perpetuate inequality and social stigma for same-sex couples.

        1.1.2.1.2. Pro:
        A civil union is a legal arrangement between two individuals that provides them with state-level protections equal to those [12] offered in a marriage.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      Marriage is a legal status [13]. It is not a right.

      1.1.2.3. Pro:
      Individuals should have the freedom to marry whomever they choose, as long as it is based on mutual consent and does not cause harm to others.

      1.1.2.4. Pro:
      Placing restrictions on how individuals lead their private lives and handle their personal relationships is unjust.

        1.1.2.4.1. Con:
        Marriage involves the participation of the state [14] and public institutions, making it more than just a private matter.

        1.1.2.4.2. Pro:
        Respecting individuals' autonomy and privacy in their personal lives is a fundamental principle of human rights. [15]

    1.1.3. Con:
    Marriage is tied to procreation [16]. As same-sex couples cannot procreate, it is not unfair to limit their access to marriage.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      Marriage is used to promote [17] socially beneficial reproductive [18] partnerships. As same-sex couples cannot advance society by procreating, there is no need to support their unions.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      Same-sex partners can have children through surrogacy, adoption, or may even be able to genetically procreate [19] in the future.

        1.1.3.2.1. Pro:
        It is legal [20] for same-sex couples to adopt children in Australia.

        1.1.3.2.2. Con:
        While it is legal [21] for same-sex couples to adopt children in Australia, there are barely any cases [22] of such couples actually being granted their adoption requests.

      1.1.3.3. Con:
      Childless couples [23] will outnumber couples with children by 2023 in Australia. Thus, procreation is not a defining criteria for heterosexual marriages.

      1.1.3.4. Con:
      Societies should prioritize equity [24], meeting everyone's needs, rather than absolute equality. By extending marriage to same-sex couples, it promotes a focus on equity and fairness.

    1.1.4. Pro:
    Denying same-sex couples the option to marry is discriminatory [25] and makes LGBTQ+ people into second class of citizens.

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      State recognition of your relationship is not a prerequisite to being a first-class citizen, as those in de facto relationships [26] know.

      1.1.4.2. Pro:
      Some people see the law as a moral compass [27] and construct their value system based on what is legal.

        1.1.4.2.1. Pro:
        Legalising same-sex marriage would send a message that same-sex attraction is valid, and would reduce [28] the stigma associated with it.

      1.1.4.3. Pro:
      The government has a responsibility to be as fair as possible, as everyone cedes the same freedoms to the government [29] in exchange for protection.

        1.1.4.3.1. Con:
        In some cases, ensuring public safety and comfort may require the government to impose certain restrictions [30] on individual freedoms in the interest of the greater good.

        1.1.4.3.2. Pro:
        Adhering to egalitarian principles helps foster social cohesion and reduces the risk [31] of marginalization or exclusion of certain groups.

      1.1.4.4. Con:
      Legitimacy inheres not in state recognition, but in the eyes of one's community. It is possible to live in a very accepting community [32], and yet still be unable [33] to marry.

  1.2. Con:
  Marriage should not be recognised in law. Therefore, same-sex marriage should not be legalised.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Marriage is a cultural and religious [34] tradition [35] and so should not be overseen by the government.

      1.2.1.1. Con:
      Marriage has changed dramatically [36] over the past 100 years to meet the needs of the economy and culture. Marriage is not seen as a primarily religious activity [37] anymore.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      Legally, marriages are considered civil unions [38]. The Australian constitution acknowledges marriage as a subject for legislation, with no obligation to align with religious practices.

      1.2.1.3. Pro:
      By sanctifying marriage as an institution, governments intervene to support one set of religious or cultural beliefs over [39] another.

    1.2.2. Con:
    Legal marriages are a useful means for governments to decide which couples need additional security and protections. This utility should not be undermined by abolishing marriage.

      1.2.2.1. Con:
      Legal marriage umbrellas many laws and issues that should be considered separately, including immigration, guardianship of minors, and property.

      1.2.2.2. Pro:
      Marriage is tied to tax law. In Australian tax law 'spouse [40]', for example, has a special status.

      1.2.2.3. Con:
      Marriage is an illegitimate extension of the state's power that allows it to sanction some relationships [41] and not others.

        1.2.2.3.1. Pro:
        The sexual morality of individuals should not be in the hands of the government as it increases the risk of potential abuse of power [42].

          1.2.2.3.1.1. Pro:
          The government's desire to appease the majority of voters and win elections can increase sexual minorities' risk of discrimination (p. 19 [43]).

          1.2.2.3.1.2. Pro:
          Government control over sexual morality can violate human rights through intrusive surveillance, invasion of privacy, and punitive measures, even when individuals are not harming others or infringing upon their rights (pp. 5-6 [44]).

          1.2.2.3.1.3. Con:
          The government should protect individuals and society from the negative consequences [45] of unrestrained sexual behavior.

          1.2.2.3.1.4. Con:
          Many laws deal with sexual morality, for example by stipulating the age of consent [46], indicating the government has the tacit support of the people to legislate on such matters.

        1.2.2.3.2. Pro:
        The state's involvement in marriage imposes restrictions on individuals' freedom to form relationships as they choose, interfering [47] with the personal and private nature of marriage.

        1.2.2.3.3. Con:
        The state's involvement in marriage allows for the establishment of regulations that protect [48] individuals [49] from exploitation or abuse.

        1.2.2.3.4. Con:
        Given the continued suppor [50]t for marriage signalled by democratic populations, it is clear that the state has a mandate [51] for marriage which makes it moral.

      1.2.2.4. Pro:
      Marriage gives couples the right to legal arbitration [52] when they separate, ensuring that neither member of the union is unfairly treated. This isn't feasible with other [53] relationship set-ups.

    1.2.3. Con:
    Prohibiting same-sex marriage does little to change the legal status of marriage as a whole.

      1.2.3.1. Pro:
      The LGBTQ+ community lacks [54] the relative social power to change social norms by banning their access to marriage. Thus, disallowing same-sex marriage does little other than entrench discrimination.

      1.2.3.2. Con:
      Legalising same-sex marriage, instead of agitating for the abolition of the institution as a whole, cements its place in society making it less likely [55] it can be removed in the future.

      1.2.3.3. Pro:
      The institution of marriage is backed by centuries [56] of tradition, powerful religious institutions [57], and cultural norms of love; it is unlikely to be undermined by the continued prohibition of same-sex marriage.

    1.2.4. Pro:
    As an institution, marriage is immoral [58].

      1.2.4.1. Pro:
      Marriage often involves the denial [59] of the self in pursuit of a partnership. Enshrining such a union into law is immoral as it encourages people to deny their self-actualisation [60].

      1.2.4.2. Pro:
      Marriage often contributes [61] to the oppression of women, denying them full autonomy. Making gay marriage legal may increase the legitimacy of the institution so it becomes harder to ameliorate this oppression.

        1.2.4.2.1. Con:
        Marriage can be a source of empowerment for women, providing them with financial security [62] in many cases.

        1.2.4.2.2. Pro:
        Many women are forced to abandon their careers [63] after marriage.

        1.2.4.2.3. Pro:
        Many women face oppression and abuse [64] within their relationships, made inescapable by the legal nature of marriage.

        1.2.4.2.4. Pro:
        Many women are forced to engage in unpaid labour [65] post-marriage, creating a double burden [66] if they also take on paid work.

        1.2.4.2.5. Con:
        Marriage can improve [67] women's health, giving them the bodily integrity to do as they wish for longer.

      1.2.4.3. Con:
      Marriage is merely a signal of one's dedication to performing love-sustaining acts for their partner, strengthening their coalition. [68]

  1.3. Con:
  Legalising same-sex marriage impinges [69] on religious [70] freedom.

    1.3.1. Con:
    Having equal rights and privileges for a minority is more important than ensuring everyone in society adheres to the rules of a religion.

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      It is conceivable that some religions allow [71] same-sex marriage. Thus, legalising same-sex marriage would not impinge on their religious freedom.

        1.3.1.1.1. Pro:
        The prohibition of same-sex marriage may impinge on religious freedoms for those religious institutions that permit same-sex marriage ceremonies.

        1.3.1.1.2. Pro:
        There are many Jewish traditions [72] and Christian denominations [73] that are in favor of same-sex marriage.

        1.3.1.1.3. Con:
        A claim to absolute religious freedom would legitimate human sacrifice as well as same-sex marriage. Religious freedom should not be without limitations.

          1.3.1.1.3.1. Pro:
          Religious interpretations advocating violence [74] against other faiths necessitate limits on religious freedom to safeguard societal well-being.

          1.3.1.1.3.2. Con:
          Religious freedom should only be limited when it violates the rights of others, as would be the case of human sacrifice.

      1.3.1.2. Pro:
      Civil law is not implicated by the wants of religious groups; arguments against marriage on the grounds of religious freedoms is folly.

        1.3.1.2.1. Con:
        Basing laws solely on secular principles without considering the religious freedoms and beliefs of individuals can be seen as disregarding the diversity and pluralism within a society.

        1.3.1.2.2. Pro:
        An individual's sense of morality cannot be used to determine course of law. Law must transcend the individual's understanding of right and wrong to protect everyone.

        1.3.1.2.3. Pro:
        There are multiple religions active [75] in Australia. The law shouldn't be based on just one of them.

          1.3.1.2.3.1. Pro:
          Civil law should not privilege one particular religious perspective over others, given that the state represents every Australian.

        1.3.1.2.4. Con:
        There is no reason that law should not be based on religious principles, if it accurately reflects the values of the populace.

          1.3.1.2.4.1. Con:
          A majority [76] of Christian Australians support changing their traditions to be more inclusive of same-sex couples.

          1.3.1.2.4.2. Pro:
          Muslim Australians reported [77] little support for legalising same-sex marriage; undermining their faith may feel particularly otherising as Australia is rife with islamophobia [78].

          1.3.1.2.4.3. Con:
          A majority [79] of Australians declared that same-sex marriage should be legalized in a non-binding survey conducted by the state.

          1.3.1.2.4.4. Pro:
          Some aboriginal communities have shown opposition [80] to the legalization of same-sex marriages.

            1.3.1.2.4.4.1. Pro:
            Some aboriginal people opposed same sex marriage through the Uluru Bark Petition. [81]

              1.3.1.2.4.4.1.1. Con:
              The Uluru Bark Petition was signed by ~30 people [82] - the people who live in Mutitjulu, the aboriginal community closest to Uluru, were not even aware of it.

            1.3.1.2.4.4.2. Con:
            The LGBTQ+ aboriginal community [83] supports same-sex marriage.

            1.3.1.2.4.4.3. Con:
            Aboriginal people are a minority [84]; in democratic processes the voices of their community should be treated as important as any other, not afforded privileged status.

      1.3.1.3. Con:
      Respecting and accommodating religious values is crucial for upholding the fundamental human right of freedom of belief and maintaining a diverse society that values different perspectives and religious freedoms.

    1.3.2. Con:
    As religion is not demonstrably true, [85] it's foolish to base laws around it.

      1.3.2.1. Con:
      Religion, by providing moral guidance and laws of social order, has helped many civilizations develop and grow [86] and is, thus, a valid structure to base laws around.

      1.3.2.2. Pro:
      Given that the rules of every religion vary widely and there is no means by which to prove one religion's accuracy over another's [87], it is impossible to determine which religion to follow.

      1.3.2.3. Pro:
      Many religious commandants have been abandoned, such as suttee [88], with no divine punishment or other repercussions for such actions.

      1.3.2.4. Con:
      Laws are not solely based on objective truth but also on subjective values [89] and societal norms, so it is fine for them to have a religious valence.

    1.3.3. Pro:
    Marriage is a religious tradition [90] that should remain unchanged due to the comfort it gives people through familiarity.

      1.3.3.1. Pro:
      Organised religion is necessary [91] to bond society and give everyone a common moral road-map for life. Undermining this by dissociating traditions [92] from their religious roots is harmful.

        1.3.3.1.1. Pro:
        Religious communities offer a sense of belonging and support, creating spaces for people to connect, share experiences, and provide assistance to each other in times of need.

        1.3.3.1.2. Con:
        Society can derive ethical principles from secular sources, such as philosophy, humanism, and rational discourse, without the need for religious dogma.

      1.3.3.2. Con:
      Cultural traditions change when there is a change in social values [93]. This is why marriage has evolved throughout history.

        1.3.3.2.1. Pro:
        Australians report high levels [94] of support for same-sex marriage, making it legitimate to change the traditional legal definition to include these couples.

        1.3.3.2.2. Con:
        Social values are not always changed for the better. As moral [95] leaders [96] still denounce [97] same-sex marriage [98], it is better not to legalise it and instead work to change society's values.

          1.3.3.2.2.1. Con:
          Pope Francis supports [99] same-sex civil unions and has often called for legal protections for LGBTQ+ people. Thus, it is not clear that moral leaders necessarily decry same-sex marriage.

          1.3.3.2.2.2. Pro:
          Jewish religious leaders in Australia are divided [100] over whether or not same-sex marriage is morally permissable.

          1.3.3.2.2.3. Con:
          It is unfair to place some people as moral leaders of society when large sections of society disagree with them.

        1.3.3.2.3. Pro:
        Marriage itself has transformed significantly. For example, in the past weddings were arranged between living and deceased children [101] to strengthen bonds between families.

        1.3.3.2.4. Con:
        The institution of marriage has traditionally been defined as being between a man and a woman [102], supporting the natural complementarity [103] of male and female sexuality.

      1.3.3.3. Pro:
      Traditions offer people considerable comfort [104] and even stress relief. Changing traditions without people's consent is ultimately harmful.

      1.3.3.4. Con:
      Marriage was not created [105] by religion, but rather predates [106] it.

        1.3.3.4.1. Pro:
        Given that marriage is not inherent [107] to religion, but rather has numerous bureaucratic benefits, it should not be treated as a solely religious institution (p. 6) [108].

        1.3.3.4.2. Con:
        Even if marriage predates religion, it has taken on a religious nature [109] over time. Such cultural significance should be acknowledged.

    1.3.4. Pro:
    State recognition of same-sex marriages could intrude on the freedom of clergy [110] and religious communities to decide what kind of marriages to solemnize.

      1.3.4.1. Con:
      Given the Australian government's commitment to the protection [111] of religious organisations, it seems unlikely that they will be forced to partake in practices they deem offensive.

      1.3.4.2. Con:
      Legalising same-sex marriage in Australia would not force [112] an individual to marry a same-sex couple, despite the officiant's religious beliefs.

      1.3.4.3. Pro:
      Opponents of same-sex marriage fear that the risk of prosecution [113] will deter clergy from preaching against homosexuality.

      1.3.4.4. Pro:
      Requiring [114] religious organizations to extend the same benefits to married same-sex couples as they do to opposite-sex couples could harm their ability to operate as they wish.

  1.4. Pro:
  Legalising same-sex marriage could benefit society materially.

    1.4.1. Pro:
    Many other countries [115] have allowed same-sex marriage [116] without facing repercussions. Thus, it would be fine for Australia to follow suit.

      1.4.1.1. Con:
      Cultural context differs for each country and, thus, what was feasible in one place may not work in another.

        1.4.1.1.1. Con:
        New Zealand, which is culturally quite similar [117] to Australia, legalised same-sex marriage in 2013 [118].

        1.4.1.1.2. Pro:
        The Netherlands [119] legalised same-sex marriage in 2001 with little backlash. Yet, the nation is more diverse [120] than Australia [121], and so may be more accepting of different ways of life.

        1.4.1.1.3. Pro:
        Though Denmark and Australia [122] are both majority-Christian countries, the former [123] has a low level of religiosity. That may have made legalising [124] same-sex marriage in Denmark more successful.

      1.4.1.2. Pro:
      Ireland legalized same sex marriage following a constitutional referendum in 2014 [125], with the main negative outcome being borne by LGBTQ+ people [126] who found it distressing to have their personhood consistently debated.

      1.4.1.3. Con:
      The idea that because other countries have legalized same-sex marriage, Australia should follow suit is an example of the bandwagon fallacy [127].

        1.4.1.3.1. Con:
        Pointing out that other nations have legalised same-sex marriage with few repercussions does not use the bandwagon fallacy, but rather effectively rebukes fearmongering.

    1.4.2. Con:
    Legalised same-sex marriage may increase the chance [128] of illegitimate marriages [129] for immigration purposes.

      1.4.2.1. Pro:
      When same-sex marriage is socially accepted, religious pressure that prevents individuals from marrying someone of the same sex diminishes [130].

      1.4.2.2. Con:
      Any increase in illegitimate marriages is likely to be well within the bounds that Australian customs and immigration can handle, especially given it has been streamlined following a 2014 departmental merge [131].

        1.4.2.2.1. Con:
        Many Australians already express concerns [132] about the perceived high number of immigrants in Australia.

        1.4.2.2.2. Pro:
        Australia has implemented strict immigration policies [133], including measures to control their entry, which are considered among the toughest in the world.

      1.4.2.3. Pro:
      People of the opposite sex already use illegitimate marriage [134] for immigration purposes. This trend is likely to continue for same-sex couples.

      1.4.2.4. Con:
      Immigration is not immoral, everyone should have the right to move [135]. If legalising same-sex marriage makes this easier for people, then it is a major social benefit.

        1.4.2.4.1. Pro:
        The freedom to move and seek a better life is a basic human right [136]. Restricting immigration can be seen as a violation of individuals' rights to seek safety, economic opportunities, and a better future for themselves and their families.

        1.4.2.4.2. Con:
        Allowing unrestricted immigration can lead to social and economic problems [137], such as overpopulation, strain on public services, and job market competition, which can negatively impact both native and immigrant populations.

    1.4.3. Pro:
    Same-sex marriages can be a boon [138] for the economy.

      1.4.3.1. Con:
      As money spent on same-sex weddings increases, there's a possibility that other industries could shrink [139] as funds are diverted from those sectors due to the fixed total amount of money available.

        1.4.3.1.1. Con:
        Legalising same-sex marriages could lead to the creation of new jobs and increased business [140] in marriage-adjacent industries.

          1.4.3.1.1.1. Pro:
          Same-sex weddings in the US have been estimated to contribute up to $70 billion [141] in growth to the wedding industry.

          1.4.3.1.1.2. Con:
          The impact on business growth in marriage-adjacent industries may be limited, as the overall market size for wedding-related services seldom varies [142].

        1.4.3.1.2. Pro:
        Few weddings generate any form of multiplier effect [143], making it more likely that increased expenditure on them removes money from more beneficial industries.

      1.4.3.2. Con:
      Marriage equality opponents would stop paying for advertisements if same-sex marriage was legalized, removing a significant income source [144] for Australian TV stations.

        1.4.3.2.1. Con:
        Given the moral significance anti-same-sex marriage campaigners place on the issue, they are unlikely [145] to stop spending on advertisements if it is legalised.

      1.4.3.3. Pro:
      Marriage is often necessary to safely merge financial assets [146] and access a range of economic benefits. Allowing more couples to do so could, thus, increase economic growth.

      1.4.3.4. Pro:
      The legalization of same-sex marriages enhances employment opportunities [147] for individuals in same-sex relationships, empowering [148] both LGBTQ+ people and the nation economically [149].

    1.4.4. Con:
    Same-sex marriage could have a negative impact on education.

      1.4.4.1. Con:
      Legalising same-sex marriage is unlikely to negatively affect education as children are more open-minded [150] than adults and are likely to be understanding.

        1.4.4.1.1. Pro:
        Children do not inherently know if a social construct is good or bad. Children only believe that 'homosexuality is wrong' when they are taught it. [151]

        1.4.4.1.2. Con:
        Children seldom possess a full understanding of societal issues such as same-sex marriage; their beliefs can be influenced by external factors [152] such as family or culture.

      1.4.4.2. Con:
      Making schools more welcoming to LGBTQ+ students is a positive development, given the high level of bullying [153] they often experience.

        1.4.4.2.1. Con:
        Introducing discussions about same-sex marriages in schools may subject LGBTQ+ students to increased bullying due to potential backlash. [154]

        1.4.4.2.2. Pro:
        Many Australian LGBTQ+ students do not learn at a school that has features supporting sexual diversity. [155]

      1.4.4.3. Pro:
      Changing the content of classes will necessitate a change to the curriculum, which may be costly both in time and financial terms.

        1.4.4.3.1. Con:
        Curriculae should change [156] with time to keep up with the latest scientific theories and social norms.

        1.4.4.3.2. Pro:
        Changing the curriculum often involves training teachers to ensure they are equipped with the necessary knowledge and skills to effectively teach the new content (p. 1 [157]).

        1.4.4.3.3. Pro:
        Curriculum changes may necessitate the development or acquisition of new instructional materials, textbooks, and resources that align with the updated content (pp. 3-6 [158]).

        1.4.4.3.4. Con:
        Modifying the content of classes can be implemented through curriculum updates and professional development programs, minimizing the resource burden associated with change.

      1.4.4.4. Pro:
      Discussions about same-sex marriage in schools may be perceived as promoting a specific ideological or political agenda, causing discomfort in those who hold different views.

        1.4.4.4.1. Pro:
        Some parents may pull their children from school [159] if they believe they are being taught inappropriate content, potentially jeopardising their child's education.

        1.4.4.4.2. Con:
        This would hold true for any political or social issue, as teaching about such topics inherently involves discussing different perspectives and stances that may be disagreed upon by some individuals.

        1.4.4.4.3. Pro:
        Introducing same-sex marriage in educational curricula may contradict religious beliefs held by students and their families, potentially leading to conflicts [160] and divisions within schools.

        1.4.4.4.4. Con:
        The education system should not cater to parents who attempt to censor discussions relevant to the student body and society.

      1.4.4.5. Con:
      If children are not educated about same-sex couples in school, they may acquire information from less reliable sources. [161]

        1.4.4.5.1. Pro:
        Learning about same-sex couples solely outside of educational settings may lead to misinformation, stereotypes [162], and biases [163] that can perpetuate discrimination and prejudice.

        1.4.4.5.2. Pro:
        School environments offer an opportunity for comprehensive and accurate information [164] about same-sex couples to be shared, making life better for LGBTQ+ students.

        1.4.4.5.3. Con:
        Children can acquire knowledge about same-sex relationships in a similar manner as they do about other types of relationships, through personal experience. [165]

        1.4.4.5.4. Con:
        Due to the presence of ingrained [166] homophobia, biphobia, and transphobia, schools can be unreliable sources of information regarding same-sex couples.
