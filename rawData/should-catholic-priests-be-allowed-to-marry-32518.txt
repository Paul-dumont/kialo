Discussion Name:
Should Catholic priests be allowed to marry?

1. Thesis:
Catholic priests should be allowed to marry.

  1.1. Con:
  Catholic priests would be less effective at their job if they were married.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Marrying would unleash the potential for conflict [1] between a priest's spiritual duties and their familial duties.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      To prevent one aspect of their lives from negatively affecting the other, priests can compartmentalise [2] their family lives from the work they do.

      1.1.1.2. Pro:
      Married priests will not be good fathers or husbands.

        1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
        It will be difficult for a priest's wife to remain secure in her relationship as she may be inclined to compare [3] herself to God.

          1.1.1.2.1.1. Con:
          If the priest's wife is also religious, then she is likely to understand how it is possible to be devoted to God and love one's partner simultaneously.

            1.1.1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
            If the priest's wife is equally religious, then she will share the same experience as the priest at being infinitely devoted to both God and her husband.

          1.1.1.2.1.2. Pro:
          Social comparison theory [4] says that people are naturally inclined to compare themselves to others.

          1.1.1.2.1.3. Con:
          Comparison is not inherently bad because it can motivate [5] people to improve. As such, a healthy amount of self-comparison to God may improve the relationship.

            1.1.1.2.1.3.1. Con:
            Seeing as Catholics believe that God is perfect [6], it is unlikely that comparing oneself to God will have a positive outcome.

        1.1.1.2.2. Pro:
        It would be difficult for the wife [7] to establish boundaries with her partner. Should she view him as her husband first or as her priest whom she can confide in for spiritual guidance?

          1.1.1.2.2.1. Con:
          Boundaries could easily established by communicating expectations for the relationship.

            1.1.1.2.2.1.1. Con:
            Ineffective communication is a key reason [8] why many relationships breakdown showing that it isn't easy to communicate and agree on what boundaries to establish.

          1.1.1.2.2.2. Con:
          The priest's wife could seek spiritual guidance from a different priest in order to better separate her marriage from her spiritual activities.

        1.1.1.2.3. Pro:
        Guidelines [9] provided by the Church for priests who father offspring state that they should leave the clergy to focus on raising their children. This suggests that it isn't possible to commit yourself to God and your children.

        1.1.1.2.4. Pro:
        Almost 50% of married pastors agreed with the statement that their job has been difficult for their family [10].

          1.1.1.2.4.1. Pro:
          Priests still work longer hours [11] than most workers.

          1.1.1.2.4.2. Pro:
          Pastors' families often come underintense scrutiny [12] and face expectations of perfect behaviour from the church community, making them stressed and even resentful towards the pastor.

        1.1.1.2.5. Con:
        Priests are likely to be good fathers and husbands as they understand that how they behave in their home should reflect biblical teachings. [13]

          1.1.1.2.5.1. Con:
          Some biblical teachings are likely to harm children.

            1.1.1.2.5.1.1. Pro:
            Catholic parents are more likely to be homophobic and transphobic [14], which can create an abusive home environment for LGBT children.

              1.1.1.2.5.1.1.1. Pro:
              In 2019, the Vatican rejected the possibility that people can be transgender [15].

              1.1.1.2.5.1.1.2. Con:
              A study found that knowing an LGBT person can reduce homophobia [16] among religious people.

                1.1.1.2.5.1.1.2.1. Con:
                This study involved adults who were capable of engaging in discussions around homophobia. Children still living at home are unlikely to wield the same powers of persuasion or have comparable self-esteem.

              1.1.1.2.5.1.1.3. Pro:
              The Church disapproves of homosexuality [17].

                1.1.1.2.5.1.1.3.1. Pro:
                The Catholic Church refers to gay sex as an "intrinsic moral evil" [18] and says homosexuality is a "disorder".

                1.1.1.2.5.1.1.3.2. Con:
                Pope Francis has called for increased respect for LGBT people. [19]

            1.1.1.2.5.1.2. Pro:
            Catholic parents who stay together in unhappy marriages [20] rather than divorcing can psychologically harm their children.

              1.1.1.2.5.1.2.1. Pro:
              The Church generally does not allow divorce or remarriage [21].

                1.1.1.2.5.1.2.1.1. Con:
                There is some evidence that Pope Francis is rethinking [22] the strict Catholic rules against divorce.

                1.1.1.2.5.1.2.1.2. Con:
                The Pope has made it easier for couples to get an annulment [23], which indicates a growing recognition of the need for some couples to part ways.

              1.1.1.2.5.1.2.2. Con:
              Divorce can also harm children [24].

          1.1.1.2.5.2. Pro:
          The Bible strongly encourages people to treat others with empathy and compassion [25].

            1.1.1.2.5.2.1. Pro:
            Women are happier [26] in relationships in which their partners empathise with their pain.

        1.1.1.2.6. Con:
        Married American pastors rate their relationships [27] with their spouse and children more highly than the average American does.

      1.1.1.3. Pro:
      Men who did not have families would have more time [28] to devote themselves to the Church.

      1.1.1.4. Con:
      A priest's day-to-day spiritual duties are no busier to a typical job. Few would say that having a family severely compromises being employed or vice versa.

        1.1.1.4.1. Pro:
        Many parents juggle having children and going to work.

        1.1.1.4.2. Con:
        Many people struggle to maintain a work-life balance [29].

        1.1.1.4.3. Con:
        Priests' working hours are more unpredictable [30] than a typical job as they have to respond quickly should a crisis occur, such as a parishioner dying or becoming seriously ill.

          1.1.1.4.3.1. Con:
          Many other jobs [31] offer a poor work-life balance, such as on-call doctors, police officers, paramedics, firefighters, pilots, and journalists. The priesthood is not unique.

        1.1.1.4.4. Pro:
        Priests work fewer hours than people working in very busy careers such as doctors [32], yet no one would suggest doctors should be prohibited from marrying.

          1.1.1.4.4.1. Pro:
          Priests' working hours have declined over time [33] to become more in line with typical 9-5 jobs.

          1.1.1.4.4.2. Con:
          -> See 1.1.1.2.4.1.

        1.1.1.4.5. Con:
        Marriage would involve large changes to the day-to-day life of a priest.

          1.1.1.4.5.1. Con:
          Even if this were true it is likely this impact would be short-term as one would begin to adapt to their circumstances.

      1.1.1.5. Con:
      It is not for the Church to preemptively determine that this conflict is unmanageable by continuing to forbid priests from marrying. Priests should be free to take this risk if they so choose.

        1.1.1.5.1. Con:
        If this change might harm a priest's ability to do their jobs of promoting the word of God, then the Church should not allow such a risk to be taken.

          1.1.1.5.1.1. Con:
          Applying this logic, employers should not have to provide parental leave because this interferes with their job. Yet many support parental leave.

            1.1.1.5.1.1.1. Pro:
            Americans overwhelmingly support [34] paid parental leave.

          1.1.1.5.1.2. Pro:
          The Catholic Church is a charitable organisation [35] on which many depend. This justifies its risk aversion to the work of the church being compromised.

    1.1.2. Con:
    Priests will be able to adapt to this change in the same way that they will adapt should there be another shift in Catholic teachings as directed by the leaders of the Catholic church.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      Priests adapted to the major changes [36] introduced after Vatican II, for example celebrating Mass facing the congregation rather than the altar.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      There is a fundamental difference between adapting to an enduring change in one's personal circumstances, such as having a family, and refining a sermon following a shift in teachings. The former is much more difficult than the latter.

        1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
        70% [37] of fathers say that their stress levels increased in the year after their first child was born. Adapting to changes in Church teachings is unlikely to be as stressful as this.

        1.1.2.2.2. Pro:
        56% [38] of fathers report that their physical health deteriorated in at least one way after becoming a father. It is unlikely that a change in Church teachings would have a comparable physical impact to this.

        1.1.2.2.3. Pro:
        Having a baby often entails sleep disruption [39], which affects mood, memory, mental health, and perceived quality of life. Changes to Church teachings are unlikely to disrupt sleep as much as having a baby.

      1.1.2.3. Pro:
      The Catholic church demonstrated the ability to adapt to the 2014 Extraordinary Synod on the Family, [40] where a number of changes effectively meant that the synod became a more deliberative body. This was a body that now encouraged more debate and resulted in strong participation from bishops.

    1.1.3. Con:
    Celibacy can negatively impact a priest's mental health, which is likely to hinder his spiritual duties.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      Many priests feel profoundly anxious [41] about being sexually attracted to others.

        1.1.3.1.1. Con:
        This is not exclusive to priests, many people [42] feel anxious when in the proximity of someone they are attracted to.

          1.1.3.1.1.1. Con:
          Owing to a priest's vow of celibacy to God, whom they devote their entire lives too, it is likely that they experience a more acute sense of anxiety.

      1.1.3.2. Pro:
      One study found that 60% [43] of Catholic priests have sought help through counselling, citing reasons such as depression and stress. Some of this may be attributable to the pressures of maintaining celibacy.

        1.1.3.2.1. Pro:
        Sexual desire is one of the strongest of human needs. [44] Denying this for life is a challenge.

        1.1.3.2.2. Pro:
        Many priests live highly isolated lives, which can challenge mental well-being. [45] Celibacy enhances this isolation.

        1.1.3.2.3. Pro:
        Sexual intimacy can aid in the managing of mental health issues. [46]

        1.1.3.2.4. Con:
        Priests are some of the happiest members [47] of American society.

        1.1.3.2.5. Con:
        Celibacy is a sacrifice priests make in order to ensure they are not distracted from God's word.

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      Many priests feel lonely or isolated [48], leading to mental health issues, which is likely linked to their celibacy requirements.

        1.1.3.3.1. Con:
        Priests tend to be at the centre of the religious community, so they are in a good position to increase their social interactions to combat loneliness and isolation.

      1.1.3.4. Con:
      The mental health benefits of being religious [49] and feeling closer to God due to celibacy could outweigh any negative effect.

    1.1.4. Con:
    If priests were married they would be better equipped to counsel members of the Church on issues involving family and marriage.

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      Sociologist and former monk, Richard Sipe, argues that celibacy promotes psychosexual immaturity [50]. This likely impairs a priest's judgement and counsel with respect to intimate relationships.

      1.1.4.2. Con:
      One does not have to [51] commit adultery to counsel someone who has committed adultery. In other words, it's possible to counsel someone without having the same experience as them.

      1.1.4.3. Pro:
      An important role of the parish priest is to prepare engaged couples [52] for married life.

      1.1.4.4. Con:
      Priests can advise these couples about the religious teachings [53] of the Church in relation to marriage without needing an experience of marriage.

        1.1.4.4.1. Con:
        Not all issues that arise in marriages can be directly resolved with religious teachings.

          1.1.4.4.1.1. Pro:
          One common marital issue is a lack of sexual intimacy [54], which is likely better addressed by sex therapy than religious teachings from the couple's priest.

        1.1.4.4.2. Pro:
        Priests can advise on issues of marriage better when they are unmarried because they are better able to detach themselves from similar worldly experiences and give objective advice based on religious teachings.

      1.1.4.5. Pro:
      Married couples sometimes ask their priest [55] for marriage counselling or advice during difficult periods of their relationship.

        1.1.4.5.1. Pro:
        A priest can help couples implement techniques [56] to improve the quality of their relationship.

    1.1.5. Pro:
    Married Catholic priests would be following the examples of Jesus less closely.

      1.1.5.1. Pro:
      Jesus practiced celibacy. [57]

        1.1.5.1.1. Pro:
        Priests are supposed to emulate [58] Jesus Christ.

          1.1.5.1.1.1. Pro:
          Priests are supposed to act in place of Jesus [59] on Earth, so need to follow his example as closely as possible.

      1.1.5.2. Con:
      Jesus allowed others [60] the freedom to choose whether or not to live celibate lives.

  1.2. Con:
  Catholic teachings forbid priests from marrying.

    1.2.1. Con:
    The Catholic Church did not formally ban priests marrying until around 1,000 years ago [61], indicating that even the Church had not identified a clear message in the Bible in which priests marrying was forbidden.

      1.2.1.1. Con:
      This could merely indicate the a different interpretation became more favoured at a later time.

      1.2.1.2. Pro:
      The Bible does not lay out clear rules [62] surrounding priesthood.

        1.2.1.2.1. Pro:
        Priestly celibacy is merely a discipline of the Church [63], not a doctrine or dogma, meaning it is a man-made rule without strict biblical foundations which can be changed.

          1.2.1.2.1.1. Pro:
          Examples of other disciplines which have been changed include the reduction in the length of fasting required before receiving Communion. [64]

    1.2.2. Con:
    Catholic law [65] permits priests who father children to remain in the priesthood. It should morally follow from this that they can marry too.

      1.2.2.1. Pro:
      The Vatican has guidelines [66] on what should occur when priests father children, suggesting it is common.

      1.2.2.2. Pro:
      Many children suffer as a result of the contradiction that priests are tacitly permitted to have children, yet cannot marry their mothers.

        1.2.2.2.1. Pro:
        Coping International, a charity which supports children of religious clergy, has 50,000 users [67] worldwide.

        1.2.2.2.2. Pro:
        A child's very existence may be treated as a secret [68].

        1.2.2.2.3. Con:
        A priest could perform his duties as the father of the child while remaining in the priesthood and without marrying the mother.

      1.2.2.3. Pro:
      To tacitly allow priests to have children, while forbidding marriage, unnecessarily forces the mothers into single parenting and the hardships that come with it.

        1.2.2.3.1. Con:
        Vatican guidelines state that priests should provide economic support [69] for any children, reducing the burden on single mothers.

        1.2.2.3.2. Pro:
        It is often difficult for single mothers to balance [70] being able to care for their children and maintaining a job in order to provide for their children.

      1.2.2.4. Con:
      The fact Catholic law tacitly permits priests having children by not removing them for having done so is not the same as morally condoning their broken vows of celibacy.

        1.2.2.4.1. Pro:
        Catholicism puts great emphasis on forgiveness [71], which is not the same as excusing someone from their sins. Therefore, the rules relating to priests who father children likely reflect forgiveness.

        1.2.2.4.2. Pro:
        The rules could reflect an aversion to scandal from the Church, as it would reflect badly on the Church if priests were leaving due to their broken vows.

      1.2.2.5. Con:
      Guidelines issued by the Vatican recommend [72] that a priest leaves the Church to be able to dedicate himself to his child, rather than remaining in the priesthood.

    1.2.3. Pro:
    Allowing priests to marry would undermine traditional practices.

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      There has generally been a striking lack of consensus on the question of clerical celibacy.

        1.2.3.1.1. Pro:
        Priests from other denominations [73] of Christianity who were already married can legitimately convert to Catholicism.

        1.2.3.1.2. Pro:
        If individual lapses are made by priests in their commitment to maintaining celibacy, it is likely that these will be forgiven [74].

        1.2.3.1.3. Pro:
        The Eastern Catholic Churches [75] do not follow the same traditions as the Roman Catholic Churches.

          1.2.3.1.3.1. Pro:
          Priests are permitted to marry in the Eastern Catholic rites. [76]

      1.2.3.2. Pro:
      Celibacy of priests has been a part of mainstream Catholicism for at least 1,000 years [77].

    1.2.4. Pro:
    The bible forbids priests marrying.

      1.2.4.1. Pro:
      1 Corinthians 7:32-34 [78] suggests that only unmarried men are able to put God first. Therefore, for a priest to be properly devoted to God, he must be unmarried.

        1.2.4.1.1. Con:
        Married priests will be able to better fulfil some spiritual duties.

      1.2.4.2. Con:
      1 Corinthians 7:28 [79] says "if you do marry, you have not sinned." It follows that it would not be a sin for a priest to be married.

      1.2.4.3. Pro:
      In Matthew 16:24 [80] Jesus tells his disciples, "[w]hoever wants to be my disciple must deny themselves and take up their cross and follow me."

        1.2.4.3.1. Pro:
        The fact that Jesus uses the word "must" implies that "denying themselves" is obligatory in order to be properly devoted to him.

          1.2.4.3.1.1. Pro:
          "Denying oneself" can be interpreted as sacrificing one's desires, including taking a vow of celibacy.

        1.2.4.3.2. Pro:
        Similarly, Matthew 19:12 [81] says 'Some are eunuchs because they were born that way; others were made that way by men; and others have renounced marriage because of the kingdom of heaven. The one who can accept this should accept it.'. This suggests that those who can renounce marriage should do so to follow Jesus.

      1.2.4.4. Con:
      The Bible should not be taken literally in relation to the rules of priesthood.

        1.2.4.4.1. Pro:
        The Bible is a product of its time. As such, it offers outdated views about priests marrying.

          1.2.4.4.1.1. Con:
          The Bible is the word of an all-seeing and perfect God. The meaning behind these words cannot thus be outdated.

            1.2.4.4.1.1.1. Con:
            The earliest accounts [82] in the Bible were passed down orally for generations before becoming a part of it. As such, it is highly likely that they evolved to apply to the specific cultural circumstances at the time, rendering them less applicable today.

              1.2.4.4.1.1.1.1. Con:
              Despite being written over 1,600 years [83] the Bible retains a fairly unified and coherent message, suggesting cultural influences were not strong.

            1.2.4.4.1.1.2. Con:
            Many Christians ignore parts [84] of the Bible, believing them to be obsolete and outdated.

              1.2.4.4.1.1.2.1. Con:
              "Many Christians" refers here to imperfect human beings and thus they are more likely to be wrong than divine beings.

                1.2.4.4.1.1.2.1.1. Con:
                The Bible was written by human beings [85] too; their propensity to be wrong presumably made its way into parts of the Bible as well, thus justifying why some parts should be ignored.

            1.2.4.4.1.1.3. Pro:
            As God is all-knowing and perfect He would have anticipated social changes, and therefore provided more adaptable or different rules on marriage if He had intended the Church's doctrine on marriage to change alongside social ideas.

        1.2.4.4.2. Pro:
        An early Latin interpretation [86] of the Gospels shows that they should be interpreted as allegories and not literal truth.

      1.2.4.5. Con:
      The messages in the bible are contradictory. For this reason, the content of the bible should not be considered the final authority on this issue.

        1.2.4.5.1. Pro:
        It is futile trying to discover the "true" message of the bible when there are so many different interpretations.

          1.2.4.5.1.1. Con:
          All forms of expressive language are subject to diverging interpretation. This does not strip language of any significance.

          1.2.4.5.1.2. Con:
          It is possible to resolve these contradictions by receiving spiritual guidance from those who are closer to God.

            1.2.4.5.1.2.1. Con:
            Even Catholic spiritual leaders disagree [87] about the teachings. Therefore, they are unlikely to offer the clarification sought.

            1.2.4.5.1.2.2. Pro:
            The doctrine of papal infallibility [88] means that if the Pope gives spiritual guidance on the Bible, then it cannot be questioned.

            1.2.4.5.1.2.3. Pro:
            The hierarchy within the Catholic Church exists partly to resolve any disputes over biblical interpretation.

              1.2.4.5.1.2.3.1. Pro:
              The local priest can provide members of the community with biblical guidance, even when those higher up in the structure of the Catholic Church have not commented on the specific matter.

        1.2.4.5.2. Con:
        When read in their correct context [89], many so-called contradictions can be understood as not contradicting each other.

      1.2.4.6. Con:
      According to the Bible, Pope Peter [90] was married.

        1.2.4.6.1. Pro:
        Pope Peter's mother-in-law [91] has been referenced in biblical texts, suggesting that he was married.

      1.2.4.7. Pro:
      -> See 1.1.5.

      1.2.4.8. Con:
      Other Christian denominations [92], which also use the Bible, allow priests to marry.

        1.2.4.8.1. Pro:
        -> See 1.2.3.1.3.1.

        1.2.4.8.2. Pro:
        Protestant [93] clergy are allowed to be married.

        1.2.4.8.3. Con:
        This does not mean that the Bible allows priests to marry, as these faiths could just be misinterpreting the Bible's teaching.

  1.3. Pro:
  Catholic priests being allowed to marry would be beneficial to the church.

    1.3.1. Con:
    Marriages within the clergy can breakdown causing suffering throughout [94] the Church.

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      If priests are too preoccupied with the breakdown of their marriage, the amount of attention they pay to their pastoral duties may decline.

      1.3.1.2. Con:
      As long as the priest has not been adulterous or abusive, parishioners are unlikely to feel upset that his marriage has broken down, as it is a normal occurrence.

        1.3.1.2.1. Pro:
        Most parishioners will know other couples who have had marital breakdowns, as divorce rates are high [95].

        1.3.1.2.2. Con:
        Divorce is not a normal occurrence for religious couples, who have significantly lower divorce rates [96] compared to the general population.

          1.3.1.2.2.1. Pro:
          Couples who regularly practise their religious faith report above average marital satisfaction [97] and commitment to their partners.

          1.3.1.2.2.2. Con:
          Many of the statistics on divorce rates among religious couples are flawed [98] for a number of reasons.

            1.3.1.2.2.2.1. Pro:
            The percentage of religious couples divorcing is often calculated against all adults [99], rather than just married adults. This skews the statistics rendering them inaccurate.

            1.3.1.2.2.2.2. Pro:
            The statistics overlook that many Catholics seek an annulment [100], which is another way of ending a marriage.

      1.3.1.3. Con:
      Any suffering caused to parishioners by the priest's marriage breaking down is unlikely to be that acute because they are unlikely to be that emotionally connected to the priest's marriage in the first place.

    1.3.2. Con:
    Allowing priests to marry may lead to financial complications for the Church.

      1.3.2.1. Pro:
      If priests marry young and they do not use contraception, it is likely [101] they will have large families who will in all likelihood be financially supported by the church.

        1.3.2.1.1. Con:
        If priests do have large families, they can be supported financially by their wife instead of the Church.

          1.3.2.1.1.1. Pro:
          In the majority [102] of American households, both men and women are employed.

            1.3.2.1.1.1.1. Con:
            The Catholic Church identifies women as being more "natural caregivers for children [103]", compared to men who "more naturally work outside the home". This indicates that a priest's wife is more likely to remain at home to raise the family.

              1.3.2.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
              58% [104] of white Catholics in the US think that children are better off if a parent remains at home to care for the family.

              1.3.2.1.1.1.1.2. Pro:
              73% [105] of Hispanics in the US - 57% [106] of which self-identify as Catholic - say children are better off with a stay-at-home parent.

              1.3.2.1.1.1.1.3. Con:
              The Catholic Church does not strictly prescribe set roles for mothers; at times it even commends working women [107].

          1.3.2.1.1.2. Con:
          36% [108] of Catholic households earn less than $30,000 a year, which puts them at the brink of financial jeopardy [109]. Therefore, financially supporting a priest's large family will be extremely difficult without at least some help from the Church.

      1.3.2.2. Con:
      There is no reason why this will amount to financial complication when the income of a priest can merely be raised in accordance with their changing needs.

        1.3.2.2.1. Pro:
        An increase in income would not count as excessive accumulation of wealth [110] because it would only be provided so priests can better fulfil new responsibilities. Thus, it would not be outside religious teachings.

        1.3.2.2.2. Pro:
        The Roman Catholic Church has the wealth [111] to fund more generous financial compensation programmes for priests.

          1.3.2.2.2.1. Con:
          This wealth can be put to better use elsewhere.

            1.3.2.2.2.1.1. Pro:
            There are 429 million Catholics [112] in Latin America, with around 30% [113] of the total population living in poverty. It is more imperative to invest in alleviating this.

            1.3.2.2.2.1.2. Con:
            The huge scale [114] of this wealth means it can easily be split between multiple purposes.

          1.3.2.2.2.2. Pro:
          The Vatican is estimated to own up to billions of euros [115] worth of assets.

            1.3.2.2.2.2.1. Con:
            Not all of this is liquid, but includes buildings and artworks [116] which the Church is unlikely to sell.

              1.3.2.2.2.2.1.1. Con:
              If the Church is morally called upon to better compensate priests, then its financial unwillingness to sell certain assets should be inconsequential.

              1.3.2.2.2.2.1.2. Con:
              The Catholic Church's artworks could likely be liquidated very quickly if needed because they would be highly sought after by art dealers, museums, and private owners.

          1.3.2.2.2.3. Con:
          While the Church may be wealthy, individual parishes and dioceses are often not [117], as they are self-funded [118] and rely on donations from parishioners and their own trust funds.

          1.3.2.2.2.4. Pro:
          One of the ways the Church generates wealth is by renting out [119] access to chapels.

        1.3.2.2.3. Con:
        Raising salaries will lead to financial complications in poorer areas, as priests' salaries are paid for by parishioners' donations [120].

          1.3.2.2.3.1. Pro:
          Increased salaries will mean other parish services may have to be cut back, such as charitable services [121].

          1.3.2.2.3.2. Pro:
          Some dioceses are so underfunded at present that they are already struggling to pay [122] priests' wages. This would likely get worse if salaries were raised.

      1.3.2.3. Pro:
      Inheritance rights and widows' pensions [123] would need to be given by the Church to families of priests who die.

      1.3.2.4. Con:
