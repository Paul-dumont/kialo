Discussion Name:
Should the US sell weapons with nuclear capabilities to Ukraine?

1. Thesis:
The US should sell weapons with nuclear capabilities to Ukraine.

  1.1. Pro:
  As per deterrence theory [1], the likelihood of a full-scale war between Russia and Ukraine (and their allies) would be reduced by the presence of nuclear weapons in Ukraine.

    1.1.1. Con:
    Transferring nuclear-capable weapons to Ukraine could increase the likelihood of global nuclear conflict.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      This could create a pretext for Russia to use tactical nuclear weapons in Ukraine. The United States has repeatedly emphasized that this is a red line [2] in the conflict.

      1.1.1.2. Pro:
      The lack of nuclear weapons expertise [3] within the Ukrainian military heightens the risk of mismanagement, potentially leading to catastrophic outcomes.

      1.1.1.3. Con:
      Existing tensions [4] between Russia, Ukraine, and their allies already make a global nuclear conflict likely. [5]

      1.1.1.4. Con:
      Nuclear weapons are often hosted [6] by states allied with nuclear powers. History has shown that this does not categorically result in nuclear conflict.

        1.1.1.4.1. Con:
        Cuba hosting nuclear weapons for Russia almost resulted [7] in a global nuclear conflict.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    Many conventional weapons also have dual-use nuclear capabilities [8]. These weapons are necessary for Ukraine to resist Russian occupation of their territory.

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      Representatives from Russia's state Duma have warned that supplying Ukraine with conventional arms capable of striking Russian cities could provoke [9] a nuclear response.

        1.1.2.1.1. Con:
        Ukraine has successfully completed [10] several UAV attacks on Russian territory without provoking a nuclear response.

        1.1.2.1.2. Pro:
        Russia reserves the right [11] to use nuclear weapons in response to a large-scale conventional attack on its territory.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      To counteract Russian air superiority, it is critical to provide Ukraine with state-of-the-art fighter jets such as the F-16, which is recognised as having nuclear capabilities [12].

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        The US can help Ukraine by providing conventional anti-aircraft weapons that lack nuclear capabilities, such as surface-to-air [13] missile units.

        1.1.2.2.2. Pro:
        The Ukrainian air force is mostly composed of outdated Soviet-era aircraft. Without significant modernization it is unlikely to threaten [14] their technologically superior Russian counterparts.

    1.1.3. Con:
    Conversely, arming Ukraine with nuclear weapons reduces the possibility of reaching a negotiated peace settlement with Russia.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      As it might worsen the pursuit of peace, the United States should only consider selling nuclear weapons to Ukraine after the war.

        1.1.3.1.1. Con:
        Giving Ukraine a nuclear arsenal would likely prevent the permanent cessation of hosilities, as it would directly contradict many of Russia's key security concerns [15].

        1.1.3.1.2. Pro:
        Waiting until after the war might allow the US to deliver the weapons discreetly without alarming Russia.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      As a strongman [16] leader, Putin is unlikely to concede the conflict without a strong deterrent.

        1.1.3.2.1. Pro:
        Russia's willingness and ability to resist extreme sanctions [17] from the entire West shows Putin's determination to claim victory.

        1.1.3.2.2. Pro:
        Conceding the war on any other terms would constitute a humiliating defeat for Putin. [18]

        1.1.3.2.3. Con:
        A stronger show of strength might merely cause Putin to escalate the conflict.

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      Russia has repeatedly emphasized that the demilitarization [19] of Ukraine is a critical strategic goal in their ongoing conflict.

        1.1.3.3.1. Con:
        Russia's push for demilitarisation might be so as to make it easier to capture and retain key Ukrainian territories.

        1.1.3.3.2. Pro:
        As Russia's objective is for Ukraine to be less of a threat, arming Ukraine with more weapons would only prolong the war.

    1.1.4. Pro:
    Mutually assured destruction [20] is a key aspect of nuclear deterrence. Without own nuclear capabilities, Ukraine is incapable of deterring the potential use of nuclear weapons by hostile states.

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      Human intervention [21], rather than the threat of mutually assured destruction, prevented the Cuban Missile Crisis from ending in global nuclear warfare.

        1.1.4.1.1. Pro:
        The Cuban Missile crisis demonstrated how states may use negotiation and diplomacy to avert nuclear confrontation in this scenario.

      1.1.4.2. Pro:
      Thermonuclear weapons are the most powerful weapons [22] available to any military and hence only thermonuclear weapons can match their own threat.

        1.1.4.2.1. Pro:
        The singular use of nuclear weapons in history caused the immediate surrender [23] of the nations that lacked nuclear weapons.

        1.1.4.2.2. Con:
        The strategy of deterrence through nuclear weapons remains unproven in situations where a full-scale conflict is already underway.

      1.1.4.3. Con:
      It is unlikely that Ukraine would pose a threat to Russia by acquiring nuclear weapons from the United States.

        1.1.4.3.1. Pro:
        Ukraine does not have the economic power [24] to purchase enough nuclear weapons to threaten Russia.

        1.1.4.3.2. Con:
        Even a few nuclear weapons could cause major damage. [25]

        1.1.4.3.3. Con:
        If Ukraine acts first, even with a small arsenal, it could eliminate Russia as a threat.

          1.1.4.3.3.1. Pro:
          Nuclear theory emphasizes the tactical importance of a 'first strike [26]', as it can potentially neutralize the opponent's capacity to retaliate.

        1.1.4.3.4. Pro:
        Russia has the largest nuclear arsenal [27] in the world and would likely not be intimidated by Ukraine's new and smaller arsenal.

        1.1.4.3.5. Pro:
        Russia has had nuclear missiles for decades longer [28] and hence has the expertise to use and defend against them.

  1.2. Pro:
  The United States is morally and politically obliged to provide Ukraine with whatever it needs to deter Russian aggression, including nuclear weapons.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Providing Ukraine with assistance that could lead to the end of the conflict would ease the pressure on the United States.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      The US has already spent $75 billion [29] on Ukraine since the war began, which it could have used for its own economic needs.

        1.2.1.1.1. Con:
        While large, this amount is only a small fraction [30] of the United States' $2 trillion annual military budget. [31]

        1.2.1.1.2. Pro:
        If spent in the US, these funds could tangibly improve the lives of thousands of American citizens, who have a greater claim to these funds.

        1.2.1.1.3. Con:
        A significant amount of US military aid has been provided to Ukraine on a lend-lease [32] basis. Consequently, the US stands to be reimbursed for a portion of this spending.

        1.2.1.1.4. Con:
        Around $26bn (34% of the total) of this funding was spent on financial aid [33]. Continuing this support is inherently in the interests of the United States, as it is supports [34] the economic development of an ideologically aligned partner.

        1.2.1.1.5. Pro:
        Large amounts of the USA's soaring national debt is owned [35] by its geopolitical rivals. Nations such as China, therefore, may profit from the reckless US investment strategy in Ukraine.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      Continuing to assist Ukraine only reinforces the United States' obligation to Ukraine, which might be invoked in future crises.

        1.2.1.2.1. Con:
        Providing Ukraine with nuclear weapons would likely remove the need to support its military any further.

        1.2.1.2.2. Pro:
        The proximity of Ukraine to Russia and the tensions [36] between the two countries make it likely that further crises will occur.

    1.2.2. Con:
    The United States is only obliged to provide for its own needs.

      1.2.2.1. Con:
      Global influence [37] is central to protecting and promoting US interests, both domestically and internationally. As such, the US must protect the interests of its international partners.

        1.2.2.1.1. Pro:
        Acting as a reliable partner to its geopolitical allies is essential for the US to maintain its soft power [38].

        1.2.2.1.2. Con:
        Global influence is not considered to be a particularly crucial policy by the US electorate [39]. If anything, there is a significant level of bipartisan ambivalence [40] towards greater US involvement in geopolitical affairs.

      1.2.2.2. Pro:
      Pursuing a globalist [41] policy agenda has failed the United States.

        1.2.2.2.1. Pro:
        The globalist philosophy [42] is not grounded in laws or a social contract, and hence the only fixed political obligations are to citizens.

      1.2.2.3. Pro:
      The United States exists to serve the interests [43] of its own citizens. These interests are best protected by focusing on domestic policy rather than engaging in external conflicts.

        1.2.2.3.1. Con:
        Historically speaking, the doctrine of isolationism [44] has only served to undermine the interests of both the United States and the Western world at large.

        1.2.2.3.2. Pro:
        US engagement in foreign conflicts [45] and proxy wars [46] has largely failed to achieve its stated geopolitical goals, while incurring massive economic [47] and human costs.

      1.2.2.4. Con:
      The US has frequently cited its moral and political obligations to other nations as a justification for its policy of interventionism (p. 3 [48]).

        1.2.2.4.1. Pro:
        John F. Kennedy justified US military involvement [49] in Vietnam by citing their moral obligation [50] to prevent the spread of communism.

        1.2.2.4.2. Con:
        On several occasions, the US has used moral rhetoric as a justification for intervention to then install brutal dictators [51] or authoritarians. As such, there is a disconnect between American rhetoric and the reality of their actions.

        1.2.2.4.3. Pro:
        In several cases, US interventions legitimately [52] targeted regimes that had committed gross human rights violations or crimes against humanity.

        1.2.2.4.4. Con:
        Moral and political obligations have historically been used by the US as a means of circumventing [53] international law to further its own foreign policy goals [54].

    1.2.3. Pro:
    As the leader of the free world [55], the United States has a moral duty to promote freedom and sovereign rule across the world.

      1.2.3.1. Pro:
      Taking control of Ukraine would allow Russia's undemocratic [56] and repressive [57] form of governance to spread and gain more power, which is a threat to the free world.

        1.2.3.1.1. Con:
        Democratic backsliding and autocratization are representative of a broader ideological shift [58] in global politics. As such, this cannot be solely attributed to Russia.

        1.2.3.1.2. Pro:
        The US failed Ukraine when it did nothing during the annexation of Crimea [59], and it owes it to Ukraine to make up for that by supplying nuclear weapons.

      1.2.3.2. Con:
      The US has not acted [60] as the leader of the free world in recent years.

        1.2.3.2.1. Con:
        Providing crucial assistance would help restore [61] the US's image as a world leader.

    1.2.4. Pro:
    It is in the political interests of the United States to support Ukraine and undermine Russia.

      1.2.4.1. Pro:
      Undermining Russia benefits the United States since Russia is a competitor [62] for the role of global leader.

        1.2.4.1.1. Pro:
        Through its assistance to Ukraine, the US is able to display [63] the strength of its military and the value of its allyship.

      1.2.4.2. Con:
      Ukraine is unlikely to be a useful ally to the United States beyond opposing Russia.

        1.2.4.2.1. Pro:
        Trade between the United States and Ukraine [64] is smaller than trade with Russia. [65]

        1.2.4.2.2. Con:
        Russia is largely viewed [66] as the greatest enemy of the United States, and hence its suppression is, on its own, enough incentive for the United States to prioritise its relations with Ukraine.

      1.2.4.3. Pro:
      In a climate of political polarization, support for Ukraine is largely bipartisan [67] in the US. As such, continuing this support is politically advantageous for the Biden administration.

        1.2.4.3.1. Con:
        While this may be true of congressional parties, US public opinion on financial support for Ukraine is divided [68] along partisan lines.

      1.2.4.4. Con:
      The Western policy of politically and economically isolating Russia could help to create a multipolar world order that challenges the current US hegemony.

        1.2.4.4.1. Pro:
        Isolating Russia is likely to push the regime to strengthen [69] its relations with China, which together would pose a greater threat to the United States.

          1.2.4.4.1.1. Pro:
          Russia and China have grown closer [70] in the aftermath of the Ukraine war, as a necessity due to the sanctions placed on Russia.

        1.2.4.4.2. Pro:
        Economically isolating Russia has been largely ineffective due to its use of fortress economics [71].

        1.2.4.4.3. Con:
        The petrodollar [72] system effectively ensures that the US will always have significant influence over the global economy.

    1.2.5. Con:
    It is not in the interests of the United States to inflame tensions with Russia.

      1.2.5.1. Pro:
      Russia is an important source [73] of energy for the West.

        1.2.5.1.1. Con:
        The United States no longer depends on Russia for energy.

      1.2.5.2. Pro:
      Russia has been a strategic ally [74] of the United States in the past.

        1.2.5.2.1. Pro:
        Russia is an important ally of the US in combating terrorism [75], which is a highly significant security threat to the United States.

          1.2.5.2.1.1. Con:
          Russia has been formally declared a state sponsor of terrorism by the EU [76], which is a key strategic partner of the US.

        1.2.5.2.2. Con:
        In more recent years, Russia and the United States have been on opposite sides [77] of most issues.

      1.2.5.3. Con:
      In recent years, Russia has been the instigator of tensions. Therefore, the US retaliating through its support for Ukraine would not be unprovoked.

        1.2.5.3.1. Pro:
        The Kremlin has been implicated [78] in cyberattacks and election interference in the United States.

    1.2.6. Con:
    The US does not have the right or jurisdiction to act independently on the matter.

      1.2.6.1. Pro:
      Both Russia and Ukraine are sovereign countries [79] and are not under the authority of the United States.

        1.2.6.1.1. Con:
        The principle of sovereignty refers to internal matters [80] and does not apply when countries are engaged in external conflict.

        1.2.6.1.2. Con:
        Russia violated the sovereignty [81] of Ukraine by invading it and, hence, does not deserve to have its sovereignty respected.

        1.2.6.1.3. Pro:
        Both the US and Russia (then the USSR) legally codified their mutual commitment to respecting state sovereignty [82] in the Helsinki Accords (1975).

      1.2.6.2. Pro:
      Engaging the issue of the war in Ukraine is the purview [83] of the UN Security Council (UNSC).

        1.2.6.2.1. Con:
        The UN Security Council is, by design [84], an ineffective mechanism for preventing aggression by major world powers.

          1.2.6.2.1.1. Pro:
          Permanent members of the UNSC can unilaterally veto [85] resolutions.

            1.2.6.2.1.1.1. Pro:
            The UN Security Council cannot act on the matter of Ukraine due to the influence [86] and veto powers [87] of permanent members [88] Russia and China.

            1.2.6.2.1.1.2. Con:
            There are mechanisms within the UN, such as a majority General Assembly vote [89], that can be used to overrule a veto.

          1.2.6.2.1.2. Con:
          Inbuilt mechanisms, such as the “Uniting for Peace” resolution [90], preserve the UN's ability to resolve conflicts when the security council fails to act.

        1.2.6.2.2. Con:
        The US has historically ignored UN Security Council resolutions when they counteract the aims of American foreign policy.

          1.2.6.2.2.1. Pro:
          The US has blocked [91] dozens of UNSC resolutions against Israel, a key strategic ally of the US in the Middle East.

          1.2.6.2.2.2. Con:
          The fact that the US has done so in the past does not mean it should continue to do so, especially since such actions have made the UNSC essentially toothless.

        1.2.6.2.3. Pro:
        Relying on the UNSC to act on the matter of the Russia-Ukraine war reinforces the importance of abiding by a rules-based international order. [92]

      1.2.6.3. Con:
      The United States is a sovereign state with the right to engage in bilateral diplomacy [93] and collective military agreements [94], irrespective of the wishes of other parties.

        1.2.6.3.1. Con:
        The US is a member of several military treaties (such as NATO [95]) with codified obligations and processes. As such, US foreign policy cannot supersede these agreements without the prior consent of other parties.

          1.2.6.3.1.1. Pro:
          As per Article 5 [96] of the NATO Charter, signatories are required to defend one another in the event of an attack.

          1.2.6.3.1.2. Con:
          Congress [97] is the only body capable of regulating the US' ability to make diplomatic or military alliances with other states.

      1.2.6.4. Con:
      As a guarantor of the Budapest Memorandum [98], the US is obliged to protect the territorial integrity of Ukraine.

        1.2.6.4.1. Con:
        The Budapest Memorandum ensured Ukraine's accession [99] to the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons. Supplying Ukraine with nuclear-capable arms would violate this aspect of the agreement.

          1.2.6.4.1.1. Con:
          The treaty has been violated [100] several times, and is effectively nullified by Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

        1.2.6.4.2. Pro:
        By encouraging [101] Ukraine's entry into the Memorandum, the US claimed responsibility for Ukraine's security.

  1.3. Con:
  Ukraine is not fit to be a nuclear power; making it one could lead to several negative consequences.

    1.3.1. Con:
    Much less deserving nations have developed or recieved nuclear weapons without causing a global catastrophe.

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      North Korea performs worse [102] on most metrics than Ukraine but possesses nuclear weapons.

        1.3.1.1.1. Con:
        North Korea is the perfect example of why some countries should not get nuclear weapons, as they have incited global tensions [103] and panic with their arsenal.

        1.3.1.1.2. Pro:
        Despite North Korea having nuclear weapons and having tensions with South Korea, no conflict has ensued, even though South Korea lacks [104] nuclear weapons.

        1.3.1.1.3. Pro:
        North Korea [105] has a worse economy than Ukraine [106], but has still managed to preserve and develop its nuclear arsenal.

      1.3.1.2. Pro:
      Pakistan and India, two countries with questionable internal politics and tension [107] between them, have possessed nuclear weapons for decades without resulting in their use.

        1.3.1.2.1. Pro:
        The nuclear status of both countries has arguably prevented the outbreak of a conventional war between India and Pakistan.

        1.3.1.2.2. Con:
        The continued degradation of bilateral relations between India and Paskistan increases the likelihood of an actual nuclear confrontation [108] occurring.

      1.3.1.3. Con:
      The acquisition of nuclear weapons is driven by regional and geopolitical ambition [109], rather than merit. As such, states with the ambition to develop these weapons are deserving of them.

        1.3.1.3.1. Pro:
        For smaller states, the possession of nuclear weapons can be an effective means of both deterring [110] aggression and gaining concessions from major geopolitical powers.

        1.3.1.3.2. Pro:
        States with nuclear ambitions often form alliances with existing nuclear powers that share similar geopolitical goals [111].

          1.3.1.3.2.1. Pro:
          Iran, for example, has received help from Russia [112] in developing nuclear weapons. Both of these parties are geopolitically opposed to the goals of the United States.

        1.3.1.3.3. Con:
        Ambition is a dangerous metric for determining who gets nuclear weapons, as geopolitical ambition is often the incentive [113] for starting wars.

      1.3.1.4. Con:
      States that have pursued or achieved the aquisition of nuclear weapons have done so at the cost of destablizing regional and international relations [114], to catastrophic effect.

        1.3.1.4.1. Pro:
        Theoretically, agreeing to denuclearization treaties [115] with states such as Iran could vastly reduce intra-regional tensions and improve stability in the Middle East.

        1.3.1.4.2. Con:
        This is largely true of regions with existing regional tensions. The acquisition of nuclear weapons in European states such as France or the UK did little to affect regional harmony and cooperation.

    1.3.2. Pro:
    Ukraine is in a desperate geopolitical position, which increases the likelihood of it misusing nuclear weapons.

      1.3.2.1. Con:

        Ukraine has increased unity [116] and political cohesion amongst its Western partners.

        Ukraine has severed diplomatic ties with several nations [118] due to their support for Russia.

      1.3.2.2. Con:

      1.3.2.3. Pro:
      Unlike many former Soviet bloc states [119], Ukraine is not a NATO member state. In lieu of the protection offered by NATO, Ukraine may be more likely to use nuclear weapons.

        1.3.2.3.1. Con:
        The accession of Ukraine to NATO would much more effectively deter Russian aggression than supplying Ukraine with its own nuclear-capable weapons.

          1.3.2.3.1.1. Con:
          Although it is not yet a member, Ukraine is a strategic partner [120] of NATO. Therefore, it is likely to avail of its full military and logistical support if necessary.

        1.3.2.3.2. Con:
        As a close partner of NATO, Ukraine is unlikely to use nuclear weapons in a manner that is inconsistent with their policy of deterrence [121].

        1.3.2.3.3. Pro:

    1.3.3. Con:

      1.3.3.4. Con:

      1.3.3.5. Con:

    1.3.6. Pro:

      1.3.6.2. Con:

  1.4. Con:
  The proliferation of nuclear weapons is unethical, irrespective of the context in which they are developed or transferred.

    1.4.1. Pro:

        1.4.1.1.2. Con:
        Ukraine acquiring nuclear weapons could also put an end to the war and the ecological damage it has caused.

      1.4.1.2. Con:

    1.4.2. Pro:
    Major religions, including both Islam [148] and Catholicism [149], generally oppose the use of nuclear weapons in all contexts.

      1.4.2.1. Con:
      Both religions have texts supporting large-scale wars, and neither of them has direct mentions of nuclear weapons. [150]

        1.4.2.1.1. Con:
        Contemporary Catholic doctrine has made a steadfast commitment to peace and justice [151], despite its history of endorsing violent conquest.

        1.4.2.1.2. Pro:

        Both religions have a history of partaking in wars [153] of theocratic conquest [154].

        1.4.2.2.1. Con:

        1.4.2.2.2. Pro:

    1.4.3. Pro:
    Selling weapons to Ukraine would be a violation [158] of the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons (NPT [159]).

      1.4.3.1. Pro:
      Transferring nuclear-capable weapons to Ukraine would undermine international arms control efforts, increasing the likelihood of rogue states acquiring nuclear weapons.

          The US has not [164] ratified or adopted the Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons.

        1.4.3.2.2. Con:
        If the U.S. supports Ukraine's nuclearization, it will lose the moral high ground in opposing the nuclear weapons programs of nations like North Korea [165] and Iran [166].

        1.4.3.2.3. Pro:
        Russia, which has suspended [167] many of the same treaties, has signalled its intent to host tactical nuclear weapons in Belarus [168]. Therefore, the US would be ethically justified in following suit.

    1.4.4. Con:
    Non-proliferation perpetuates the existing asymmetric [169] global power dynamics, which is unethical.

      1.4.4.1. Pro:
      Non-proliferation is a tool used by the most powerful countries to maintain military dominance over other nations.

      1.4.4.2. Con:
      The threat of global annihilation through the use of nuclear weapons is a worse outcome than persistent inequality.

        1.4.4.2.1. Con:
        The annihilation of the entire world is already possible [170] with the existing nuclear weapons.

        1.4.4.2.2. Pro:
        Despite the monopoly of violence [171] possesed by nuclear-armed states, smaller non-nuclear states have been able to win economic, political, and diplomatic concesssions [172] from grand powers.

    1.4.5. Con:
    The use of nuclear weapons is morally justifiable if it serves the 'greater good'.

      1.4.5.1. Pro:
      The use of nuclear weapons in World War II was ethically justified as it brought an end [173] to the war, saving countless lives in the process.

        1.4.5.1.1. Pro:
        The estimated casualties [174] for the proposed invasion of Japan (Operation Downfall) ran close to a million on the Allied side and tens of millions for the Japanese.

        1.4.5.1.2. Con:
        The targeting of civilians is inherently wrong and incompatible with the conduct of a just war [175]. As such, the bombing of Hiroshima and Nagasaki was unethical, regardless of how many military casualties were averted.

          1.4.5.1.2.1. Pro:
          Hundreds of thousands of initial survivors of the bombings in Japan, including both adult and child civilians, suffered slow painful deaths [176] consistent with torture.

        1.4.5.1.3. Pro:
        Consequentialism [177] dictates that an action should be judged by its outcomes.

        1.4.5.1.4. Con:
        The use of nuclear weapons in World War II was excessive [178] and occurred when the war was already ending [179]. Justifying the bombings supports a drastic approach to ending conflicts where better alternatives exist.

      1.4.5.2. Con:
      The concept of a 'greater good', especially as it pertains to international relations, is inherently relative [180]. As such, it is not a valid ethical justification for the use of nuclear arms.

        1.4.5.2.1. Pro:
        The subjectivity and vagueness of the term make it dangerous to associate with the use of nuclear weapons, as leaders prioritise outcomes differently and the greater good for one state might mean grave ends for another.

        1.4.5.2.2. Con:
        The greater good is often used to refer to the cessation of bloodshed. In the case of World War II, the deaths caused by the bombings were weighed against the projected deaths [181] if the war continued.

    1.4.6. Con:
    Nuclear non-proliferation requires multilateral cooperation [182] between all nuclear powers. Therefore, if any state pursues unilateral proliferation, all other states are ethically justified in following suit.

      1.4.6.1. Pro:
      International arms control [183] is essential to preserve the current global security architecture.

      1.4.6.2. Con:
      This is akin to claiming that if one citizen breaks a law, all others are justified in doing the same.

      1.4.6.3. Pro:
      The acquisition of nuclear weapons by some states necessitates their acquisition by others in order to create deterrence. A good example is the case of India and Pakistan. [184]

      1.4.6.4. Con:
      The non-compliance of some parties to the non-proliferation agreement creates a need for others to follow suit for their own security.
