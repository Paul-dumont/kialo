Discussion Name:
Is Gender Reassignment Surgery the Best Option for Transgender People?

1. Thesis:
Gender reassignment surgery is the best transition method for transgender individuals.

  1.1. Con:
  Gender reassignment surgery does not resolve gender dysphoria, which is a primary challenge for many transgender individuals.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Allowing a person suffering from gender dysphoria to undergo serious, permanent surgery is inhumane.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      It would be harmful to support the false perception that somebody with body dysmorphia [1] has of their body. This warped reality is no different from transgender people's perception of their gendered body or identity.

        1.1.1.1.1. Con:
        A person with gender dysphoria is different [2] from a person with body dysmorphia; the source of stress for a transgender person is that their body does not reflect their true gender, as opposed to perceived flaws in their body or weight that do not exist.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      Just like the removal of ovaries can be made to prevent or treat cancer [3], gender reassignment surgery can alleviate psychological distress. The nature of the distress is usually debilitating and/or may lead to physical harm.

      1.1.1.3. Pro:
      Gender reassignment surgery poses many health risks [4] (p. 337 [5]).

        1.1.1.3.1. Con:
        The risks posed by gender reassignment surgeries are not greater than those inherent to any other surgery (p. 2 [6]).

        1.1.1.3.2. Pro:
        Those who have undergone reassignment surgery have a significantly higher mortality rate than those who have not (p. 4 [7]).

          1.1.1.3.2.1. Con:
          This compares those transgender individuals who have undergone the surgery against the general population rather than against transgender individuals who have not undergone surgery. As such, this does not prove that the surgery, in this case, was the risk factor.

          1.1.1.3.2.2. Pro:
          Long-term studies have reported higher mortality rates [8] among transgender people who have undergone surgery, compared to the general population.

            1.1.1.3.2.2.1. Con:
            Comparing post-operative transgender people to the general population is not meaningful. Instead, they should be compared to transgender people who have not undergone surgery, to determine the protective effects of the procedure.

            1.1.1.3.2.2.2. Con:
            Studies have found no increased risk [9] for cardiovascular disease or cancer, following surgery. Instead, higher mortality among transgender people are attributable to external factors, including homicide.

              1.1.1.3.2.2.2.1. Pro:
              Top surgery significantly reduces breast cancer risk [10] in transgender men, despite the fact that they remain biologically female.

                1.1.1.3.2.2.2.1.1. Pro:
                Top surgery for transgender men is comparable to preventative mastectomy [11] for cisgender women. The more breast tissue is removed, the lower the risk of cancer for both groups.

                  1.1.1.3.2.2.2.1.1.1. Con:
                  Breast cancer risk is not exclusive to one specific gender [12]. Like women, therefore, cisgender and transgender men must ensure that they are regularly screened for breast cancer.

                1.1.1.3.2.2.2.1.2. Con:
                In transgender men, breast cancer risk is significantly reduced by androgen therapy [13], meaning surgery does not confer unique benefits compared to other transitioning methods.

                  1.1.1.3.2.2.2.1.2.1. Con:
                  Androgen therapy may not have a preventative effect on transgender men who are genetically at risk for breast cancer [14]. In their case, therefore, surgery would be a safer alternative.

                  1.1.1.3.2.2.2.1.2.2. Pro:
                  Estrogen receptor signaling [15] is a major risk factor for breast cancer and accounts for a vast majority of breast cancer cases. Androgen therapy mitigates cancer risk by counteracting against this risk factor.

              1.1.1.3.2.2.2.2. Con:
              The prostate is not removed during bottom surgery [16], meaning transgender women are as much at risk for prostate cancer as cisgender men. Yet, due to their gender presentation, they may fly under the radar for prostate cancer screenings, leading to adverse outcomes.

            1.1.1.3.2.2.3. Pro:
            The types of surgeries [17] involved with transgender care are associated [18] with long-term complications, which increase postoperative mortality.

            1.1.1.3.2.2.4. Pro:
            Transgender people already have a lower life expectancy [19], making any increases in mortality rates very concerning.

      1.1.1.4. Con:
      Gender dysphoria does not prevent the patient from giving informed consent to the procedure if it is deemed medically necessary.

    1.1.2. Con:
    There are transgender people who do not [20] experience gender dysphoria.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      Gender dysphoria in trans individuals is a Eurocentric phenomena [21].

        1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
        Many non-Western cultures [22] welcome and celebrate transgender individuals, leading to greater inclusion and less cause for dysphoria.

        1.1.2.1.2. Con:
        While this may have been true historically, modern transgender identities around the world can and do coincide with dysphoria [23].

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      While it is not synonymous with trans identity, gender dysphoria occurs in the majority [24] of transgender individuals from an early age.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        Approximately 60-90% [25] of trans-identifying children no longer want to transition by the time they reach adulthood.

        1.1.2.2.2. Pro:
        Gender dysphoria in transgender individuals typically first manifests between the ages of 3 and 7 years (p. 1 [26]).

      1.1.2.3. Pro:
      Transmedicalism (assuming that suffering gender dysphoria is intrinsic to being transgender) is a harmful perspective [27] that erases some trans identities.

        1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
        Transmedicalism reframes being transgender as a disorder 'suffered' by cisgender individuals and suggests that those who come to accept their physical biology are not really transgender (p. 59 [28]).

    1.1.3. Con:
    Gender-affirming surgery results in a reduction in the mental health symptoms [29] brought on by gender dysphoria, with symptoms further reducing [30] over time. This suggests that gender reassignment surgery is an effective treatment for gender dysphoria.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      Adults and adolescents [31] experiencing gender dysphoria are more likely to contemplate suicide than the general population (p. 283 [32]). Gender transition improves symptoms that contribute to this.

        1.1.3.1.1. Con:
        A longitudinal study conducted in Sweden found that, even after gender reassignment surgery, suicide rates were higher among transgender people than among the general population (p. 1 [33]).

          1.1.3.1.1.1. Con:
          In the latter part of the study (pp. 4-5 [34]), suicide rates between transgender people and controls were comparable, implying that external factors, such as outdated treatments and social acceptance (p. 7 [35]), are the underlying causes (p. 2 [36]).

        1.1.3.1.2. Pro:
        Gender-affirming care, including surgical interventions, have been found to significantly reduce the risk of suicide [37] among transgender people.

        1.1.3.1.3. Pro:
        Gender reassignment surgery is unique among gender-affirming interventions in that it decreases the utilization of mental health services among transgender people (p. 731 [38]), implying significant psychological improvement.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      Even though people are satisfied with the procedures, there is a paucity of evidence for long-term outcomes (p. 3 [39]).

        1.1.3.2.1. Pro:
        Up to 20% of transgender individuals have remained dysphoric after gender reassignment surgery, suggesting that surgery alone does not fully address dysphoria (p. 2 [40]).

        1.1.3.2.2. Con:
        This is because, in the past, there has been a general lack of interest and accuracy in research pertaining to the trans community (p. 289 [41]).

          1.1.3.2.2.1. Con:
          A study [42] conducted on the general happiness of transgender people showed that the happiness level of transgender individuals who went through GRS slowly returned to their state of happiness prior to GRS causing no real long term change in overall happiness.

    1.1.4. Pro:
    Cognitive and hormone therapy are better at treating gender dysphoria.

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      A systematic review of the effect of cross-sex hormone treatment found that the symptoms from which people with gender dysphoria suffer were alleviated by hormone therapy (p. 1965 [43]).

        1.1.4.1.1. Pro:
        Among young adults, puberty suppression and gender reassignment improve psychological function and well-being (p. 1 [44]).

          1.1.4.1.1.1. Con:
          Children receive puberty-blocking medication [45] to delay their development of secondary sexual characteristics until gender reassignment surgery. This may be detrimental to their health.

            1.1.4.1.1.1.1. Con:
            Puberty blockers for youth are considered to be very safe [46] overall.

            1.1.4.1.1.1.2. Pro:
            A long-term side effect of puberty-blocking medication is lower bone density (p. 4 [47]), which increases the risk of fractures [48].

            1.1.4.1.1.1.3. Con:
            Detrimental health impacts, provided they are disclosed and made clear, are preferable to completely discarding potential life-saving treatments (p. 2 [49]).

            1.1.4.1.1.1.4. Pro:
            The long-term effects of puberty blockers are not yet fully known [50], which makes them a riskier option.

          1.1.4.1.1.2. Pro:
          Puberty-blocking medication and psychological support show greater efficacy than psychological support alone (p. 2206 [51]).

        1.1.4.1.2. Con:
        The systematic review of cross-sex hormonal treatment suffered from a number of methodological limitations.

          1.1.4.1.2.1. Pro:
          All but one of the studies reviewed in the systematic review came from the same clinic, while also lacking consistency and diligence when considering the doses of the hormones used (p. 1963 [52]).

          1.1.4.1.2.2. Con:
          The majority of systematic reviews suffer from limitations, yet they are still considered worthwhile resources for decision-making in healthcare (p. 13 [53]).

      1.1.4.2. Pro:
      Avoidant coping [54] is a general strategy of transgender individuals to cope with external stress which causes depression.

        1.1.4.2.1. Pro:
        The vast majority of mental health issues that transgender people face—feeling suicidal, depressed, or isolated—come from society's harsh response and are not necessarily a direct consequence of being transgender (p. 2 [55]).

        1.1.4.2.2. Con:
        Avoidant coping does not address [56] the root cause of an individuals distress and can exacerbate their suffering long-term.

      1.1.4.3. Con:
      Exposure to conversion therapy - under the guise of cognitive therapy - has been demonstrated to have a detrimental effect [57] and is considered unethical (p. 1 [58]).

        1.1.4.3.1. Pro:
        A 2020 UN report on conversion therapy practices around the world concludes by recommending a global ban with appropriate sanctions for contraventions on the basis that these practices are inhuman, degrading, cruel, and torturous (p. 21 [59]).

        1.1.4.3.2. Con:
        Conversion therapies are condemned (and even outlawed [60]) in many jurisdictions (p. 8 [61]).

          1.1.4.3.2.1. Con:
          Many of the bans on conversion therapies do not protect [62] transgender individuals.

  1.2. Pro:
  Gender reassignment surgery allows transgender individuals, who often feel that they are born in the wrong body [63], to actualize their true identity.

    1.2.1. Con:
    Sex and fetal development are influenced by chromosomes [64]. Since present technology makes it impossible to alter these, a person's sex can never be truly changed.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      Among primates, males and females behave differently [65] since birth. Based on this, we can establish that our gender behavior is determined primarily by biology.

        1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
        There are over 6,000 differences [66] in human gene expression between males and females; this suggests our genetic information [67] has a profound effect on our gender behaviour.

          1.2.1.1.1.1. Con:
          Primary sex determination in mammals does not have a default state [68], so the formation of reproductive organs as an active, gene-directed process has the potential to diverge from the widely understood common precursor.

        1.2.1.1.2. Con:
        Within the animal kingdom, some species exhibit transgender behaviour. For example, lionesses roar [69] and otherwise act and behave like lions.

        1.2.1.1.3. Con:
        Transgender boys and girls have been shown to exhibit cross-sex tendencies very early in their childhood (p. 635 [70]).

        1.2.1.1.4. Con:
        While sex is determined by genetic factors and is therefore physical, gender is a social construct [71].

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      The aspects of sex that current medical technology cannot alter are shrinking as science progresses.

        1.2.1.2.1. Pro:
        Genital reconstruction didn't exist but is now possible [72] due to scientific advancement.

        1.2.1.2.2. Pro:
        Technological advancement has made the performance of uterus transplants possible (p. 894 [73]).

        1.2.1.2.3. Con:
        Even if science completely shrinks the gap between perceivable physical characteristics, it cannot erase one’s gender history, which is important to disclose.

          1.2.1.2.3.1. Pro:
          Gender history provides important information, for example, about genetic risk for certain diseases, especially those associated with the sex chromosomes [74].

          1.2.1.2.3.2. Con:
          Transgender people do not seek to erase their gender history. This is why "trans" is an essential prefix, underscoring the exercise of agency to transition from one gender to another.

        1.2.1.2.4. Con:
        There is a difference between what science can do and what medical professionals should do. This is why, though the prostate can technically be removed through surgery, this is not a part of bottom surgery [75].

          1.2.1.2.4.1. Con:
          The prostate is partially or completely removed [76] in cisgender men with prostate cancer, so there is no reason why the same cannot be safely performed on transgender women requesting the procedure.

          1.2.1.2.4.2. Pro:
          Removing the prostate as part of bottom surgery can increase the risk of several side effects and injury [77] to the surrounding organs.

      1.2.1.3. Con:
      The typically understood presentation of chromosome correlation and gender has been shown to be over simplified [78] (p. 452 [79]). Ignoring this truth marginalises those born on the spectrum of intersex [80].

        1.2.1.3.1. Pro:
        A person's sex is a combination of different features [81]. Many of these features can be changed and vary across the population, independent of a person's gender identity.

      1.2.1.4. Pro:
      Because of the genetic nature of sex [82] and its influence on gender [83], all gender-reaffirming surgeries can only be superficial.

        1.2.1.4.1. Con:
        As humans have had a definition of sex long before discovering genes, genes cannot be what make up our definition of sex.

        1.2.1.4.2. Pro:
        Transitioning requires constant aftercare [84] and subsequent surgeries [85] to maintain the features.

    1.2.2. Pro:
    Neuroscientific studies suggest that the brains of transgender individuals more closely resemble [86] the patterns associated with their desired gender than the gender they were assigned at birth (p. 15 [87]).

      1.2.2.1. Con:
      This type of thinking could ultimately lead to gender essentialism [88]—the idea that, in the end, our biology can and will determine our gender.

        1.2.2.1.1. Pro:
        Relying on biological features as proof of one's gender leads to the exclusion of transgender individuals who might not display such features.

        1.2.2.1.2. Con:
        The neuroscientific studies are merely factual and not the product of any agenda. They could merely be used to better understand the phenomenon of transgenderism.

      1.2.2.2. Pro:
      Findings suggest slight physical differences in the brains of cis and transgender individuals (p. 6 [89]). This indicates that being transgender begins at birth.

        1.2.2.2.1. Con:
        Some studies [90] have found that the brains of transgender people resemble those of those sharing their natal sex and that they begin to change only following hormonal treatment. This implies that the difference may not exist at birth.

        1.2.2.2.2. Pro:
        A 2000 study indicates that the number of neurons in the brain that deal with the sexual behavior of male-to-female transgender people is similar to that of cisgender women and vice versa (p. 2034 [91]).

        1.2.2.2.3. Pro:
        The interaction between sex-atypical levels of sex hormones and the brain permanently affects gender identity (pp. 302, 305 [92]).

        1.2.2.2.4. Pro:
        There are sexually dimorphic sections of the brain, including the part thought to control gender identity (p. 318 [93]).

        1.2.2.2.5. Con:
        'Female' or 'male' brain just means 'belongs to a female', not that it has 'female' qualities. Individual people have individual [94] brains, comprised of a mosaic of brain features (p. 15472 [95]).

    1.2.3. Con:
    Like most surgeries, gender reassignment surgery leaves behind potentially permanent scars, [96] which may inform the world that a person is transgender.

      1.2.3.1. Pro:
      Men are expected to be topless in certain environments, such as beaches [97]. Because top surgery leaves scars, toplessness would coerce transgender men into outing themselves as transgender.

        1.2.3.1.1. Pro:
        In sports, toplessness is often expected of men, for example, as part of their swimming uniform or when dividing teammates into "shirts and skins [98]." Top surgery scars preclude transgender men from participating in such sports.

        1.2.3.1.2. Con:
        Top surgery scars are not unique to transgender people. Breast augmentation and mastectomies, for example, leave behind scars [99] in cisgender women as they do in transgender women.

      1.2.3.2. Con:
      Gender reassignment surgery is typically performed on sexual and reproductive organs [100], which are concealed by clothing. Therefore, being outed by scars is not likely to be a significant concern for transgender people.

    1.2.4. Pro:
    A positive relationship with one's body is crucially important to self-esteem and mental health.

      1.2.4.1. Con:
      This is only a result of social pressure to conform to particular patterns of physical and behavioural expression, without which people would be freer to act as they choose.

        1.2.4.1.1. Pro:
        According to Judith Butler [101], a prominent gender theorist, gender is performative [102] rather than inherent, which suggests that it is possible for human beings to cease their performance of it.

        1.2.4.1.2. Con:
        It is unrealistic to believe that there is no biological element involved in these behaviours.

          1.2.4.1.2.1. Con:
          Gender norms and standards have constantly changed [103] over time. As such, it is unrealistic to assume that all gendered behaviours have their roots in biology.

          1.2.4.1.2.2. Pro:
          For every animal, we accept that there are specific behaviors for females and males (p. 14 [104]).

        1.2.4.1.3. Pro:
        Gender stereotypes are restrictive [105] and overemphasize the differences between the genders. We ought to work to eliminate or minimize their influence on society.

          1.2.4.1.3.1. Con:
          While restrictive gender norms are harmful, it takes time for society to change them (p. 12 [106]). It is unfair to expect transgender individuals to live uncomfortably with their assigned gender while they wait for society to alter its perceptions.

          1.2.4.1.3.2. Pro:
          Building programs to reform gender norms can improve health outcomes over the long term (p. 225 [107]).

      1.2.4.2. Pro:
      Self-determination is an essential psychological need [108] that can be more easily attained [109] when an individual's body and brain align (p. 649 [110]).

        1.2.4.2.1. Pro:
        Self-determination [111] cannot occur when the very idea of the self, composed of the physical and psychological, is in conflict.

  1.3. Con:
  Not all transgender individuals want to undergo, or can access, gender reassignment surgery.

    1.3.1. Con:
    An option can be the best in a given situation even if people do not wish to opt for it.

      1.3.1.1. Con:
      Even so, the right of people to choose a sub-optimal option must be respected as part of their agency (p. 118 [112]).

        1.3.1.1.1. Con:
        Transgender people having the right to choose a sub-optimal option does not require physicians to provide sub-optimal treatment options to their patients. The latter would be unethical.

        1.3.1.1.2. Con:
        Allowing patients to choose sub-optimal options has life-and-death implications for physicians, who have been attacked and murdered [113] due to dissatisfaction with surgical results.

        1.3.1.1.3. Pro:
        Different people consider different options sub-optimal for different reasons. For example, whether a cancer patient considers chemotherapy to be optimal or sub-optimal, depends on whether they value the quantity of life or quality of life [114].

        1.3.1.1.4. Pro:
        Healthcare is meant to be patient-centred [115], and where there are options, the choice of the patient should be prioritised.

      1.3.1.2. Pro:
      Surgery saves a transgender person the time and energy needed to hide their secondary sexual characteristics [116] on a daily basis. This benefit is not negated by the personal reasons that stop them from undergoing surgery.

        1.3.1.2.1. Pro:
        Transgender people may not be aware of the medically appropriate way to hide their secondary sexual characteristics. As a result, they may end up harming themselves physically.

          1.3.1.2.1.1. Pro:
          There are many guidelines to be followed when trying to bind one's chest, failing which one can end up with broken ribs, blood clots [117], and even constricted blood supply leading to a heart attack.

            1.3.1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
            Chest binders should not be worn for too long or too often [118]. Yet, transgender women who do not undergo surgery may have no other option but to wear chest binders for long hours, for example, at work.

            1.3.1.2.1.1.2. Pro:
            Chest binders lead to negative health outcomes in 97% of cases [119], with around 75% reporting pain and more than 50% of users reporting breathing difficulties.

            1.3.1.2.1.1.3. Con:
            "Research" on the harms of using chest binders are exaggerated and misused [120] to malign the work of transgender organizations. In reality, they are both safe and effective.

            1.3.1.2.1.1.4. Con:
            Several garments can cause bodily harm if not worn with care. Yet, this does not mean that the garment is inherently unsafe or harmful.

              1.3.1.2.1.1.4.1. Pro:
              Bras, especially tight bras, pose several health problems, including tissue and nerve damage [121]. Yet, they are normalized as an integral part of women's clothing.

              1.3.1.2.1.1.4.2. Pro:
              Men with fertility concerns and problems, for example, a low sperm count, are advised to wear boxers rather than tighter briefs [122].

              1.3.1.2.1.1.4.3. Con:
              Due to cisheteronormativity, people implicitly learn how to correctly use gendered garments worn by their same-gender peers. This is unlikely to be the case for esoteric garments needed by transgender people (e.g., chest binders).

                1.3.1.2.1.1.4.3.1. Con:
                The vast majority [123] of women wear bras that are of the wrong size. This shows that the underlying problem is not whether a garment is intended for cisgender or transgender people, but societal shame and secrecy surrounding gender and sexuality.

                1.3.1.2.1.1.4.3.2. Pro:
                Information about the use of gendered garments is often transmitted between same-gender individuals, for example, a mother and daughter [124]. Due to cisheteronormativity, transgender individuals may be unable to access such information.

          1.3.1.2.1.2. Pro:
          Tucking, which transgender women employ to hide their penis [125], can lead to a range of problems, including rashes, testicular pain, and trauma to parts of the urinary system.

          1.3.1.2.1.3. Con:
          Information on how to safely hide secondary sexual characteristics [126] can be easily found on the internet. Therefore, the risk of bodily harm is likely very low.

        1.3.1.2.2. Con:
        Managing secondary sexual characteristics is a part of grooming for all genders. Men, for example, may need to shave facial hair, and women may need to shave body hair. Surgical transitioning will not eliminate these aspects of self-care.

          1.3.1.2.2.1. Con:
          The problem is not the amount of time invested in self-care, but the amount of time invested in gender-incongruent self-care. A transgender man, for example, may dislike threading his upper lip, but be happy to shave his moustache [127], which affirms his gender.

          1.3.1.2.2.2. Pro:
          Surgery does not address hair-related concerns in transgender people: whereas transgender men undergo hormone therapy [128] to grow facial and body hair, transgender women may undergo laser treatments to appear more feminine [129].

    1.3.2. Pro:
    The cost [130] of gender reassignment surgery makes it inaccessible [131] to many. Thus, other treatment options should be explored.

      1.3.2.1. Pro:
      Most genital reconstruction procedures require a two-step process (p. 1923 [132]) or follow-up surgery [133], which increases the overall cost of the procedure.

      1.3.2.2. Con:
      In places with publicly funded healthcare [134], the cost of reassignment surgery is often covered by the state.

        1.3.2.2.1. Pro:
        In the UK, transgender individuals ages 17 or over wishing to undergo surgery (or other transition treatments) are entitled to a referral to a Gender Idenity Clinic via the NHS [135].

        1.3.2.2.2. Pro:
        In 2022, Malta declared it would provide fully funded care [136] for transgender individuals.

        1.3.2.2.3. Pro:
        Canada provides nationwide funding [137] for transgender healthcare.

        1.3.2.2.4. Con:
        While the situation may be progressing, only a handful [138] of countries are currently able to offer state-covered care for transgender individuals.

        1.3.2.2.5. Con:
        The fact that transgenderism is still controversial in some societies [139] makes state-funded reassignment surgery unavailable for the forseeable future.

        1.3.2.2.6. Con:
        Even in states with publicly funded healthcare, insurance coverage for transgender care could be denied after being deemed not medically necessary. [140]

      1.3.2.3. Pro:
      Rates of poverty and homelessness [141] are disproportionately high among transgender individuals. To position an unaffordable treatment as "the best" is, therefore, privileged and elitist.

        1.3.2.3.1. Con:
        Many medical treatments, including life-saving cancer medication [142], are prohibitively expensive, yet this does not diminish the fact that they are the best available treatment option.

        1.3.2.3.2. Pro:
        High rates of poverty and homelessness are even more pronounced among transgender people of color [143]. Positioning gender reassignment surgery as the best option, therefore, runs counter to the principles of intersectional feminism.

        1.3.2.3.3. Pro:
        People with disabilities and debilitating conditions like HIV [144] are overrepresented among poor transgender communities. Instead of spending money on healthcare for these conditions, they may opt for surgery if its benefits are overstated.

        1.3.2.3.4. Con:
        Positioning an unaffordable treatment as the best may incentivize policymakers, healthcare providers, and insurance companies to develop strategies to make the procedure more accessible to transgender people.

      1.3.2.4. Con:
      The cost of gender reassignment surgery is going down.

        1.3.2.4.1. Pro:
        The increased availability of clinics [145] offering transgender surgery means that supply is rising against demand, reducing the cost.

        1.3.2.4.2. Pro:
        The increase in NGOs funds [146] and state subsidies to support transgender surgeries has cut down on out-of-pocket costs for transitioners.

        1.3.2.4.3. Pro:
        Innovation and the discovery [147] of the most cost-effective methods of surgery have the potential to further reduce the cost of transitioning.

        1.3.2.4.4. Con:
        The rise in demand [148] for gender-affirming care also places inflationary pressure on the costs of the process.

        1.3.2.4.5. Con:
        The costs of the surgery are not going down uniformly across countries and state [149]s. So while costs might be cheaper in some places, the general costs of pursuing the surgery, including travel, might still be high.

    1.3.3. Pro:
    In a 2011 survey [150], only 33% of transgender individuals said they had surgically transitioned.

      1.3.3.1. Con:
      A 2015 survey found that more than 70% [151] of transmasculine people had either already undergone or wanted top surgery—a figure that continued to increase in subsequent years (p. 4 [152]).

      1.3.3.2. Pro:
      According to a survey [153] of doctors, less than 0.5% of gender reassignment surgeries involved the genitals.

      1.3.3.3. Pro:
      Many transgender people settle for simply changing their names, speech, and clothing [154].

    1.3.4. Con:
    Reluctance to undergo surgery may be informed by negative social stigmas [155] associated with it rather than a lack of desire to transition.

      1.3.4.1. Con:
      Many civil society organizations are working to fight against negative social stigmas [156] surrounding gender reassignment surgery.

        1.3.4.1.1. Con:
        Most of the action being taken to reduce stigma and advance trans rights across various countries is through legislation, which does not guarantee protection [157] against social stigma.

        1.3.4.1.2. Pro:
        In 2020, the Human Rights Campaign partnered with WarnerMedia to help spread awareness and support [158] for transgender and non-binary individuals, including those undergoing reassignment surgery.

      1.3.4.2. Pro:
      Transgender individuals are scared of adverse societal reactions, such as not being accepted into society, after undergoing gender reassignment surgery (p. 5 [159]).

        1.3.4.2.1. Pro:
        Prior to transition, many transgender individuals feel compelled to hide their identity to avoid exclusion and discrimination [160]. Post-surgery, their trans identity may be less subtle, but they may still be required to declare their legal name [161], which would make it obvious that they are transgender.

        1.3.4.2.2. Con:
        Certain aspects of reassignment surgery [162] make it more likely that an individual will authentically 'pass' [163] as the gender they identify with. In these cases, it is unlikely anyone but those closest to them would know they are transgender.

  1.4. Pro:
  Gender reassignment surgery is supported by the majority of experts and a credible body of evidence.

    1.4.1. Pro:
    The majority of studies [164] have found that gender reassignment surgery has a positive effect on individuals.

      1.4.1.1. Con:
      Being the best option goes beyond merely having a positive effect.To be labelled the best option, gender reassignment surgery would need to outperform all other treatment options [165] and their respective outcomes (p. 795 [166]).

      1.4.1.2. Pro:
      Studies [167] show that the procedure has long-term mental health benefits for transgender people.

    1.4.2. Pro:
    Most healthcare professionals [168] support gender reassignment surgery as a response to gender dysphoria.

      1.4.2.1. Con:
      Healthcare professionals are biased sources on the subject of transgender surgery.

        1.4.2.1.1. Pro:
        Doctors make huge profits [169] from promoting gender reassignment surgery to patients.

        1.4.2.1.2. Con:
        The medical profession is heavily regulated and held to the highest ethical standards [170]. It is therefore unlikely that healthcare professionals will abuse their powers for their own benefit.

      1.4.2.2. Pro:
      Healthcare professionals take the Hippocratic Oath [171], which requires them to act in the best interest of the patient. It is unlikely that they would collectively endorse an intervention that is not best suited to the needs of transgender people.

    1.4.3. Con:
    Most forms of gender reassignment surgery are relatively new, and therefore there is insufficient research [172] on the long-term effects.

      1.4.3.1. Con:
      The first gender reassignment surgery was performed in 1959 [173], meaning there has been sufficient time to perfect the procedure.

      1.4.3.2. Pro:
      -> See 1.1.1.3.2.2.

    1.4.4. Con:
    Experts can only comment on the safety and effectiveness of an intervention. However, for an intervention to be considered the "best option," other non-clinical factors may need to be considered.

      1.4.4.1. Pro:
      In restrictive cultures, surgical transitioning could put a transgender person at high risk for negative legal or social repercussions. Therefore, surgery would not be the best option for transgender individuals in these cultures.

        1.4.4.1.1. Pro:
        In many countries, it is a crime to simply be transgender [174]. Therefore, it would not be in the best interests of a transgender person to opt for surgery.

          1.4.4.1.1.1. Pro:
          In Sudan, it is illegal to be transgender; consequently, gender reassignment surgery is also illegal [175]. Labelling gender reassignment surgery as the best option could exacerbate a sense of helplessness among transgender people in Sudan.

          1.4.4.1.1.2. Con:
          No country has a law that criminalizes an individual for being transgender. Instead, they have laws that criminalize gender non-conforming behaviors, like cross-dressing [176]. Thus, surgery does not exacerbate the risk of social or legal harms.

        1.4.4.1.2. Con:
        In restrictive cultures, transgender people dismantle gender norms and work towards overturning archaic laws [177] by vocally advocating for their right to undergo these procedures. They would not sacrifice their very lives unless they considered surgery to be central to their identity and existence.

        1.4.4.1.3. Pro:
        Surgical transitioning cannot be considered the best option if it leads to greater harassment [178], ostracism, and bullying. In turn, this could lead to adverse outcomes, for example, in the workplace.

          1.4.4.1.3.1. Con:
          Adverse work outcomes are affected less by transphobia and more by sexism. This is why, after transitioning, transgender men witness an increase in pay [179], while the opposite is true for transgender women.

          1.4.4.1.3.2. Pro:
          Legal recognition of gender identity sometimes requires surgical status. This can affect the employability of transgender people who do not wish to undergo surgery. For this reason, surgery should not be positioned as the best option for transgender people (p. 14 [180]).

      1.4.4.2. Con:
      In some jurisdictions, a transgender person can legally change their gender, only after undergoing gender reassignment surgery. Surgery is, therefore, essential to the social and legal recognition [181] of gender identity.

      1.4.4.3. Pro:
      Surgical transitioning can complicate everyday decisions regarding the use of gendered spaces, such as bathrooms and locker rooms, due to a misalignment between biological needs and social norms.

        1.4.4.3.1. Pro:
        Transgender men who menstruate or lactate may need access to menstrual products and lactation rooms, respectively. However, they may not be allowed or feel comfortable using spaces reserved for cisgender women.

        1.4.4.3.2. Con:
        Social norms can be changed to simplify decision-making for people of all genders. Introducing gender-neutral bathrooms or placing period products in both women's and men's bathrooms, for example, could mitigate these challenges.

      1.4.4.4. Con:
      Composite variables, such as quality of life, are indicators of the overall well-being of a person, and gender reassignment surgery significantly improves the quality of life of transgender people [182].

        1.4.4.4.1. Con:
        There is some evidence to suggest that post-surgical improvements in quality of life decrease over time [183]. At the very least, this underscores the need for more research on whether gender reassignment surgeries are the best option from a long-term perspective.

        1.4.4.4.2. Pro:
        Poor quality of life [184] may explain the heightened suicide risk among transgender people. By changing how transgender people evaluate their lives, gender reassignment surgery prevents suicide and fosters self-preservation.

      1.4.4.5. Pro:
      Gender reassignment surgery is not supported by many religions. A holistic approach to gender-affirming care would, therefore, not position surgery as "the best option" for transgender people with such religious beliefs.

        1.4.4.5.1. Con:
        There is no sacred book that mentions gender reassignment surgery, let alone condemns it. Therefore, there is no concrete reason why transgender people should be ideologically opposed to these procedures.

          1.4.4.5.1.1. Con:
          Deuteronomy 23:2 [185] is interpreted as condemnation of gender reassignment surgery, as it states that no man who has had his penis or testicles removed should enter the place of God.

            1.4.4.5.1.1.1. Con:
            In Matthew 19:12 [186], Jesus states that eunuchs, regardless of whether they were born that way or made that way, glorify the kingdom of God, thereby rendering earlier laws on castration defunct.

          1.4.4.5.1.2. Pro:
          Many ancient cultures [187] practiced procedures similar to modern sex reassignment surgery, yet none of the sacred books explicitly condemn them. This proves that they are not of moral significance.

        1.4.4.5.2. Pro:
        Transgender people sometimes request surgical reversal to their natal gender, not because they are dissatisfied with the results, but because of a change in their religious beliefs [188].

        1.4.4.5.3. Pro:
        Abrahamic faiths [189] require biological males to be circumcised. Gender reassignment surgery, to remove or create a penis, can create doubt and confusion about circumcision status in the minds of transgender people belonging to these faiths.

          1.4.4.5.3.1. Pro:
          Bottom surgery to create a neopenis does not include creating a foreskin [190]. Therefore, without a foreskin, a transgender man cannot be circumcised.

            1.4.4.5.3.1.1. Con:
            Some consider circumcision to be a physical indicator [191] separating devotees from their counterparts. Because transgender men have no foreskin, they do not need to be circumcised to mark themselves as believers.

            1.4.4.5.3.1.2. Con:
            Cisgender men who regret their circumcision can have their foreskin surgically restored [192]. Transgender men could be offered a similar procedure to allow them to be circumcised.

            1.4.4.5.3.1.3. Pro:
            Some who convert to an Abrahamic faith opt to undergo adult circumcision [193]. This would not be a possibility for transgender men and could pose problems for religious matters that require proof of circumcision [194].

          1.4.4.5.3.2. Con:
          Religious laws require male babies to be circumcised shortly after birth [195]; therefore, religious transgender women opting for gender reassignment surgery would most likely be circumcised.

  1.5. Con:
  Gender reassignment surgery is permanent and irreversible. Transgender individuals should, therefore, prefer reversible alternatives.

    1.5.1. Pro:
    For transgender men and women, bottom surgery [196] involves the removal of sexual and reproductive organs. Thus, gender reassignment surgery coerces transgender people into a lifetime of sterility.

      1.5.1.1. Con:
      Prior to gender reassignment surgery [197], transgender individuals can freeze their eggs, sperm, or embryos, thereby allowing them to have children even after transitioning.

        1.5.1.1.1. Con:
        Cryopreservation is not an option for someone who has not gone through puberty [198]. Therefore, a transgender person who has been taking puberty blockers will not be able to freeze their eggs or sperm prior to surgery.

          1.5.1.1.1.1. Con:
          Cancer researchers are developing a procedure that will allow sperm to be generated from testicular tissue extracted from prepubescent boys [199]. The same procedure can be offered to transgender boys opting for puberty blockers and surgery.

      1.5.1.2. Con:
      Transgender individuals can choose which organs they wish to remove [200] as part of bottom surgery. Some may, therefore, retain their natal reproductive organs for the purposes of childbearing.

        1.5.1.2.1. Pro:
        Transgender men who choose not to have their uterus removed can become pregnant [201], even though they otherwise present as transmasculine.

        1.5.1.2.2. Pro:
        Like cisgender women, transgender men can lactate and chestfeed their infants [202], even though they may otherwise hide their breasts in keeping with their transmasculine expression.

        1.5.1.2.3. Con:
        The fact that transgender individuals need to avoid certain gender reassignment surgeries because they conflict with their envisioned future is proof that gender reassignment surgery is not categorically the best option for them.

      1.5.1.3. Pro:
      There is no gender reassignment surgery that allows a transgender person to acquire the reproductive capabilities of their desired gender. Thus, their parenting roles will be limited to those of their natal sex.

        1.5.1.3.1. Pro:
        Transgender men cannot produce sperm [203], and transgender women cannot produce eggs to reproduce naturally, regardless of surgery and hormonal treatments.

        1.5.1.3.2. Con:
        Uterine transplants [204] are an emerging possibility for transgender women who desire to experience pregnancy. Thus, gender reassignment surgery can indeed bridge the gap between the reproductive capabilities of trans and cisgender women.

        1.5.1.3.3. Con:
        Lactation can be induced in transgender women [205], thereby allowing them to experience breastfeeding like their cisgender counterparts.

          1.5.1.3.3.1. Con:
          Lactation is typically induced with the aid of hormones [206], in addition to physical stimulation and medication. Surgery is, therefore, not necessary to enable this experience in transgender women.

            1.5.1.3.3.1.1. Pro:
            Top surgery, for example, breast augmentation, carries the risk of tissue or nerve damage, which can impede breast milk production [207]. Therefore, transgender women seeking to breastfeed should not opt for top surgery.

            1.5.1.3.3.1.2. Con:
            The hormones taken by transgender women can leech into breast milk [208]. The best course of action, therefore, would be to use donor breast milk and supplements.

              1.5.1.3.3.1.2.1. Con:
              The hormones themselves do not leech into breast milk. Instead, metabolites may be excreted, which are nonetheless clinically insignificant in quantity [209] to pose any risks to breastfeeding.

              1.5.1.3.3.1.2.2. Pro:
              Transgender women undergoing hormone therapy take androgen blockers [210], which can metabolize and leech into breast milk. These metabolites are potentially carcinogenic [211] and, therefore, not used in some countries, including the US.

          1.5.1.3.3.2. Pro:
          Breastmilk produced by transgender women is not only safe for infant consumption but is also comparable in quality [212] to that produced by cisgender women.

          1.5.1.3.3.3. Con:
          Lactation can be induced only in those with sufficient breast tissue [213]. Transgender women who have gone through male puberty may, therefore, lack the breast tissue needed to induce lactation.

            1.5.1.3.3.3.1. Con:
            The hormones prescribed for transgender women stimulate the development of breast tissue [214], increasing their likelihood of being able to breastfeed. Their breast tissue is indistinguishable [215] from those of cisgender women.

            1.5.1.3.3.3.2. Pro:
            Some cisgender women do not develop sufficient breast tissue [216] for breastfeeding during puberty. Yet, this does not diminish the validity of their gender identity or motherhood. Similarly, transgender women should not feel compelled to undergo surgery or hormonal treatments to induce lactation.

        1.5.1.3.4. Pro:
        The inability of the surgery to replicate reproductive capacity means that transpeople will always be limited in their gender experiences.

          1.5.1.3.4.1. Con:
          In the same way infertility does not take away one's gender identity, the lack of reproductive capacity among transpeople does not make them any less of their gender.

      1.5.1.4. Con:
      Sterility does not imply childlessness. Transgender people can become parents through adoption; in this way, they can validate both their gender identity as well as their need to become a parent.

        1.5.1.4.1. Con:
        In many countries, transgender people are not allowed to adopt [217]. Surgery would, therefore, eliminate their only path to parenthood.

        1.5.1.4.2. Pro:
        Adoption rates are elevated among transgender children [218]. Adoption, therefore, affords transgender people the unique opportunity to provide gender-affirming care to their adopted children.

          1.5.1.4.2.1. Con:
          Higher adoption rates among transgender children have been attributed to a higher likelihood of gender-affirming parenting [219] among adoptive parents. Thus, the benefit of being adopted by a transgender parent is likely to be comparatively minimal.

          1.5.1.4.2.2. Pro:
          Many transgender youth are rejected by their families, both biological and adoptive, which leads to housing instability [220]. Encouraging transgender people to foster or adopt transgender children can mitigate this crisis.

        1.5.1.4.3. Con:
        Institutional barriers and implicit transphobia [221] can make adoption a very complicated and potentially traumatizing experience for transgender people. Adoption is, therefore, not a comparable alternative to reproduction.

    1.5.2. Con:
    Gender reassignment surgery is reversible [222], so should a transgender individual later regret their decision, they can always transition back into their gender assigned at birth.

      1.5.2.1. Pro:
      Regretful transgender people can undergo "reversal surgery [223]." Phalloplasty, which entails penile reconstruction, for example, has been found to yield good surgical and psychological outcomes.

      1.5.2.2. Con:
      Surgical transitioning is a costly affair, in terms of not only money but also physical and mental health. Positioning this option as "the best," despite the possibility of post-surgical regret, is disingenuous.

        1.5.2.2.1. Pro:
        Some transgender people opt for reversal surgery to alleviate distress caused by prevalent and internalized transphobia [224]. If informed of this possibility, they may conclude that surgery is not the best option for them.

    1.5.3. Pro:
    Gender reassignment surgery entails making permanent biological changes to transition from one gender to another. This reinforces binary models of gender rooted in biological essentialism [225].

      1.5.3.1. Pro:
      Gender reassignment surgery perpetuates the same sexist stereotypes and beauty standards that harm cisgender men and women.

        1.5.3.1.1. Pro:
        Laser hair removal [226] is a gender-affirming surgery offered to transgender women, who feel distressed about their body hair. This reinforces problematic gender norms [227] that women should not have body hair.

        1.5.3.1.2. Pro:
        Many cisgender women are flat-chested [228], and many cisgender men have gynecomastia [229]. Top surgery, to remove breast tissue or augment breast size, reinforces harmful gendered beliefs about physical appearance.

        1.5.3.1.3. Con:
        Not all transgender individuals seek to "pass" as the other gender [230]. They may, therefore, opt to undergo only selected surgeries, thereby creating a mosaic of masculine and feminine gender presentations that challenges cis-heterosexist norms.

        1.5.3.1.4. Con:
        Some transgender men and women appear visibly transgender. In this way, they amplify diversity in how men and women look, thereby weakening gender stereotypes, for example, those relating to height.

          1.5.3.1.4.1. Pro:
          On average, men are taller than women. As a result, transgender women tend to be taller than cisgender women [231], while the opposite is true for transgender men.

          1.5.3.1.4.2. Con:
          Some transgender men and women undergo height-altering surgeries [232], such as limb reduction, so that they do not stand out among their cisgender counterparts.

      1.5.3.2. Con:
      By opting not to identify with their assigned sex, transgender individuals challenge deterministic views of gender and empower people to exercise more agency in their gender identity.

      1.5.3.3. Pro:
      Intersex rights activists believe that gender-normalizing surgeries erase their binary-defying identity [233]. Likewise, transgender people should embrace how nature made them [234], rather than trying to fit into one of the two binary genders.

        1.5.3.3.1. Con:
        Intersex rights activists oppose only forced gender-normalizing surgery [235] performed on infants. They do believe that intersex adults should have the freedom to opt for such surgeries.

        1.5.3.3.2. Pro:
        Through their very existence, intersex people demonstrate that masculine and feminine attributes [236] can co-exist within the same person. The same applies to transgender people, who have a body that matches one gender and an identity that matches another.

        1.5.3.3.3. Pro:
        Gender reassignment surgery is contraindicated for some intersex individuals [237], meaning that the best option for them is to live with a gender identity that does not correspond to their legal sex assigned at birth [238].

          1.5.3.3.3.1. Pro:
          Like transgender people, intersex people are at high risk for suicide [239]. However, this risk is significantly reduced through acceptance and affirmation [240], implying that psychosocial intervention is sufficient.

          1.5.3.3.3.2. Con:
          Making peace with the fact that one cannot undergo gender-affirming surgery does not negate the argument that it is the best option for eligible candidates.

      1.5.3.4. Pro:
      Gender is a malleable social construct [241]. Therefore, it should be sufficient for transgender individuals to socially transition without opting for surgery.

        1.5.3.4.1. Pro:
        A person who identifies as transgender may later realize that they are gender fluid [242]. Undergoing surgery deprives them of the option of switching between different genders.

        1.5.3.4.2. Con:
        The desire to undergo the surgery is more of a function of body dysmorphia [243] than a belief that it is necessary to become part of another gender category.

        1.5.3.4.3. Con:
        Transgender individuals may not merely be unhappy with the social construction of their assigned gender. They may also be unhappy with their secondary sexual characteristics, which is why they seek to alter them through surgery.

          1.5.3.4.3.1. Pro:
          Transgender boys engage in tucking to hide their penises [244], while transgender girls engage in binding to flatten their chests [245]. This shows that their dissatisfaction is not merely with the social construction of gender but with their biological features.

          1.5.3.4.3.2. Con:
          The social construction of gender includes secondary sex characteristics, such as breasts, which is why many cisgender women, for example, seek breast augmentation [246].

        1.5.3.4.4. Pro:
        The narrative that transgender people must undergo surgery to be accepted as their true gender makes it so that those who do not transition surgically are often excluded.

      1.5.3.5. Con:
      Gender reassignment surgery can alter only phenotypic sex, for example, genitalia, not genotypic sex, namely, chromosomes [247]. In this way, transgender people challenge the notion that perceived gender [248] is a valid indicator of biological sex.

        1.5.3.5.1. Con:
        Awareness that perceived gender is not a valid indicator of biological sex can be used to justify biological sex-verification checks in gendered spaces [249], which can prove particularly detrimental to intersex individuals.

        1.5.3.5.2. Pro:
        Weakening implicit associations between gender and sex could foster greater public awareness about the importance of not assuming the gender identity [250] of other people.

      1.5.3.6. Con:
      Modern concepts of transgenderism reject the gender binary.

        1.5.3.6.1. Pro:
        There are transgender individuals who undergo surgery [251] to reflect their non-binary status.

        1.5.3.6.2. Pro:
        The transgender community includes [252] non-binary individuals.

        1.5.3.6.3. Con:
        The language of gender reassignment often supports the gender binary.

    1.5.4. Con:
    In terms of gender-affirming care, the only other medical treatment available to transgender people is hormone therapy, but its effects are not always reversible [253].

      1.5.4.1. Con:
      The notion that transgender people must, at some point, medically transition is misguided. Transgender people are valid in their gender identity, even if they transition only socially and legally [254].

      1.5.4.2. Pro:
      Hormone replacement therapy [255] has side effects and risks, many of which may be permanent and irreversible.

        1.5.4.2.1. Pro:
        Testosterone loss has numerous [256] side effects, including increasing [257] transgender women's risk of depression, fatigue, and osteoporosis.

          1.5.4.2.1.1. Con:
          The side effects are often seen as improvements, as they are the results of desirable changes linked to a female's "second puberty [258]".

          1.5.4.2.1.2. Con:
          Trans women on hormone replacement therapy [259] have a significant enough amount of estrogen and progesterone to counter the medically relevant symptoms of low testosterone.

        1.5.4.2.2. Con:
        When administered with due care and supervision, the benefits [260] of hormone replacement therapy for transgender individuals are believed to outweigh the risks (p. 883 [261]).

        1.5.4.2.3. Pro:
        Hormones cannot be taken forever, as they carry significant health risks [262], including a higher likelihood of developing heart disease, cancer, and stroke. Therefore, surgery is a more preferable option.

  1.6. Pro:
  Gender reassignment surgery enables individuals to match their gender presentation to their gender identity.

    1.6.1. Pro:
    The right to bodily integrity [263], including the right of an individual to determine what is best for their own body and how they present themselves, is a fundamental human right.

      1.6.1.1. Pro:
      As long as it does not harm [264] anyone else, transgender individuals should be able to choose whichever transition method they are most comfortable with.

        1.6.1.1.1. Con:
        Trans people are increasingly making the decision to undergo gender reassignment surgery without a proper understanding [265] of the procedure [266] and its impacts. This implies that the decision to have this surgery is often uninformed.

          1.6.1.1.1.1. Con:
          Transgender individuals are usually required [267] to pass the "real-life test [268]" of living for one year as their realised gender before surgical transition, as well as undergo pre-surgery hormone [269] therapies, to ensure that they have the time, opportunity, perspectives, and motivation to research the risks and benefits of the surgery.

          1.6.1.1.1.2. Con:
          Transgender people often spend years researching [270] options and weighing risks and benefits, while doctors frequently have large gaps in their knowledge regarding the treatment of trans people (p. 76 [271]).

            1.6.1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
            Although most transgender people begin experiencing gender dysphoria by the age of seven [272], they are not legally able to undergo gender reassignment surgery until adulthood. This indicates that individuals often have a number of years to research treatment options.

          1.6.1.1.1.3. Pro:
          The 'gender-affirmative model [273]' that transactivist organizations promote is questioned by a new phenomenon - rapid-onset gender dysphoria [274].

            1.6.1.1.1.3.1. Con:
            The term 'rapid-onset gender dysphoria' is an example of the weaponization of scientific jargon to undermine the value of transitioning for those who have gender dysphoria (p. 779 [275]).

            1.6.1.1.1.3.2. Con:
            Rapid-onset gender dysphoria is not a recognised [276] condition.

            1.6.1.1.1.3.3. Pro:
            Rapid-onset dysphoria [277] occurs when an individual experiences dysphoria during or shortly after puberty without having displayed any of its symptoms in childhood. It can lead to individuals preemptively and often mistakenly transitioning to another gender.

        1.6.1.1.2. Pro:
        Society has a moral imperative to support transgender individuals in their choice to transition.

          1.6.1.1.2.1. Con:
          Since society formulates its own morals, it is extremely difficult—especially in societies dictated by religion—to push for change and undo years of moral policing.

          1.6.1.1.2.2. Pro:
          Given the social [278] and physical threats [279] against LGBT+ individuals, showing support for them is critical to raising awareness of their suffering and affirming the value of their lives.

      1.6.1.2. Con:
      Gender reassignment surgery could be considered a form of body mutilation that can harm the individual (p. 87 [280]).

        1.6.1.2.1. Pro:
        Unlike other cosmetic procedures, [281] gender reassignment surgery is typically accompanied by the continued use of hormones [282], which increases the medical risks by comparison (pp. 10-11 [283]).

        1.6.1.2.2. Con:
        Other kinds of body mutilations, such as cosmetic plastic surgeries [284], piercings, and tattoos [285], are common. Yet, they do not elicit the same level of stigma as gender reassignment surgery.

        1.6.1.2.3. Con:
        For individuals who want gender reassignment surgery [286], not undergoing it does more harm.

    1.6.2. Pro:
    Affirmation of an individual's gender by society is important in staving off depression and associated mental illnesses (p. 9 [287]).

      1.6.2.1. Con:
      People do not show their sexual organs in public; therefore, gender reassignment surgery does little to change the perception of the outside world.

        1.6.2.1.1. Con:
        Gender reassignment surgeries are not only related to the genitals [288] but also include the breasts and facial features.

        1.6.2.1.2. Con:
        This does not apply to relationships with potential sexual partners.

        1.6.2.1.3. Pro:
        There are other forms of treatment [289] available to transgender individuals to help them align their perceived gender with their gender identity that do not involve surgery.

        1.6.2.1.4. Pro:
        People who were previously acquainted with the transgender individual would likely not have their perceptions changed beyond acknowledging that the individual has undergone a cosmetic process.

      1.6.2.2. Pro:
      Gender affirming surgeries aid with societal acceptance.

        1.6.2.2.1. Pro:
        Social acceptance positively [290] affects one's level of confidence.

        1.6.2.2.2. Pro:
        A lack of social acceptance could exacerbate [291] gender dysphoria.

        1.6.2.2.3. Con:
        Expecting the individual to garner acceptance from society by undergoing surgery lessens the necessary pressure on governments [292] to take legislative action that would further social acceptance and reduce discrimination.

        1.6.2.2.4. Con:
        Promoting the narrative that transgender people need to undergo surgery to gain acceptance places the burden on them to earn a place in society that they already deserve.

    1.6.3. Con:
    The outside world might still not perceive the alterations of gender reassignment as legitimate.

      1.6.3.1. Pro:
      Transphobia and ignorance about trans issues are still very common [293] around the world.

      1.6.3.2. Pro:
      Society is still very cis-heteronormative. [294]

      1.6.3.3. Con:
      The existence of contrary perceptions of gender identity should not suppress the expression of the truths of transgender people.

      1.6.3.4. Con:
      While the outside world remains prejudiced against transgender people, perceptions have been bending in recent years towards tolerance.

    1.6.4. Con:
    Transgender individuals may come to regret the choice to undergo irreversible gender reassignment surgery.

      1.6.4.1. Con:
      Very few individuals regret their choice to undergo gender reassignment surgeries.

        1.6.4.1.1. Pro:
        A Swedish study of gender reassignment surgeries over a 50-year period indicated that there was only a 2.2% regret rate and that this declined over time (p. 1 [295]).

        1.6.4.1.2. Pro:
        A European study of over 100 patients found that people were overwhelmingly satisfied with the procedure (p. 147 [296]).

        1.6.4.1.3. Con:
        It might be difficult to communicate regret for undergoing the process after spending so much on the surgery.

        1.6.4.1.4. Con:
        Data on the prevalence of regret might be skewed by the negative perceptions of expressing regret [297] in the transgender community.

          1.6.4.1.4.1. Pro:
          It is believed [298] that expressing regret provides a case for transphobic individuals to criticise and attack transgender people or limit access to gender-affirming care.

        1.6.4.1.5. Con:
        There has been a rise [299] in the number of GoFundMe projects to fund detransitioning, which speaks to a growing number of post-operative trans individuals regretting their surgeries.

        1.6.4.1.6. Pro:
        The majority of research [300] would suggest that undergoing transition generally improves the well-being of transgender individuals.

      1.6.4.2. Pro:
      Many trans people have regretted [301] pursuing gender reassignment surgery.

        1.6.4.2.1. Con:
        The regret rate for gender reassignment surgeries is lower than for other surgeries.
