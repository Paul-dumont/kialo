Discussion Name:
Does science justify atheism?

1. Thesis:
Science doesn't justify [1] Atheism [2] (understood as a "lack of belief in god/s").

  1.1. Con:
  The simplest explanation for what we don't understand is that "we don't understand yet," not that "there is an all powerful supreme being that is making it happen."

    1.1.1. Con:
    Thousands of years of historical and anthropological evidence would suggest that even if it is logically easier to say "we just don't understand" it is not psychologically easier and therefore less applicable to humanity's needs.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      Science does not bend to psychological ease. Even if a position isn't psychologically easy, science may still justify that position depending on the ability to demonstrate the position as true.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      Opting for psychological ease often leads to factual inaccuracy and potentially damaging consequences in the long-term in many facets of life.

      1.1.1.3. Con:
      Something that is psychologically easier isn't always more applicable to human need. Ex: It is psychologically easier in many cases to accept parental abuse then it is to confront it as wrong. It's psychologically easier to procrastinate, even though it often carries negative consequences.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    All that Science can explain does not require a superior being as a hypothesis. As our understanding covers more questions about Nature, resorting to a deity for explaining the unknown becomes less justifiable.

    1.1.3. Con:
    This is a misunderstanding of theism, which is not an explanation for "what we don't understand." We have science for that. Theism explains the underlying cause of existence, not of natural causes in the universe.

      1.1.3.1. Con:
      Theistic claims to the underlying cause of existence require proof if they are to be believed as accurate, as any number of theistic claims exist in contradiction of each other.

        1.1.3.1.1. Pro:
        Various religions reject the existence of other religions supernatural claims, gods and supernatural creatures, despite them providing the same amount of debatable evidence as their own religion. This indicates irrationality and a refusal to maintain the same burden of proof across all theistic claims.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      While some nominally theistic belief systems, like Deism, may only posit "underlying," existential gods, the vast majority of theisms propose the existence of a god or gods who routinely violate natural causality [3] whenever they choose, and whose agency is the direct cause of a wide range of natural phenomena. The God of the Christian Bible is one such god.

      1.1.3.3. Con:
      We do not know the universe began, assuming such would be an error.

        1.1.3.3.1. Con:
        Scientists make assumptions even when we don't know. We don't know the one-way speed of light [4], only the round-trip speed of light that we measure. But scientists (tend to) assume that it travels the same speed all the time. And then they make theories based off those assumptions.

    1.1.4. Pro:
    An omnipotent, omniscient being is a self contradiction - logically absurd. Science is premised on the validity of reason and logic which do not permit self contradiction.

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      The Epicurean Paradox [5] and the problem of evil [6] makes the concept of an omnipotent, benevolent and omniscient god unreconcilable.

      1.1.4.2. Pro:
      There is a paradox in theistic claims with deities who purport to know the future. If one knows the future, one would be powerless to change the future, making said deity impotent to a degree.

      1.1.4.3. Con:
      -> See discussion #2629: God [7] exists.

      1.1.4.4. Con:
      Calling a concept "absurd" is not usually a valid argument, but a personal opinion, and many people take the opposite view, that the existence of an omnipotent, omniscient deity is credible. One could point to wave/particle duality or the many worlds hypothesis in physics as examples of self contradiction in science.

    1.1.5. Con:
    One statement is not simpler or more obvious than the other. One cannot appeal to Occam's Razor to prove a point, only to suggest a hypothesis.

      1.1.5.1. Con:
      Occam's razor may not be used to prove or disprove something, but it suits to justify a disbelief in superfluous causes, and therefore science provides a justification for atheism.

      1.1.5.2. Pro:
      Occam's razor isn't always correct. Sometimes, more complicated reasons are the correct ones.

    1.1.6. Pro:
    Belief is unfounded assumption. Science, or more particularly boolean algebra, proves that if one unfounded assumption is presumed true when it's actually false, then anything false can be proved true, leaving us with no facts and no logic. So, we shouldn't believe and science justifies Atheism.
