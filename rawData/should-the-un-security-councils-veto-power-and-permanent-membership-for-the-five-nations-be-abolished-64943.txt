Discussion Name:
Should the UN Security Council's veto power and permanent membership for the five nations be abolished?

1. Thesis:
The UN Security Council's veto power, and permanent membership for the five nations should be abolished.

  1.1. Con:
  Reforming the Security Council may be challenging and any attempts to change the current structure could lead to a breakdown in cooperation.

    1.1.1. Con:
    That literally proves the point. It should be abolished, because, the five nations have consistently abused their power to lead to a breakdown in cooperation (US shielding Israel from sanctions, China and Russia blocking sanctions during the Darfur genocide, UK and France helping to perpetuate the Suez Crisis)

  1.2. Con:
  The five permanent members are essential for maintaining stability and peace.

    1.2.1. Con:
    All five permanent members of the UN Security Council have been directly or indirectly involved in proxy wars [1] around the world.

  1.3. Con:
  Veto power acts as a safeguard against hasty and potentially destabilizing decisions.

    1.3.1. Con:
    The use of the veto has often enabled violence and global instability.

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      The US has vetoed resolutions [2] calling for a ceasefire in Gaza.

      1.3.1.2. Pro:
      Russia has vetoed a draft that demanded for a ceasefire of its war against Ukraine [3]

  1.4. Con:
  The UN should not exist at all as it is an instituting that inherently contradicts itself and undermines the sovereignty of nations. World peace can only be achieved with mutual respect and understanding between nations not economic entanglement

    1.4.1. Con:
    In order to achieve mutual respect and understanding, it's important to meet each other and communicate with each other. The UNSC meets multiple times a week. A big cause of it undermining the sovereignty of nations is that some nations have veto. Removing the veto would remove the selective justice that superpower states do (China/Russia/US)

  1.5. Con:
  It's against the realpolitik goal of UNSC to make it weak. Changing the system to be more logical drops the incentive for adversary nations like Russia to ever be part of the council. As long as they're presented with veneer of power and influence while on the council, it's easier to get them to sit down and talk with the rest.

    1.5.1. Con:
    Giving Russia more power than average in the UNSC is justifiable. Giving it veto? It's enabling it to have more power on actions that they don't want to happen than US, China, and other strong powers gathered. Should Russia have the power to cancel the withdrawal demand that asks it to withdraw from Ukraine? Even though US, UK and France voted for it? The existence of veto essentially enables a country to have more power than every other country gathered on a given issue that it dislikes

  1.6. Pro:
  The fact that one nation can disrupt the will of ALL the others in completely dumb, unfair, and against fundamentals of democracy. It is fair that some nations get more power than others due to having a bigger chunk of the population, but smaller member states still deserve a mostly equal vote in any policies that might affect them, or the bigger states will run over them. The purpose of the UN is to give all nations a say, and this veto power undermines that.

    1.6.1. Pro:
    Veto power could be considered either as safeguard or , vice versa, roadblock to global development. But looking at real situation in 2024, it's enormous roadblock and not safeguard. UN is rendered useless because of this.

    1.6.2. Con:
    What you suggest is essentially "mob rule" of nations. And in that kind of unfettered 'democracy' (often of despots), that'll be like two wolves and a sheep voting on what's for dinner.

  1.7. Pro:
  The system was built around an allience that doesn't exist anymore. There is today a big devide between the five countries with veto which renders the entire UNSC and pretty much the entire UN ineffective and broken. Also, with this system it's impossible to do anything about the mayor powers commiting war crimes, Russias war against Ukraine is a perfect example of this

    1.7.1. Pro:
    There is true, the old system is unavailable to do anything in the current landscape. And I think what the UN entire is obsolete and a great mistake

  1.8. Pro:
  The idea of a "Big Five" holding a veto power over the rest of the world makes a mockery of the "internationalism" of the U.N.

  1.9. Con:
  The five nations fairly accurately represent the basic spread of global political thought. Extending permanent membership to even more countries, sadly, would probably make the UN even more ineffective than it already is.

    1.9.1. Con:
    The five permanent members do not fairly represent global political opinion. Recent UN votes, like the Gaza ceasefire resolution, show that the Security Council can block the will of nearly the entire world. aljazeera.com [4]

  1.10. Pro:
  The current structure is outdated and does not reflect the current global power distribution

    1.10.1. Pro:
    India, Germany, and Japan, as rising global powers, are not represented as permanent members of the UNSC.

    1.10.2. Con:
    The 5 members represent well the main political tendencies in the world, meaning that almost all the voices are represented. We should not establish who should have most of power only according to their economy.

  1.11. Pro:
  It's against the spirit of justice and equality and opens the door of exploitation.

  1.12. Con:
  Without the veto, a superpower might simply leave the UN (as happened with the League of Nations), rendering the organization irrelevant and powerless.

  1.13. Pro:
  A permanent member can use the veto for its national interest, even when there is overwhelming support for a resolution. This causes deadlock and impedes collective decision-making.

  1.14. Pro:
  Emerging powers are not adequately represented, leading to a lack of equity in decision-making.

    1.14.1. Con:
    It could be argued that emerging powers are over-represented, leading to a situation where all those emerging powers continually come up with solutions that involve the developed nations paying for everything.

    1.14.2. Con:
    Emerging powers should not have the same authority or voting powers as established powers.
