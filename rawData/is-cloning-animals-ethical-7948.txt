Discussion Name:
Is Cloning Animals Ethical?

1. Thesis:
Cloning animals is ethical.

  1.1. Con:
  It is unethical to clone animals when we have incomplete knowledge of its potential harmful consequences.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    It is unethical to clone animals without further knowledge of how it would affect their emotions.

    1.1.2. Con:
    Humans often make ethical decisions, medical or otherwise, though they cannot know all the consequences.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      It is impossible to achieve complete knowledge over the future consequences of any action.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      When it comes to knowing what's going to happen in the future, the best we can do is guess [1]. Many outcomes are up to chance and are affected by factors beyond our understanding.

      1.1.2.3. Pro:
      It would be absurd to refrain from inaction in all cases where future outcomes are unknown. We would be living in a state of paralysis.

      1.1.2.4. Con:
      It is not necessary to know all of the consequences of cloning because those that are already known [2] should be sufficient to dissuade humans from cloning animals.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Currently the long-term consequences of cloning are unknown.

      1.1.3.1. Con:
      Any long-term consequences of cloning are much more likely to remain unknown, if any consequences existent at all, if we don’t have the opportunity to research and experiment.

      1.1.3.2. Pro:
      It is reasonable to establish parameters and contingencies before starting widespread cloning research.

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      Especially when dealing with living things, the possibility of a positive outcome is outweighed by unknown risks that could lead to undue suffering and death.

      1.1.3.4. Con:
      Computer modelling and simulations [3] can be used to help humans learn the consequences of cloning without risking any damage to animals.

    1.1.4. Pro:
    It is impossible to set regulations or limits on actions if we cannot predict what harms we wish to avoid.

    1.1.5. Con:
    Not knowing the potential outcomes of an act is not a reason to not do the act. Instead, it just calls for caution.

      1.1.5.1. Con:
      This standard for avoiding immoral outcomes can not apply when the risk we are seeking to avoid is as important as sparing animals immense suffering.

  1.2. Pro:
  Animal cloning could repair the large amount of damage done to this planet by humanity.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Cloning could positively impact animal extinction [4].

      1.2.1.1. Con:
      Cloning animals threatened with extinction does not change the threat the species faces - rather it allows extinction events to continue with the facade that species are unaffected.

        1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
        Humans have mitigated the extinction of animals for thousands of years while still changing their habitats and ways of life irrevocably.

      1.2.1.2. Pro:
      Sterile animals can be cloned to create fertile ones, so that their genetic material continues (p. 8874 [5]). This could be used to combat extinction.

      1.2.1.3. Con:
      An almost extinct species may have too limited of a gene pool to be cloned without risking perpetuating genetic defects.

        1.2.1.3.1. Con:
        Genetic defects could be genetically modified [6] in clones to make them more resilient.

      1.2.1.4. Pro:
      Cloned animals could be used for selective breeding [7] to decrease the effects of issues caused naturally or by humans.

      1.2.1.5. Pro:
      Science has advanced to the point that people are realistically considering the possibility of extinct animal zoos [8].

        1.2.1.5.1. Pro:
        Scientists from Edinburgh Zoo plan to clone the first Giant Panda [9], in attempts to save the endangered animal from extinction.

        1.2.1.5.2. Con:
        Zoos are already controversial [10] because of our treatment of animals. To revive animals just to mistreat them seems contradictory.

      1.2.1.6. Con:
      Not all extinct animals [11] can be revived as the success rate of cloning is low [12].

    1.2.2. Pro:
    Cloning certain animals can help replenish ecosystems [13].

      1.2.2.1. Pro:
      Bees are important [14] for the preservation of many species—cloning them would help fill a rapidly expanding ecological gap.

      1.2.2.2. Con:
      There are many less invasive methods of animal improvement, such as artificial insemination, sexing of embryos and sexing of semen [15], that can be used instead of cloning.

      1.2.2.3. Con:
      Cloning animals may harm the environment by reducing the diversity of animals [16].

        1.2.2.3.1. Con:
        Harming the environment is not inherently unethical, especially as it can be mitigated.

        1.2.2.3.2. Pro:
        Cloning animals may lead to overpopulation as reproductive technology is abused [17] to offer more products.

        1.2.2.3.3. Con:
        Cloning animals could eliminate genes that cause diseases giving animals a better quality of life.

        1.2.2.3.4. Pro:
        Commercial cloning destroys [18] livestock diversity since only specific breeds are used for human consumption, and, thus cloned.

    1.2.3. Con:
    Cloning to date has been small scale and, so, could not meaningfully alter the damage done to the planet by humanity.

      1.2.3.1. Pro:
      Cloning is unlikely [19] to become widespread given it is only applicable in niche scenarios.

      1.2.3.2. Con:
      Cloning could become more frequent in the future.

      1.2.3.3. Pro:
      Given the incredibly high rate of environmental degradation [20] currently taking place, we would need to rapidly increase the rate of cloning for it to have a large impact.

    1.2.4. Con:
    Cloning animals would not solve the cause of planetary damage, human greed.

      1.2.4.1. Con:
      Many animals became extinct because humans did not foresee the consequences of their actions. This is less likely to happen when the risks are known.

        1.2.4.1.1. Con:
        Humans have been aware for decades of the possibility of animal extinctions, and yet, in 2008, the Saudi Gazelle [21] was declared extinct.

      1.2.4.2. Pro:
      Humans are continuing to push animals towards extinction [22], and destroying biodiversity by razing habitats.

      1.2.4.3. Pro:
      The benefits cloning provides may be used by politicians to suggest that the threat of global warming is being lessened, thereby delaying time-sensitive policies.

        1.2.4.3.1. Pro:
        To stop ourselves from destroying the planet we must drastically alter the way we live [23]. Cloning does not incentivize this.

        1.2.4.3.2. Pro:
        Climate change is a time-senstive issue. If policy is delayed, our situtation will get irreparably worse [24].

        1.2.4.3.3. Con:
        Most people see climate change policy [25] as extremely important. It is unlikely that they will simply stop caring if cloning is used more frequently.

        1.2.4.3.4. Pro:
        Using cloning as a failsafe could create apathy towards environmental protections which are better for ecological preservation in the long term. Such moral hazard should be avoided.

  1.3. Pro:
  Animal cloning may reduce global hunger caused by food shortages [26].

    1.3.1. Pro:
    Livestock cloning, such as for "supercows [27]", can increase yields and thereby reduce food prices, the number of livestock, the environmental impacts of farming, and increase profits, all while feeding more people.

    1.3.2. Pro:
    If humans insist on eating meat, then animal cloning may be the best way to ensure sufficient supply as the population grows.

      1.3.2.1. Pro:
      Cloning allows us to engineer animals in a way that is more suitable for human consumption and demand, giving higher meat yields.

        1.3.2.1.1. Con:
        Per kilogram, meat requires far more water [28] than energy dense grains like wheat. Thus, cloning animals will be less efficient for targeting global hunger than producing more vegetables and grains.

        1.3.2.1.2. Pro:
        Scientists can genetically modify cloned animals [29] not to feel pain, reducing their suffering. This may be an alternative to veganism for those concerned about the suffering of animals.

      1.3.2.2. Con:
      Bringing sentient beings to life only to kill them afterwards for food consumption is not ethical, nor a sustainable way of supplying food.

        1.3.2.2.1. Con:
        Raising animals for food consumption was a sustainable [30] way of supplying food to humans for thousands of years before the industrial revolution.

      1.3.2.3. Pro:
      Cloning allows farmers and ranchers to accelerate the reproduction of their most productive livestock to better produce safe and healthy food.

        1.3.2.3.1. Con:
        Cloning is a time-consuming process [31] using hundreds of surrogate females, kept on high doses of estrogen, to produce one healthy clone. This is likely to make the process prohibitive for farmers.

      1.3.2.4. Con:
      It currently costs more money and resources to create a clone than to raise food animals traditionally.

        1.3.2.4.1. Pro:
        Cloning is still done using an animal host [32], making the process expensive and no more efficient than traditional animal husbandry.

          1.3.2.4.1.1. Pro:
          The length of time taken to gestate a clone is roughly the same [33] as an animal born through a normal pregnancy.

          1.3.2.4.1.2. Con:
          The advances necessary to get cloning to the point where it can be done without gestation require continued experimentation, requiring further cloning research.

        1.3.2.4.2. Con:
        Though at the moment, cloning has not reached a state where it can be used for food production on a massive scale, with more research it could in the future [34].

        1.3.2.4.3. Con:
        China has already used cloned animals [35] to reduce high-quality beef prices.

        1.3.2.4.4. Pro:
        It cost $15,000 [36] to clone a cow in 2009. A cow cost no more than $3,000 [37] in Ohio in 2017. Such a difference in costs makes cloning an unattractive prospect.

    1.3.3. Con:
    Producing more food via cloning will not solve global hunger if the distribution system [38] is not also changed.

      1.3.3.1. Pro:
      Creating more food through cloning could reduce the impetus and urgency to fix the global food distribution system.

        1.3.3.1.1. Con:
        The United Nations World Food Program [39] identifies six ways to effectively address global food inequality; none of them involve cloning.

        1.3.3.1.2. Pro:
        Policy-makers would face less pressure to eradicate the problems with food distribution if they can point to the increased food that cloning has brought into existence.

      1.3.3.2. Pro:
      In the US, the richest country in the world, many lack food security because of difficulties getting produce to supermarkets [40].

      1.3.3.3. Con:
      Genetic modifications to clones that increase the shelf-life of food [41] may make it easier and more cost effective to supply food deserts.

    1.3.4. Con:
    As we do not have sufficient data, it is not possible to know if cloned food contains harmful toxins.

      1.3.4.1. Pro:
      Clones animals are more prone to sickness [42] than non-cloned animals, which may lead to consequences for those who eat the meat.

      1.3.4.2. Con:
      The FDA [43] has said that meat from cloned animals is safe to eat.

        1.3.4.2.1. Con:
        The FDA has been wrong [44] before regarding food safety.

  1.4. Pro:
  Cloning animals can provide a way for beloved family pets to live on.

    1.4.1. Con:
    Cloning animals intervenes in a natural process which humans should not do.

      1.4.1.1. Con:
      Humans are part of nature so our interference is natural.

        1.4.1.1.1. Pro:
        There are no fixed rules for what humanity should or should not do when it comes to natural processes.

        1.4.1.1.2. Con:
        Making clones is unnautural contrary to what is the school of thought, as the clones are made in labs rather than by human fertility/reproductivity.

      1.4.1.2. Con:
      Humans have intervened with natural processes for centuries, for example, by growing produce [45].

        1.4.1.2.1. Pro:
        Major changes in human development, like the Industrial Revolution [46], have caused widespread ecological change.

        1.4.1.2.2. Con:
        Just because humans intervene in nature regularly does not mean that that is desirable to do so.

      1.4.1.3. Pro:
      Cloning animals reduces the value of life solely to functionality. As most humans seek something greater in life, we should not reduce animals in such a manner.

      1.4.1.4. Con:
      The naturalistic fallacy states that what is "natural" isn't necessarily "better".

      1.4.1.5. Pro:
      Many religions object to cloning of any kind as they see it as a "rebellion" against God, the only legitimate creator (p. 49 [47]).

        1.4.1.5.1. Con:
        Considering cloning as immoral, while not considering other interventions in natural processes as such, is hypocritical.

          1.4.1.5.1.1. Pro:
          Some religious parents refuse medical care for their children [48], as it could be seen as "playing God". Yet, we would not exist as a healthy society today were it not for scientific advancement.

        1.4.1.5.2. Pro:
        According to the Bible, God created animals [49]; cloning them is intervening in his creation, which is immoral.

        1.4.1.5.3. Con:
        Religious opposition to cloning has diminished [50] in the last few years. There is no longer a universally accepted opposition.

        1.4.1.5.4. Con:
        Insofar as not everyone believes in the same God or God at all, decisions with wide-ranging scientific and socio-political consequences should be judged secularly.

        1.4.1.5.5. Pro:
        Even when issues are discussed 'secularly', people select the arguments which appeal to them on the basis of what beliefs they already hold. As religion is an important part [51] of many people's lives, religious objections ought to be considered.

    1.4.2. Con:
    Cloning [52] animals creates the faulty expectation that owner will get the same animal, when in fact the clone will be a similar, yet distinct, animal.

      1.4.2.1. Pro:
      Though they are genetically identical, clones do not have to look like [53] their source animal. Rather, different genes may be expressed creating a perceptually different animal.

      1.4.2.2. Pro:
      As memories are not carried over to the clone, the cloned pet has no experience of the love their family has given their source, and does not even know them.

        1.4.2.2.1. Pro:
        Since the clone of an animal has its own experiences and memories, it is more akin to adopting a similar pet than reviving a lost one. Given this, it makes more sense to just adopt a similar pet.

      1.4.2.3. Con:
      Some personality traits are heavily influenced by genetics so while the cloned animal has no memories, they will likely have a similar personality.

        1.4.2.3.1. Pro:
        Much like prey drive [54] being bred into cattle dogs and tameness [55] in domesticated foxes, pets may have genetically heritable personalities.

    1.4.3. Pro:
    The death of a pet can lead their owners to experience immense levels of grief [56]. Cloning animals could provide some relief for this turmoil.

      1.4.3.1. Pro:
      Many people consider pets a part of their family [57], and thus may grieve in similar ways for their pets that they would for the death of a close relative.

        1.4.3.1.1. Con:
        As owners will still experience pet death - both through the loss of their first pet, and later of the clone itself. Thus, cloning cannot fully allay their grief.

      1.4.3.2. Pro:
      People [58] who have cloned their pets have expressed incredible happiness with the genetic clones of their original pet.

      1.4.3.3. Con:
      The idea that one can bring an animal back to life through cloning interrupts the grieving process, which is unhealthy.

        1.4.3.3.1. Pro:
        Cloning pets could deliberately mislead people about the natural order of life just to prevent the heartache of a lost pet.

        1.4.3.3.2. Con:
        Many people seek closure [59] by getting a new animal after their pet dies. Being able to clone a known pet could make achieving closure easier.

        1.4.3.3.3. Pro:
        Despite learning about death elsewhere, encouraging children to believe that animals could be cloned to bring them back to life is false and problematic.

      1.4.3.4. Con:
      It is unlikely that the pet owner will feel their new, cloned pet is the same [60] as they one they lost.

    1.4.4. Pro:
    Cloning of service dogs [61] can help those who rely on their work, such as those who are blind or have other disabilities.

      1.4.4.1. Con:
      A cloned service dog still needs to be trained; there is no guarantee it will do as well as its original.

      1.4.4.2. Pro:
      Service dogs may have qualities that are both rare and highly desirable [62], making the impact of cloning them hugely important.

      1.4.4.3. Con:
      Service dogs can be bred [63], which leads to far fewer ethical questions.

  1.5. Con:
  Cloning causes animals to suffer.

    1.5.1. Pro:
    Cloning is highly inefficient [64], meaning that surrogate mothers often go through many procedures for little benefit.

      1.5.1.1. Pro:
      Researchers [65] understand there are many failed attempts before an animal is successfully cloned and thus a high likelihood of suffering for those who do not survive.

    1.5.2. Con:
    Cloning an animal does not require harming an animal.

      1.5.2.1. Pro:
      Researchers may be able to harvest living cells or tissues to use in cloning without harming the animal.

      1.5.2.2. Pro:
      Laboratories already use naturally born animals for experimentation, arguably cloning would require less animals to be exploited because breeding stock would be unnecessary.

        1.5.2.2.1. Con:
        The fact that an animal is cloned does not imply that it suffers or feels less the exploitation.

      1.5.2.3. Con:
      Even with a low success rate there shouldn't be an assumption that they don't suffer. Many clones are born with diseases [66] and deformities [67], dying within hours or days.

      1.5.2.4. Con:
      A cloned animal is still a living creature that can feel pain [68], which is unfortunately inherent to the cloning process.

      1.5.2.5. Pro:
      If an animal could be cloned without a central nervous system, it could be spared pain [69].

    1.5.3. Con:
    The act of cloning an animal does not impact the quality of life of either the clone or the original since clones are genetically identical to the original. Only environmental factors can cause the clone to have a different quality of life.

    1.5.4. Pro:
    Cloning animals weakens their gene pool.

      1.5.4.1. Pro:
      Cloned animals have weaker immune systems, as seen in the case of the cloned monkeys [70] in China.

        1.5.4.1.1. Con:
        Cloning by itself doesn't cause [71] the weakening of animals' immune systems. It could also be due to lacking the support that breast milk [72] provides.

      1.5.4.2. Con:
      Selective breeding already weakens the natural gene pool of animals. Cloning animals makes no significant difference.

      1.5.4.3. Pro:
      Cloning reduces [73] genetic variation, even more than in-breeding.

      1.5.4.4. Con:
      If cloned animals are bred then the risk of disease [74] is reduced. If the total number of individuals increase, then other concerns can be overcome.

        1.5.4.4.1. Con:
        The quantitative effect of cloning on the gene pool would be negligent, far smaller than the effect of current practices such as breeding.

      1.5.4.5. Pro:
      Clones often have more fragile health than their parent and require more attention to survive.

        1.5.4.5.1. Con:
        Overtime, technology will perfect cloning, improving the health and genetic replication of the animal.

      1.5.4.6. Con:
      "Strength" of genetics is contextual [75] and subjective [76].

    1.5.5. Pro:
    Cloned animals may be born with severe abnormalities [77].

      1.5.5.1. Con:
      Conventionally breeding animals may also result in animals with severe abnormalities.

      1.5.5.2. Pro:
      It's possible for cloning to produce an animal with an inherently high chance of suffering depending on the genetic disorders/predispositions of the original.

      1.5.5.3. Pro:
      Dolly [78] the sheep, the first animal to be cloned, was fraught with debilitating health issues that resulted in her premature death.

        1.5.5.3.1. Con:
        Dolly had four sisters [79] that developed normally and lived normal lives without major complications.

        1.5.5.3.2. Con:
        It is undetermined [80] if Dolly's early death was a result of cloning or if it was because she had other unrelated health issues.

      1.5.5.4. Con:
      In the future, after more scientific study, it is possible that cloning could lessen the suffering of both animals and humans.

        1.5.5.4.1. Pro:
        As with developments [81] in gene editing, cloning could be improved to avoid the severe abnormalities that lead to animal suffering.

        1.5.5.4.2. Pro:
        Unfortunately, the current state of science can cause harm, still progress on this field, as in any other, requires experiments. With enough caution we can reduce suffering until we eliminate all of it.

        1.5.5.4.3. Con:
        The hypothetical benefits of future improvements in cloning do not justify the current harm that it does.

          1.5.5.4.3.1. Pro:
          The act of improving methods means experimenting on more animals to perfect results, adding to suffering.

      1.5.5.5. Con:
      An existence with some suffering may be preferable to never having existed.

        1.5.5.5.1. Pro:
        Humans who live with disabilities [82] after an accident often say they are happy to be alive, rather than wishing they were dead.

        1.5.5.5.2. Con:
        Many people choose euthanasia [83] rather than live in suffering.

      1.5.5.6. Pro:
      In a sample of cloned mice, 1 in 25 had severe abnormalities [84].

    1.5.6. Con:
    The possible scientific advancements of cloning outweigh the ethical issues.

      1.5.6.1. Con:
      The possibility of progress cannot supercede [85] ethical study as it devalues those it is meant to serve and can lead to considerable harm.

      1.5.6.2. Pro:
      Cloning would allow the preservation of species and biodiversity for ecosystems, including by facilitating the resurrection of extinct species.

        1.5.6.2.1. Pro:
        The knowledge gained from cloning may save many other animals from suffering in the long run.

        1.5.6.2.2. Pro:
        Cloning could allow scientists to save endangered species at a faster rate.

          1.5.6.2.2.1. Pro:
          Cloning may be the only option in some cases to save a species.

          1.5.6.2.2.2. Con:
          Cloning animals could lead people to remove protections from endangered animals, putting them in more danger.

        1.5.6.2.3. Con:
        The money used for conservation of resurrected species would be more efficiently [86] spent preventing extinction in the first place.

        1.5.6.2.4. Con:
        Bringing a species back from extinction without their environmental [87] or ecological niche could lead them to die again, live in zoos, or become a dangerous invasive species.

        1.5.6.2.5. Con:
        Animals exist in a dynamic and connected ecosystem. Cloning could have a butterfly effect that causes unintended and unanticipated havoc.

      1.5.6.3. Pro:
      Cloning animals can aid in stem cell research which would improve the lives of many people.

        1.5.6.3.1. Con:
        Private patenting and control over genetic improvements could exacerbate existing social inequalities, as only some would be able to afford it.

        1.5.6.3.2. Pro:
        Cloning could allow humanity to genetically modify food and fight off ailments like blood clots [88].

        1.5.6.3.3. Pro:
        To develop therapeutic cloning, which could help humans treat diabetes and Alzheimer's, research on stem cells needs to be done with animal cloning [89].

      1.5.6.4. Con:
      Progress does not justify all scientific experimentation. Thinking otherwise is how eugenics [90] was legitimated by scientists pursuing progress.

        1.5.6.4.1. Con:
        The harm created by an act is justified [91], if the primary intention of that act was moral.

  1.6. Con:
  Animals cannot consent to being cloned.

    1.6.1. Con:
    Consent of animals is irrelevant, since consent is a human construct developed to facilitate human coexistence.

      1.6.1.1. Con:
      Consent is important because we are able to discern what is 'good' and 'bad' for us. Insofar as animals can make such choices, they have a right to consent [92] to what happens to them.

        1.6.1.1.1. Pro:
        Insofar as animals can experience pain [93] and take measures to avoid it, they have the sufficient mental capacity to identify conditions they do not wish to be in.

      1.6.1.2. Con:
      Animals can consent and dissent [94], but humans simply do not understand the languages of animals.

      1.6.1.3. Pro:
      There are cases when consent is secondary [95] to public benefits. For example, people often do not consent to go to prison but are still sent for the benefit of society.

    1.6.2. Pro:
    Animals cannot understand the consequences of cloning, so cannot give informed consent. [96]

      1.6.2.1. Pro:
      Behind a veil of ignorance [97], it is more likely that humans would consent to their existence as most live good lives or are likely to see their situation improve [98]. Animals do not [99].

      1.6.2.2. Con:
      No animal, including humans, can consent to their own creation.

    1.6.3. Pro:
    As the animal gains nothing from being cloned, it might not wish to undergo even slight discomfort.

      1.6.3.1. Con:
      Animals are regularly discomforted and killed for human benefit. Cloning is not unique.

        1.6.3.1.1. Pro:
        Animals suffer consistently in the status quo. For example, many animals are born into factories or industrial farms where they live [100] purely for human benefit.

        1.6.3.1.2. Pro:
        While there exist strict regulations, animal testing during medical trials [101] is widespread.

        1.6.3.1.3. Con:
        Just because an activity is widespread [102] does not mean it is morally justified.
