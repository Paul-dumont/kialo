Discussion Name:
Should juveniles who commit violent crime be treated as adults in the criminal justice system?

1. Thesis:
Juveniles who commit violent crimes should be treated as adults in the criminal justice system.

  1.1. Pro:
  Treating juveniles who commit violent crimes as adults in the criminal justice system will encourage more serious punishment, therefore better protecting society from these offenders.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    There is a genetic basis for violent offending. Rehabilitation is unlikely to be effective in reforming juveniles whose violent behaviors are a manifestation of this genetic endowment [1].

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      The argument of abandoning rehabilitative measures on the grounds of genetic predisposition could be expanded to adult offenders, rendering the point of rehabilitative programmes and prisons worthless.

      1.1.1.2. Pro:
      A genetic analysis of 900 offenders in Finland found that those with 2 particular genes are 13 times more likely to have a history of repeated violent behaviour [2].

      1.1.1.3. Con:
      There has been no causal link [3] established between the gene environment and a specific act of violence for which a defendent is put on trial.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    Juveniles commit violent crimes in high numbers. This suggests that there is a need to protect society from juvenile perpetrators of violent crime.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      There is an average of 35,001 assaults [4] committed by teenagers just in the US. This is an unacceptable rate and if the government doesn’t act, these numbers are likely to increase.

        1.1.2.1.1. Con:
        Just because violent crime committed by juveniles is increasing does not necessarily mean that trying them as adults is an effective deterrence.

          1.1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
          Adolescents are not able to rationally weigh the longer term risks [5] of committing violent crime, and so are unlikely to be deterred by a change in the criminal justice system.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      Proving that juveniles commit a significant number of violent crimes does not make an argument for what the most effective method of protecting society from these criminals are.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    The majority of violent crime tends to be committed by a small group of repeat offenders [6]. This strongly suggests the need to protect society from that small group of offenders.

      1.1.3.1. Con:
      Most juveniles who commit violent crimes do not reoffend [7].

    1.1.4. Con:
    More serious punishment will increase the danger posed by criminals who commit violent crimes.

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      The chance of recidivism for juvenile offenders that are placed in adult prisons is higher [8] than if they are placed in juvenile detention centers.

      1.1.4.2. Pro:
      Longer prison sentences will reduce the opportunities available for juvenile offendors to improve life prospects.

      1.1.4.3. Con:
      Increasing the length of prison sentences will worsen the problem of overcrowding [9] and thus undermine the safety and rehabilitation of prisoners.

    1.1.5. Con:
    There is no demonstrated link between increased public safety and increasing the severity of criminal punishment.

      1.1.5.1. Pro:
      Long sentences are ineffective at improving public safety for violent drug related crimes [10] as drug dealers are easily replaced.

      1.1.5.2. Pro:
      Deterrence is not a function of the severity of punishment but instead, is a function of the certainty of punishment [11].

      1.1.5.3. Con:
      When prisoners are incarcerated, they have no contact with wider society. Thus, if they are incarcerated for a longer period, there is an increased period of time in which they cannot harm wider society.

        1.1.5.3.1. Con:
        A lot of European nations prioritise rehabilitation and let them life a normal life in prison, even encouraging them to engage with the broader society once a month.

          1.1.5.3.1.1. Pro:
          Reduced crime rates

      1.1.5.4. Pro:
      According to experts [12], the severity of punishment, known as marginal deterrence, has no real deterrent effect, or the effect of reducing recidivism.

        1.1.5.4.1. Pro:
        A meta-analysis [13] found that the thread of punishment did not deter from more serious crimes, such as homicide.

  1.2. Con:
  The criminal justice system should treat juveniles who commit violent crimes more leniantly, as this benefits society more.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Rehabilitative programmes over regular prison sentences could be structured so that they involve extensive community service.

      1.2.1.1. Con:
      -> See 1.1.1.

      1.2.1.2. Pro:
      Teaching minor juvenile offenders the value of community effort and hard work, by putting them in community service or hospitals, might have a rehabilitative effect.

      1.2.1.3. Con:
      If community service involves social interaction with the public, this may be dangerous for the wider public.

    1.2.2. Con:
    Adults also have the capacity to benefit wider society. Thus, the argument does not sufficiently explain why criminal justice system should be more forgiving of juveniles in particular.

      1.2.2.1. Con:
      Relative to juveniles, adults on average have less potential to benefit society. Therefore, an argument that calls for criminals to be given more leniant treatment based on their capacity to benefit wider society would be more applicable to juveniles.

        1.2.2.1.1. Pro:
        Due to the relatively average age of the adult prison population, adults will generally contribute to society for a shorter period of time than juveniles.

    1.2.3. Con:
    If policy was implemented based on this logic, the length of prison sentences would be determined by the defendents capacity for socio-economic productivity.

      1.2.3.1. Pro:
      A policy like this would undermine the 'rule of law' as it would suggest moral culpability is linked to socio-economic productivity. In theory, even the most serious criminals could be given more leniant treatment.

      1.2.3.2. Con:
      When determining the length of sentencing, the capacity for the defendent to benefit society could be considered alongside other factors, rather than in isolation.

    1.2.4. Pro:
    More leniant sentencing for juveniles will help reduce overcrowding in prisons and therefore better ensure the health of the prison system at large.

      1.2.4.1. Pro:
      As of 2015, the prison population per capita in the US quadrupled over the period of 40 years [14] (p.1).

      1.2.4.2. Pro:
      Prison overcrowding undermines the health of prisoners.

        1.2.4.2.1. Pro:
        Prison overcrowding may undermine the ability for prisoners to receive essential medical care [15].

        1.2.4.2.2. Pro:
        Prison overcrowding leads to greater prevalence of disease. [16]

      1.2.4.3. Pro:
      Prison overcrowding undermines the safety of prisoners.

        1.2.4.3.1. Pro:
        One study found a correlation between prison overcrowding and prison violence [17].

      1.2.4.4. Con:
      It is more fair to build additional prisons rather than giving some criminals leniant sentences.

      1.2.4.5. Con:
      The need to provide leniant sentences due to a lack of space in prisons indicates that the prison system isn't working.

    1.2.5. Pro:
    Lengthy prison sentences will undermine the education of juveniles and thus limit their ability to develop a skillset and contribute to society.

      1.2.5.1. Pro:
      Giving juveniles more leniant sentences means that they are sooner able to contribute and participate in the economy.

      1.2.5.2. Con:
      Realising individual potential through the development of a skillset is a privilege that should not be granted to someone who is being punished for breaking the law, regardless of whether it benefits society.

      1.2.5.3. Con:
      Juveniles are able develop skillsets while inside prison.

        1.2.5.3.1. Pro:
        Juvenile prisoners retain "special civil rights to education, vocational training, and other services that may require additional or special program [18]" (p.9).

        1.2.5.3.2. Con:
        A report from Human Rights Watch found that youth serving life without parole are often denied access to many classes [19] (p.2).

      1.2.5.4. Pro:
      Education is linked to [20] personal satisfaction and professional success.

  1.3. Pro:
  Juveniles who commit violent crimes should be held accountable for their actions.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    If a juvenile receives less harsh punishment than an adult, justice will not be appropriately served.

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      Failure to provide adequate punishment may prevent victims and their families from receiving closure.

        1.3.1.1.1. Pro:
        Appropriate sentencing [21] can often provide a sense of closure to victims and their families, for example, when the death penalty is recommended (p. 490).

          1.3.1.1.1.1. Pro:
          About one-fourth [22] of anecdotal reports showed that the victims' families said, usually without elaboration, that 'justice was done" by the execution (p. 494).

        1.3.1.1.2. Pro:
        A survey [23] found that 60% of those surveyed "agreed either strongly or somewhat" that capital punishment brought closure to homicide families (p. 383). This would imply that harsher punishments to criminals provide closure to families of victims.

        1.3.1.1.3. Con:
        Healing is a dynamic and lengthy process [24] that requires victims themselves to take active steps to facilitate their own recovery. The justice system has limited influence on this process (p. 458).

        1.3.1.1.4. Con:
        Trials can retraumatize victims [25], making it harder for them to gain closure. Trials for violent crimes, in the case of adults, can take as long as several years [26] to conclude.

          1.3.1.1.4.1. Con:
          Juvenile courts tend to take less time [27] because of the absence of juries.

          1.3.1.1.4.2. Con:
          A study [28] found that the retraumatizing hypothesis was unfounded and lacked substantive support.

        1.3.1.1.5. Con:
        The ambiguity [29] surrounding emotions such as closure makes it a problematic priority to focus on when attempting to create and maintain effective and just legal institutions (p. 28).

      1.3.1.2. Con:
      Many victims and the families of victims may accept that the age of the perpetrator should have an impact on the severity of punishment.

      1.3.1.3. Con:
      Victims and their families are likely to have diverging notions of justice [30].

      1.3.1.4. Pro:
      Public support leans towards increasing the severity of punishment [31] for juvenile delinquents (p. 4).

      1.3.1.5. Pro:
      Many members of the public [32] are generally dissatisfied with the leniency of juvenile courts (p. 4).

    1.3.2. Pro:
    Failing to hold juveniles accountable legitimizes their actions.

      1.3.2.1. Con:
      Changing the legal treatment of juveniles so that they are treated more leniantly does not equate to failing to hold juveniles accountable entirely.

      1.3.2.2. Pro:
      If juveniles are not held fully accountable, they are more likely to assume that they are able to commit violent crimes without significant risk of punishment.

        1.3.2.2.1. Pro:
        Juveniles who are not held to account may display a pattern of criminal behaviour where the nature of the crime becomes more serious over time.

        1.3.2.2.2. Con:
        Juvenile detention facilities still exist to punish juveniles in the same fundamental ways that an adult prison would by taking away their freedom of movement. Thus, juveniles would not hold the view that engaging in violent crime comes without severe risk of punishment.

        1.3.2.2.3. Pro:
        The threat of more severe punishment is an effective deterrent to crime.

          1.3.2.2.3.1. Pro:
          By increasing the perception [33] that criminals will be caught and punished, the police is able to deter crime (p. 1).

          1.3.2.2.3.2. Con:
          -> See 1.1.5.2.

          1.3.2.2.3.3. Con:
          -> See 1.1.5.4.

      1.3.2.3. Con:
      Consideration of contexual factors, such as the age of the defendent does not amount to legitimizing the actions of the defendent altogether.

        1.3.2.3.1. Pro:
        If contextual factors are not considered, the implication is that all sentences should be equal for the same crimes.

          1.3.2.3.1.1. Pro:
          It is not reasonable to treat someone who commits a crime unintentionally the same as someone who intentionally commits a crime.

          1.3.2.3.1.2. Con:
          This implication would only be true if age was the only contextual factor considered when determining sentancing length. It is plausible that other components of the crime would outweigh any leniancy proposed due to the age of the perpetrator.

        1.3.2.3.2. Con:
        In most cases, age is not relevant to the circumstances of the crime and thus should not be considered as a relevant contextual factor.

      1.3.2.4. Pro:
      Juveniles will not appreciate the scale of harm they have caused, or the ramifcations of their choices without appropriate sentencing.

        1.3.2.4.1. Pro:
        Children can cause a lot of social and economic damage if they aren't punished for their actions before the age of maturity.

          1.3.2.4.1.1. Pro:
          Petty crimes, like vandalism and theft, can still have severe economic consequences on small businesses and community health.

          1.3.2.4.1.2. Con:
          Putting children into adult prisons would cause severe economic and social damage, as they would be exposed to adult criminal activity and may come back with other issues [34].

        1.3.2.4.2. Con:
        Juveniles may have values that are not aligned with those reflected in the criminal justice system. In these cases, increasing the severity of punishment is unlikely to make them any more conscious of the harm they have caused.

    1.3.3. Con:
    Juveniles do not have the same capacity to make reasonable decisions when compared to adults.

      1.3.3.1. Con:
      It is possible to take the mental capacity of the defendent into account when determining the sentence, regardless of whether the defendent is an adult or a juvenile.

        1.3.3.1.1. Pro:
        Even where adults don't have full mental capacity to make judgement calls, this is likely to be factored in [35] with sentencing.

        1.3.3.1.2. Pro:
        If mental capacity can be lacking in both adults and juvenile perpatrators, age is not the variable through which a rule should be established.

      1.3.3.2. Pro:
      Children who end up in custody are more likely to have a learning disability [36].

        1.3.3.2.1. Con:
        Even if children in custody are relatively more likely to have a learning disability, the majority of children in custody do not have a learning disability.

        1.3.3.2.2. Pro:
        Children in prison are twice as likely to have special educational needs [37].

        1.3.3.2.3. Con:
        Having a learning disability does not automatically mean that person cannot make reasonable decisions. There needs to be a link established between the specific learning disability and the ability to make specific decisions relevant to the context in which the crimes occured.

      1.3.3.3. Pro:
      The lack of prefrontal cortex development [38] undermines the decision-making ability of juveniles.

        1.3.3.3.1. Pro:
        The lack of development to the prefrontal cortex results in juveniles taking less time to think and delay before acting [39].

        1.3.3.3.2. Pro:
        Juveniles are less likely to consider the consequences of their actions [40] before acting.

        1.3.3.3.3. Pro:
        The lack of prefrontal cortex development means that juveniles are more likely to act on impulse without considering all the options available to them [41].

          1.3.3.3.3.1. Pro:
          Violent crime can be committed impulsively [42] when juveniles react emotionally to something.

        1.3.3.3.4. Con:
        Even if juveniles have a less developed prefrontal cortex, the vast majority of juveniles do not commit violent crimes. This suggests that the lack of prefrontal cortex cannot alone explain why some juveniles commit violent crimes.

      1.3.3.4. Con:
      People do not have equal capacity to make reasonable decisions, thus it does not make sense to implement legal policy based on a generalised assumption.

        1.3.3.4.1. Pro:
        There is no need to have a general rule regarding juvenile defendants in particular and their mental capacity. Instead, mental capacity of all defendents can be considered on a case-by-case basis.

        1.3.3.4.2. Pro:
        Some juveniles may have more capacity to make reasonable decsions than some adults.

        1.3.3.4.3. Con:
        Even if all people do not have equal capacity to make reasonable decisions, policy can be implemented based on general tendanices that apply to the majority.

      1.3.3.5. Pro:
      Juveniles tend to have worse logical reasoning skills which undermines their ability to decisions.

        1.3.3.5.1. Pro:
        Research shows [43] that juveniles are not viewed as capable enough of performing many tasks that adults are viewed as capable enough of performing.

          1.3.3.5.1.1. Pro:
          Minors aren't allowed to vote in national elections or drink alcohol, showing that the system doesn't treat them like adults. Hence, it is unclear why they should be punished like adults.

        1.3.3.5.2. Pro:
        Juveniles are not allowed to make their own medical decisions because they are not believed to be rational and informed enough to be able to make high-stake decisions.

          1.3.3.5.2.1. Con:
          In the UK, juveniles aged 16 or 17, are presumed to have sufficient capacity [44] to decide on their own medical treatment.

        1.3.3.5.3. Con:
        Logical reasoning skills vary from person to person, regardless of whether the individual is a juvenile or not.

        1.3.3.5.4. Con:
        One study suggests that children can be more logical than adults [45].

      1.3.3.6. Pro:
      Many juveniles find it difficult to cope with school life, peer pressure, and mental health issues. This may influence their behavior.

        1.3.3.6.1. Con:
        Most juveniles and adults who likely experience similar life pressures, do not commit violent crimes. Thus, these life pressures cannot persuasively explain or justify violent crime.

  1.4. Con:
  If juveniles are treated as adults in the criminal justice system, they are likely to be incarcerated in adult prisons. This is dangerous for juveniles.

    1.4.1. Con:
    Prisons have safeguards in place for juvenile prisoners in adult prisons.

      1.4.1.1. Pro:
      Federal law has been passed to protect juveniles in the prison system. The Juvenile Justice and Delinquency Prevention Act of 1974 (“JJDPA”) and the Prison Rape Elimination Act of 2003 (“PREA”) require that juvenile offenders are housed seperated from adults [46].

      1.4.1.2. Con:
      Even though prisons have safeguards in place, that does not mean that each type or category of prisoner are equally safe.

      1.4.1.3. Pro:
      Adult prisons have administrative segregation [47], which can provide a means of dealing with especially violent or problematic inmates.

      1.4.1.4. Pro:
      Federal guidelines call for the elimination of contact in common areas between juvenile and adult inmates. [48]

      1.4.1.5. Pro:
      Federal guidelines call for increased supervision of youth in adult prisons. [49]

      1.4.1.6. Con:
      The disproportionate danger for juvenile prisoners incarcerated in adult prisons suggests that even if there are safeguards in place, they are often not effective.

    1.4.2. Pro:
    Juveniles placed in adult prisons are more likely to suffer from mental health issues.

      1.4.2.1. Pro:
      Juveniles in adult prisons are disportionately more likely to commit suicide.

        1.4.2.1.1. Pro:
        Juveniles in adult prisons are up to nine times more likely to commit suicide [50] than those in juvenile facilities.

        1.4.2.1.2. Pro:
        The 2018 report, 'Getting to Zero' finds that youth in adult facilities are as much as thirty-six times more likely [51] to commit suicide than youth in juvenile facilities.

        1.4.2.1.3. Con:
        Comparison of suicide statistics at best gives us a comparion of the number of individuals with the most serious of mental health issues. This is a small subset of the overall number of individuals with some degree of mental health issues and therefore not an appropriate comparison.

        1.4.2.1.4. Pro:
        The suicide rates for juveniles held in jails is eight times [52] higher than the rate for adolescents in juvenile detention facilities (p. 8).

      1.4.2.2. Pro:
      Juveniles that are incarcerated in adult prisons have worse long-term mental health outcomes [53] than those incarcerated in juvenile detention centres.

      1.4.2.3. Con:
      Juveniles have access to mental health support in adult prisons.

      1.4.2.4. Con:
      Research suggests there is a link between having serious mental health issues and committing violent crime [54]. Therefore, in many cases, the perpetrator of the crime will already have mental health issues going into prison.

        1.4.2.4.1. Pro:
        According to a 2017 report from the Department of Justice, around 37% of people in prison have a history of mental health issues [55].

      1.4.2.5. Pro:
      One study [56] found that youth incarcerated in adult prisons are at greater risk of depression.

    1.4.3. Con:
    If there are issues with prisoner safety, the focus should be on making adult prisons safer, not removing juveniles from adult prisons.

      1.4.3.1. Pro:
      Removing juveniles from adult prisons does not deal with these safety issues or prevent similar issues involving adult prisoners from arising in the future.

      1.4.3.2. Pro:
      The presence of juveniles in adult prisoners may incentivize governments to make prisons more safe for the entirety of the prison population.

      1.4.3.3. Con:
      Improving safety standards in general does not seek to address the fact that adult prisons are disproportionately dangerous for juveniles.

      1.4.3.4. Con:
      Improving safety standards for adult prisons is less likely to suceed as it is more of a generalized aim than removing juveniles from adult prisons.

      1.4.3.5. Con:
      The goal of improving safety for juvenile prisoners in adult prisons is not mutually exclusive with the goal of making prisons more safe generally.

    1.4.4. Pro:
    Juveniles placed in adult prisons are at significant risk of sexual abuse.

      1.4.4.1. Pro:
      Data from the National Inmate Survey finds that 1.8% of 16 and 17-year-old [57] inmates in adult prisons are being sexually abused by other inmates or prison staff.

        1.4.4.1.1. Pro:
        Only 10% of the sexual abuse cases in the survey were reported [58], suggesting that statistics may drastically underplay the risk posed to juveniles.

      1.4.4.2. Con:
      The problem of sexual abuse in prisons could be more seriously addressed without changing incarceration procedures through means such as increasing the presence of prison officers.

      1.4.4.3. Pro:
      Juveniles incarcerated with adults are at the highest risk [59] of sexual abuse at the hand of other inmates.

    1.4.5. Pro:
    Juveniles placed in adult prisons are at significant risk of physical violence.

      1.4.5.1. Pro:
      Children tend to be physically weaker and therefore are less likely to be able to protect themselves from violence.

        1.4.5.1.1. Pro:
        Maximal muscle force is lower [60] in children than in adults.

      1.4.5.2. Con:
      There are many other risk factors [61] other than whether a prisoner is a juvenile or not that also increase the risk of having violent encounters in adult prisons.

      1.4.5.3. Con:
      By some accounts [62], bullying and violent assult are more pronounced in youth offenders institutions.

      1.4.5.4. Pro:
      A study found that although both adults and juveniles incarcerated in adult prisons were likely to be the victims of property crimes, juvenile victims of property crimes were more likely to be subject to violence (p.8) [63].

      1.4.5.5. Pro:
      One study found that youth in adult prisons were 50% more likely of being physically assulated by prison staff [64] (p.1).

    1.4.6. Con:
    Adult prisons are best equipped to contain violent offenders.

      1.4.6.1. Pro:
      Adults prisons have different levels of security [65] and are thus more appropriately designed to contain inmates.

        1.4.6.1.1. Pro:
        Federal prisons have five levels of security [66].

      1.4.6.2. Pro:
      Many practicioners have stated that younger offenders are often more difficult to manage than older offenders [67] (p.9).

        1.4.6.2.1. Pro:
        One study [68] found that prison misconduct and violence is higher amongst juvenile inmates when c against young adult inamtes.

        1.4.6.2.2. Pro:
        In a period of five years, assults in youth prisons in England and Wales have increased by around two-thirds [69].

        1.4.6.2.3. Con:
        Younger offenders being more challenging for staff to cope with is a more persuasive argument for why they should be incarcerated seperately to adults. Staff could be more specifically trained to deal and interact with younger offenders.

      1.4.6.3. Con:
      Not all individuals convicted of violent crimes will need to be contained to the same degree. Therefore, a single type of prison cannot be considered the best solution for all violent offenders.

    1.4.7. Pro:
    Treating juveniles as adults may turn them into violent repeat offenders, which is not good.

      1.4.7.1. Pro:
      Germany [70] keeps juveniles out of the adult prisons to avoid repeated offenders

  1.5. Pro:
  Nowadays, juveniles are used by syndicates to do crime because of their advantages [71] on penalties. Reducing those advantages will dissuade the syndicates from using them by some degree. This shall be governed carefully to avoid moral problems to the juvenile.

  1.6. Pro:
  The age of the criminal makes no difference to the victim and their families, hence juveniles should not get special treatment.
