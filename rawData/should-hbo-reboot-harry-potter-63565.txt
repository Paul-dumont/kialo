Discussion Name:
Should HBO reboot Harry Potter?

1. Thesis:
HBO should reboot Harry Potter.

  1.1. Con:
  A Harry Potter reboot is likely to be financially harmful for HBO and take away time and money from other projects.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    The Harry Potter series has been given a ten-year timeline [1], which means it would monopolize HBO resources for a significant period of time.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      HBO has experience shooting multiple blockbuster shows [2] at a time and therefore has experience optimizing resources across various projects.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      HBO has multiple long-term contracts, so it is obligated to continue [3] shooting new original projects.

      1.1.1.3. Pro:
      Adapting a beloved fantasy series like Harry Potter demands meticulous attention to detail in terms of sets, costumes, special effects, and post-production work.

      1.1.1.4. Pro:
      A ten-year timeline indicates a long-term commitment from HBO's creative team, which may limit their capacity to work on other innovative projects during that time.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    HBO's original programming is already delayed [4] due to the Hollywood Writers' Strike. A massive new venture could delay this programming even further.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      HBO's original programming is also being delayed by the SAG strike [5].

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      Many HBO shows can continue filming despite [6] the strike.

      1.1.2.3. Pro:
      HBO chairman and CEO predicted a SAG strike could severely impact [7] their programming capacities.

      1.1.2.4. Con:
      It is going to be easier to adapt Harry Potter compared to new projects since the source material is largely already available and fan fiction has provided supplemental material [8] for writers to use.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Producing Harry Potter is going to be more expensive [9] than producing other shows.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      Harry Potter is set in a fantastical realm, and recreating this world demands a multitude of costly special effects. [10]

      1.1.3.2. Pro:
      Similar to the original series [11], the Harry Potter reboot will probably require a distinct location apart from the Warner Bros. film studio, contributing to increased production expenses. [12]

      1.1.3.3. Con:
      The Harry Potter reboot, like the original series [13], is expected to feature relatively unknown child actors in lead roles, which could be a more cost-effective choice compared to recruiting established adult actors for HBO's other shows.

    1.1.4. Con:
    A Harry Potter franchise will help redefine HBO's offerings and public perception.

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      The Harry Potter franchise appeals to a wider age group [14] than HBO's current hits.

      1.1.4.2. Con:
      HBO is well-known for massive fantasy series such as Game of Thrones [15] and House of the Dragon [16]. Pursuing Harry Potter will merely affirm this perception.

        1.1.4.2.1. Pro:
        The increase in fantasy shows has likely created genre fatigue [17], and therefore, tying HBO to the genre will likely not increase patronage.

        1.1.4.2.2. Con:
        Focusing solely on fantasy adaptations might limit HBO's ability to diversify its content portfolio and cater to a broader range of viewer interests.

      1.1.4.3. Pro:
      Since most users [18] would not want to have subscriptions for every service, having good shows for all ages and genres [19] is the safest bet for survival and growth.

    1.1.5. Con:
    HBO's parent company, Warner Bros., is currently valued at $27.54 billion [20], meaning it likely has the resources to produce multiple high-quality shows in tandem.

      1.1.5.1. Con:
      Warner Bros. had over $53 billion in debt [21] last year.

      1.1.5.2. Con:
      Warner Bros. has admitted that they've had to cut costs on producing various shows as revenues have declined [22].

      1.1.5.3. Con:
      Warner Bros. has had to delay [23] movies because they did not have the funds to produce them.

      1.1.5.4. Pro:
      Warner Bros. streaming profits were $50 million, signaling an upturn [24] from the previous year.

      1.1.5.5. Pro:
      Warner Bros. has increased [25] their subscriber rates, showing a strong base of income for the future.

      1.1.5.6. Pro:
      Warner Bros. already owns the rights [26] to the Harry Potter franchise and, hence, should maximise its earnings from it.

        1.1.5.6.1. Con:
        Warner Bros. paid $2 million [27] to J.K. Rowling for the rights to the intellectual property and has already made a huge profit, in the billions [28], through the massive success of the films.

      1.1.5.7. Con:
      Warner Bros. lost [29] money in the first quarter of 2023, signaling their profitability isn't guaranteed.

    1.1.6. Con:
    New and original projects are risky ventures for studios to greenlight in comparison with a widely beloved [30] property such as Harry Potter.

      1.1.6.1. Pro:
      It is going to be easier to adapt Harry Potter compared to new projects since there is already guaranteed profit potential from an existing fan base. [31]

      1.1.6.2. Con:
      The growing critique [32] of remakes and sequels for lacking originality is likely to eventually lead to a decline in viewership.

      1.1.6.3. Con:
      The current Harry Potter fanbase is likely to diminish over time as individuals age and their preferences evolve. [33]

      1.1.6.4. Con:
      Past reboots of successful movies have often failed. [34]

      1.1.6.5. Pro:
      Any Harry Potter reboot is likely to generate massive revenue and consequently increase employment in the entertainment and allied industries.

      1.1.6.6. Pro:
      As HBO is currently trying to get [35] more subscribers to its Max platform, relying on popular stories is more likely to guarantee sign-ups.

      1.1.6.7. Pro:
      Having guaranteed streams of revenue [36] from popular franchises such as Harry Potter allows HBO to take risks on new projects.

      1.2.3.4. Con:
      Departing significantly from the original books can lead to disappointment among fans [55] who have strong emotional connections to the source material.
