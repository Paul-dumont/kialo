Discussion Name:
Is the constitution nearly dead?

1. Thesis:
Laws, superseding the constitution, have become a serious problem.

  1.1. Pro:
  Special group protection laws have superseded the "all men (woman too) are created equal" clause allowing discrimination.

    1.1.1. Con:
    Laws that purport to guarantee equality before the law often overlook the very real inequalities that exist [1] in the status quo, such as the failure to consider race as an important factor while Black people face active discrimination in society.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    President Johnson's Executive Order 11246, which mandated affirmative action, contradicted the Supreme Court's decision in Brown v. Board of Education, which interpreted the Constitution as being "colorblind" and non-discriminatory (pp. 11-13 [2]).

  1.2. Con:
  Constitutions are neither alive nor dead. They are enforced through the capacity for collective action in society (e.g. see Weingast 1997).

  1.3. Pro:
  The separation between the church and state has weakened.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    The Supreme Court ruled in favor of a Washington high school football coach [3] who sought to continue his practice of praying at midfield after games, citing his right to religious freedom.

    1.3.2. Pro:
    The U.S. Supreme Court ruled against laws [4] that prevent parents from using public funding to enroll their children in religious schools.

    1.3.3. Con:
    Many Americans support [5] the erosion of the separation between church and state, and therefore the Constitution must evolve to account for this.

  1.4. Pro:
  Antidrug laws, have removed (drugs used as "the excuse") a plethora of our constitutional rights.

    1.4.1. Pro:
    Various regulations allow law enforcement to infringe on an individual's Fourth Amendment [6] rights under the guise of combating drugs.

      1.4.1.1. Pro:
      The Supreme Court has ruled that if a police officer has reason to believe there are drugs in a car, they can search it without a warrant [7].

      1.4.1.2. Pro:
      Anti-drug laws permit law enforcement to enter properties without prior notice [8].

        1.4.1.2.1. Con:
        No-knock warrants are essential to prevent the destruction of evidence.

    1.4.2. Pro:
    Mandatory minimum sentencing [9] requirements for drug offenses infringe on a citizen's Fifth Amendment rights.

    1.4.3. Con:
    In many instances, public safety takes precedence over individual rights.

      1.4.3.1. Pro:
      The primary responsibility of any government is to ensure the safety and security of its citizens. If public safety is compromised, the very fabric of society can be threatened.

      1.4.3.2. Pro:
      Governments aim to maintain public order. If individual actions threaten to disrupt this order, then restrictions might be necessary.

      1.4.3.3. Con:
      In most cases, public safety can be balanced with individual rights.

  1.5. Pro:
  Terrorism (used as "the excuse") laws, have removed all of our constitutional rights.

    1.5.1. Pro:
    The USA PATRIOT Act [10] grants law enforcement expanded powers that some argue infringe upon Fourth Amendment rights against unwarranted searches and seizures.

      1.5.1.1. Con:
      In 2015, the USA FREEDOM Act [11] succeeded the PATRIOT Act, reducing the government's ability to gather information from its citizens.

    1.5.2. Con:
    Individual constitutional rights can be suspended in the interest of national security (pp. 81–82 [12]).

  1.6. Con:
  The Constitutional supremacy of the US Constitution [13] prohibits any law from superseding its authority.

    1.6.1. Pro:
    The US Constitution explicitly states [14] that the document itself is the supreme law of the land, and no law can contradict it.

      1.6.1.1. Con:
      The US Constitution can be amended [15].

      1.6.1.2. Pro:
      Throughout history, the US Supreme Court has struck down laws [16] that attempt to violate the Constitution.

    1.6.2. Con:
    The U.S. Supreme Court interprets the Constitution and determines whether a law indeed violates the Constitution, which does not always result in consistent outcomes.

      1.6.2.1. Con:
      The U.S. Supreme Court is still bound by historical precedent [17] and the exact words of the Constitution.

      1.6.2.2. Pro:
      The decisions of the U.S. Supreme Court often align along partisan lines [18].

  1.7. Con:
  Viewed as a living document [19], the constitution can retain its authority while evolving with the law.

    1.7.1. Pro:
    The founding fathers envisioned the constitution being regularly renewed. That is, for it to evolve.

      1.7.1.1. Con:
      If the intention were for the Constitution to undergo significant evolution, it would not have been so difficult to amend [20] the Constitution.

      1.7.1.2. Pro:
      Thomas Jefferson intended the constitution to naturally expire after 19 years [21] because one generation has no right to bind another.

        1.7.1.2.1. Con:
        If Jefferson intended the constitution to expire every 19 years, he would have stipulated this in the constitution itself.

      1.7.1.3. Pro:
      The constitution was one of the greatest documents ever seen but technology has overridden much of it, thus calling for new laws to protect it's intended purpose.

    1.7.2. Con:
    As with other laws, the constitution should be interpreted in the way intended by its authors. This means it should not evolve.

      1.7.2.1. Pro:
      There is a large number of judges and legal experts who actively identify themselves as originalists [22], advocating that the Constitution must be interpreted literally in line with the text.

      1.7.2.2. Con:
      An originalist interpretation of the Constitution can lead to unfavorable outcomes [23].

  1.8. Pro:
  Free speech rights have been eroded as organizations have special laws to stop it.

    1.8.1. Pro:
    The First Amendment [24], which guarantees free expression in the U.S., has a number of exceptions.

      1.8.1.1. Pro:
      Speech that incites hate against others is prohibited under U.S. law.

        1.8.1.1.1. Pro:
        Restrictions on hate speech are particularly controversial because it is difficult to define [25] precisely what constitutes hate speech.

        1.8.1.1.2. Con:
        The censorship of some speech is necessary because it can violate the freedom of others.

      1.8.1.2. Con:
      It is impractical to suggest that a law should have no limitations.

        1.8.1.2.1. Pro:
        The revelation of state secrets can harm society as a whole; therefore, restrictions should be placed on people's free expression of such knowledge they may possess.

        1.8.1.2.2. Con:
        Specific limitations on free expression can create avenues for state censorship. Therefore, it is better to avoid venturing down such a path.

    1.8.2. Con:
    The U.S. still ranks among the top 30 countries [26] in the world for freedom of speech.

      1.8.2.1. Con:
      The U.S. score of 7.4 [27] suggests that there is ample room for improvement in the area of freedom of expression.

      1.8.2.2. Pro:
      Freedom of speech is a fundamental principle [28] in the U.S., protected by the First Amendment to the U.S. Constitution. This cements the U.S.'s position among countries with a high degree of freedom of speech.
