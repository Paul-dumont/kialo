Discussion Name:
Are Ghosts Real?

1. Thesis:
Ghosts aren't real.

  1.1. Pro:
  There is no scientific proof that ghosts are real.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Only objects that are objectively verifiable [1] by the scientific method should be considered as real. Ghosts are not verifiable in such a manner and are therefore unreal.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      Not all objects are verifiable using the scientific method. Abstract objects such as art, money, or love cannot be verified in a scientific setting but we would still consider them as real. Ghosts are abstract objects in the same way.

        1.1.1.1.1. Con:
        Abstract objects like money are real because they are experienced by multiple people together and at different points in time. Ghosts, however, are usually experienced by one person and in specific situations. Therefore, it is difficult to objectively assess its reality.

      1.1.1.2. Pro:
      Repeated studies and experiments have failed to produce reliable scientific evidence [2] that proves the existence of ghosts.

        1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
        Parapsychology [3], which studies such apparitional phenomena, is considered a psuedoscience by most scientists.

          1.1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
          The history of parapsychology [4] is filled with instances of fraud where evidence was tampered with to produce the desired result.

          1.1.1.2.1.2. Con:
          Parapsychology has produced a rich body of theoretical knowledge that explains the existence of ghosts through psychological theories of perception [5].

            1.1.1.2.1.2.1. Con:
            Psychological theories explaining experiences of ghosts don't entail that they are real. They can, on the contrary, suggest that these experiences are hallucinatory.

    1.1.2. Con:
    There is no scientific proof that ghosts aren't real.

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      If something hasn't been found yet, it does not mean, that it doesn't exist.

        1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
        It is not possible to prove something doesn't exist.

          1.1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
          In order for a theory to be scientific [6], it needs to be falsifiable. It is impossible to falsify the non-existence of ghosts and therefore belief in ghosts is unscientific.

        1.1.2.1.2. Con:
        There is some scientific proof to suggest that the soul does not exist [7]. This applies to ghosts as well because they are both conceived as sentient entities without physical bodies.

          1.1.2.1.2.1. Con:
          The scientific method [8] cannot answer questions about souls, ghosts, and other spiritual entities. Therefore, scientific evidence concerning these entities should not have a bearing upon whether ghosts are real or not.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      There has yet to be an agreed [9] upon definition for ghosts, therefore without this agreement, they cannot yet agree upon what is/is not proof.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        In most instances, it seems that people use the word ghost to refer to the spirit of a dead person. This could be used as a basic definition of a ghost.

        1.1.2.3.1. Pro:

          1.1.2.3.1.1. Con:
          Other researchers have found fault in this study, postulating that the participants were responding to the suggestion they would feel a ghostly presence [12] rather than any actual effect of the electromagnetic waves.

        1.1.2.3.2. Pro:
        Scientists have found that some areas that have high reportings of paranormal activity often feature unusual magnetic fields [13].

      1.1.2.4. Con:
      Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence [14] to support them. The existence of ghosts is an extraordinary claim that departs from all scientific understanding and therefore the onus of evidence is on the ones making such a claim.

      1.1.2.5. Pro:
      Experiencing the sensation of ghosts could be a psychological issue.

        1.1.2.5.1. Con:
        Observed reality could very well be made up for anyone. Your brain hallucinates your conscious reality [15]

          1.1.2.5.1.1. Pro:

          1.1.2.5.1.2. Con:
          If hallucination is what produces most of our conscious experience, such experiences should not be considered as real even if they are common place. Only objective entities should be considered real.

        Ghosts are a manifestation of phantom senses [17].

          1.1.2.5.2.1. Pro:
          Eye disease [18] is one cause of visible 'hallucinations'.

          1.1.2.5.2.2. Con:
          Phantom senses [19] produce an experience of objects and limbs which do not actually exist. If the experience of ghosts is a manifestation of phantom senses, then it entails that they are not real.

        Some people who have experiences of ghosts cease to have them after they undergo counselling or therapy [20]. This suggests that the experiences could be psychological.

          Scientists have found [25] paranormal experience has nothing to do with ghosts, but one's sleep pattern.

                1.1.2.5.5.1.1.1.1. Pro:

                  1.1.2.5.5.1.1.1.2.2. Pro:

                    1.1.2.5.5.1.1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
                    The human mind often tries torationalize incorrect neuron responses [32] to make sense of what a person is experiencing.

              1.1.2.5.5.1.1.3. Pro:

              1.1.2.5.5.1.1.4. Pro:

            1.1.2.5.5.1.2. Pro:

              1.1.2.5.5.1.2.1. Pro:

          1.1.2.5.5.2. Pro:

            1.1.2.5.5.2.1. Pro:

          1.1.2.5.5.3. Con:
          Even if mental illnesses cause experiences that other people cannot validate or experience, these experiences are real [41] to those experiencing them.

          1.1.2.5.5.4. Pro:
          Seeing ghosts or shadows can be a sign of psychosis [42].

            1.1.2.5.5.4.1. Pro:
            Visions of ghosts are related to cognitive processes [43] involving fantasy and schizotypy.

            1.1.2.5.5.4.2. Pro:
            Scientists have postied a link between bad air quality and ghost sightings [44]. This arguably is because some fungi that grow under conditions of bad air quality cause psychosis in humans.

              1.1.2.5.5.4.2.1. Pro:

        1.1.2.6.1. Pro:

      There are scientific proofs [48] that show that ghosts aren't real and that experiences of ghosts are caused by physical phenomena.

        When enough people believe [129] in an idea and interweave some of their actions and relationships around it, it shapes their social reality.

          1.2.9.1.2.1. Pro:
          More than half the population of the US and UK believe in ghosts [130].

          1.2.9.1.2.2. Pro:
          Even if it is not true that ghosts exist, within the social universe of beliefs, they can be considered as real [131] because they guide people's actions and contribute to their sense of identity.

      1.2.9.2. Con:
      Within a cultural universe, there are sets of beliefs that together form a coherent reality [132] to those who identify with that culture.

      1.2.9.3. Con:
      Widespread common beliefs indicates a common truth.

        1.2.9.3.1. Con:
        Just because something is believed by a lot of people doesn't make it true. This is an ad populum fallacy.

        1.2.9.3.2. Con:
        The truth of claims concerning ghosts does not entail that ghosts are real. Truth [133] is a property of linguistic assertions whereas reality is about the existence of an object.

          1.2.9.3.2.1. Pro:
          If I said that Batman wears a cape, it would be true. But it would not demonstrate whether Batman really exists.

    1.2.10. Con:
    There is no mainstream religion that says ghosts are real.

      1.2.10.1. Con:
      The world doesn't revolve around mainstream religion.

        1.2.10.1.1. Con:
        The nature of one's belief in ghosts seems to depend on their religious beliefs [134]. This means that religion does play a role in whether or not people consider ghosts to be real.

          1.2.10.1.1.1. Pro:
          A study [135] found that people identifying as religious believers are twice more likely to believe in ghosts than people who are not religious.

          1.2.10.1.1.2. Pro:
          The content of ghost experiences [136] is impacted by religious traditions and symbolism. They also determine which ghosts are unwelcome and which ones are benign.

        1.2.10.1.2. Pro:
        Not all phenomena are acknowledged or embraced by mainstream religions.

      1.2.10.2. Con:
      Religion is not the authority on the existence of ghosts, or any other entity. It is a belief system that does not rely on provable information.

      1.2.10.3. Pro:
      In Christianity [137], ghosts are not acknowledged.

        1.2.10.3.1. Con:
        Jesus Christ ascended to heaven, it would be naive to say we grasp the detail in this process and there is no opportunity for a "ghost" to appear during it.

        1.2.10.3.2. Pro:
        -> See 1.2.8.1.

        1.2.10.3.3. Con:
        Not all denominations in Christianity denounce belief in ghosts. Many traditions [138] exist in Christianity which indicate a belief in ghosts or ghost-like entities.

        1.2.10.3.4. Con:
        One of the three members of the trinity is the "Holy Spirit," who is also called the "Holy Ghost." At least lexically, Christianity believes in the existence of at least one ghost.

          1.2.10.3.4.1. Con:
          A ghost is defined as the soul of a deceased person [139]. This is incompatible with the biblical conception of the Holy Ghost, as an eternal member of the triune godhead.

      1.2.10.4. Pro:
      The belief in ghosts is believed to predate Islam [140]. They are considered a superstition.

      1.2.10.5. Con:
      There are ghosts in Buddhism [141].

    1.2.11. Con:
    Many cultural references to ghosts found in movies, TV shows, video games, and books with ghosts in them are classified as fantasy, not as realistic fiction.

      1.2.11.1. Pro:
      The 1984 movie Ghostbusters [142] is considered a supernatural comedy, not based in reality.

      1.2.11.2. Con:
      The classification of something that is meant for entertainment purposes does not invalidate the existence of a phenomenon.

  1.3. Con:
  An individual or group’s experience with a ghost is real to them and their perception of reality.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    When we are dreaming, we perceptually experience the contents of the dream as real even if they are not physically occuring. Our experience with ghosts is similar and therefore should be considered real.

    1.3.2. Con:
    -> See 1.1.1.

    1.3.3. Pro:
    If more than one person has experienced a ghost, they can compare the characteristics they perceived and validate each other's experience as a real phenomenon with consistent characteristics.

      1.3.3.1. Pro:
      One in five people claims to have seen or experienced a ghost encounter [143].

        1.3.3.1.1. Con:
        It is possible for many people to undergo an illusion. A higher number of people experiencing ghosts does not demonstrate it's reality.

      1.3.3.2. Con:
      -> See 1.1.4.2.1.4.1.

    1.3.4. Con:
    Accepting that ghosts are real based on the beliefs of one group of people would negate the beliefs of their ideological counterparts, who believe that ghosts do not exist.

  1.4. Pro:
  Ghosts are mental projections [144] and have no objective basis in reality.

    1.4.1. Pro:
    Communication with ghosts [145] has often involved the use of psychoactive substances. This suggests that experiencing ghosts is a mental phenomenon.

      1.4.1.1. Pro:
      There have been instances where chemical compounds such as carbon monoxide [146] have induced ghost experiences.

      1.4.1.2. Con:
      Experiences induced by psychoactive substances are not necassarily unreal. Many people consume coffee [147], which is a psychoactive substance. However, we would not question their experential claims only because of that.

      1.4.1.3. Pro:
      Common over-the-counter prescription [148] medicines have also, in some instances, produced hallucinations with ghosts.

    1.4.2. Pro:
    In many instances where people believed a particular house to be haunted, it was shown that this belief came about as a result of confirmation bias [149] towards random occurences.

      1.4.2.1. Pro:
      Experiences of ghosts occur in environments [150] where the human mind is vulnerable to making explanations that make sense of the data perceived.

        1.4.2.1.1. Pro:
        Our brain is evolutionarily wired [151] to seek causal connections where we are unable to see causes behind seemingly unexplainable phenomenon. This makes us vulnerable to positing spirits and ghosts to explain certain kinds of natural phenomena.

          1.4.2.1.1.1. Pro:
          We also tend to see human faces in random images [152], because our brain is wired to see patterns where they aren't. Experiencing ghosts is an extension of the same evolutionary tendency.

    1.4.3. Con:
    Not all mental projections are unreal. Before it is written, a poem or a story is a mental projection but this does not mean that it is unreal. Ghosts can similarly be mental projections and still real.

    1.4.4. Pro:
    It has been shown that one's cultural environment [153] plays a significant role in determining the content of ghost experiences. This entails that these experiences are not objective and are a result of our subjective processes.

      1.4.4.1. Pro:
      Different cultures assign different specific characteristics [154] to the idea of ghosts.

    1.4.5. Con:
    Ghosts are mental responses to physical phenomena [155], and therefore do have an objective basis in reality.

    1.4.6. Con:
    There have been claims that exorcism [156] rituals have cured people of possessions. If ghosts were merely mental projections, such claims would not have been made.

      1.4.6.1. Con:
      Exorcism can take the role of psychotherapy [157] that addresses underlying issues that are psychological in nature but express as a possession.

        1.4.6.1.1. Pro:
        People requesting exorcism are frequently seen to be mentally disturbed [158].

      1.4.6.2. Con:
      Exorcism [159] sometimes appears to work because of the placebo effect and effects of powerful suggestions.

      1.4.6.3. Pro:
      There has been a sharp rise in the number of people requesting exorcism [160] from their church.

      1.4.6.4. Con:
      It has sometimes been seen that people supposedly possessed are actually suffering from narcissistic personality disorders [161] and engage in such behavior for attention.

      1.4.6.5. Con:
      Exorcisms are used against demonic entities [162] not ghosts.

        1.4.6.5.1. Pro:
        Ghosts are considered the spirits [163] of dead people, while demons are evil [164] spirits.

        1.4.6.5.2. Con:
        In some traditions, the category of demonic entities [165] includes dead souls who are evil.

    1.4.7. Con:
    Experiences of ghosts often involve physical phenomena, suggesting that ghosts cannot be purely mental phenomena.

      1.4.7.1. Pro:
      Anecdotal accounts of experiences with ghosts include them being able to cause physical disturbances [166]. It is not possible to achieve this with a mere mental projection. Ghosts, therefore, are more than mental projections.

        1.4.7.1.1. Pro:
        There are many reported cases of poltergeist [167] activity.

          1.4.7.1.1.1. Con:
          Many experiences claimed to be instances of poltergeist [168] activity have been shown to be hoaxes.

        1.4.7.1.2. Con:
        Most experiences of ghosts do not seem to trigger consequences. Physical impressions [169] normally expected out of a real entity, such as leaving footprints, are largely absent in experiences of ghosts.

          1.4.7.1.2.1. Con:
          Accounts of ghosts suggest that they are spiritual entities without physical extensions. However, they can still choose to influence physical objects [170] upon will.

      1.4.7.2. Pro:
      Some people have claimed to capture ghosts on photographs [171].

        1.4.7.2.1. Con:
        Many photographs of ghosts or spirits were found to be fraudulent [172].

          1.4.7.2.1.1. Con:
          There are some ghost photos, such as Sybell Corbet's photo of the Combermere Abbey Library in 1891 that are too old [173] to have been physically or digitally manipulated.

          1.4.7.2.1.2. Pro:
          False "ghost photography" [174] has a long history and has been used by psychics, mediums, and rabble-rousers since the early 1800s.

            1.4.7.2.1.2.1. Pro:
            New visual effects coming from stereoscopic images and double exposures [175] allowed photographers to manipulate cameras to make it appear as if photos have "ghosts" in them.

        1.4.7.2.2. Con:
        Orbs [176], which are sometimes seen as visual photographic evidence of spirit presence, are shown to be common artifacts that occur because flash photography interacts with dust particles in the air.

        1.4.7.2.3. Pro:
        Plenty of paranormal entities have been caught in photographs and in videos [177] alike. Much of this ghostly photo evidence can be found all over the internet and also in books and other literature.

      1.4.7.3. Con:
      Hallucinations [178] can involve experiencing physical sensations that seem real to the person experiencing them.

        1.4.7.3.1. Con:
        Displacing objects, opening and closing doors, drawers, and cabinets, and knocking on doors and walls are impossible to explain as physical sensations being hallucinated.

    1.4.8. Pro:
    Some people have a tendency to enjoy being afraid [179], making it likely that they project experiences of ghosts to experience fear.

      1.4.8.1. Con:
      Not everyone enjoys being afraid [180]. It is unlikely that only people who enjoy being afraid are the ones that experience ghosts.

      1.4.8.2. Pro:
      Fear triggers emotional and hormonal responses [181] that engage the human brain in a way that some people enjoy.

  1.5. Con:
  There are sometimes strange phenomena that can't be explained in other ways; ghosts are one of them.

    1.5.1. Con:
    Attributing a strange phenomenon to being a ghost simply because we cannot explain it any other way does not mean it is absolutely a ghost. It means we are inconclusive in our findings and attaching a label, for lack of a better explanation.

      1.5.1.1. Pro:
      'There are many unexplained phenomena at one time. As technology progresses, the logical reasons for these phenomena become clear. The same may be true for what is perceived as ghosts.

      1.5.1.2. Pro:
      This is a logical fallacy called 'an argument from ignorance'. Lack of explanation does not warrant a claim for any explanation.

    1.5.2. Con:
    The existence of ghosts would contradict many things we have good reason to believe.

      1.5.2.1. Pro:
      The existence of ghosts requires that a mind does not need a brain; this contradicts our understanding of brains.

        1.5.2.1.1. Con:
        Consciousness is what observes the existence; this should not be limited to a brain simply because that is, thus far, all our research has been able to see.

        1.5.2.1.2. Con:
        There is anyway no strong argument that establishes how we are able to have mental experiences through a physical brain. The existence of ghosts will only be an another example of this philosophical problem [182].

      1.5.2.2. Pro:
      The existence of ghosts suggests energy with no material source, in contradiction to the first law of thermodynamics.

        1.5.2.2.1. Con:
        The law states energy can not be created or destroyed, only transferred. Ghosts may be a portion of that energy transfer into a system we are currently unable to measure.Law of conservation of energy [183]

      1.5.2.3. Con:
      There are still many things for which we do not understand.

    1.5.3. Pro:
    Ghost is a legitimate label used to categorize certain experiences that are unexplainable through scientific ways.

      1.5.3.1. Con:
      Labels are not real. If ghosts are merely linguistic tools to refer to experiences, this does not entail that they are real.

        1.5.3.1.1. Con:
        Some abstract labels are real objects. Money is a label that is used to refer to paper and metal which are used as means of exchange. This does not mean that money is not real.

          1.5.3.1.1.1. Con:
          Abstract objects [184] should not be considered as real.

      1.5.3.2. Pro:
      UFOs [185] are a label used to identify objects that are not identifiable using existing categories or data. Ghosts are similarly a label to identify certain kinds of experiences and purported entities.

        1.5.3.2.1. Con:
        The term "ghost" is not as much of a catch-all term for unexplained phenomena as the term UFO is.

      1.5.3.3. Con:
      They are only unexplained until they are explained and not unexplainable scientifically, which is also thought [186] to be the case for UFOs [187].

    1.5.4. Pro:
    -> See 1.4.7.1.1.

    1.5.5. Con:
    Most phenomena that people consider to be experiences of ghosts can be successfully explained through scientific theories [188].

      1.5.5.1. Pro:
      Many experiences of ghosts can be explained through exposure to sound frequencies [189] which are below the human range of hearing.
