Discussion Name:
Is Chess 960 superior to regular chess?

1. Thesis:
Chess 960 is superior to regular chess.

  1.1. Pro:
  Chess 960 allows for more creative play.

    1.1.1. Con:
    Creativity is not exclusive to Chess 960. Regular chess allows [1] for creative moves as well.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    There are more moves available, allowing for more creative combinations.

  1.2. Con:
  It is not suited to beginners, so might seem unwelcoming to budding chess players.

    1.2.1. Con:
    Opening tricks in traditional chess can also be unwelcoming to new players.

      1.2.1.1. Con:
      New players can quickly learn opening tricks as they continue to play. In Chess 960, they are still left in the dark about possible plays.

    1.2.2. Pro:
    Chess 960 is more complex in strategy than regular chess. This may be discouraging to beginners.

    1.2.3. Con:
    The rules for chess 960 are easy [2].

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      The application of the rules are the challenge.

    1.2.4. Con:
    This depends heavily on the beginner's game preference.

      1.2.4.1. Pro:
      Some beginners may enjoy a challenge.

  1.3. Con:
  Chess 960 makes it challenging for the player to improve on their mistakes.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    The chances of a player being presented with the same moves again are low.

      1.3.1.1. Con:
      Players would still be able to see occasional similar positions.

    1.3.2. Pro:
    There isn't a way for players to practice Chess960, while regular chess is predicated on practice.

      1.3.2.1. Con:
      Chess puzzles would still be a useful way to practice seeing certain patterns.

    1.3.3. Con:
    Chess 960 requires a skill set and focus that makes mistakes obsolete. Thus, it is superior.

  1.4. Pro:
  Chess 960 allows for a more intellectually engaging game.

    1.4.1. Pro:
    The middle part of the game is reported to be unfamiliar [3] in terms of available moves. This allows the player to rely more on intellect than memory.

    1.4.2. Pro:
    Regular chess moves can be memorized. For more experienced players, this can become boring.

      1.4.2.1. Pro:
      Modern regular chess relies [4] too heavily on computer generation. Players can after awhile recognize these moves.

    1.4.3. Con:
    The appeal of an intellectually engaging game is subject to personal opinion and therefore is not a static reason for superiority.

      1.4.3.1. Pro:
      Some players may enjoy the familiarity regular chess provides.

      1.4.3.2. Pro:
      Some people may prefer a more relaxed playing experience.

      1.4.3.3. Pro:
      What is intellectually engaging for one, may be considered confusing for another.

  1.5. Pro:
  Playing Chess 960 may improve a player's regular chess skills.

    1.5.1. Pro:
    Players will have a more randomized skill set making it easier to spot intricate and maneuver through intricate moves.

    1.5.2. Con:
    This implies that Chess 960 is good for practice, not necessarily that the game itself is superior in nature to regular chess.

    1.5.3. Con:
    Playing regular chess consistently can improve a player's skill. Thus Chess 960 isn't superior, but equal in this regard.

    1.5.4. Con:
    Each Chess 960 game is randomized; concepts such as openings (first few moves of chess) don't exist, which are crucial in regular chess.

  1.6. Con:
  Some people watch regular chess to learn openings.

    1.6.1. Con:
    Chess 960 can be used to help learn opening theory.

  1.7. Pro:
  Chess 960 allows for new positions very early in the game, which is more interesting than previously played positions.

    1.7.1. Con:
    Positions are imbalanced in Chess 960 [5]; some positions give the white side an advantage compared to classical chess.

  1.8. Con:
  Regular chess may improve one's memory.

    1.8.1. Pro:
    With the variations of moves available, coupled with practice, regular chess offers its player chances to memorize key plays.

    1.8.2. Pro:
    Playing chess can protect against dementia [6] in senior players.

  1.9. Con:
  It is much harder to prepare for randomized chess games.

    1.9.1. Con:
    This challenge is what makes it superior to regular chess; it takes more preparation.
