Discussion Name:
Does Islam Need A Reform To Be Compatible With The Modern World?

1. Thesis:
Islam needs a reform to be compatible with the modern world.

  1.1. Con:
  The thesis is too broad. There are already many liberal strains within Islam that already are compatible with the modern world.

    1.1.1. Con:
    This proves the need for additional reformation. If some strains are compatible, that implies others are not.

    1.1.2. Con:
    Some central tenets of Islam make it hard for this religion to be compatible with the modern world in fields such as gender equality, religious tolerance, and science-based debate.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      Islam teaches that men should have power over women.

        1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
        But not to use that power in an evil way.

        1.1.2.1.2. Con:
        This is true of most major religions.

          1.1.2.1.2.1. Con:
          This is true, however, the assertion is not within the scope of the central question.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      Muslim principles of lending do emphasize community over capital. In times of raising income inequality this is very much needed.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        The previous claim is that "some" tenets are incompatible, not all.

        1.1.2.2.2. Con:
        There are many other philosophies which encourage this which aren't Muslim and which are more modern.

      1.1.2.3. Pro:
      If we look at how science has evolved in this world, we see that up until the sacking of Baghdad in 1258 almost all of the scientific breakthroughs were from the Islamic parts of the world. There is a reason that most stars have Arabic names. Now [1], however, the Islamic faith says that manipulation of numbers is a sin. Out of 1 billion Muslims, only 3 have won a Nobel Prize [2] in science.

      1.1.2.4. Pro:
      Islam is very far behind. Part of this is due to Wahhabism, sponsored by Saudi [3]. The progress made in the latter 1800s by the Ottomans is gone. Beheadings, stonings, the list goes on. If majority were against it, they would boycott Mecca. They don't. Human Rights simply not something the Muftis support. The laypeople are perhaps waking up, but slowly. They have been taught to listen to their local Shaykh [4]. Lack of authority structure makes Reformation impossible. Must be from the layfolk.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    There are many Muslims [5] who are creating bodies of work supporting progressive and liberal Islam.

    1.1.4. Con:
    Although such people and movements exist, they are under threat [6] and are met with widespread disapproval by many Muslim communities.

  1.2. Pro:
  -> See 1.1.2.

  1.3. Con:
  Islam is a religion. Religion needs human minds to exist. Changing a religion's text or structure does not change its exegesis. Hence, Islam is as modern as the people practicing it and their epistemologies.

    1.3.1. Con:
    There's more than one text [7]. Quran in itself wouldn't be a problem once put into a historical perspective. There are other texts that interpret the Quran and claim to know details about prophet Muhammad life. These text don't appear to be historically sound, but are commonly accepted and these are the texts that promote violence and mistreatment of other people based on their gender or religion.

    1.3.2. Pro:
    The belief structure behind it is more important than the specifics of religious doctrine; Islam can therefore be left alone to develop as it needs.

    1.3.3. Con:
    Reform isn’t only about changing texts, but also to change how to deal with text and how to interpret it.

  1.4. Pro:
  Without reform, the demographic trends in the West will generate more and more conflicts between established politics and values and the religious orthodoxy.

  1.5. Pro:
  Over time any idea or philosophy naturally becomes less relevant and fitting the objective reality

    1.5.1. Pro:
    Any 1400 year-old idea, methodology or concept has to be reformed to be compatible with modern world, you can’t use 1400 years old teachings in the modern world.

      1.5.1.1. Con:
      The very fact that unreformed Islam is not only being practiced, but also thriving [8] in the current times should be evidence enough to dispute this.

  1.6. Pro:
  Islam has internal conflicts (mainly sunni vs shia) due to divergent interpretations of the main tenets and traditions. Reform could help with solving it by providing a new unified interpretation

  1.7. Con:
  Every religion needs a reform to be compatible with the modern world. We need to simply detach politics/life with religion and ideology. They can co-exist but one should not influence the other.

    1.7.1. Con:
    No, as many political Islamists inform Muslims it is modern, it is the only path to follow. There can be no escape. Changing the texts of Islam is not to be. A new Furqan is needed; as for Christians there was a New Testament. Who could write a new message, having less hatred, greater tolerance, even no apostasy laws, and be charismatic enough to promote and survive? Yes Islam needs reform, but there is no way it can be reformed.

    1.7.2. Pro:
    To separate Mosque and State is necessary for reform, leading to a secular government. Laws cannot be Sharia or Islam, but must be common law. No Imam or cleric of Islam will subscribe to Islam be less than superior.

    1.7.3. Con:
    Religion is based on the tenet that morality is absolute and is derived divinely. Secularists believe (generally) that morality is relative and is derived through thoughtful contemplation and constant review of ideas. This is particularly true of the Abrahamic religions. If they are wholly separate, this generally leads to the subservience of religion to the secular and can ultimately lead to the state attempting to abolish it, as Russia did in support of atheism.

    1.7.4. Con:
    Yes, however, such reform may be impossible. The Scripture and tenets of Islam are clear and as an adherent delves more deeply, studies and learns, becoming convicted is to be a jihadist, as a minimum a political Islamist. Superiority over all non-believers, even heretics is key. The objective of a world All for Allah is clear. Religious purity, no separation of mosque and state, is mandated by Muhammad. Muslims proposing reform live in free nations. Elsewhere they would be punished.

  1.8. Pro:
  All religions need constant reform to be compatible with the ever-changing moral value structure of the modern world. Islam is no exception to this rule.

    1.8.1. Pro:
    All religions need to change, paradoxically, because they all claim to be the unchanging truth. However, the actual unchanging truth is that most of the world, itself, always changes and we must change along with it if we are to survive. All religions try to evade the Evolutionary Principle [9] of Adapt or Die [10] and, so, ultimately can end up either rather like the Dodo [11] or the Inca [12].

    1.8.2. Pro:
    Christianity [13] is acknowledging a need to adapt; Islam ought to do the same.

    1.8.3. Con:
    Just because the populace and youth do not desire to follow the rules of the religion does not mean the rules of the religion should be changed. The word of God does not have a time limit, per say. There are 'soft' things that can be interpreted and considered, but one cannot expect a religion to change very much at a fundamental level.

      1.8.3.1. Con:
      The Pope acknowledged [14] the creation myth as allegory of a different era. Going from everything appeared by the will of God over 6 days to understanding that things developed over billions of years to become what they are now is a pretty fundamental change. Religion does and should change with culture.

      1.8.3.2. Pro:
      The Qur'an says the heavens and the Earth were created in 6 "Ayyam". A better English translation would be 6 epochs or 6 time periods. After-all, there was no Earth, so how could there be a "day"? The Qur'an doesn't say how long this time period was but science teaches us that it was billions of years. Hence, the two statements are compatible.

      1.8.3.3. Con:
      All religions already adapted to the changing world multiple times. Interpretations of the God's words have changed based on sociocultural context.

        1.8.3.3.1. Pro:
        Many religious doctrines changed their teachings about slavery (e.g. case of Christian Abolitionism [15]) after certain social reforms.

        1.8.3.3.2. Pro:
        Many new religions emerged from different interpretations of the same holy books (Rise of Christianity, Islam, Evangelism).

  1.9. Con:
  It is the modern world that should be made compatible with Islam.

    1.9.1. Pro:
    Islam has solutions to many contemporary societal problems.

      1.9.1.1. Con:
      The average GDP of contemporary non-muslim countries is much higher that muslim ones

    1.9.2. Pro:
    The fact that Islam is unchanging, as the Qu'ran is protected by God, provides a vitally stable and consistent set of morals with which to navigate the modern, ever-changing world. This is beneficial to humanity.

    1.9.3. Pro:
    Islam cannot be upgraded because the divine book (Quran) says it is already perfect.

      1.9.3.1. Con:
      Each religion's followeres necessarily likely believe that their faith and its holy teachings are perfect, but this does not equate to objective truth.

      1.9.3.2. Con:
      Like all holy books, much of the Quran is open to interpretation. So there is room for some reform.

      1.9.3.3. Pro:
      In order for this to work, Muslims would have to accept that there is only one perfect Quran and it exists in heaven alone. Any version that has been passed down through humans, should be considered imperfect and therefore subject to critique and questioning.

      1.9.3.4. Con:
      Being believed by Muslims to be perfect doesn’t make it compatible with modern world.

      1.9.3.5. Con:
      Quran, as any other sacred religious text has no supernatural origin.

    1.9.4. Con:
    The world shouldn't change itself to one religion. Some religions are incompatible. And saying the society should change to accept all the spectrum of interpretations of one and only one religion isn't only not inclusive at all, but also only based on the subjective argument that this one belief is better than all the others.

  1.10. Pro:
  Without adapting to modern realities, Islam will eventually lose followers and become irrelevant.

    1.10.1. Con:
    Iran, Turkey, Egypt, Lebanon and many more have capital cities which were much more cosmopolitan [16] and liberal in values a few decades ago. The evidence suggests that in terms of population and strength of adherence Islam is thriving [17] with this anti-modern direction.

  1.11. Con:
  There is an ontological difference between Islam and modernity. Islam embraces the very idea of submission to God (Allah); whereas modernity adheres the philosophy of anthropocentrism that is 'man is the centre of the universe' this means 'man' will determine the course of action of this planet or beyond with no divine intervention. Therefore there is a thick line between two ideologies and hence Islam cannot be compatible with the modern world.

    1.11.1. Con:
    However no divine element created Islam, it was a mortal. Muhammad is not god, but interpreted as a human Allah's words, from an intermediary no less. The only religion having a divine party presence is Christianity. It is possible Muhammad was interpreting for himself, modernizing at the time, his understating of a monotheistic belief in God. He did not live a sin free life, nor a life that followed the essentials of the way of life, ideology, he imposed on others.

    1.11.2. Con:
    There is no such homogenous thing as "modernity".

  1.12. Pro:
  Surely any religion that has caused a degree of violence [18] against those who don't practise it, even if this is a tiny minority of its followers, must be reformed in order to be compatible with a world in which tolerance is promoted to such an extent.
