Discussion Name:
Is Donation Charity or a Moral Obligation?

1. Thesis:
People From Affluent Countries Have a Moral Obligation in Helping the Needy.

  1.1. Con:
  Providing donations as one who lives in said affluent money, however is not as wealthy as the higher percentages could harm oneself. One who is not making an abundance of money would need to keep money for themself in order to live and succeed.

  1.2. Pro:
  Human nature gives those who are rich the innate desire to help others. The problem lies in whether or not they choose to act on this human nature.

  1.3. Pro:
  Yes but there should be national programs. Also, there is some that have not the obligation to do.

  1.4. Con:
  Nadie tiene la obligación de darte dinero

  1.5. Con:
  Until there is effective population control, relieving famine merely postpones starvation.

    1.5.1. Con:
    Postponing starvation can help millions of people [1] survive.

    1.5.2. Con:
    Food assistance is just one aspect of aid; other crucial areas such as healthcare, education, and materials for security and safety also require attention and resources.

    1.5.3. Con:
    People could help with factors related to effective population control such as improving education and contraception [2] availability [3] while reducing the largely economics-based reasons why [4] people in third-world want to have many children.

  1.6. Con:
  No, las personas se ganan lo que tienen mediante su esfuerzo y sudor y tener que hacerlo por obligación seria invalidar y hacer de cuenta que lo que pasaron para lograrlo ahora no es propiedad privada si no que se debe repartir a los mas pobres, si pueden y quieren claro que muy bueno ayudar a los necesitado, pero mejor seria que les dieran trabajo intentando crear una estructura para que ellos mismo construyan y creen su riqueza.

  1.7. Pro:
  Poverty levels can decrease overtime

  1.8. Con:
  While I could argue that people have a moral obligation to be kind and helpful, how they choose to do that should be up to the individual.

  1.9. Con:
  Wealthy individuals have the right to use their wealth as they wish, including the freedom to choose whether and how to help the needy.

    1.9.1. Pro:
    Imposing a moral obligation to help the needy may undermine the notion of personal responsibility, discouraging individuals from striving for success and independence.

    1.9.2. Pro:
    People should have the judgment when to donate and when not to.

    1.9.3. Con:
    Asserting a moral duty to practice charity and criticizing those who don't does not impose limitations on individuals' freedom to decide how to utilize their wealth.

    1.9.4. Con:
    This claim is offtopic as it does not address the proposition that it would be their moral obligation which is different from a mandatory practice and either based on voluntary action or about an ethical question of principle.

    1.9.5. Pro:
    Overseas aid should be a government responsibility, and that therefore one ought not to give to privately run charities

      1.9.5.1. Con:
      Government is an inefficient distributor of wealth, as centralized control often leads to inefficiency and corruption.

        1.9.5.1.1. Pro:
        It is preferable to have numerous charities with reliable monitoring and evaluation systems to ensure their effectiveness.

      1.9.5.2. Pro:
      The government bears the responsibility for the remaining aspects of societal provision.

  1.10. Pro:
  Wealthy individuals have a shared responsibility due to the power and influence their wealth confers in society.

    1.10.1. Pro:
    Power, money and influence in society comes from imposing power on less powerful people. [5] Wealthy individuals would participate in a more fair society by donating to charities.

      1.10.1.1. Con:
      Accumulating money and power can be achieved through various means that do not involve imposing power on less powerful individuals.

        1.10.1.1.1. Pro:
        The acquisition of money is clearly a form of power. The utilization of that money to purchase goods and services, invest in companies and perhaps even to donate to charities are common uses of that power that help others rather than imposing on them.

        1.10.1.1.2. Pro:
        Professions such as doctors, builders, inventors, authors, artists, and even celebrities are examples of groups that typically accumulate wealth without relying on the imposition of power.

  1.11. Pro:
  The excessive accumulation of wealth in a world with many people in precarious conditions is morally problematic.

    1.11.1. Pro:
    Encouraging the expression of moral responsibility for charity can foster deeper contemplation on the appropriate use of excess wealth by individuals.

    1.11.2. Con:
    While this is true, a nonstructural patchfix approach that is essentially based on some people temporarily acting morally voluntarily is not necessarily the best solution – for example as it may lead to less sustained improvements that quickly wane once support drops and legitimizes the general excessively unfair structure by which such wealth accumulates this much.

      1.11.2.1. Con:
      This partially depends on the help provided. For example, there can be long-term ground-up support that build up education, media, and sustainable industries in those countries or mentor-type help for various tasks.

    1.11.3. Con:
    If the moral standard of the argument is "precarious" then the proposition could focus on defining, precarious. The moral standard should not be "your degree of envy towards how much others accumulate". The thing is, that people don't want to work anymore. And "precarious" is more often equated to "having to work".

  1.12. Con:
  Wealthy people already pay taxes to the government, fulfilling their moral responsibility, and should not be required to pay more.

    1.12.1. Con:
    No one is fulfilling their moral responsibility by paying tax because it does not have any global impact. Donating to charity is direct and intentional. Tax allocation is not controlled by us. Fulfilling moral responsibility is donating to charities that you KNOW will have a positive impact on people who are less fortunate.

    1.12.2. Pro:
    In a well-planned society, where the needs of both the rich and the poor are accounted for, there is no requirement for extra payments.

    1.12.3. Pro:
    -> See 1.9.5.2.

    1.12.4. Con:
    The ultra-wealthy are very adept at unfairly avoiding paying much taxes [6].

    1.12.5. Con:
    Wealth concentration is enormous, increasing, and a major problem. For example, the richest 1% [7] made twice as much wealth as the rest of the world together in 2020-22 and just 8 people [8] control as much value as the bottom 50%.

    1.12.6. Con:
    This claim makes the false assumption that taxes are the only or the best approach to this issue – better alternatives may include limiting [9] pay and/or wealth in some way to some degree.

    1.12.7. Con:
    As only some wealthy individuals may be required to pay, this could disadvantage them in relation to other asset managers and wealthy people. This could be problematic for several reasons, including strategic issues, fairness, efficacy, and rising influence from less ethical individuals.

  1.13. Pro:
  People from affluent countries have the means to help people in need without sacrificing their means of survival.

    1.13.1. Pro:
    "If it is in our power to prevent something very bad from happening, without thereby sacrificing anything else morally significant, we ought, morally, to do it." rintintin.colorado.edu [10]

  1.14. Con:
  wealty people earned the money they should be ablo to do what they wish with it

    1.14.1. Con:
    Many of them haven't really earned it and e.g. got it from capital returns.

  1.15. Con:
  Imprudent tries to help with donations can cause greater problems than they solve. It can be difficult to spend the money for ends or in channels that are indeed efficacious in being a net positive, especially if the selection, judgement, and so on is done by one or few people or based largely on personal opinion. More systematic approaches that also move away from the appeal to morality approach would be better.

  1.16. Con:
  It is too drastic a revision of our moral scheme

  1.17. Con:
  I prefer moral duty over moral obligation for charity. Obligation implies enforceability, while duty rests on a moral character. Charity should stem from voluntary compassion, not coercion. Due to a shortfall in resources, we must define who is "in need" and who is not. As things stand, the state primarily serves its citizens. Not giving isn't inherently immoral if civic duties are fulfilled.

  1.18. Con:
  Why should one be morally obliged to give out… doesn’t that notion simply erase the act of charity?

    1.18.1. Con:
    Because it would help the needy (more effectively). If it would erase the act of charity, what is missing is why that would be so important.

  1.19. Pro:
  They have but they also have the permission to not do it. The rich, the real rich have not to do that if they don't want. For religious and universe reason.

  -> See 1.9.5.

  1.21. Con:
  Helping the needy often doesn't accomplish much or only short term results that are not durable. It often can also have larger negative impacts such as the needy growing up to be dependent on the help or more violence or environmental destruction than otherwise. So instead of helping the needy, it's often much better to address the causes of them being needy and similar causes.
