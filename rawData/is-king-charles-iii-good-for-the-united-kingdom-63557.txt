Discussion Name:
Is King Charles III good for the United Kingdom?

1. Thesis:
King Charles III is good for the United Kingdom.

  1.1. Con:
  As Britain is a constitutional monarchy [1], the country will remain unaffected regardless of who holds the position of head of state.

    1.1.1. Con:
    The British monarch remains the head of the Church of England [2].

      The British monarch appoints significant Church officials [3], including archbishops, bishops, and deans of the Church of England.

        1.1.1.1.1. Con:
        Church appointments are made by the monarch based on recommendation [4] from the elected prime minister.

        1.1.1.1.2. Pro:
        Appointed church officials swear an oath of allegiance [5] to the monarch.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      The monarch's authority over the church is largely symbolic [6]; they do not exercise any tangible control over church affairs.

        1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
        The monarch is neither a religious minister nor an ordained priest [7] and, therefore, cannot perform any religious duties.

        1.1.1.2.2. Con:
        By appointing, as head of the church, a man who cheated on his wife [8] and married his mistress, the church promotes unbiblical and problematic messages about marital fidelity.

          1.1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
          Never before in British history has a divorced man, let alone one who admitted to adultery, been coronated. The coronation of King Charles has, therefore, been said to pose constitutional problems [9] that violate oaths and statutes.

        1.1.1.2.3. Con:
        Appointing the monarch as the head of the church runs counter to the principle of separation of church and state [10], thereby impacting his ability to maintain political neutrality.

    1.1.2. Con:
    The British monarch still possesses some significant residual powers (p. 9 [11]).

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      The British monarch appoints the prime minister [12] of the UK.

        1.1.2.1.1. Con:
        The monarch invites a Member of Parliament to form a government and assume the role of Prime Minister based on parliamentary confidence [13] or the recommendation of the outgoing Prime Minister.

        1.1.2.1.2. Pro:
        The monarch's ability to appoint a prime minister becomes vital [14] when no party holds a clear majority in parliament.

        1.1.2.1.3. Con:
        The king's decision to appoint a prime minister is guided by government advisors [15].

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      The monarch's residual powers do not supersede the powers of parliament.

        1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
        Royal assent for bills is automatically granted [16] if both houses of parliament pass the bill.

        1.1.2.2.2. Pro:
        The parliamentary cabinet manual [17] covers all potential political scenarios, ensuring that the king's intervention isn't required.

        1.1.2.2.3. Con:
        In the past, the late queen had invited an MP to form the government against the parliament's wishes [18].

      1.1.2.3. Pro:
      The monarch is endowed with the right to pardon, officially called "the royal prerogative of mercy [19]."

        1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
        Posthumous pardons are an important way to rectify historical wrongs [20]. Notably, in 2014, Queen Elizabeth pardoned Alan Turing, who was convicted of homosexuality, which was criminalized when he was alive.

        1.1.2.3.2. Con:
        Pardon powers are a relic from authoritarian institutions [21] that run counter to the principles of modern democracy. Ideally, King Charles should abolish this power, and at the very least, he should not exercise this power. In either case, this power is defunct.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    The monarch "reigns, but does not rule [22]," meaning their role is primarily ceremonial. They have no governing power.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      The British monarch holds no executive authority [23] over government affairs.

        1.1.3.1.1. Con:
        King Charles has promised to significantly alter the role of the monarchy [24] in national governance. Therefore, King Charles will have a profound impact on the future of the country.

          1.1.3.1.1.1. Pro:
          King Charles has promised to make the monarchy more accessible to the public [25].

          1.1.3.1.1.2. Con:
          King Charles has focused primarily on restructuring how the monarchy functions [26] and enacting staffing changes within the royal family. This does not necessarily mean that the monarchy will have an increased or improved role in national governance.

            1.1.3.1.1.2.1. Con:
            King Charles aims to downsize staffing by eliminating duplicative and redundant positions [27]. This could save large sums of taxpayer money that could be spent to solve issues facing the public.

            1.1.3.1.1.2.2. Con:
            Staffing changes are not merely functional. King Charles wants to eliminate the practice of having separate kitchen staff [28] for the royal family and supporting staff. This erases the line between royalty and commoner, promoting equality.

            1.1.3.1.1.2.3. Pro:
            King Charles intends to eliminate duplicative and redundant [29] positions and family members. By definition, therefore, nothing significant will change, not only at the national level, but also within the royal family.

        1.1.3.1.2. Pro:
        Only parliament can propose amendments to the law, to which the monarch is bound (p. 6 [30]).

          1.1.3.1.2.1. Con:
          Nothing prevents a monarch from publicly supporting, proposing, or opposing legislation, as the crown is formally immune from all legal action [31].

          1.1.3.1.2.2. Pro:
          The Bill of Rights of 1689 [32] established the limitations of royal power and the supremacy of parliament, thereby disallowing the monarch from passing or proposing legislation.

      1.1.3.2. Con:

        1.1.3.2.1. Con:

        1.1.3.2.2. Pro:

    1.1.4. Pro:

      1.1.4.3. Con:

      1.1.4.4. Pro:
