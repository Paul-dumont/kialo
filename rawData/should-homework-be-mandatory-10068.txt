Discussion Name:
Should homework be mandatory?

1. Thesis:
Homework should be mandatory.

  1.1. Pro:
  Homework is academically beneficial for students.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Homework is a tool for teachers to make sure that their students have learned the things that they were taught in school that day.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      In many subjects, expanding on school exercises at home ensures that students have grasped the concept of a lesson.

        1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
        In math, students need to practice [1] using a variety of variables in equations they learned in school in order to understand its full implications.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      Teachers can know whether or not a student has learned the material by their performance on tests rather than homework.

        1.1.1.2.1. Con:
        Homework performance can also be used as an indicator for the teacher to see what in-class lessons he or she needs to clarify and expound upon prior to having students take a test on said material.

        1.1.1.2.2. Pro:
        Testing ensures that the students are all judged in the same environment, as opposed to the environment of their homes, which may differ substantially.

          1.1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
          Environmental factors [2] can have an impact on student performance; thus, thoughtful classroom arrangements can help students better succeed.

          1.1.1.2.2.2. Pro:
          Students perform best when their testing environment is like their normal learning environment [3].

          1.1.1.2.2.3. Con:
          Taking an exam at home could be better for students, as there is less pressure [4] for them to complete it within a certain time.

      1.1.1.3. Con:
      Homework doesn't provide any proof that the child understood the topic. At home, students can ask for help or copy the answers.

        1.1.1.3.1. Pro:
        It would be more useful to have a small test at the end of the day, during school time, to gauge what a child has learned.

          1.1.1.3.1.1. Pro:
          Exit tickets [5] are an quick and easy way for teachers to assess their students' understanding of what they have learned in the day.

        1.1.1.3.2. Pro:
        Some students attend after school tutoring [6], in which the tutor may provide extra guidance to students to help them complete their homework.

        1.1.1.3.3. Con:
        Students commonly cheat [7] inside the classroom as well.

        1.1.1.3.4. Con:
        It is not necessarily bad for students to ask for help [8] on homework, as asking for help allows students to practice the skill of acquiring resources to solve problems.

      1.1.1.4. Pro:
      Practicing skills and revising knowledge from that day's class is essential for their retention [9]. That evening the concepts are still relatively fresh, and thus easier to commit to memory.

        1.1.1.4.1. Con:
        It is not true that studying in the afternoon is the best way to encode memory. Spreading out [10] memory commitment periods is more effective in the short term.

          1.1.1.4.1.1. Con:
          Consistently prescribed homework spreads out periods of study throughout the school year.

        1.1.1.4.2. Pro:
        This is important for students who might not have fully understood the lesson in school and require extra study time [11] to grasp the concept being taught.

        1.1.1.4.3. Con:
        Because children get stressed [12] about their homework, they start disliking school, and will have a harder time retaining information.

    1.1.2. Con:
    Children can perform well academically without doing the homework designated to them by a teacher. They may develop a personalised learning process.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      Homework often forces students to spend additional time and resources on irrelevant concepts without immediate feedback, resulting in insufficient levels of learning.

        1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
        Receiving positive feedback [13] for correct answers would improve a child's self esteem and confidence, but this is often not possible with homework.

        1.1.2.1.2. Con:
        What is or is not irrelevant to learn is not up for the students to decide.

          1.1.2.1.2.1. Con:
          Most school curricula are full of irrelevant information [14] that do not help students in real life.

          1.1.2.1.2.2. Pro:
          Even older, college-age students can struggle to pick a major [15], which increases costs and time in completing their degree.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      A student can use their personalised learning process to complete their homework.

        1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
        Some students use instructional Youtube videos [16] to help them complete their homework assignments.

      1.1.2.3. Con:
      Only very gifted children [17] would be able to consistently grasp new material by processes they've invented, rather than established methods like homework.

        1.1.2.3.1. Con:
        More complex and challenging types of homework can keep advanced students [18] in a class engaged in their own learning, while students not as advanced can do typical assignments to not fall behind.

    1.1.3. Con:
    Systems in some countries have shown that students can excel without homework.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      Finland produces [19] amazing students with less homework and little standardised testing or stressful evaluation in comparison to other countries. (p.4)

        1.1.3.1.1. Con:
        Finland [20] is a small, wealthy, and homogeneous country that values education. These cultural values might be behind these student achievements and may not apply to other places.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      Homework is not necessarily a good indicator of student performance, as some high performing countries [21] like China and Singapore assign [22] a lot of homework, while others like Finland and Japan do not [23].

        1.1.3.2.1. Pro:
        A more rigorous scientific study comparing different countries’ academic achievements and homework policies needs to be carried out before definitive conclusions can be made.

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      The specs grading model [24], which is mastery-based and doesn't rely on the completion of homework, is shown to increase student learning.

    1.1.4. Con:
    Some children may already understand the content of their syllabi and not need to do homework. Making homework mandatory for these children would likely cause them to resent [25] the educational system.

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      Teachers are often forced to teach to the middle [26], so it is inevitable that some students will be less challenged than others.

      1.1.4.2. Pro:
      Often homework assignments involve low effort, repetitive assignments [27] that do not foster critical thinking skills [28].

        1.1.4.2.1. Con:
        This could be rectified by more teacher training on how to assign higher quality homework [29] assignments.

        1.1.4.2.2. Con:
        Repetitive work [30] is sometimes necessary to increase a student's skill and understanding of a subject.

        1.1.4.2.3. Pro:
        Students who are not challenged enough at schoolwork can go on to have bigger problems [31], such as lowered motivation to learn and difficulties meeting challenges later in life.

      1.1.4.3. Con:
      If students already understand the material, then completing homework should be quick and easy, allowing them more free time.

        1.1.4.3.1. Con:
        Rushing [32] through homework assignments can have a negative impact on a student's overall academic performance.

        1.1.4.3.2. Pro:
        There are strategies that students can use to complete their homework faster [33], giving them more free time.

    1.1.5. Pro:
    Studies have found [34] that a moderate amount of homework boosts academic achievement, especially for secondary school students.

      1.1.5.1. Pro:
      One study found [35] a positive relationship between math homework and academic achievement in elementary and middle school students.

        1.1.5.1.1. Pro:
        Middle school teachers have found that the utilization of online math homework [36] significantly increased students' scores.

      1.1.5.2. Pro:
      By sticking to the rule of 10 minutes of homework per grade [37], students can maximize the effectiveness of homework.

        1.1.5.2.1. Pro:
        Multiple studies have found that high quality homework [38] is more effective than a large quantity of homework.

        1.1.5.2.2. Con:
        Though a moderate amount of homework is recommended, it has been found that students are assigned up to three times [39] the recommended amount.

      1.1.5.3. Con:
      The moderation of homework needs to be carefully adhered to, as students who did more than 90 minutes of homework per night saw their test scores decline [40].

    1.1.6. Pro:
    If homework is assigned, students are ultimately able to cover more topics more quickly.

      1.1.6.1. Pro:
      Homework could solidify [41] and deepen knowledge on a lesson taught in school.

      1.1.6.2. Con:
      Assigning too much homework risks burnout [42], reducing a student's concentration level and ability to retain information.

        1.1.6.2.1. Pro:
        Academic burnout is a serious concern [43] as it can cause added mental and emotional anxiety, and can have physical symptoms.

      1.1.6.3. Con:
      Assigning less homework [44] could produce better results in which students acquire a deeper love of learning.

      1.1.6.4. Pro:
      Often students are required to meet deadlines [45] for standardized tests or performances. Homework helps ensure that students are familar with the required material.

        1.1.6.4.1. Pro:
        Assigning homework for music classes [46] ensures that students can play the required pieces ahead of a concert deadline.

        1.1.6.4.2. Pro:
        Students who spend time completing homework may get better standardized test scores [47].

        1.1.6.4.3. Con:
        Standardized tests do a poor job [48] of measuring student achievement and mastery of content.

        1.2.2.2.4. Con:

    1.2.3. Con:
    Hobbies should be prioritized below homework, as it is a student's primary goal to become well educated.

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      Hobbies can instill a passion [67] for an activity that will shape the rest of a child's life.

      1.2.3.2. Pro:
      Some popular hobbies, like playing video games [68], can have a negative impact on a child's development.

        1.2.3.2.1. Con:
        Some studies have found [69] that playing video games can actually benefit children by helping them develop problem-solving skills.

        1.2.3.2.2. Pro:
        Intensive gaming can lead to a decline in a student's academic achievement [70].

      1.2.3.3. Con:
      Homework is less about learning and more about seeing how well the student received the education.

    1.2.4. Pro:
    Children need time both to study and to have fun.

      1.2.4.1. Pro:
      Spending too much time on homework reduces the likelihood that a student will participate in physical activities.

        1.2.4.1.1. Pro:

        1.2.4.1.2. Con:

      1.2.4.2. Con:

        1.2.4.2.1. Con:

        1.2.4.3.2. Con:

      1.2.4.4. Pro:
      The lack of time to complete homework often results in stress [81] and other mental health problems.

        1.2.4.4.1. Pro:
        Studies have shown [82] that excessive homework results in poorer sleep habits that can be detrimental to children's heath.

    1.2.5. Pro:
    In some cases, teachers provide a large workload of homework, which can lead to burnout if it is too much.
