Discussion Name:
Is Earth flat instead of a sphere?

1. Thesis:
Earth is not flat but spherical.

  1.1. Pro:
  A great deal of evidence supports the belief in a spherical Earth.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    The existence of time zones demonstrates that the Earth is round.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      Flat Earth theory explains time zones. The sun and the moon in the flat Earth model are rotating above the disk Earth and act as spotlights [1], so they do not light up the entire Earth at once.

        1.1.1.1.1. Con:
        The FE Moon-Sun system is flawed since it shows the sun and moon at opposite positions in the sky. Eclipses would not be possible if this were the case.

        1.1.1.1.2. Con:
        If the sun and moon were spotlights we would not see any sunset or sunrise.

      1.1.1.2. Pro:
      Time zones exist because of the difference in the illumination of different regions [2] due to the spherical shape of the Earth.

        1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
        Images of the Earth from space show a sharp, curved demarcation between night and day on the surface of the Earth. If the Earth were flat, and night and day were caused by the fall-off of light from the Sun due to distance, then the pattern of day and night on the Earth would appear circular.

          1.1.1.2.1.1. Con:
          This assumes that the Sun radiates equally in all directions, rather than being a "spotlight-like" directed light source.

          1.1.1.2.1.2. Con:
          Light does not always appear to travel in a straight line. Light can curve [3] and refract [4], which can lead to optical illusions such as a curved day/night demarcation.

            1.1.1.2.1.2.1. Con:
            The refractive index of the atmosphere diminishes with altitude. Following the Snell-Descartes' law of refraction, as an object approaches the horizon, the atmospheric refraction makes it appear higher [5] than it actually is, not lower. Therefore, refraction can't be what causes night.

        1.1.1.2.2. Con:
        On a flat Earth, time zones are created by the Sun passing along the equator everyday.

          1.1.1.2.2.1. Con:
          The Sun does not pass directly over the equator every day. The latitude at which it passes directly overhead varies between 23.5°N and 23.5°S [6] throughout the year due to the tilt of Earth's axis [7].

        1.1.1.2.3. Pro:
        If instead, the Earth was a disk, everybody would have the same time everywhere without the need for different time zones.

          1.1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
          If the surface of the Earth were a flat plane, then direct light from the Sun would not reach the surface once the Sun moved below the horizon.

          1.1.1.2.3.2. Con:
          This claim assumes that the other features (apart from Earth's shape) associated with the heliocentric model are also true. In the flat Earth model, this would not be the case.

      1.1.1.3. Con:
      Time zones only demonstrate that the sun and moon are moving in consistent, predictable ways.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    There are currently around 2,500 satellites orbiting the Earth [8]. These rely on the spherical nature of the Earth in order to remain in orbit.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      Composite images from these satellites have been used to create the interactive Google Earth [9] project, which enables the user to view and zoom in on locations around the spherical Earth.

        1.1.2.1.1. Con:
        The spherical Earth shown on Google Earth is fabricated from photographs taken by airplanes [10], not satellites, and is not indicative of the shape of the Earth in reality.

        1.1.2.1.2. Pro:
        The fact that this model remains consistent with millions of photographs taken by completely different sources is strong evidence that it is an accurate representation.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      It is unclear why satellites could not orbit above a flat Earth.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        Satellites are kept in orbit by balancing [11] the centripetal force of gravity, which pulls them towards center of the spherical Earth, and the centrifugal force resulting from their velocity.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    There are pictures of the Earth that show it is not flat.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      NASA’s Johnson Space Center has over 1.5 million catalogued photographs of the Earth [12] taken by astronauts from spacecraft and the International Space Station.

        1.1.3.1.1. Con:
        Evidence from NASA is not trustworthy, as the organization has a vested interest in maintaining belief in a spherical Earth.

          1.1.3.1.1.1. Pro:
          If the Earth was uncovered to be flat, then it would be proof that NASA has been lying for decades, and would call many of their past projects into question.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      It is impossible to conclusively prove that something is 3-dimensional using 2-dimensional evidence.

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      Two-dimensional photographs of the Earth taken at different times show patterns of light and shadow that confirm the Earth is spherical.

      1.1.3.4. Con:
      Independent, non-governmental launches of weather balloons and rockets have captured images [13] that show a flat Earth.

        1.1.3.4.1. Con:
        Most private weather balloons and rockets only reach a few kilometres above Earth. From this altitude [14] a regular camera cannot capture Earth's curvature.

          1.1.3.4.1.1. Pro:
          Mike Humes, a famous flat Earth believer, was only able to launch himself 600m in the air using his rocket [15].

          1.1.3.4.1.2. Pro:
          Earth has massive dimensions compared to a human. In order to see Earth's curvature, one must be very far away from Earth's surface; thus, balloons are not ideal instruments to record such evidence.

            1.1.3.4.1.2.1. Pro:
            Earth's curvature can be seen from the ISS [16], and can also be seen from high-altitude jets [17].

        1.1.3.4.2. Pro:
        Using a weather balloon, even amateur astronomers can send cameras into the atmosphere to take pictures of the Earth.

          1.1.3.4.2.1. Con:
          Many of these images are taken with a fisheye lens [18], similar what is used on a GoPro camera, this creates visual distortion in the image.

            1.1.3.4.2.1.1. Pro:
            Fisheye lenses are not suitable for many scientific applications as they deform the picture [19], which makes analysis of a picture tedious. A scientist would have to undo the deformation to obtain the original image, and there would be a loss of information in the process.

      1.1.3.5. Con:
      The pictures that do exist of a whole spherical Earth drifting in space have been fabricated with photo editing, paintings, CGI and other techniques. This has lead to inconsistencies [20] between different images of the Earth.

        1.1.3.5.1. Pro:
        Special effects or image manipulation could easily fabricate images of a spherical earth.

          1.1.3.5.1.1. Pro:
          With the advent of computer-generated imaging, it is possible that the images showing a spherical Earth have been artificially created.

            1.1.3.5.1.1.1. Con:

              1.1.3.5.1.1.1.1. Con:

          1.1.3.5.1.2. Con:
          It is highly unlikely that all of the millions of photos of Earth from space are fabricated.

        1.1.3.5.2. Pro:
        NASA's whole spherical Earth images are actually computer composites. The clouds appear to remain in the same place [23] during a half-day of Earth's rotation, proving these are composite images.

          1.1.3.5.2.1. Pro:
          Until 2018, there was only one non-composite image [24] of the entire Earth.

          1.1.3.5.2.2. Con:
          Composite images are produced from actual pictures.

        1.1.3.5.3. Con:
        These are real pictures of the Earth. If you take a sphere, draw the continents on it, and rotate the sphere in your hands, only certain parts of the surface will be visible based on your perspective.

          1.1.3.5.3.1. Pro:
          Pictures taken from outer space capture cloud formations that move around and evolve dynamically with weather, so their aspects are also always changing.

      1.1.3.6. Pro:
      If the Earth was a flat disk, it wouldn't appear round in every picture from outer space, since the shape would appear more elliptical from different angles. Every picture of the Earth from outer space has shown it as round despite the angle.

    1.1.4. Con:

        1.1.4.3.2. Pro:

    1.1.5. Con:
    There are empirical examples that show the Earth is flat.

      1.1.5.1. Pro:
      The fact that hundreds of miles of railroad track can be cut, measured, and constructed without any mathematical consideration of the Earth's curvature proves that the Earth is flat.

        1.1.5.1.1. Con:
        Sections of railroad track have bending tolerances designed into them [29], and are already flexible enough to accommodate hills and turns. The minor curvature of the Earth is far less than the range included in the mathematical calculations.

          1.1.5.1.1.1. Pro:
          The same sections of track can be used to navigate uneven terrain, such as hills [30]. This would not be possible if track sections could not bend.

            1.1.5.1.1.1.1. Pro:
            If this were not the case, each hill or dip would need to have sections of railroad custom-made to fit the obstacle.

        1.1.5.1.2. Pro:
        "The London and Northwestern Railway forms a straight line 180 miles long between London and Liverpool ... If the world were actually a globe, however, curveting 8 inches per mile squared, the 180 mile stretch of rail would form an arc with the center point at Birmingham raising a full 5,400 feet above London and Liverpool..." - David Wardlaw Scott, Terra Firma (1901) [31]

          1.1.5.1.2.1. Con:
          The curvature of the Earth does not rise in front of an observer. From any observation point on a smooth landscape, all points drop away [32]. The observer's height and the atmospheric conditions define the distance to the horizon.

          1.1.5.1.2.2. Con:
          The calculation for the curvature of the Earth that is used for shorter distances is ~8 inches per mile squared [33]. This works out to be 8" x 180^2 = 259,200" (21,600' or 4.09 miles) of curvature between the two points.

          1.1.5.1.2.3. Con:
          This argument doesn't identify a reference point or reference axis, and also fails to address altitude and sea level. If all the points are at the same distance from the center of the Earth, then they share the same altitude.

      1.1.5.2. Con:
      There are no empirical studies conducted in modern times with scientific rigor and independent verification that show the Earth is flat.

      1.1.5.3. Pro:
      In a 19th century French experiment by M. M. Biot and Arago [34] a powerful lamp with good reflectors was placed on the summit of Desierto las Palmas in Spain and was able to be seen all the way from Camprey on the Island of Iviza.

        1.1.5.3.1. Con:
        There were a number of mathematical and procedural errors with the experiment.

          1.1.5.3.1.1. Pro:
          According to the "Mémorial du Dépôt Général de la Guerre, Volume 7 [35]", the lamp at Desierto de las Palmas was at 2392 ft while the observer at Camprey was at 1312 ft. The distance between the two being actually 100 miles, the spherical geometry predicts the light was 327.5 ft above the line of sight or 841.23 ft above when accounting for refraction. Therefore, the light [36] could be seen.

            1.1.5.3.1.1.1. Pro:
            Looking at the original source, we see absolute heights of 1312.34 feet and 2378 feet, or in the original 400 m and 725 m. The expected drop over 100 miles is 6668.41 feet.

          1.1.5.3.1.2. Pro:
          The experiment's calculation that the lamp should have been "6600 feet ... below the line of sight" is false as it treats the distance between Camprey and the straight line passing through Desierto las Palmas as tangential to Earth. The actual height needed at Camprey is 1046 feet [37].

        1.1.5.3.2. Pro:
        Since the elevations of the two points were identical and the distance between them was nearly 100 miles, if Earth were a ball 25,000 miles in circumference, the light should have been more than 6600 feet below the line of sight.

"As late as the 17th century, some biologists thought that some simpler forms of life were generated by spontaneous generation from inanimate matter. 
...
To test the hypothesis, Francesco Redi placed fresh meat in open containers. As expected, the rotting meat attracted flies, and the meat was soon swarming with maggots, which hatched into flies. When the jars were tightly covered so that flies could not get in, no maggots were produced. To answer the objection that the cover cut off fresh air necessary for spontaneous generation, Redi covered the jars with several layers of porous gauze instead of an air-tight cover.
