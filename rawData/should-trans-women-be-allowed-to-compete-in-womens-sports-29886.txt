Discussion Name:
Should trans women be allowed to compete in women's sports?

1. Thesis:
Trans women should be allowed to compete in women's sports.

  1.1. Con:
  Cis women will not feel comfortable competing with trans women.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Several women athletes find it unfair to have to compete with trans women.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      World Cup-winning soccer player, Megan Rapinoe [1], has been vocal in supporting transgender inclusion in sports.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      Billie Jean King [2] has been a staunch advocate for gender equality in sports, expressing support for transgender athletes.

      1.1.1.3. Pro:
      Many athletes have complained about spaces [3] for women being stripped away because of trans athletes.

        1.1.1.3.1. Con:
        Men occupying spaces designated for women outnumber instances of trans women in such spaces significantly, making the impact of trans women negligible in comparison.

          1.1.1.3.1.1. Con:
          Title IX [4] of the Education Amendments of 1972 in the United States prohibits gender-based discrimination including sports that receive federal financial assistance.

            1.1.1.3.1.1.1. Con:
            The majority of college athletic departments do not meet [5] the standards outlined in Title IX.

          1.1.1.3.1.2. Pro:
          Girls have more than one million fewer opportunities than boys to participate in sports (p. 1) [6].

          1.1.1.3.1.3. Pro:
          86% of NCAA institutions offer more athletic opportunities to men than women in college sports (pp. 5-7) [7].

        1.1.1.3.2. Pro:
        Trans athletes use up resources [8] meant for women when they participate against them in sports.

        1.1.1.3.3. Con:
        Trans people make up roughly 1% [9] of the population, it is unlikely they will dominate much of the space women have in status quo.

          1.1.1.3.3.1. Con:
          A few dominant [10] trans women in a sport can render the entire activity uncompetitive for everyone else, negating the need for multiple trans athletes to occupy women's spaces.

          1.1.1.3.3.2. Pro:
          The NCAA permitted trans women to compete based on hormone therapy around 11 years ago [11]. Despite this, only a handful of trans athletes participate each year.

        1.1.1.3.4. Pro:
        The purpose of women's sports categories is to eliminate male advantages [12] and create fair competition. If male advantages persist in women's sports, the rationale behind having separate categories becomes obsolete.

        1.1.1.3.5. Pro:
        Trans athletes secure positions on teams that might otherwise go [13] to cis women.

      1.1.1.4. Pro:
      A survey [14] of female British Olympic athletes showed that they found the IOC guidelines for trans women unfair and frustrating.

    1.1.2. Con:
    Discomfort due to change is inevitable. The comfort of cis athletes should not be prioritized over equitable treatment of people.

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      Cisgender athletes have expressed concerns about unequal treatment, feeling silenced [15] in discussions regarding the inclusion of transgender athletes in women's sports.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      The discomfort experienced by cisgender athletes often stems from [16] transphobia.

        1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
        566 anti-trans bills [17] have been introduced in US state legislatures in 2023 against trans athletes that are discriminatory and transphobic based on made-up moral panic.

        1.1.2.2.2. Con:
        Many athletes have complained about being unfairly labeled [18] as transphobic when raising any legitimate concerns regarding transgender participation in sports.

        1.1.2.2.3. Pro:
        Transphobia often arises from misconceptions and stereotypes about transgender individuals. Cisgender athletes have expressed unfounded beliefs and stereotypes [19] about trans athletes.

      1.1.2.3. Con:
      Cis-women may feel intimidated by the size and strength of trans athletes, giving them an unfair psychological advantage.

        1.1.2.3.1. Con:
        Trans athletes suffer from higher rates of bullying, anxiety and depression [20]—all of which makes it more difficult for them to train and compete.

          1.1.2.3.1.1. Pro:
          People who identify as transgender have significantly higher [21] rates of suicide and suicide attempts than the rest of the population.

        1.1.2.3.2. Pro:
        Allowing trans women to participate alongside other women can exacerbate [22] body image issues among cis-women.

        1.1.2.3.3. Con:
        There already exists women who are naturally larger and stronger in size in sports. Players continue to play alongside these women, therefore trans women will not be a problem.

          1.1.2.3.3.1. Pro:
          Serena Williams [23], one of the most dominant players in tennis history, is known for her strength and power.

          1.1.2.3.3.2. Pro:
          Margo Dydek [24], a former professional basketball player known for being the tallest woman to ever play in the WNBA, stood at 7 feet 2 inches tall.

          1.1.2.3.3.3. Con:
          Size and strength variations among cisgender women may exist within a spectrum that's still within the bounds [25] of a biologically female physiology.

      1.1.2.4. Pro:
      Many people were likely uncomfortable playing with people of color in the past leading to laws that banned their participation [26].

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Some cisgender women might have personal or privacy-related concerns whilst competing against trans women.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      Athletes often share locker rooms and communal spaces, and some may feel uneasy [27] about the presence of transgender women in these environments.

      1.1.3.2. Pro:
      Former collegiate swimmer Riley Gaines complained about a trans athlete exposing 'male genitalia' [28] in a women's locker room.

      1.1.3.3. Con:
      Offering options for athletes uncomfortable with shared spaces to use private changing areas or providing more privacy options can alleviate concerns without excluding transgender athletes.

  1.2. Con:
  Trans women get an unfair biological advantage when competing against cis women in sports.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    A trans woman has the biological body of a male, thus giving her a physiological advantage over biological females.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      USA Powerlifters (USAPL) reject the inclusion [29] of trans women under the premise that biological males have an unfair advantage over biological females; as much as 20%.

      1.2.1.2. Pro:
      Trans women have an advantage over cis women because male hormones such as testosterone [30] are present in their bodies since their birth.

        1.2.1.2.1. Con:
        Between 8-13% [31] women are affected by Polycystic ovarian syndrome [32] (PCOS), which leads to heightened testosterone levels yet, they are not prohibited from participating in women's sports.

          1.2.1.2.1.1. Pro:
          There are likely many more women affected by Polycystic ovary syndrome given that, up to 70% [33] of affected women remain undiagnosed worldwide.

        1.2.1.2.2. Con:
        Research on athletes' testosterone levels reveals an absence [34] of a clear and consistent correlation between testosterone and athletic performance.

        1.2.1.2.3. Pro:
        Testosterone plays a massive role [35] in the development of muscle bulk and strength.

        1.2.1.2.4. Pro:
        Male puberty can bring about physiological changes, such as increased lung capacity [36] which may impact athletic performance.

      1.2.1.3. Con:
      Transgender women might face challenges [37] as their larger frames operate with decreased muscle mass and reduced aerobic capacity.

      1.2.1.4. Pro:
      Men typically possess inherent advantages [38] such as larger bone structure, higher bone density, stronger connective tissue, and greater muscle density compared to women.

      1.2.1.5. Con:
      Many athletes have genetic traits [39] that give them advantages over their competitors.

        1.2.1.5.1. Pro:
        Height [40] in basketball players, for example gives them an advantage over other players.

        1.2.1.5.2. Con:
        Genetic advantages can enhance an athlete's potential, but they typically don't singularly determine [41] someone's success in sports.

      1.2.1.6. Con:
      Many trans women use various substances including estrogen, which results in [42] reduced muscle mass, strength and speed.

        1.2.1.6.1. Pro:
        Some studies [43] have shown transgender women athletes with lowered testosterone performed no better on various athletic tests than women athletes.

        1.2.1.6.2. Con:
        Some studies show that trans women do not lose [44] significant amounts of muscle mass when their testosterone is suppressed.

        1.2.1.6.3. Con:
        Men produce more [45] type II fast twitch muscle fibers than women. This makes them typically faster and stronger than women, characteristics that are not dissipated [46] by transgender hormone therapy.

      1.2.1.7. Pro:
      In comparison [47] to biological men, women have a 'lower proportion of lean tissue distributed in their upper body'.

    1.2.2. Pro:
    It incentivizes people to become or advocate for becoming transgender because that enables a much greater chance of winning.

    1.2.3. Con:
    Trans athletes competing against women use medication [48] to suppress testosterone.

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      The majority of women undergoing testosterone suppression medication find it challenging to bring their testosterone levels within the typical range [49] observed in cisgender females.

      1.2.3.2. Con:
      Many trans individuals who attempt to lower testosterone levels through medication are unable [50] to do so.

      1.2.3.3. Pro:
      The International Olympic Committee policy shares that only trans women that have maintained a testosterone level below [51] 10 nM for at least a year may compete.

      1.2.3.4. Con:
      This doesn't affect their prior body composition and training.

      1.2.3.5. Con:
      Testosterone levels and androgen receptors are not the only major sports-relevant difference between the two sexes.

    1.2.4. Pro:
    Cis women are unable to compete [52] against trans women on an equal level, making sports uncompetitive.

      1.2.4.1. Con:
      Cis women have defeated [53] trans athletes in sports on numerous occasions.

      1.2.4.2. Con:
      The Olympics have had trans-inclusive policies since 2004; yet, no athlete even qualified to participate in the Games until 2021. [54]

      1.2.4.3. Pro:
      Transgender sprinters finished [55] first and second at a Connecticut girls track meet.

      1.2.4.4. Pro:
      A trans woman [56] that competed as a man the year before won a NCAA women's track championship recently.

  1.3. Pro:
  As trans women are ineligible to participate in men's sports, their exclusion from women's sports effectively denies them access to sports altogether.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    Access to sports is a fundamental human right [57], akin to health or education, and should be afforded to trans women as well.

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      Exclusion from sports denies transgender women opportunities [58] for growth and success through scholarships, careers, and education.

      1.3.1.2. Con:
      Letting trans women compete in women's sports hinders the rights [59] of female competitors to participate fairly.

        1.3.1.2.1. Pro:
        Cisgender women face higher injury risks [60] when competing against transgender women, making it highly risky for them to compete head-to-head.

          1.3.1.2.1.1. Pro:
          A World Rugby report [61] highlighted that biological females faced higher injury risks when tackled by transgender women who experienced male puberty, even if these trans athletes followed testosterone regulations set by the IOC.

          1.3.1.2.1.2. Con:
          The higher risk in sports doesn't prevent cisgender women from competing. In various extremely risky sports [62], athletes participate and excel despite the inherent dangers.

          1.3.1.2.1.3. Pro:
          In combat sports like boxing [63], weight classes are established to create fair match-ups and reduce the risk of injury due to significant size discrepancies between competitors.

        1.3.1.2.2. Con:
        Efforts to exclude transgender women from sports, often involving invasive tests, also impact cisgender women [64] who have to undergo these procedures.

        1.3.1.2.3. Con:
        Such male involvement in women's sports reinforces stereotypes [65] that women are weak and in need of protection.

      1.3.1.3. Pro:
      The International Olympic Committee (IOC) places the practice of sport as a human right [66].

      1.3.1.4. Con:
      Casual play should be open to all people, but professional competition is not a human right.

        1.3.1.4.1. Pro:
        Professional sports have specific requirements [67] and regulations; participation isn't universally open to everyone.

        1.3.1.4.2. Con:
        Any woman who trains can try out for a professional sport of her choice. A trans woman is not afforded the same opportunity.

    1.3.2. Con:
    Reasonable exclusion from elite sports due to an unfair advantage is not tantamount to exclusion from sports entirely.

      1.3.2.1. Pro:
      There are many local leagues both competitive and non-competitive where trans individuals may engage in sport among peers without facing any kinds of testing or restriction.

      1.3.2.2. Con:
      Transgender women may not find a suitable category in sports due to policies that exclude them from both men's and women's [68] leagues.

        1.3.2.2.1. Con:
        Policies regarding trans women in sports differ significantly. In Germany [69], they may choose to compete in men's or women's leagues based on their preference.

      1.3.2.3. Con:
      Trans women who's testosterone levels are similar to those of women could still be allowed to compete in women's sports.

        1.3.2.3.1. Con:
        A 'hormone grading' system would be very intrusive and stigmatizing [70] for many athletes.

    1.3.3. Pro:
    Sports leagues lack the expertise and understanding to determine who qualifies as a woman, and therefore should not hold authority in making such determinations.

      1.3.3.1. Con:
      Sports leagues possess extensive experience in understanding the physical conditions required for competitive outcomes, given their years of involvement in the field.

      1.3.3.2. Pro:
      Few experts worldwide have conducted research on this topic, and even they believe that the current available information is insufficient [71] to make a conclusive decision on the matter.

      1.3.3.3. Con:
      Leagues often collaborate [72] with medical and scientific experts to create their policies.

      1.3.3.4. Pro:
      Gender identity is a multifaceted concept [73] that involves social, psychological, and medical aspects.

    1.3.4. Con:
    The inclusion of transgender women in sports jeopardizes the sport's vitality and limits access for a more expansive demographic.

      1.3.4.1. Pro:
      The disparity between cisgender women and trans athletes diminishes the viewing experience [74] for fans, resulting in a lack of excitement.

      1.3.4.2. Con:
      Incorporating trans women expands the sports audience by engaging a demographic that had previously been overlooked.

      1.3.4.3. Pro:
      Incorporating trans women into sports is seen as politicizing the field [75], prompting some fans to disengage or exit from the sports sphere.

      1.3.4.4. Con:
      Society evolves, and sports must reflect these changes. The inclusion of transgender athletes aligns with societal shifts [76] in understanding gender diversity.

    Transgender woman that are biologically male should compete in men's sports because they are male
