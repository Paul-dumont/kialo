Discussion Name:
Should Protesting in Front of Abortion Clinics Be Prohibited?

1. Thesis:
Protesting in front of abortion clinics should be prohibited.

  1.1. Con:
  Introducing protest-free zones will lead to more radical and violent forms of protest.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Pro-lifers will protest outside the exclusion zones, which will be worse because they will be harder to anticipate and police, and will have less media scrutiny.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      The area will presumably be wide enough to make it impossible to line the perimeter with individuals. It also means that any protest cannot be targeted directly at women.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      Less media scrutiny is a good thing because it doesn't give a voice to protestors.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    Extremist pro-lifers will commit terrorist attacks [1] against abortion clinics, such as arson and shootings. This already occurs occasionally under the status quo, but will become more common when they have no other way to stop people from getting abortions.

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      This implies that pro-lifers should be considered akin to terrorists, and that they should not be permitted to manifest in public, or even exist at all.

        1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
        It is a fact that members of the antiabortion movement [2] are engaging in an escalation of criminal behaviors [3] on an international scale [4], and that some protest actions have been legally defined as acts of terror [5] as committed by groups labelled as Christian terrorists [6].

        1.1.2.1.2. Pro:
        In Canada [7], these acts of terror are defined as special-interest or single-issue terrorism.

        1.1.2.1.3. Pro:
        Most anti-abortionists have views because they are anti-woman [8]. The ones who care about life seem to be the minority of anti-abortionists.

          1.1.2.1.3.1. Con:
          The fact that many prominent pro-life organizations are run by women makes it unlikely that the pro-life movement is anti-woman.

            1.1.2.1.3.1.1. Pro:
            The president of Students for Life of America [9] is a woman.

            1.1.2.1.3.1.2. Pro:
            The president of Live Action [10] is a woman.

            1.1.2.1.3.1.3. Pro:
            The president of the Pro-Life Alliance of Gays and Lesbians [11] is a woman.

            1.1.2.1.3.1.4. Pro:
            The president of the pro-life organization Susan B. Anthony's List [12] is a woman.

          1.1.2.1.3.2. Con:
          There are too many confounding variables correlated with being pro-life (such as general conservatism, religiosity, etc.) to make a survey like the one linked [13] useful for determining the impact of pro-life beliefs.

            1.1.2.1.3.2.1. Pro:
            More religious people tend to be pro-life [14], and many religious people ascribe to complementarianism [15], which asserts that women and men have different roles in society. This may be partly responsible for the survey results. However, many pro-lifers do not believe in complementarianism and do not share these views.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      Since 1977 [16], there have been eight murders, 17 attempted murders, 42 bombings, and 186 arsons targeted at abortion clinics and providers across the United States.

    1.1.3. Con:
    No matter where protests occur, antiabortion activists will continue to engage in a pattern of violence, abuse, and lawlessness.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      With a reported [17], number of [18] over 300 [19] extreme acts of violence since 1973, and a major spike in reported crimes [20], the continued escalation of criminal acts by antiabortion protesters is a foregone conclusion.

        1.1.3.1.1. Con:
        There are a lot of cases of violence from pro- [21]abortion [22] advocates [23], too, that are [24] often ignored. [25]

          1.1.3.1.1.1. Con:
          Each of these sources is from an anti-choice organization, implicating a high level of bias and thus unreliability.

            1.1.3.1.1.1.1. Con:
            The sources linked in the great-grandparent claim (Vox, CNN, and CBS News) have a liberal bias according to AllSides [26]. Therefore, if we need to question any sources that could be biased we would need to question the evidence linked in the great-grandparent claim.

              1.1.3.1.1.1.1.1. Con:
              The mentioned sources are still all news sources, dedicated to reporting on matters occurring in the world, as opposed to organizations that at the outset take a position on the specific subject in discussion.

          1.1.3.1.1.2. Con:
          Even assuming the parent claim is true, it is a whataboutism, with no bearing on or refutation of the fact of the acts of violence by anti-choice activists.

        1.1.3.1.2. Con:
        According to the Reuters [27], there were over 78,000 instances of "protests and picketing at clinics" but only 62 "threats of danger or death." Thus, only a tiny fraction of abortion clinic protests ever lead to threats.

          1.1.3.1.2.1. Pro:
          The Washington Post [28] writes that about 3.7% of Black Lives Matter protests involved "involved property damage or vandalism," but this is not a sufficient reason for categorically banning Black Lives Matter protests. Similarly, if only a tiny minority of abortion clinic protests turn violent then this cannot be a reason for prohibiting them.

    1.1.4. Con:
    In so far as there are individuals who have the potential to react violently and dangerously, they are more likely to be trigger and provoked by constantly standing outside a centre and seeing "murder" occurring in front of their eyes every day. If they cannot stand outside abortion clinics, they will be less directly provoked.

    1.1.5. Con:
    Most anti abortion activists feel very strongly about preventing murder. It seems highly unlikely that they would condone committing murder.

      1.1.5.1. Con:
      Anti abortion activists feel strongly about abortion. Their feelings on murder while seemingly tied to it is not synonymous with it.

      1.1.5.2. Con:
      One might justify killing a person if that person is a murderer. Hence, pro-life activists have killed doctors in the past. [29]

      1.1.5.3. Con:
      -> See 1.1.2.1.3.

    1.1.6. Con:
    Many people feel strongly about abortion, however this doesn't mean that they are murderers with no morals. Despite strong anti-abortion feelings, protestors are regular members of society and do not engage in widespread violence.

      1.1.6.1. Con:
      Legal violations and reports of extreme violence and harassment rose precipitously in 2018 [30]. This shows an alarming upward trend of flagrant violations of the law, to include a protracted campaign of digital harassment, threats of death, and extreme violence. Antiabortion protests have spun out of control and suggests the opposite of this position.

        1.1.6.1.1. Con:
        The total number of protestors [31] also rose in 2018, so one would expect there to be more incidents even if the fraction of protestors that turn violent is as low as it has always been.

      1.1.6.2. Pro:
      -> See 1.1.3.1.2.

  1.2. Con:
  Protests at abortion clinics draw attention to the abortion debate and improve the quality of discourse.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Protests outside clinics are dramatic and highly symbolic, which attracts media attention to the abortion debate.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      The media scrutiny of protests at abortion clinics allows bigotry and radicalism to be called out publicly.

        1.2.1.1.1. Con:
        Allowing protests in front of abortion clinics is not necessary to obtain media scrutiny and call out bigotry and radicalism.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      More media attention is not positive if it overstates the amount of support for anti abortion movements.

    1.2.2. Pro:
    The debate over abortion rights often fades into the background of political discourse, which means that important policy reforms are delayed.

    1.2.3. Pro:
    Pro-choice activists are galvanised by protests at abortion clinics to hold their own counter-protests.

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      Counter protests do not effectively counter the effect that anti abortion protests have on women. Messages of support are less impactful than the visceral violent actions of a few anti abortion protesters, such as the instance of throwing fake blood on women [32], and screaming that they are a murderer.

    1.2.4. Con:
    Protest free zones will reduce vitriol and improve the quality of the discourse around abortion, benefiting all sides of the debate.

      1.2.4.1. Con:
      Protestors won't be able to inform those who want an abortion about their position. Shutting out one position will not improve the discourse because it becomes one-sided.

        1.2.4.1.1. Con:
        Social media and marches remain as methods to inform other of their position without intimidating people just trying to access clinic services.

          1.2.4.1.1.1. Con:
          Protesting at clinics is more valuable than social media and marches because it promotes the pro-life position where it matters most.

      1.2.4.2. Pro:
      Protests are inherently confrontational, with the protestors frequently incensed, and desperately trying to prevent abortion which in their eyes is murder.

      1.2.4.3. Pro:
      Removing the confrontation, and ensuring that protestors and women do not come face to face with each other reduces the immediacy of the debate, and calms both sides.

      1.2.4.4. Pro:
      Without protest free zones, anti abortion activists would be less able to directly target women, or identify women who got abortions.

      1.2.4.5. Pro:
      The attitudes of pro-choice individuals are worsened when the anti-abortion movement is portrayed as violent and abusive. Without this picture, there may be less hostility towards the other side, enabling a more inclusive discourse.

      1.2.4.6. Con:
      People get more vitriolic when they feel that their right to express themselves is threatened. Pro-lifers who are barred from protesting outside abortion clinics will take to social media and other outlets to vilify women who get abortions.

    1.2.5. Con:
    A ban on protests around abortion zones would be an equally effective public condemnation of the harassment of women, without the cost of those women having to experience harassment.

      1.2.5.1. Con:
      A modification on where protests happen and what is publicly acceptable, like the existing protections regarding abusive and threatening language in other venues, rather than an language that suggests a ban, is a reasonable expectation.

        1.2.5.1.1. Pro:
        Such actions preserve the right to free speech, but clearly define and outline when free speech crosses the line into harassment and the fear of violence.

    1.2.6. Con:
    Anti abortion protests, and the large amounts of press coverage they gain, suggests that there is equal support for both sides of the debate. By eliminating these shock tactics it is likely that the perceived levels of support for anti choice activism will decrease.

      1.2.6.1. Con:
      Just because the media sometimes show pro-life protests, it is unclear how that communicates that there is "equal support for both sides of the debate." Most people are well aware that groups holding a minority opinion on a subject can hold protests.

      1.2.6.2. Con:
      Pew Research [33] found that roughly as many Americans identify as pro-life as pro-choice.

  1.3. Con:
  Introducing mandatory protest free zones will increase political opposition to abortion, and limit the capacity of women to access abortion in the long term.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    This forces people to choose between supporting abortion rights and supporting freedom of speech. Many people will opt for the latter.

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      In many jurisdictions, such as the US South, freedom of speech is seen as an inviolable right [34], while abortion rights are hotly contested. These are the jurisdictions in which there is the greatest need to secure access to abortion for women, and in which protest-free zones will be most unpopular.

        1.3.1.1.1. Con:
        Freedom of speech is contested as well, e.g. in terms of political correctness. Thus, there is no difference. Both abortion and freedom of speech are hotly contested.

      1.3.1.2. Con:
      This argument presents a false dichotomy between supporting abortion and supporting free speech. There is a possibility for a nuanced response from the public that will not necessarily encroach on abortion rights.

    1.3.2. Pro:
    Locking up activists who protest outside abortion clinics will make them into martyrs of the pro-life cause.

      1.3.2.1. Con:
      Prohibiting protestation outside of abortion clinics does not necessitate "locking up activists."

        1.3.2.1.1. Pro:
        Civil penalties can be imposed instead.

        1.3.2.1.2. Con:
        Carrying out the state's goals would at some point necessitate the use of force, thus leading to "locking up activists".

    1.3.3. Con:
    There are other public places where people are not legally permitted to protest in complete freedom (e.g., places that have established free speech zones [35] that protesters are limited to), and they have not caused increased political opposition to every cause protested in these public spaces.

      1.3.3.1. Pro:
      Freedom of speech is not affected. Free zones only serve to protect abortion clients on their way to fulfil their deed. To insinuate that this affects freedom of speech (i.e. to protest anywhere and everywhere with no free zones) is an unrealistic thought.

      1.3.3.2. Pro:
      Federal legislation was created to prevent Westboro Baptist supporters from disrupting military funerals [36] but these restrictions have not increased general public support for the Westboro Baptist Church.

    1.3.4. Pro:
    If fewer people support abortion rights, there will be greater social stigma against people who choose to have abortions. As a result, individuals will feel pressured to carry their unwanted children to term.

      1.3.4.1. Con:
      The social stigma is only effective when women are outed as having abortions. Our policy will ensure that women are less likely to be personally identified and directly shamed for having an abortion.

      1.3.4.2. Con:
      Our policy makes anti abortion activism less prominent, therefore it is unclear that there would necessarily be less support for abortion rights.

    1.3.5. Con:
    Currently, political opposition to abortion is not linked to public opinion [37]. Most people believe that abortion should be available to women, however there is still strong political opposition to this idea. Therefore, the ability or inability to protest is not currently linked to the strength of the political opposition to abortion.

    1.3.6. Pro:
    If the pro-choice movement is weakened relative to the pro-life movement, politicians and voters will move to restrict abortion rights. This will be achieved by defunding clinics, criminalising abortion under certain circumstances, and permitting doctors to discourage patients from receiving abortions.

      1.3.6.1. Con:
      This argument assumes that the anti abortion movement has a capacity for growth. Empirically, the support for anti abortion has been declining [38]. Therefore it is unlikely that this policy will shift the balance in favour of anti abortion activists.

    1.3.7. Con:
    There has been a demonstrable and dangerous spike in protest behaviors outside clinics that very clearly violates Federal law [39], with a 78% increase [40] in reports of barring entry or exit alone. This suggests that the absence of laws curtailing protest behaviors and location emboldens antiabortion activists to extreme measures, not the opposite.

      1.3.7.1. Pro:
      Threats of of extreme violence, against people seeking medical treatment and against medical facility staff have also increased exponentially [41].

    1.3.8. Pro:
    Many people view Roe vs. Wade as federal overreach and a states rights issue. If a ban was legislated at a federal level, this issue would become more serious.

    1.3.9. Con:
    In the short term there may be a backlash from the pro-life community. However the likelihood that this will lead to long term increase in support for anti abortion activism is low. This is because the cause will be further from the public's mind.

    1.3.10. Con:
    Although this may enrage free speech activists, it is unclear why those free speech activists would respond by trying to limit women's abortion rights. In so far as there would be backlash it would be regarding the policy, and not conflated with the rights of women.

      1.3.10.1. Con:
      If more media attention is attracted to the subject, any free speech activists who have not made a decision may be swayed against abortion due to the negative media attention that will be received.

    1.3.11. Con:
    The ban on protests will send an important social message that the government and society are unwilling to stand for women being shamed and humiliated for making their choices. This will delegitimise anti abortion activism and reduce societal stigma.

      Some US States have one or very few [76] clinics which provide abortions. Some abortion seekers have limited means of independent transportation. For these people, there are no 'other' clinics.

        1.9.6.3.1. Pro:
        -> See 1.7.2.1.

        1.9.6.3.2. Pro:
        In many conservative rural communities, women only have access to one abortion clinic [77]. These are the places where anti-abortion protests are most common and extreme.

  1.10. Pro:
  The government has a role in protecting the most vulnerable members of society, and should actively reduce the stigma surrounding abortion by implementing protest free zones.

    1.10.1. Con:
    -> See 1.1.

    1.10.2. Pro:
    Setting aside the opinion of abortion, it comes down to providing reasonable protections from damages caused by the menacing behaviors of clinic protestors, whose tactics are meant to cause fear and distress, as well as long-term mental and emotional anguish. Instituting preventative measures to curtail alarming behaviors is not unreasonable or unusual.

      1.10.2.1. Con:
      Many pro-life organizations reject the use of gore or "invectives" in their signs for protesting at clinics.

        1.10.2.1.1. Pro:
        None of the sign slogans recommended by Students for Life of America [78] involve the use of gore or "invectives."

        1.10.2.1.2. Con:
        University of Texas at Austin reports that "sidewalk counseling" routinely involves shouting, intimidation, and displays of graphic medical imagery. Given the current state of affairs, confrontations have escalated, and include overt racist rhetoric targeting minorities. Oversized placards depicting gore are not only the norm, but a signature move by groups protesting clinics across the nation.

    1.10.3. Pro:
    Abortion, and abortion consultations are medical procedures. No one should have to face threats of harm and harassment to access any medical procedures.

      1.10.3.1. Con:
      Medical procedures that cause harm to another person, such as a doctor prescribing thalidomide to a pregnant woman, should be opposed.

    1.10.4. Pro:
    -> See 1.7.6.

    1.10.5. Pro:
    The government is the only body that can effectively enforce and protect the rights of these women to safely access abortions.

  1.11. Con:
  Banning people from protesting at abortion clinics represents a violation of their right to free speech.

    1.11.1. Con:
    Believing more fervently in your righteousness does not mean you have an increased right to speak.

      1.11.1.1. Con:
      The right to protest outside of facilities isn’t an increased right to speak; its the same one held by all Americans.

    1.11.2. Con:
    This is not a legitimate form of discussion, because it's intent is to coerce women out of abortions. This is evidenced by the tactics used by protesters, which is not that of a calm discussion but instead involves harassment and shouting.

      1.11.2.1. Con:
      Pro life protesters hold signs and pictures that they hope will persuade people seeking abortions to not have them. The prerogative to have an abortion remains with the person seeking it. Therefore no coercion takes place here.

        1.11.2.1.1. Pro:
        It would be coercive if protesters blocked the entrance to the clinic, blackmail women or physically restrain them from entering the clinic.

      1.11.2.2. Con:
      Free speech is the right to express what's on a person's mind at any given time. Willingness to have a discussion or the form of discussion someone is pursuing has no bearing on the the legitimacy of the speech.

      1.11.2.3. Pro:
      Given the alarming rate at which violence and obstruction by antiabortion protesters has risen, and the tactics employed by protesters across the nation, like threats of violence, menacing, and actual violence, or by simply harrassing patients while holding explicitly gory signs and pictures alone, the legal definition of coercion [79] is easily met.

        1.11.2.3.1. Con:
        -> See 1.1.3.1.2.

        1.11.2.3.2. Con:
        Nothing in the linked source [80] makes it clear how using gory signs is a form of "coercion"

      1.11.2.4. Pro:
      -> See 1.8.1.

      1.11.2.5. Con:
      Many pro-life groups do not use harassment and shouting and instead focus on calm discussion.

        1.11.2.5.1. Pro:
        Students for Life of America [81] lists "Be peaceful and helpful" and "Have compassion" as things to do during sidewalk counseling, and it lists "Yell" and "Be aggressive" as things not to do.

    1.11.3. Pro:
    It should be the right of the individual to voice their opposition to any action taken by the state or another private individual to the extent that public order is not disrupted.

      1.11.3.1. Con:
      Freedom of speech stops where it infringes on the rights of others.

      1.11.3.2. Con:
      Enacting and enforcing free speech zones away from the services provided to patients of a clinic offering abortion procedures preserves free speech and prevents the disruption of public order.

      1.11.3.3. Pro:
      From the perspective of a pro-lifer, abortion is a moral atrocity [82]. It is thus extremely important for them to be able to express their opposition to it, and to have the opportunity to convince individuals to forgo it.

        1.11.3.3.1. Con:
        From the respective of a pro choicer [83], whether some view it as a moral atrocity or not it is still the woman’s right, just as it is the right of opposers to protest it. However, if that protest stops or damages the woman’s right, by scaring or threatening her, then it is no longer acceptable.

        1.11.3.3.2. Pro:
        It is especially important that pro-lifers have the opportunity to protest around abortion clinics because these protests represent a unique opportunity to talk directly to people who are considering abortions, and also because they garner media attention.

          1.11.3.3.2.1. Con:
          Protestors can still access their right to freedom of speech, this merely restricts the area in which that speech is practiced.

            1.11.3.3.2.1.1. Con:
            Limitations on being able to express legitimate opinions by definition is an infringement on free speech.

              1.11.3.3.2.1.1.1. Con:
              Freedom of speech does not mean that a person can say whatever they want to. Freedom of speech means [84] that someone's right to say something is protected within certain limits, reasonably applied across a myriad of venues and situations. Hanging the entirety of the pro-life movement on the notion that criminal behavior is protected speech has been exhausted.

              1.11.3.3.2.1.1.2. Pro:
              If a person is only allowed to express their true opinions at one place but nowhere else, they would argue that their freedom of speech has been limited, even though they can still express the viewpoint in the one place where they are allowed to do so.

                1.11.3.3.2.1.1.2.1. Con:
                The issue of protesting in front of abortion clinics does not contemplate limiting the location where one can "express their true opinions" one a single spot, but rather creates a single spot where one cannot "express their true opinion" because of compelling private and public interests.

          1.11.3.3.2.2. Con:
          This limitation of a right should be compared to the harm of allowing the protests, which is to totally eliminate the right to an abortion for some women.

          1.11.3.3.2.3. Con:
          These protests are not framed as mutual discussion with abortion seekers, nor do prolifers have the right to scrutinize the medical procedures and history of strangers.

        1.11.3.3.3. Con:
        Under the thesis, a pro-lifer can still make their claims and "convince" people not to obtain an abortion, they just can't do it in front of a medical facility.

      1.11.3.4. Pro:
      So long as there is no physical preventative actions or altercations occurring, freedom of demonstration/speech supersedes over problems with opposing positions.

        1.11.3.4.1. Con:
        Physical preventative actions and altercations [85] are occuring at abortion clinics.

        1.11.3.4.2. Con:
        No one should have to deal with verbal harassment [86] just for seeking medical advice and assistance.

      1.11.3.5. Con:
      Freedom of speech should not be at the expense of ones physical and mental health and bodily autonomy, especially when you do not know the circumstances around one's decision to have the abortion.

    1.11.4. Pro:
    Protesting is an individuals way of communicating their views and ideas to the government; similar to a pressure group. This is essential for the government to continue to serve the people.

      1.11.4.1. Pro:
      The government cannot shut down opinions, just because some people disagree. Shutting down protests in front of abortion clinics would create a precedent to shut down common protests against banking sector, death penalty, the oil lobby etc. as well.

        1.11.4.1.1. Con:
        The proposal is not to shut down the protest in and of itself, but to exclude protesting from specific defined areas.

      1.11.4.2. Con:
      Moving protesters to safe and legal free speech zones will prohibit protests from harming patients and staff, will protect against disrupting the peace, and will further ensure the right to patient privacy without interfering with protester's freedom of speech. This isn't a violation of free speech, but helps in upholding the law.

      1.11.4.3. Con:
      Prohibiting protest in front of abortion clinics would not eliminate the ability to protest altogether.

      1.11.4.4. Pro:
      It is illegitimate for the state to stop people from voicing their opinion to one another. Opinions are subjective and it is not the role of any authority to determine which ones are normatively acceptable. This policy limits the capacity of pro-lifers to express their opinions.

        1.11.4.4.1. Con:
        The state frequently intervenes in free speech where that speech has harmful outcomes [87], such as the criminalisation of hate speech, holocaust denial and racial abuse.

        1.11.4.4.2. Con:
        Anti choice activists are still able to express their opinions, however their right to free speech does not automatically give them a right to deliver that speech directly to a vulnerable and threatened audience.

          1.11.4.4.2.1. Con:
          Defining "vulnerable" populations or groups is inherently difficult, controversial and subjective. As such, that being an exception gives government ability to limit free speech in the name of protecting any group they can make a semi-plausible case for, regardless of any actual harm done.

            1.11.4.4.2.1.1. Con:
            Not being able to scream at people while standing right outside the clinic does not prevent the screamers from screaming nor from overall delivering their message.

    1.11.5. Con:
    There already exists court-tested practices that protect free speech while ensuring the rights of others aren't impinged due to threats and abuse. Introducing such safe zones for the protection of innocent victims from abuse and the preservation of free speech is not only exceedingly reasonable, it is morally responsible.

      1.11.5.1. Con:
      Most people who protest outside of abortion clinics do not engage in "threats or abuse."

        1.11.5.1.1. Pro:
        -> See 1.1.3.1.2.

        1.11.5.1.2. Con:
        Protests can turn violent [88] without notice. It is then very difficult to control them.

          1.11.5.1.2.1. Con:
          The source [89] is talking about protests in general, not pro-life protests in particular, and in fact uses the specific example of George Floyd-related protests. Thus, if this is an argument for banning pro-life protests it is an argument against all protests.

      1.11.5.2. Pro:
      Free speech zones [90] are an extreme, but legal, example of a such a practice.

    1.11.6. Con:
    Unlike the US, not all states have a blank protection of free speech, and many can limit free speech if somebody's security is at risk.

    1.11.7. Pro:
    The right to non-violent protest is ensured in the US Constitution under the 1st amendment [91]. While rules preventing assembly could be passed on the state or local level, it would be against the spirit of a free country.
