Discussion Name:
Should Cinco de Mayo be celebrated in the US?

1. Thesis:
Cinco de Mayo should not be celebrated in the US.

  1.1. Pro:
  The continued celebration of this holiday distorts Mexican history.

    1.1.1. Con:
    All holidays distort history. This is part of humanity's universal culture of myth-making [1] and not something inherently negative.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      Jesus was almost certainly not born on December 25, but the day is celebrated [2] as his birthday anyway.

        1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
        Christian churches acknowledge [3] this fact but still continue to celebrate the holiday on Christmas Day.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      There are now movements to accurately address problematic holidays like Columbus Day [4].

        1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
        Countries around the world are grappling with how to change outdated holiday customs [5] that no longer reflect contemporary values.

    1.1.2. Con:
    The holiday brings to prominence an event in Mexican history, even if it is misunderstood.

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      It is preferable for historical events to be forgotten than misremembered.

        1.1.2.1.1. Con:
        Misremembering events can cause them to be exploited [6] by groups today to serve their own ends.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      Cinco de Mayo [7] commemorates the defeat of the French army by the Mexican army. This does not distort history but has evolved into a celebration of it.

        1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
        The celebration [8] not only commemorates the defeat against the French, but also celebrates the Mexican-US union.

        1.1.2.2.2. Con:
        This incident does not merit celebration.

          1.1.2.2.2.1. Pro:
          Although Mexico declared a victory at the Battle of Puebla on May 5, the French went on to win the Franco-Mexican war and occupied the area for the next five years [9].

      1.1.2.3. Pro:
      Hundreds of thousands [10] of Americans attend celebrations for the event annually. At least some of them are likely to learn the true significance of the date.

        1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
        Educating the population slowly over time is better than having the holiday ignored altogether.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Most Americans that celebrate Cinco de Mayo are not interested in the history of the holiday, but simply in having a day to celebrate.

      1.1.3.1. Con:
      This is no different from other holidays like Valentines Day, Halloween, or St. Patrick's Day. People don't need to be experts in history to enjoy the festivities.

        1.1.3.1.1. Con:
        It is irrelevant whether Americans enjoy celebrating it. If it distorts the image of Mexican culture, it is wrong.

      1.1.3.2. Pro:
      Ninety percent [11] of US Americans have no idea what Cinco de Mayo is about.

        1.1.3.2.1. Pro:
        US popular culture has a bigger impact than Mexican public diplomacy on the American people. As such, images perpetuated by the US will create a more lasting impression on them.

          1.1.3.2.1.1. Pro:
          Most Americans cannot name [12] the president of Mexico, nor do they know much about its geography or history.

        1.1.3.2.2. Con:
        Educating [13] the public on the true history of Cinco de Mayo may change the way it is viewed and celebrated in the future.

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      Cinco de Mayo in the US is one of the top five drinking days of the year [14].

        1.1.3.3.1. Con:
        This is good for Mexico's economy, as Americans are more likely to consume Mexican-made alcohol [15] on that day.

        1.1.3.3.2. Con:
        US Americans consume alcohol [16] at almost every holiday. Cinco de Mayo is no different.

          1.1.3.3.2.1. Con:
          Two wrongs do not make a right. If Americans consume alcohol at every holiday, that does not justify the consumption of alcohol on Cinco De Mayo.

            1.1.3.3.2.1.1. Con:
            No country, including America, needs to justify their drinking customs in order to enjoy them.

    1.1.4. Pro:
    Mexican Independence Day [17] is overshadowed by this holiday.

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      Mexican independence is only important to Mexican-American citizens.

        1.1.4.1.1. Pro:
        Because Cinco de Mayo is often mistaken [18] for Mexican Independence Day, this shows how little Americans actually know about it.

      1.1.4.2. Con:
      Americans are under no obligation to celebrate Mexican Independence Day.

        1.1.4.2.1. Pro:
        Each country's independence day should be celebrated by that country only.

      1.1.4.3. Pro:
      Cinco de Mayo lacks a connection to a national celebration [19]. Celebrating Mexican Independence Day is more important to Mexicans.

        1.1.4.3.1. Pro:
        Cinco de Mayo is mostly celebrated in the state of Puebla [20].

  1.2. Con:
  Cinco de Mayo adds to the multicultural tapestry of the US.

    1.2.1. Con:
    Cinco de Mayo adds to the miseducation [21] of Americans about authentic Mexican culture and history, which makes the US misinformed about a portion of its own population.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      Cinco de Mayo is not recognized by Americans for what it truthfully and historically stands for, but rather a holiday of alcoholic indulgence [22], similar to St. Patrick's Day.

        1.2.1.1.1. Con:
        People in numerous countries frequently recognize holidays without understanding their historical significance.

          1.2.1.1.1.1. Pro:
          Christmas is celebrated in China as a novelty day [23] like Valentine's Day, rather than a religious holiday commemorating the birth of Jesus.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      Schools in the US can educate [24] students about the original meaning and intention of Cinco de Mayo in order to combat any misinformation.

        1.2.1.2.1. Con:
        History teachers and students are already under enough pressure to teach and learn mandated topics [25] without going into a relatively minor incident like the origins of Cinco de Mayo.

    1.2.2. Pro:
    As a melting pot [26] of cultures, it is appropriate that Americans celebrate holidays that represent the diversity of its people.

      1.2.2.1. Con:
      When immigrating to America, people should assimilate into American culture, rather than try to hold on to the cultures of their home country.

        1.2.2.1.1. Pro:
        When Americans move abroad, they are seen as rude [27] if they do not assimilate. The same standard should be held to immigrants coming to America.

        1.2.2.1.2. Con:
        America should provide the freedom for people to live as they please, so long as it does not violate any laws.

      1.2.2.2. Con:
      It is not feasible for Americans to celebrate holidays for all the cultures that live in the country.

        1.2.2.2.1. Con:
        As the largest [28] minority group from one country, Mexicans should have their culture represented in America.

      1.2.2.3. Pro:
      This can benefit all Americans, as they can be introduced to fun and new holidays [29] that they might enjoy.

        1.2.2.3.1. Con:
        This would only result in more cultural appropriation from Americans, which would eventually erase the original meaning behind these holidays.

      1.2.2.4. Con:
      Cinco de Mayo is not about the diversity of America. Therefore, regardless of whether the concept of America being a melting pot is accurate or not and if that justifies celebrating other cultures' holidays, this argument is irrelevant.

    1.2.3. Con:
    Cinco de Mayo commemorates Mexican history, but it is often portrayed as a day to celebrate [30] Mexican-American or Latino cultures more generally. This is a misrepresentation of these cultures.

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      There are some cultural similarities [31] amongst Latino communities, many of whom embrace Cinco de Mayo celebrations in the US.

        1.2.3.1.1. Con:
        Central and South America is a diverse region [32] with many unique cultural practices.

        1.2.3.1.2. Pro:
        Because Spanish is the most common language spoken, many of these communities follow the same Spanish speaking pop culture [33].

      1.2.3.2. Pro:
      America has large Hispanic populations from over a dozen [34] Latin American countries with their own diverse cultures and traditions.

        1.2.3.2.1. Con:
        There are more people of Mexican origin than of all the other Latin American countries combined [35].

    1.2.4. Pro:
    Cinco de Mayo raises awareness about Mexican culture in the US.

      1.2.4.1. Pro:
      Mexican and American people have a chance to meet, spend time together, and share experiences on Cinco de Mayo.

        1.2.4.1.1. Con:
        Mexican and American people have a chance to do this every day. A holiday revolving around Mexican kitsch and alcohol is neither needed nor helpful.

      1.2.4.2. Con:
      Mexican culture is already prevalent [36] in America. It does not need a holiday to be engaged with.

        1.2.4.2.1. Pro:
        Mexican television programs are regularly featured on television channels [37] included as part of the cable package for many American households.

      1.2.4.3. Pro:
      Americans are able to experience Mexican food and culture they may not otherwise come in contact with.

        1.2.4.3.1. Con:
        There are over 60,000 [38] Mexican restaurants in the US. Most Americans could eat Mexican food every day if they wanted to.

  1.3. Pro:
  Cinco de Mayo, as it is celebrated in the US, perpetuates negative Mexican stereotypes.

    1.3.1. Con:
    There are ways people can celebrate Cinco de Mayo without promoting negative Mexican stereotypes [39].

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      Supporting local Mexican businesses on Cinco de Mayo can provide more authentic exchanges with Mexican culture.

        1.3.1.1.1. Con:
        Other than restaurants, many Mexican-run businesses have nothing to do with Mexican culture itself and provide ordinary services [40] like any other business.

      1.3.1.2. Con:
      The average American is unlikely to do this, given the fact the stereotypes are so ingrained [41] in the American consciousness.

        1.3.1.2.1. Con:
        As people are becoming more aware [42] of the harms caused by stereotypes, fewer people are likely to engage with them.

          1.3.1.2.1.1. Con:
          Celebrating Cinco de Mayo makes it harder for people to unlearn the harmful stereotypes.

    1.3.2. Pro:
    Wearing sombreros and stick-on mustaches while drinking is a stereotype many Mexicans [43] would likely denounce for its offensiveness.

      1.3.2.1. Con:
      This article does not seem to be written a Mexican person nor does it consult Mexican people on their opinion about this issue.

        1.3.2.1.1. Pro:
        Non-Mexicans assuming they know how Mexicans feel about this issue is a form of cultural erasure [44].

      1.3.2.2. Con:
      The vast majority of Americans do not wear sombreros and stick-on mustaches for Cinco de Mayo, as multiple [45] pictures [46] prove [47].

        1.3.2.2.1. Pro:
        People have become more attuned to cultural sensitivities [48] over the years and are less likely to wear offensive costumes.

      1.3.2.3. Pro:
      Former ABC new anchor, Lara Spencer [49] apologized after wearing a sombrero and drinking a margarita for a Cinco de Mayo segment.

        1.3.2.3.1. Con:
        Spencer's Mexican-American co-host, John Quiñones, did not seem offended by the segment, saying [50] he knew she meant no harm.

    1.3.3. Pro:
    This amounts to cultural appropriation that is disrespectful to Mexican people.

      1.3.3.1. Con:
      When engaging in any multicultural activity, one has to partake in that culture. This is not appropriation but simply engagement.

        1.3.3.1.1. Pro:
        It is impossible to live in a globalized world without borrowing from other cultures. This should be celebrated, not discouraged.

          1.3.3.1.1.1. Con:
          Naturally adopting and integrating aspects of a culture is not the same as superficially imitating an uneducated image of what that culture is for one day.

        1.3.3.1.2. Con:
        The way America engages in Mexican culture does not actually address the culture itself, only America's harmful perceptions of it and its people.

      1.3.3.2. Con:
      If Mexicans and Mexican-Americans found Cinco de Mayo disrespectful, they would not go out of their way to promote [51] their Mexican businesses on the holiday.

        1.3.3.2.1. Con:
        Given the fact that Mexican-Americans earn on average a lower annual wage [52] than white Americans, they are forced to use this opportunity to make up this disparity.

        1.3.3.2.2. Con:
        They don't find Cinco de Mayo disrespectful, they have an issue with the way that non-Mexican Americans celebrate it. This does not prevent them from celebrating their own holiday and promote their businesses, especially if their targets are other Mexicans.

      1.3.3.3. Pro:
      Caring only about the enjoyment of Americans without taking into account the feelings of Mexicans is selfish and racist.

        1.3.3.3.1. Con:
        Americans cannot always care for others' feelings, putting aside their own enjoyment.

        1.3.3.3.2. Pro:
        Americans tend to think that they are more important than the others. This is simply wrong.

          1.3.3.3.2.1. Con:
          This is a generalization is not true for all US citizens.

          1.3.3.3.2.2. Con:
          The US is considered to be the most powerful country [53] in the world, thus fueling the view that they are most important.

          1.3.3.3.2.3. Pro:
          Research showed [54] that most US Americans think that they are superior to everyone else. A central feature of moral superiority, also known as “self-enhancement,” is irrational thinking.

      1.3.3.4. Pro:
      Imbibing alcohol and listening to mariachi music without understanding their cultural contexts is not a celebration, but an insult.

        1.3.3.4.1. Con:
        Alcohol and mariachi music are enjoyed [55] by many Mexicans. There is nothing inherently insulting about this.

  1.4. Pro:
  Many businesses in America use Cinco de Mayo as a way to profit off of Mexican culture.

    1.4.1. Con:
    Cinco de Mayo benefits Mexican businesses as well.

      1.4.1.1. Pro:
      Mexican-owned restaurants generally have one of their busiest days of the year [56] on Cinco de Mayo.

        1.4.1.1.1. Pro:
        Most Mexican restaurants have food and drink specials [57] on Cinco de Mayo to draw in bigger crowds.

      1.4.1.2. Pro:
      Americans' consumption of Mexican food benefits companies that import these ingredients from Mexico.

        1.4.1.2.1. Pro:
        Americans eat a lot of avocados on Cinco de Mayo. This is good for Mexico's economy 82% of America’s avocados [58] are imported from there.

          1.4.1.2.1.1. Pro:
          Americans eat 81 million pounds [59] of avocados on Cinco de Mayo.

      1.4.1.3. Con:
      Mexican businesses only benefit if they play into the stereotypes surrounding the holiday. They should not be forced to do this to make money.

        1.4.1.3.1. Con:
        Mexican businesses who offer authentic experiences and food are likely to be more popular than those who don't.

          1.4.1.3.1.1. Pro:
          Taco Bell, which does not offer authentic Mexican food, has seen interest levels decline [60] around Cinco de Mayo in some years.

    1.4.2. Pro:
    Non-Mexican companies in America exploit the holiday to make money.

      1.4.2.1. Con:
      America has a mostly free market [61], capitalist economy that allows businesses to profit off anything that consumers want, as long as it does not break the law.

        1.4.2.1.1. Pro:
        Forbidding companies from profiting off Cinco de Mayo would set a bad precedent of government intervention in the economy.

      1.4.2.2. Pro:
      In the 1950s and 1960s, American beer companies [62] capitalized off the increased recognition of Cinco de Mayo.

        1.4.2.2.1. Con:
        This benefited both Hispanic and non-Hispanic beer drinkers by raising the standards of Mexican brands to the point that Modelo, from Mexico, is now America's most popular beer [63].

    1.4.3. Con:
    Businesses in other countries profit off of American culture all the time. There is nothing inherently wrong about this.

      1.4.3.1. Pro:
      There are countless American-themed bars and restaurants [64] around the world that play into American stereotypes.

        1.4.3.1.1. Pro:
        Many American-themed restaurants portray outdated stereotypes of the "wild west [65]" involving cowboys and Indians.

      1.4.3.2. Pro:
      American cultural products have been appropriated by groups around the world in ways that benefit these borrowing countries.

        1.4.3.2.1. Pro:
        Baseball [66], basketball [67], jazz [68], and hip hop [69] are just a few American cultural products that have become transmuted through other cultures, to the benefit of both the cultures and the products themselves.

        1.4.3.2.2. Con:
        America has actively sought to export its culture in order to reap the monetary and soft power [70] benefits.

      1.4.3.3. Con:
      American culture is the most dominant [71] in the world. Thus, it is okay for others to profit from it, but not okay for America to profit off others.

        1.4.3.3.1. Con:
        While American culture may be the most prevalent around the world, it is never more dominant than the home culture of any specific country.

          1.4.3.3.1.1. Pro:
          For example, while Japan may be somewhat 'Westernized', American culture is not more dominant in Japan than Japanese culture [72].

  1.5. Con:
  Cinco de Mayo allows Mexican-Americans to celebrate their culture.

    1.5.1. Pro:
    The celebration of this festival in the US proves that Mexican culture is valued. This allows Mexican communities to feel more comfortable.

      1.5.1.1. Pro:
      Starting with the Trump administration, anti-immigrant sentiment was on the rise [73]. It is thus more important than ever for Mexican communities to not feel alienated.

        1.5.1.1.1. Con:
        It would be better to make substantive changes [74] to immigration laws in order to make Mexican communities feel more welcome, not make token gestures like celebrating Cinco de Mayo.

      1.5.1.2. Con:
      There is no evidence to suggest celebrating Cinco de Mayo makes Mexican communities feel more comfortable.

        1.5.1.2.1. Pro:
        It is more likely that Mexican-American communities, like other minority communities, are concerned with more important issues [75] like job security and education rather than what Americans are doing on Cinco de Mayo.

    1.5.2. Con:
    Mexican-Americans can celebrate their culture any day of the year, especially on actual Mexican holidays.

      1.5.2.1. Pro:
      Other ethnic minorities, such as Korean-Americans, celebrate [76] their culture despite not having a widely recognized Korean holiday that's celebrated in the US.

        1.5.2.1.1. Con:
        There are far fewer Korean-Americans [77] than Mexican-Americans [78]. Because of their outsized influence on American culture, Mexican holidays should be given priority.

      1.5.2.2. Con:
      Without nationally recognized public holidays, Mexican-Americans may not have the time to celebrate their culture.

        1.5.2.2.1. Pro:
        Many Mexican-Americans work [79] long hours and hold multiple jobs. They might not have time to hold celebrations unless there are government supported holidays.

        1.5.2.2.2. Con:
        Cinco de Mayo is not a public holiday in the US, but it is still widely celebrated.

    1.5.3. Con:
    Cinco de Mayo traditions in America do not faithfully represent the full array of Mexican culture, but only a bastardized American version of it.

      1.5.3.1. Con:
      Many of the traditions that Americans celebrate on Cinco de Mayo have their roots in Mexican culture, even if they have changed.

        1.5.3.1.1. Pro:
        Pinatas [80] have been a part of Mexican celebrations since the Aztec Empire in the 14th century.

      1.5.3.2. Pro:
      Each of Mexico's regions has unique and diverse cultures [81], which Cinco de Mayo ignores.

        1.5.3.2.1. Con:
        This is true for any holiday associated with a country. July 4th [82], for example, does not celebrate the diversity of American regions, but the nation as a whole.

        1.5.3.2.2. Pro:
        Mexican regions have specific food [83] and dialects [84] that makes each area unique.

    1.5.4. Pro:
    Cinco de Mayo is a celebration originating from Mexican-Americans [85].

      1.5.4.1. Pro:
      The holiday’s popularity began when news of the Mexican victory in Puebla reached California-based Latinos, many of whom were supportive [86] of Mexico’s victory.

        1.5.4.1.1. Con:
        Cinco de Mayo as it is known today only began [87] in the 1970s and 1980s when US beer companies began targeting Spanish speaking populations.

      1.5.4.2. Con:
      It is unlikely that those original proponents of Cinco de Mayo would have foreseen the kind of holiday that it has evolved into.

        1.5.4.2.1. Con:
        It is not necessary to hold true to the original vision of a holiday, as all holidays evolve [88] to fit with the times.

      1.5.4.3. Pro:
      To this day, cities and towns with large Mexican-American populations [89] usually hold parades, put on shows with mariachi music and folk dances, and enjoy traditional food.

        1.5.4.3.1. Pro:
        Most large American cities will host Cinco de Mayo themed festivals [90] in which the local Latino community takes part.

        1.5.4.3.2. Con:
        This doesn't mean that the Mexican-American population supports these activities, but only that this is what the American population has come to expect.
