Discussion Name:
Should US internet platforms be able to remove or demote speech by elected public figures?

1. Thesis:
Internet platforms in the US should not prohibit or demote any speech by elected officials.

  1.1. Con:
  The act of being elected does not afford an individual undue privileges - if they break the terms of service of an internet platform, that platform has a right to remove that individual.

    1.1.1. Con:
    It is important that elected individuals are allowed speak, given that they represent the interests of a constituency of people. They should be above the terms of service of a platform as they have been given a mandate to speak.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      We cannot silence politicians for being disruptive, as many historical advancements [1] have been brought as a result of unpopular or unexpected action. Social media provides an opportunity for these politicians to communicate innovative ideas.

        1.1.1.1.1. Con:
        While innovation and political disruption is important, there is nothing to be gained from the content that tends to be flagged or removed. Hate speech [2], for example, can do harm.

          1.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
          Hate speech is anti-democratic. To protect tolerance and equality, internet platforms must always remove it.

            1.1.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
            The spread of hate speech on social media has contributed to exacerbating violence against minorities, and in some cases, these sites have been a key tool [3] for the perpetrators.

            1.1.1.1.1.1.2. Con:
            It is undemocratic to selectively grant free speech rights. If we do not stand for the right to say even the vilest of opinions than we stand to risk losing the right to free speech totally [4].

              1.1.1.1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
              The American Civil Liberties Union has fought for the rights of free speech [5] for hate groups like the Ku Klux Klan and the American Nazi party on this basis.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      The rules of internet platforms are intended to allow an even and fair environment [6] for people to speak. If politicians don't consent to be civil, they lose the right to consequence-free speech.

        1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
        Some politicians use internet platforms to shatter public opinion for their own political and personal gain [7]. They create an unfair environment and abuse their position of power, so internet platforms should not allow this kind of speech.

      1.1.1.3. Con:
      One's views can be controversial without being misleading or violent. Elected officials in congress are beholden to rules of interaction in Congress [8]. Therefore, they can be held to the same standards in public life.

    1.1.2. Con:
    While internet platforms have the right to remove elected officials, they should choose not to do so.

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      Elected public figures can be dangerous. Internet platforms have a responsibility to temper that danger.

        1.1.2.1.1. Con:
        Internet platforms are ill-suited to solve this problem, as identifying which posts should rightfully be removed or demoted brings great technical, legal, and moral challenges.

          1.1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
          Much of the content that is subjectable to be removed is not clearly illegal. There are many grey areas [9] that make this removal difficult.

            1.1.2.1.1.1.1. Pro:
            The UK Government abandoned plans to define extremism in law, as it proved too difficult [10], and would have criminalised legitimate activities.

            1.1.2.1.1.1.2. Con:
            While there are some grey areas, this alone does not justify giving all speech carte blanche on social media. There is some that is clearly illegal, [11] such as terrorist propaganda.

          1.1.2.1.1.2. Pro:
          There are great technical [12] challenges when deeming which speech is removable or demotable. Some internet platforms are using artificial intelligence to solve this problem, but it is not yet an effective solution.

            1.1.2.1.1.2.1. Pro:
            The large volume [13] of content makes it impossible to only use moderators to evaluate all of the content, so companies need to use less trustworthy tools such as artificial intelligence.

            1.1.2.1.1.2.2. Con:
            Until artificial intelligence is an effective solution, social media sites have hired large amounts of content moderators [14].

              1.1.2.1.1.2.2.1. Con:
              Content moderators have reported long term mental health issues [15], with many suffering from secondary traumatic stress.

                1.1.2.1.1.2.2.1.1. Con:
                The content of politicians and elected officials is likely not to appear on the desk of a regular content moderator, but rather it would fall to top executives and lawyers [16] as there are broader political implications.

              1.1.2.1.1.2.2.2. Con:
              Content moderators at TikTok [17] have been told to bury political content, ensuring it reaches as few people as possible.

          1.1.2.1.1.3. Con:
          Most social media platforms have the capital [18], tech [19] and potential to overcome these issues.

            1.1.2.1.1.3.1. Con:
            Facebook has recently been incapable of handling the use of their site as a platform for hate and violence speech against the Rohingya [20] in Myanmar.

              1.1.2.1.1.3.1.1. Con:
              Facebook had full capacity to deal with the Rohingya hate speech crisis, but they chose [21] not to allocate sufficient resources to solve the issue.

            1.1.2.1.1.3.2. Con:
            Tech giants, like Google [22], are still struggling to build effective technology to flag inappropriate content. Even highly powerful companies are not capable of solving this problem.

            1.1.2.1.1.3.3. Pro:
            Facebook has implemented the use of an algorithm [23] that identifies and removes hate speech.

              1.1.2.1.1.3.3.1. Con:
              Algorithms reflect the nature of the people who code them and can be implicitly biased [24].

                1.1.2.1.1.3.3.1.1. Pro:
                Some of the leading AI models for detecting and reporting online hate speech have been found to be biased [25], and one and a half times more likely to flag tweets written by African Americans as "offensive."

                1.1.2.1.1.3.3.1.2. Con:
                The more artificial intelligence is used, the less biased it becomes. Removing bias from AI is easier [26] than removing bias from people.

                1.1.2.1.1.3.3.1.3. Pro:
                Algorithms and artificial intelligence learn from the training data inputted by humans. This includes biased historical data which can prejudice minorities [27] and their content on social media.

              1.1.2.1.1.3.3.2. Pro:
              Facebook's algorithm detected almost 90 percent of the hate speech it removed in the first quarter of 2020 [28], before users flagged it

            1.1.2.1.1.3.4. Con:
            Repeat harassers and trolls have proven extremely resilient [29] at finding ways around changes in site policies and bans.

            1.1.2.1.1.3.5. Pro:
            Social media have shown their ability to censor effectively in the past. Pinterest [30] reduced people's ability to access misinformation about vaccines, limiting information available on the website about vaccines to credible sources.

            1.1.2.1.1.3.6. Pro:
            Twitter and Google are taking measures to prevent the use of their websites to tamper with the 2020 US Presidential Election [31]. Being aware of how their platforms were used to influence the election in 2016, they are now taking steps to prevent this misuse.

        1.1.2.1.2. Con:
        Internet platforms are not impartial, so they cannot be trusted to evaluate this danger nor they should be given the legal authority to censor.

          1.1.2.1.2.1. Pro:
          Many people in the US believe that social media sites censor certain political views [32] that they find objectionable.

        1.1.2.1.3. Pro:
        Politicians who espouse hateful and violent rhetoric have been found to enable or inflame [33] those who are predisposed to commit violent acts.

          1.1.2.1.3.1. Pro:
          Many far-right politicians use social media to incite violence [34] against minorities and political opponents.

            1.1.2.1.3.1.1. Pro:
            In Spain, far-right parties have used [35] social media to create fear of immigrants.

              1.1.2.1.3.1.1.1. Con:
              Fear or immigrants can be stoked up with or without social media. UKIP in the UK put up billboards [36] inciting racial hatred.

            1.1.2.1.3.1.2. Pro:
            Left-wing politicians also use social media to create division [37] between people of different socio-economic backgrounds.

            1.1.2.1.3.1.3. Con:
            Social media is also being used by right-wing politicians [38] to criticize and expose this undemocratic behavior.

            1.1.2.1.3.1.4. Pro:
            In the wake of the George Floyd protests in Minneapolis, Donald Trump seemed to encourage the shooting of political protestors [39] on social media.

              1.1.2.1.3.1.4.1. Pro:
              In response to this, Twitter hid Trump's tweet [40], stating the tweet, "violated the Twitter Rules about glorifying violence," while allowing it to still be viewed if a user wished to see it.

              1.1.2.1.3.1.4.2. Pro:
              The particular phrasing Trump used, "When the looting starts, the shooting starts." has a long history [41] of being used to incite violence against African-American communities.

            1.1.2.1.3.1.5. Con:
            Politicians don't use social media alone to incite violence against their political opponents and minority groups.

              1.1.2.1.3.1.5.1. Pro:
              Republican Senator Tom Cotton, wrote an Op-Ed in the New York Times, calling for the military to use force against protestors [42] in the wake of the George Floyd murder in the U.S.

                1.1.2.1.3.1.5.1.1. Con:
                The publishing of this Op-Ed was the result of a failed editorial process [43] that saw the resignation of the editor of the New York Times editorial page.

              1.1.2.1.3.1.5.2. Con:
              Social media, in particular, has the potential for incendiary and racist speech to reach and incite a larger audience due to the ease of access both politicians and their audiences have to the content.

              1.1.2.1.3.1.5.3. Con:
              Other forms of violent speech, like public statements, will gain greater traction on social media. Videos of offensive speeches therefore should also be removed or demoted.

          1.1.2.1.3.2. Pro:
          Online voter suppression [44] can occur, which discourages people from voting, thus entrenching those hateful politicians. When less people are involved in politics and voting, democracy is inherently harmed.

          1.1.2.1.3.3. Con:
          Politicians alone are usually unable to inspire violence. It is when violent rhetoric is repeated by media and other prominent voices that incitement happens [45]. Simply censoring politicians will therefore have little effect.

            1.1.2.1.3.3.1. Con:
            Censoring violent politicians is necessary. Politicians who are excellent public speakers have been capable of radicalizing a nation through hateful and violent rhetoric without the existence of social media. Adolf Hitler [46] exemplifies this case.

        1.1.2.1.4. Pro:
        Some politicians (and their supporting organizations) are known to spread false information to manipulate people into voting for them. Internet platforms should not allow this deceit to take place on their sites.

          1.1.2.1.4.1. Pro:
          There have been multiple [47] instances [48] of U.S. politicians and pundits sharing doctored content on social media sites of their political rivals.

          1.1.2.1.4.2. Pro:
          Facebook banned [49] a pro-Trump political action committee from posting ads on their site after they repeatedly posted false information.

        1.1.2.1.5. Con:
        People believe fake news due to "information storms" [50], where many different sources all say the same thing. Removing or demoting politician's speech will not stop these other posters.

          1.1.2.1.5.1. Con:
          While removing prominent officials posts won't stop the spread of misinformation, flagging them at least attaches a warning to all those who view them, which does not occur with most posters.

          1.1.2.1.5.2. Con:
          When "info-storms" [51] occur, people are inundated with information from a variety of different sources, and do not know what to believe. As a result, politicians are often looked to for guidance and truth, as they are in a position of authority. Therefore demoting their content would have an effect on the spread of misinformation.

            1.1.2.1.5.2.1. Pro:
            Fake news and misinformation spread most rapidly [52] when it is targeted at a small number of influential people with large followings.

        1.1.2.1.6. Pro:
        Presidents like Donald Trump [53] or Jair Bolsonaro [54] actively downplay the role of scientific findings and experts. The dismissal of science in today's society leads to environmental, democratic, and health issues.

          1.1.2.1.6.1. Pro:
          Donald Trump's denial of scientific evidence and advice is believed to have caused a delay in the US' response to the Coronavirus pandemic and the Climate Crisis.

          1.1.2.1.6.2. Con:
          Questioning science does not necessarily make elected public figures dangerous. In some cases, questioning science is reasonable and expected.

            1.1.2.1.6.2.1. Pro:
            Some scientific findings concerning the Coronavirus have been contradictory and misleading [55]. In these cases, it is logical to question science.

              1.1.2.1.6.2.1.1. Con:
              When scientific knowledge changes rapidly, as was the case with COVID-19, shifts in understanding are inevitable [56]. It is incumbent for politicians to understand and communicate that, rather than use it as a political weapon.

              1.1.2.1.6.2.1.2. Pro:
              It is legitimate [57] for politicians to question different scientific views in the midst of COVID-19, as we are constantly learning more. This engagement can help stimulate academic debate.

            1.1.2.1.6.2.2. Con:
            As politicians are (usually) not scientists themselves, they are not in a position to criticize the findings of the scientific community. This is why they use scientific advisors [58].

        1.1.2.1.7. Con:
        There are many other ways that politicians can make dangerous speech beyond social media. Internet platforms taking action would have little effect.

          1.1.2.1.7.1. Pro:
          In June, Senator Tom Cotton wrote an op-ed [59] for the New York Times calling for Trump to violently crackdown on Black Lives Matters protests.

          1.1.2.1.7.2. Pro:
          At least on social media, the dangerous posts of politicians do not receive the clout or prestige [60] that comes with publishing their opinions in major newspapers.

            1.1.2.1.7.2.1. Con:
            Major news outlets often repost or quote the tweets [61] and other social media content from politicians, thus affording this content the prestige of traditional media.

            1.1.2.1.7.2.2. Con:
            Americans do not have particularly high trust [62] in major media outlets. 2019 saw the number of people who trust mass media decline to 41 percent.

          1.1.2.1.7.3. Con:
          Publishing in prestigious magazines or newspapers can consolidate a clearer opposition to bad views, whereas tweets are seen as less consequential, and less worth news coverage [63].

            1.1.2.1.7.3.1. Con:
            Donald Trump has made multiple major announcements [64] over Twitter, and so his tweets have significant consequences.

          1.1.2.1.7.4. Con:
          There are over 3.6 billion [65] people on social media. Limiting politicians' ability to espouse hate on these platforms would curtail their audience hugely.

          1.1.2.1.7.5. Con:
          Just because there are other ways politicians can create dangerous speech does not mean that we should not try and temper that danger in any arena we can.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      Elected officials must be treated differently than regular users. Their speech not only is inherently newsworthy, [66] but also has broad implications for their constituents.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        Representative democracy [67] is based on the notion that politicians should represent and embody the perspectives of average people. Therefore, politicians should be subject to the same rules as others.

        1.1.2.2.2. Pro:
        Transparency is vital to democracy [68]. Constituents must know what their representatives views are especially if they are controversial.

          1.1.2.2.2.1. Pro:
          Elected official's tweets are part of the public record [69].

            1.1.2.2.2.1.1. Con:
            Politicians' speeches are also part of the public record. Yet, there is no obligation for media to prominently show all of their speeches.

              1.1.2.2.2.1.1.1. Con:
              The prominence of a post on social media is only determined by how many individuals interact with it, not the internet platform.

                1.1.2.2.2.1.1.1.1. Con:
                Since the popularity of posts is determined by the quantity of user interactions, there's an incentive for politicians to purposefully write shocking things to maintain news coverage [70].

                1.1.2.2.2.1.1.1.2. Con:
                Social media use algorithms [71] that determine how likely a user is to interact with a post, not how many other users have interacted with it.

              1.1.2.2.2.1.1.2. Con:
              Many TV networks directly broadcast Donald Trump's campaign rallies and daily briefings, largely unfiltered during prime-time [72].

                1.1.2.2.2.1.1.2.1. Con:
                Many cable news networks have changed their standards [73], limiting the unfiltered coverage of Trump campaign and press events.

                1.1.2.2.2.1.1.2.2. Pro:
                Just because TV networks decide to show political speeches does not been they are obliged to.

                  1.1.2.2.2.1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
                  TV networks will mostly decide to air things based on ratings [74].

              1.1.2.2.2.1.1.3. Pro:
              Social media sites are content platforms [75], which offer a space where users are able to post content generated by them. It is not the site's purpose, nor their obligation, to publish any type of content.

                1.1.2.2.2.1.1.3.1. Pro:
                Social media sites are, therefore, not meant to be keepers of the public record. They must balance their obligations to the users with the desires of politicians to spread their messaging.

            1.1.2.2.2.1.2. Pro:
            Under the 1978 Presidential Record act [76], all electronic communications of the President are considered public property.

            1.1.2.2.2.1.3. Pro:
            The public record must be accessible. If social media platforms remove content, then that part of the public record could be lost.

              1.1.2.2.2.1.3.1. Pro:
              The National Archives and Records Administration determined that deleted posts by President Trump were still required to be archived [77] under the Records Act.

      1.1.2.3. Pro:
      The public should be able to view the behavior of their elected officials on social media and judge it for themselves.

        1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
        The best thing one can do [78] if they disagree with a politician's behavior online is continue to let the public see their comments and actions.

          1.1.2.3.1.1. Pro:
          As a response to a controversial Trump tweet, the staunchly right-wing Washington Examiner editorial board denounced Trump [79] and claimed the tweet may be looked upon as, "the instant when things began to unravel."

          1.1.2.3.1.2. Con:
          Many people are victimised by seeing politician's content.

            1.1.2.3.1.2.1. Pro:
            Statements made by Donald Trump have led to immigrants feeling unsafe [80].

            1.1.2.3.1.2.2. Pro:
            Donald Trump's comment about transgender people have legitimised employment discrimination and contributed to a climate where transgender people feel unsafe [81].

        1.1.2.3.2. Con:
        The public can judge politicians for themselves when they are being properly informed, not in situations where lies and facts are intermingled, which is the case of politicians like Donald Trump [82]. In these cases, people's judgement will be compromised.

      1.1.2.4. Pro:
      Removing posts by elected officials may foster backlash from their supporters.

        1.1.2.4.1. Pro:
        Supporters of politicians who have been censored may leave mainstream social media sites, for smaller ones, like Parler [83]. Many of these websites lack even the most basic levels of censorship and so hateful ideas can grow more easily there.

          1.1.2.4.1.1. Pro:
          The popular alternative internet forum 4chan has been one of the main breeding grounds for conspiracies like QAnon [84], Pizzagate [85], and the Sandy Hook shooting hoax [86].

          1.1.2.4.1.2. Con:
          Parler has a much smaller number of users [87] than it's mainstream competitors, significantly limiting the number of people users can reach and limiting the growth of hate speech and conspiracy theories.

            1.1.2.4.1.2.1. Con:
            Even if there are less users, those on smaller sites become radicalised more easily, and can do great harm to others. Three mass shootings [88] have been linked to 8chan.

            1.1.2.4.1.2.2. Pro:
            If dangerous views are pushed off of mainstream social media, less people will see and be victimised by hateful content.

          1.1.2.4.1.3. Con:
          Alternative platforms like Parler, 8chan, and Gab operate on extremely thin margins [89] and have difficulty remaining operational for very long.

            1.1.2.4.1.3.1. Pro:
            8chan has been consistently dropped [90] by their site's service providers [91].

            1.1.2.4.1.3.2. Con:
            Despite being started on 4Chan and moving to 8Chan and now 8kun [92], QAnon has reached and convinced millions in the US.

          1.1.2.4.1.4. Pro:
          8chan, another popular alternative social media platform, became the go-to website for mass-shooters [93], including the perpetrators of the El Paso, Christchurch, and Poway shootings.

          1.1.2.4.1.5. Pro:
          Users of Stormfront, a neo-nazi alternative platform, have been connected to nearly 100 different murders [94].

        1.1.2.4.2. Pro:
        Trust in social media companies is very low [95], so supporters are more likely to side with their politicians.

          1.1.2.4.2.1. Pro:
          Trust in media organizations is also low [96], so attempts to fact-check arguments will be ineffective.

            1.1.2.4.2.1.1. Con:
            Television news is still, by far, the most trusted source [97] of news.

        1.1.2.4.3. Pro:
        For example, when Twitter decided to fact-check one of Donald Trump's tweets, the ensuing backlash [98] led to far more people seeing it.

          1.1.2.4.3.1. Con:
          Even if more people saw it, they were doing so with the warning [99] that the tweet was potentially untrue.

        1.1.2.4.4. Con:
        Over time, the novelty of banning posts will wear off. There was uproar when Alex Jones was banned from social media, but now his visibility is far smaller [100].

          1.1.2.4.4.1. Con:
          Alex Jones' main platforms were social media, and so he had little ability to reach viewers once he was banned. Politicians still have a platform by virtue of their office, through mainstream media.

          1.1.2.4.4.2. Con:
          The short term surge in traffic to the content of users who have been censored and banned, as was seen with Alex Jones' infowars site [101], can be harmful in and of itself, as it exposes people to radical content that they may not have otherwise seen.

          1.1.2.4.4.3. Pro:
          The net effect of removing harmful users from social media is that their reach is less, and so fewer people are exposed to their views.

        1.1.2.4.5. Con:
        Removing or labelling bad posts makes non-supporters less likely to believe their claims [102], even if supporters remain convinced.

          1.1.2.4.5.1. Pro:
          Many who are uncertain what to believe are happy to defer to authority [103] over one individual's opinion.

          1.1.2.4.5.2. Con:
          This may make more people disillusioned, as they trust neither the internet platform nor the politician, making them think neither side cares for them [104].

            1.1.2.4.5.2.1. Con:
            We are living in the "Digital Information Age" [105], where the most important skills are information analysis and critical thinking. Disillusionment with one's preferred sources of information is a consequence of critical thinking, thus, this is a positive sign.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Facebook's Terms and Conditions [106] (Pt. 2) clearly state that users are not allowed to use their platform to post unlawful, misleading, discriminatory, or fraudulent content. If a user, including a politician, does so, Facebook is entitled to remove them.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      Facebook warns any user against spreading false information, including businesses [107].

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      For media sites, labelling misleading information poses important challenges, and in many cases, they are doing more harm than good.

        1.1.3.2.1. Pro:
        Some users have started to "cheat" [108] Twitter's automated labelling system to test it out. If users see that this system does not work properly, they will distrust these labels.

        1.1.3.2.2. Pro:
        The majority of the content labeled as misleading continues to be visible to users [109] of these sites. Keeping information that is known to be false online will misinform some readers.

        1.1.3.2.3. Con:
        Google [110] has successfully protected many of its users by removing search ads containing misleading information. These ads were charging large fees to users who were searching for voting information (or voter registration) or harvested their personal data.

        1.1.3.2.4. Con:
        Social media can make distinctions between 'black' and 'grey' [111] lies, and only enforce the most egregious cases.

    1.1.4. Pro:
    Elected officials are not required to use social media. By choosing to sign up and post on these private sites, they also agree to follow the terms and conditions set forth by the companies.

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      Many elected officials do not have accounts on social media, and choose their own official sites to communicate with the public. Angela Merkel [112], Chancellor of Germany, does not own a Twitter account.

        1.1.4.1.1. Pro:
        Even though most Spanish politicians [113] have official Twitter accounts, the Spanish government uses the Official State Bulletin [114] (in Spanish, BOE) to notify the public on new resolutions and official matters.

        1.1.4.1.2. Con:
        Elected officials who are not active on social media are an exception for most developed countries. Most politicians have Twitter accounts because it allows them to connect with millions of potential voters [115].

          1.1.4.1.2.1. Pro:
          Most politicians are choosing to communicate through social media, as it allows them to connect with more people. This is because the website traffic of social media platforms [116] is far greater than the traffic of official government websites [117].

      1.1.4.2. Con:
      Politicians are not required to use social media, but refusing to do so will diminish their visibility, which would put them in a position of disadvantage.

        1.1.4.2.1. Pro:
        Social media is an effective way to connect with young people [118], who would potentially vote for them. If they refuse to participate on social media, they have access to less voters.

          1.1.4.2.1.1. Pro:
          Young voters are often hesitant to vote [119]. Social media is a powerful way to increase enfranchisement, which is good for democracy.

        1.1.4.2.2. Con:
        Many politicians use social media as a means of diffusing official communications, which are proofread by their team. They can keep their visibility without using social media to express the whims of the politician.

        1.1.4.2.3. Pro:
        79 percent of Americans have some form of social media [120] and almost a quarter of all Americans use Twitter alone. [121]

          1.1.4.2.3.1. Con:
          Many of the users of twitter are extremely inactive [122] so the reach of political tweets is actually much lower than it may seem. Roughly 10 percent of users are responsible for 80 percent of all tweets.

    1.1.5. Pro:
    The spread of fake news and misleading information is a serious problem that is growing amongst internet platforms [123]. Elected public figures are a part of this problem, so they should not have immunity.

      1.1.5.1. Con:
      Fake news is not a new phenomenon nor is it unique to social media.

        1.1.5.1.1. Pro:
        In the 1950s, American politicians, led by Joseph McCarthy, dramatically exaggerated [124] the threat of domestic Communists, justifying mass firings and arrests.

        1.1.5.1.2. Pro:
        If anything, with social media, individual politicians' claims are more visible [125] and thus more likely to be criticized.

          1.1.5.1.2.1. Con:
          It can be difficult to criticise politicians on social media as their followers may attack [126] you.

          1.1.5.1.2.2. Pro:
          It is much easier to criticise a politician through social media than through any other forum.

        1.1.5.1.3. Con:
        Fake news and lying on the media is not new, but social media is having a magnifying effect [127] on this phenomenon, so they have a responsibility to temper this effect.

        1.1.5.1.4. Con:
        Social media creates specific incentives [128], largely through advertising revenue, to create fake news.

      1.1.5.2. Pro:
      Politicians have greater visibility [129] on internet platforms than individual users do. If they spread manipulative information on internet platforms, the impact is much higher, so they must be held accountable.

  1.2. Con:
  Private companies have the right to remove or ban any users for any reason.

    1.2.2. Pro:

    1.5.4. Pro:
    Many people are now using social media as a news source [270]. The general populace should be able to stay informed about what their elected figures (or not elected) say.

      1.5.4.1. Con:
      If people are using social media for news, there must be editorial standards [271].

        1.5.4.1.1. Con:
        The burden to create and enforce editorial standards should be on the institutions and officials who write content, not on internet platforms.

      1.5.4.2. Con:
      People can hear what their elected officials believe through things like interviews, where there can be pushback whenever they say falsehoods.

        1.5.4.2.1. Con:
        There can be pushback to tweets as well. Frequently, Trump's tweets [272] are subject of media discussion and clarification.

        1.5.4.2.2. Pro:
        Some journalists interview politicians from a critical perspective [273], by asking for clarifications or even by questioning responses. This conversational tone encourages critical thinking from readers or viewers, so interviews are a preferable source.

      1.5.4.3. Pro:
      Platforms like Twitter allow individuals to read official comments from public figures, without going through the interpretation of an intermediary. Censoring these comments would mean tampering with this direct communication.

        1.5.4.3.1. Pro:
        News sources often form their own interpretation [274] of what elected public figures have said. Allowing internet users to read official comments allows them to make their own interpretations about what was being said.

    1.5.5. Con:
    Winning the election does not mean that citizens will not be critical. Internet platforms would not be challenging the public consensus, as there is none.

      1.5.5.1. Con:
      There is a consensus among supporters. When one is elected it means a critical mass of individuals feel themselves represented by the ideas and actions of a politician.

        1.5.5.1.1. Pro:
        Removing that representative's speech, therefore, is a means of invalidating their constituents' opinions and challenging their consensus.

      1.5.5.2. Con:
      It is precisely the responsibility of the citizens to be critical [275]. To do so, they must be able to evaluate all the thoughts and views of their representative, regardless how controversial or dangerous.

      1.5.5.3. Pro:
      There is no public consensus over the Trump Administration's handling of the COVID19 crisis. He has received widespread criticism [276], even though he has defended the contrary view.

        1.5.5.3.1. Con:
        It is pretty clear that the US has mishandled the COVID-19 crisis [277]. Continuing to deny this truth will only harm people.

      1.5.5.4. Pro:
      Many politicians are distrusted by the public. Some elected officials are known to lie or make up false facts [278] to manipulate public opinion.

  1.6. Pro:
  The removal or flagging of elected officials' speech constitutes a First Amendment violation.

    1.6.1. Con:
    Slander, libel and some other forms of false speech are not protected by the constitution [279].

      1.6.1.1. Con:
      Politics is often really just about the personalities of the figures [280]. Therefore, criticizing an opponent often means criticizing their personality, making it difficult to enforce rules of slander and libel.

        1.6.1.1.1. Pro:
        The bar to prove libel in the US is incredibly high, as was seen with Stormy Daniels' unsuccessful attempt to sue Donald Trump. [281]

          1.6.1.1.1.1. Pro:
          Tesla CEO Elon Musk also won a defamation [282] case which reaffirmed that the bar to prove libel or defamation on social media is incredibly high.

      1.6.1.2. Pro:
      Politicians have regularly used social media to slander people, such as Donald Trump's attacks of Joe Scarborough [283]. These falsehoods should not receive constitutional protection.

      1.6.1.3. Pro:
      Inciting violence [284] is also not protected by the First Amendment.

        1.6.1.3.1. Pro:
        Donald Trump has had tweets flagged as 'glorifying violence [285]', which would likely not be covered by the First Amendment.

    1.6.2. Pro:
    The First Amendment is important as it is a prerequisite to all other rights [286]. It gives people an ability to publicly express the ways the wish to exercise their authorities.

      1.6.2.1. Pro:
      This is especially important in politics, where many feel like politicians and movements represent more than just interests, but entire ways of life [287].

      1.6.2.2. Con:
      If free speech was indeed a necessary prerequisite to access other rights, then it could never be limited in a functional society. Even in the US, there are multiple exceptions [288] to the First Amendment, such as incitement, or distributing obscene material.

        1.6.2.2.1. Pro:
        Many countries do not have absolute free speech but are still free. New Zealand is top of the Human Freedom Index [289], but has limits [290] of free speech. These include offensive speech, and laws aiming to deter harm through digital means.

        1.6.2.2.2. Con:
        Without free speech, one does not have the freedom to express their desires, and cannot state their intentions to access other rights. Therefore, to have access to other rights, one needs freedom of speech.

      1.6.2.3. Con:
      Even if the bounds of what is acceptable have been curtailed, people still have a range of choices they can make.

    1.6.3. Con:
    In the case of flagging content, freedom of speech does not equal freedom from consequences. [291] The content is still visible, and so the First Amendment is not violated.

      1.6.3.1. Pro:
      Just because a politician's content is not amplified [292] does not mean their First Amendment would be violated.

        1.6.3.1.1. Pro:
        One's First Amendment right does not confer a duty on others to listen.

          1.6.3.1.1.1. Pro:
          There are strong arguments for the US Courts to recognise a First Amendment right against compelled listening [293].

    1.6.4. Con:
    Private organizations and individuals, under their First Amendment rights, are protected from Compulsory Speech [294] and as a result they cannot be forced to publish or host things they do not want on their platforms oyez.org [295]

      1.6.4.1. Pro:
      The Supreme Court has established the principle that the First Amendment prohibits the government telling people what to say. [296]

      1.6.4.2. Con:
      There are certain instances where compulsory speech is supported by law, such as requiring a cable system to carry local stations [297], and it is unclear what the position is regarding social media companies.

    1.6.5. Pro:
    The Supreme Court [298] has recognized the vital role of social media sites as a venue for citizens to express free speech rights.

      1.6.5.1. Con:
      While the Supreme Court has recognized the importance of social media sites, they have also confirmed that First Amendment protections do not extend to social media sites. [299]

    1.6.6. Con:
    First Amendment constraints do not apply to private entities such as internet platforms, only state institutions [300].

      1.6.6.1. Pro:
      Social media sites have repeatedly won lawsuits [301] from banned users who claim that their First Amendment rights had been violated by their bans.

      1.6.6.2. Con:
      First Amendment violations can also occur if a platform can be considered a "state actor" [302] by providing a forum for government or public speech.

        1.6.6.2.1. Con:
        In a 5-4 decision by the Supreme Court, the court established that providing a forum for speech was not enough [303] to constitute a non-profit operating a public-access channel, as a state actor and mandate free speech rights.

          1.6.6.2.1.1. Con:
          The minority's dissenting opinion [304] from the case argued that by agreeing to run the public access channels, the nonprofit had, "stepped into the City’s shoes and thus [qualified] as a state actor, subject to the First Amendment like any other.”

            1.6.6.2.1.1.1. Pro:
            A change in the Supreme Court composition could see this dissenting opinion become the majority opinion in the future.

          1.6.6.2.1.2. Con:
          There was no mention of the internet or social media [305] platforms in any of the legal documents of the case or in questioning posed to the Supreme Court.

            1.6.6.2.1.2.1. Con:
            The wording of the original decision reached by the 2nd Circuit Court bases its finding of the non-profit as a state actor on whether it operated as a public forum [306], which has direct implications [307] for internet platform companies

          1.6.6.2.1.3. Pro:
          Social media does not do more than provide a forum for speech, and so even though the judgement did not explicitly mention social media, it can be reasoned that social media platforms would not come under the First Amendment.

        1.6.6.2.2. Pro:
        Social media platforms are a "public forum," [308] which can entitle users to First Amendment rights under the U.S. Consitution.

          1.6.6.2.2.1. Pro:
          Several prominent U.S. politicians have lost legal battles [309] for blocking users from following them as their tweets are considered a public forum.

            1.6.6.2.2.1.1. Pro:
            Congresswoman Alexandria Ocasio Cortez had to settle a lawsuit and unblock a critic [310] after she was sued for violating their First Amendment rights.

            1.6.6.2.2.1.2. Pro:
            A federal court stated that Donald Trump, as President of the United States, was not legally allowed [311] to block Twitter users from viewing his social media account.

              1.6.6.2.2.1.2.1. Con:
              This ruling stood mostly on the grounds that Trump has only one account, for both official and personal communications [312]. Many elected officials have separate personal and official accounts.

        1.6.6.2.3. Pro:
        Many government officials are increasingly using social media as a platform for official government communications. [313]

          1.6.6.2.3.1. Pro:
          The United Kingdom has an official Twitter account for the Prime Minister, Boris Johnson. The government uses this platform to post official communications [314].

          1.6.6.2.3.2. Con:
          Government officials give speeches that are broadcast on television news channels. This does not make these privately owned channels subject to the First Amendment.

          1.6.6.2.3.3. Pro:
          The Department of Justice in 2017 asserted to a federal court [315] that the government of the U.S. is treating Donald Trump's tweets, "as official statements of the President of the United States.”
