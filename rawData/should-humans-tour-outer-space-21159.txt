Discussion Name:
Should Humans Tour Outer Space?

1. Thesis:
Humans should tour outer space.

  1.1. Con:
  Space tourism is dangerous.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Contamination from other planets may be brought back to Earth, causing danger to those who have never engaged in space tourism.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      Space tourism might lead to new diseases in human populations.

        1.1.1.1.1. Con:
        We have already sent humans into space and so far no diseases have been shown to occur, as we take proper precautions for the astronauts to adapt to life in space.

          1.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
          If space tourists visit other planets, quarantine [1] can be set up upon their return to Earth in order to eradicate possible diseases they may have contracted.

        1.1.1.1.2. Pro:
        People may experience diseases (or different manifestations of the same diseases) never known to mankind. These may be difficult to treat at first, as there may be no known treatment for the new conditions.

          1.1.1.1.2.1. Con:
          Medicine would likely move fast to adapt to these difficulties, as has been done in the past (like with vaccines [2] and outbreaks).

            1.1.1.1.2.1.1. Con:
            There have been several times [3] through history where medicine has been slow to adapt to the difficulties of a new disease.

            1.1.1.1.2.1.2. Con:
            Alien diseases could be completely different from those on Earth and possibly resistant [4] to any human medicine.

        1.1.1.1.3. Con:
        Space tourism will not necessarily take place on other planets, but just in spaceships, so diseases could not appear.

          1.1.1.1.3.1. Pro:
          Space tourism, as currently envisioned [5], would likely orbit [6] celestial bodies to avoid touching them.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      -> See 1.1.1.1.1.1.

      1.1.1.3. Con:
      Planetary protection [7] is a principle in place that demands the sterilization of space crafts so foreign bodies from Earth will not contaminate celestial bodies and vice versa. The same can be used in space tourism.

    1.1.2. Con:
    Many popular activities [8] are dangerous. As long as people consent to the danger, they should be allowed to participate.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      Every time humans have ventured into the unknown it has involved an element of danger, but this has not stopped humanity's progress before.

        1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
        It was common for ships to sink during the Age of Discovery [9], yet humanity continued to explore new regions of the world.

        1.1.2.1.2. Con:
        Human beings have never attempted to visit an environment as hostile [10] as space before.

          1.1.2.1.2.1. Con:
          The deep ocean [11] is arguably as hostile to human life as space.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      Space travel contains so many potential unknown variables [12] that people may not be fully aware of the risks involved, and thus may not be able to give informed consent.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        The number of unknown variables will decrease over time as more and more people visit space and return safely to Earth.

      1.1.2.3. Pro:
      It is estimated that over one percent [13] of people who attempt to climb Mt. Everest die during the expedition, yet the activity remains highly popular and lucrative to guide companies.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    The spacecrafts themselves are subject to breaking down.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      NASA estimated [14] that the risk of 'loss-of-crew' catastrophic failure for one space launch in 2021 was 1 in 270. By comparison [15], fatal crashes for commercial airliners is 1 in 3.7 million.

        1.1.3.1.1. Con:
        Some evidence suggests [16] that space flights are still less likely to result in fatalities than driving a car over the lifetime of a driver.

      1.1.3.2. Pro:
      Private companies could cut corners on construction in order to save money.

        1.1.3.2.1. Pro:
        After the Challenger disaster in 1986 [17], the government made safer spacecrafts, but with civilian companies there is no guarantee of this.

        1.1.3.2.2. Con:
        Given the enormous negative repercussions for a space disaster that results in loss of life, the companies have every incentive to ensure they are creating high quality spacecraft.

      1.1.3.3. Con:
      Spacecraft safety has improved [18] over time and is likely to continue to improve as more regulations and innovations occur.

    1.1.4. Con:
    Space tourism is not dangerous to everyone involved, just those that are doing the riskiest of activities and being the first.

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      Something does not need to be dangerous to everyone involved to still be prohibitively dangerous.

      1.1.4.2. Con:
      Those who are doing the riskiest part of space tourism are the space tourists - the consumers - who are not necessarily qualified to conduct proper risk-analysis themselves. As a result, space agencies have a duty of care towards them, which is broken when they are put in danger.

        1.1.4.2.1. Con:
        Individuals are trusted to evaluate whether they are willing to accept the risks of all sorts of dangerous activities, from base jumping to bull riding. It is not clear why space tourism and its risks should be handled in a different manner.

  1.2. Pro:
  Space tourism is good for the economy.

    1.2.1. Con:
    Space tourism is a waste of resources.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      People don't need to spend resources being tourists in space when there are so many places to visit on Earth.

        1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
        Most of the cosmological features around Earth are pretty similar to it. So there's not much need to tour what we already have here.

          1.2.1.1.1.1. Con:
          Planets and other celestial bodies have features not seen on Earth, which are worth exploring.

            1.2.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
            Mars has amazing geological features [19] that are different from those on Earth.

          1.2.1.1.1.2. Pro:
          The Moon [20], Mars [21], and other nearby rocky planets are mostly comprised of similar materials to Earth [22].

          1.2.1.1.1.3. Con:
          Space offers the unique experience of weightlessness in orbit and low gravity [23] on other celestial bodies.

            1.2.1.1.1.3.1. Con:
            It is possible to experience the feeling of zero gravity [24] without venturing into space.

          1.2.1.1.1.4. Pro:
          Touring even the most remote regions [25] of Earth is still significantly cheaper than traveling to space.

        1.2.1.1.2. Pro:
        Humans have yet to explore most of Earth, thus it makes sense to explore the unknowns here rather than even thinking about attempting space.

          1.2.1.1.2.1. Pro:
          The most that anyone's ever traveled down towards the center of the Earth is just less than 10 miles [26]. Given that the radius is almost 4000 miles [27] long, humans have a long way to go to even 'scratch the surface'.

            1.2.1.1.2.1.1. Con:
            It is unlikely for explorers to find anything besides rocks and magma [28] under the surface of the earth, whereas space offers infinite possibilities for discovery.

          1.2.1.1.2.2. Pro:
          Humans have explored only 5% [29] of the ocean, leaving 95% untouched. It makes sense to explore this before leaving to explore other planets.

            1.2.1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
            Underwater [30] and deep sea [31] tourism are burgeoning industries that can compete with space tourism.

          1.2.1.1.2.3. Con:
          Given that there are billions [32] of people on Earth, it is reasonable for some to continue to explore here, while others explore space.

        1.2.1.1.3. Con:
        A person should be free to use their personal resources however they deem fit. If they have the means to travel to space, they should be free to do so.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      There is no way of telling if space tourism is a waste of resources or not. It may have health benefits, and it will almost certainly result in new scientific discoveries [33].

      1.2.1.3. Pro:
      There are big challenges on Earth that our time and resources are better spent creating solutions for. Having an increased knowledge of space doesn't have much impact or practical implications on the daily lives of humans.

        1.2.1.3.1. Con:
        The fact that there are allegedly bigger challenges has never been a good reason against taking action.

          1.2.1.3.1.1. Pro:
          Renaissance Europe faced significant challenges [34] on the continent, but that did not stop monarchies from funding [35] exploration, leading to the discovery of the Americas.

            1.2.1.3.1.1.1. Pro:
            The discovery of the New World [36] had a profound and lasting impact on human history. It is likely that the discovery of habitable worlds or alien life would be just as consequential.

        1.2.1.3.2. Con:
        We have enough people, resources, and time to work on our issues on Earth and explore other options as well.

          1.2.1.3.2.1. Pro:
          -> See 1.2.1.1.2.3.

        1.2.1.3.3. Pro:
        Money would be better spent on tackling human rights issues [37] like access to food and shelter than funneling money into space tourism programs and companies.

          1.2.1.3.3.1. Pro:
          Touring space does not solve basic humanitarian issues that humans face on Earth.

        1.2.1.3.4. Con:
        Challenges solved in space can help solve challenges on Earth, making space tourism a worthy endeavor.

          1.2.1.3.4.1. Pro:
          Virgin Galactic's space tourism revenue could lead to more eco-friendly [38] airplane travel.

            1.2.1.3.4.1.1. Pro:
            Space tourism can help promote the development of hypersonic jets [39] that could take people to the opposite side of the world in just a few hours.

          1.2.1.3.4.2. Pro:
          Space exploration has already contributed to a diverse array [40] of fields, including medicine, water filtration, and search-and-rescue.

        1.2.1.3.5. Pro:
        Helping people on Earth will benefit more individuals than the few (most likely wealthy) people that go to space.

      1.2.1.4. Con:
      The profits from space tourism could help fund further scientific research.

        1.2.1.4.1. Con:
        It could also lead to research dollars ending up in the pockets of corporations instead of researchers.

        1.2.1.4.2. Pro:
        The amount of money NASA is saving [41] by partnering with space tourism companies can be used for other scientific endeavors.

      1.2.1.5. Con:
      Space based industries such as asteroid mining [42] have dramatic potential to improve our access to valuable natural resources. More activity in space, of any sort, is progress toward this end.

        1.2.1.5.1. Con:
        Progress would be more efficient if companies were dedicated exclusively to asteroid mining, rather than space tourism.

          1.2.1.5.1.1. Con:
          While some asteroid mining companies already exist [43], many believe that asteroid mining will never become [44] a viable industry.

    1.2.2. Con:
    Space tourism is not profitable.

      1.2.2.1. Pro:
      There's not enough demand, as the average consumer will not be interested in space tourism.

        1.2.2.1.1. Pro:

          1.2.2.1.1.1. Pro:

            1.2.2.1.1.1.1. Con:
            It is impossible to put a price on a unique and invaluable experience like seeing the Earth from space. Each person will value that experience differently.

          1.2.2.1.1.2. Con:
          If the initial tours go well, it is likely added perks will be added for repeat customers.

            1.2.2.1.1.2.1. Pro:
            Virgin Galactic took its first customers over 50 miles [46] to the edge of space. This limit may be extended in the future.

          1.2.2.1.1.3. Con:
          Space tourism is still in its infancy. The sustainability of it cannot be predicted based on its current customer base.

            1.2.2.1.1.3.1. Pro:
            Many new innovations [47] aren't universally affordable, but become so over time. The same can happen for space tourism.

              1.2.2.1.1.3.1.1. Pro:
              One NASA astronaut predicts [48] space tourism will be affordable in 10 years.

        1.2.2.1.2. Pro:
        A majority of Americans surveyed [49] said they would not be interested in space travel.

          1.2.2.1.2.1. Con:
          According to the survey, four-in-ten Americans said they would be interested. That is a potential market of over a hundred million people in the US alone, more than enough to generate lasting demand for the industry.

        1.2.2.1.3. Con:
        Space tourism is new and fascinates people. Therefore, it will attract many customers.

          1.2.2.1.3.1. Pro:
          Space tourism should be pursued solely on the basis of the human desire to explore.

          1.2.2.1.3.2. Pro:
          Such travel will be the natural progression of adventurous vacations.

          1.2.2.1.3.3. Pro:
          Space exploration satisfies the human curiosity for the unknown.

            1.2.2.1.3.3.1. Pro:
            Space tourism can open people's eyes to something miraculous they haven't been a part of before or don't get to see everyday.

            1.2.2.1.3.3.2. Pro:
            Seeing the planet from outside allows humans to fathom our profound connection with nature and our very existence in the universe. This is called the overview effect [50].

            1.2.2.1.3.3.3. Con:
            Most people will satiate this curiosity after one trip in space, making return visitors less likely.

          1.2.2.1.3.4. Con:
          Being new and fascinating is not enough to generate demand if the price of space travel remains high [51].

          1.2.2.1.3.5. Con:
          Some people are put off by the thought of space tourism because of their fear [52] of traveling into space.

            1.2.2.1.3.5.1. Pro:
            Astrophobia is a real condition [53] in which people are severely and irrationally afraid of space.

              1.2.2.1.3.5.1.1. Pro:
              Even many astronauts have admitted [54] that they are afraid when going into space.

        1.2.2.1.4. Con:
        As spaceship technologies become more accessible, demand may rise so much that stopping humans from visiting space will likely become challenging.

          1.2.2.1.4.1. Pro:
          -> See 1.2.2.1.1.3.1.1.

          1.2.2.1.4.2. Con:
          The question is not whether humans will or can tour space, but whether it is ethical to do so.

      1.2.2.2. Con:

        1.2.2.2.1. Pro:

          1.2.2.2.1.1. Pro:
          Humans tend to achieve what they put their minds to, and space tourism is no different. It won't be a waste of resources if the end-goal of achieving our desire for space tourism is reached.

      1.2.2.3. Con:
      Just because an activity is not profitable at the moment does not mean that it cannot become profitable over time through economies of scale and technological progress.

      1.2.2.4. Pro:

        1.2.2.4.1. Con:

          Current costs for space tourism range [58] from hundreds of thousands to millions of dollars.

        1.2.3.3.1. Pro:

        1.3.2.4.2. Con:
        Scientific exploration is more invasive than touring space since researchers have to interact with their environment.

          1.3.2.4.2.1. Pro:
          Tourists would stay in the spacecraft, meaning there is less tampering with anything from outer space.

        1.3.2.4.3. Con:
        Scientific discoveries could be an unintended consequences of space tourism.

      1.3.2.5. Pro:
      Touring space could lead to new scientific discoveries such as alien life or another habitable planet.

        1.3.2.5.1. Con:
        Space tourism in the foreseeable future is unlikely to venture far beyond a low Earth orbit [102].

        1.3.2.5.2. Con:
        Discovering a new habitable planet would require interstellar travel [103], which is currently beyond humanity's technological capabilities.

        1.3.2.5.3. Pro:
        -> See 1.2.1.3.1.1.1.

      1.3.2.6. Pro:
      Space tourism will help make space science common knowledge and disprove ideas such as flat earth theory [104].

        1.3.2.6.1. Con:
        This could be done more effectively and cheaply by improving science education in schools.

    1.3.3. Con:
    There are more noble and effective methods to advance human knowledge than space tourism.

      1.3.3.1. Pro:
      Space exploration, terraforming, and colonization all lead to the advancement of technology in the same way as space tourism does, but they are better for researching. These endeavors have a larger impact than space tourism.

        1.3.3.1.1. Pro:
        The result of space tourism has no clear discernible benefits besides enjoyment. However, successful investment into research about space exploration, space colonization, and terraforming has tangible and direct benefits [105] to society.

        1.3.3.1.2. Pro:
        Space tourism is just for a few moments of a person's life, whereas colonization is for a lifetime.

        1.3.3.1.3. Pro:
        Space tourism might introduce humanity to space, but space colonization helps humans permanently expand into space.

        1.3.3.1.4. Con:
        It is common to visit a place before deciding to move there. Space tourism is the same principle, and thus impacts the willingness of humans to colonize in space in the first place.

      1.3.3.2. Con:
      Space tourism will develop more easily in the private sector, opening space technologies to a wider audience than government projects or university research.

        1.3.3.2.1. Pro:
        Government projects on sensitive technology are often classified [106], making it much less likely that the public would benefit from any discoveries.

        1.3.3.2.2. Pro:
        Even if academics produce important findings, their work is seldom read [107] by anyone outside their narrow field of expertise.

        1.3.3.2.3. Con:
        The private sector has been known to run with the motivation of only profit, which can increase risk for consumers.

          1.3.3.2.3.1. Pro:
          Seating in planes are becoming more cramped for those riding, all just to increase more profits [108], it is quite possible this will be done for space tourism as well.

          1.3.3.2.3.2. Pro:
          Boeing has been caught with poorly-manufacturing their aircraft [109], and this can lead to far more lethal results if space tourism companies start digging in and potentially follow similar trends.

            1.3.3.2.3.2.1. Pro:
            Underwater tourism company OceanGate has also been caught with the same practice [110], and has directly lead to a disastrous incident due to the subpar manufacture of the Titan submarine [111].

          1.3.3.2.3.3. Con:
          The space tourism sector could be regulated, nationalized, inter-nationalized, or taken over by public organizations at a later point where risks are only high early on.

    1.3.4. Pro:
    It could represent a method of generating revenue for the development, research and maintenance of space travel technologies, infrastructures, and techniques.

  1.4. Con:
  Space tourism is unethical.

    1.4.1. Pro:
    Space tourism might contaminate other celestial bodies.

      1.4.1.1. Con:
      -> See 1.1.1.1.3.1.

      1.4.1.2. Con:
      Space tourism will not contaminate other planets, only space colonization would.

        1.4.1.2.1. Con:
        Simply entering another planet's atmosphere carries the risk of living organisms attaching themselves to a spaceship.

          1.4.1.2.1.1. Con:
          -> See 1.1.1.3.

      1.4.1.3. Pro:
      Humans have already begun polluting Mars [112] and the moon [113] with leftover materials from our space programs. This will likely increase with space tourism.

    1.4.2. Pro:
    Space tourism would have harmful effects on the Earth.

      1.4.2.1. Con:
      -> See 1.3.1.2.1.

      1.4.2.2. Pro:
      Space tourism might increase space junk [114] around Earth.

        1.4.2.2.1. Pro:
        The space debris/junk issue [115] is so severe that it could cause accidents involving spaceships.

          1.4.2.2.1.1. Pro:
          The number of space debris is going to increase. As soon as a collision between two objects occurs, several smaller objects are created. These would all be traveling at very fast speeds [116], and can easily penetrate satellites or spaceships.

          1.4.2.2.1.2. Con:
          Several companies [117] are working on ways to remove space debris. It is likely that they will continue to innovate new ways to resolve this issue in the coming years.

        1.4.2.2.2. Con:
        Most space junk doesn’t stay in orbit forever. Maintaining an orbit requires adjustments, otherwise (very) slowly objects lose speed and eventually fall back [118] into the atmosphere.

          1.4.2.2.2.1. Con:
          Humans are adding space junk at a much higher rate [119] than it takes for the pieces to fall back into the atmosphere.

        1.4.2.2.3. Con:
        The chances of collision with space junk are extremely low considering the volume of space available (the whole surface of earth times the height of the altitude), the size of space junk, and the size of vehicles.

          1.4.2.2.3.1. Con:
          The probability of a space debris impact is small, but the cost and consequences are vastly huge. [120]

          1.4.2.2.3.2. Con:
          Although the issue is small now, we are continually sending more stuff into space. This will only get worse once people start colonizing/mass touring space, so that we could reach a point that the problem is too difficult to overcome [121].

      1.4.2.3. Pro:
      Space tourism can make climate change worse on Earth due to the industrial and human activities required for space travel.

        1.4.2.3.1. Con:

This wouldn't just reduce pollution created through space tourism but could potentially be used to replace less green energy technology on Earth.

          1.4.2.3.1.1. Pro:
          -> See 1.3.1.1.1.1.

        1.4.2.3.2. Con:

          1.4.2.3.2.1. Pro:
          The super rich customers [122] currently purchasing tickets on space tours typically have the largest carbon footprint [123]. The resources they're spending on space travel might be less harmful to the planet than other alternatives.

            1.4.2.3.2.1.1. Pro:
            Superyachts [124] and private planes emit much more carbon in the long term than one trip to space.

        1.4.2.3.3. Pro:
        We do not have the technology to make space tourism sustainable right now, which is why climate change would get worse with humans touring space.

          1.4.2.3.3.1. Pro:
          Space tourism has the potential to increase [125] black carbon emissions, which is "100,000 times the effect of the carbon dioxide [emitted by rockets]."

          1.4.2.3.3.2. Pro:
          The only current technology to put a rocket in space is burning fuel in the atmosphere. No clean energy exists yet [126] that is powerful enough to put a manned vehicle into space.

            1.4.2.3.3.2.1. Con:
            -> See 1.3.1.2.1.1.

            1.4.2.3.3.2.2. Pro:
            'Green' fuel [127] is still in its testing phase and has not been shown to react as intended.

            1.4.2.3.3.2.3. Con:
            This is avoided if we use hydrogen/oxygen fuel cells [128], which only produce water as a combustion byproduct.

          1.4.2.3.3.3. Con:
          It is only by testing new technologies via space tourism now that we will innovate more sustainable methods in the future.

        1.4.2.3.4. Pro:
        Touring space would turn our focus away from climate change, leading to fewer efforts geared towards helping the planet. This action will make Earth suffer climate change even greater than now.

          1.4.2.3.4.1. Con:
          It is possible for a few individuals and private companies to focus on space travel while the majority of governments and corporations commit to fighting climate change.

          1.4.2.3.4.2. Pro:
          If we do not tour space, then we can focus on healing the Earth to the point that space tourism can be a viable, sustainable industry.

    1.4.3. Con:
    Space tourism creates a more ethical industry to participate in than many other industries existing today.

      1.4.3.1. Pro:
      Space tourism creates open opportunities and momentum to allow more people to freely contribute to scientific development than ever before.

        1.4.3.1.1. Pro:
        People can financially support space tourism companies through buying their stocks, like Virgin Galactic [129] and for employees SpaceX [130] for high-tech, scientific development not previously available.

          1.4.3.1.1.1. Con:
          People in most countries already support space exploration, as part of their taxes go to government space programs [131].

        1.4.3.1.2. Pro:
        Space tourism companies decrease the gender gap [132] that plagues the aerospace industry - thus allowing more individuals to participate than ever before.

          1.4.3.1.2.1. Con:
          Only 12% [133] of the founders, owners, or entrepreneurs in the "new space" market identify as female.

        1.4.3.1.3. Con:
        Due to the complexity [134] of space travel, only those who work for space-related companies will have the resources to make important contributions to the field.

      1.4.3.2. Pro:
      Jobs generated by space tourism (and its spinoff industries) tend to be more ethical than other existing industries.

        1.4.3.2.1. Pro:
        The jobs in space tourism are more ethical than the jobs of the past, as they don't involve weapons, animals, or drugs to generate a profit.

          1.4.3.2.1.1. Con:
          Weapons, animals, and drugs are only a small subset [135] of all currently existing industries in the world.

          1.4.3.2.2.1. Pro:
          SpaceX is a US company that builds [136] their rockets in the US, creating jobs for those in these communities.

            1.4.3.2.2.1.1. Con:
            SpaceX has faced backlash [137] from local residents for their invasive manufacturing methods.

          1.4.3.2.2.2. Con:
          Outsourcing jobs can give opportunities to workers in developing countries that they otherwise wouldn't have.

        1.4.3.2.3. Con:
        Most space tourism jobs will be similar [138] to jobs on Earth, but just in the environment of space.

      1.4.3.3. Pro:
      Financing this industry is more ethical than putting money into industries with less of a positive return.

        1.4.3.3.1. Pro:
        For investing, space tourism companies are more ethical than putting money into funding sin stocks [139].

  1.5. Con:
  Space tourism cannot be easily and effectively regulated.

    1.5.1. Pro:
    The current international legal framework regarding commercialised space activities is not sufficient to support space tourism. Regulations [143] are barely set up for space tourism at the moment.

      1.5.1.1. Pro:
      In order to apply legal regulations, a line of demarcation [144] between outer space and air space has to be clearly defined. This has yet to be done.

        1.5.1.1.1. Pro:
        Many of the biggest startups are only going past the Kármán line [145], which is legally space [146], but still is within Earth's atmosphere [147].

        1.5.1.1.2. Con:
        Since the demarcation line will likely be somewhat arbitrary, considering the amount of disagreement [148] on the issue, it is only up to governments to settle on an altitude for this to be resolved.

      1.5.1.2. Con:
      There are precedents that lawmakers can draw upon, such as the law of the sea [149] and aviation law [150], to help devise new regulations for space.

        1.5.1.2.1. Pro:
        Both the law of the sea and aviation law involve individuals confined to vessels in places beyond the jurisdiction of states. This will also apply to space laws.

    1.5.2. Pro:
    There is currently not enough cooperation between nations [151] who would want to be space-faring or are now to support setting up a sufficient legal framework.

      1.5.2.1. Pro:
      Competition [152] between the US and China in space is likely to hinder progress on space regulations.

        1.5.2.1.1. Con:
        This competition is likely to be focused on security issues, which is a separate domain from space tourism.

          1.5.2.1.1.1. Con:
          A 2021 report [153] by the US Director of National Intelligence predicts that the US-China rivalry in space will encompass commercial, civil and military issues.

      1.5.2.2. Con:
      It is likely that legal frameworks [154] for outer space will be under the guidance of international bodies such as the UN, not individual states.

    1.5.3. Con:
    There are always challenges [155] to regulating new industries. It is likely that relevant laws will evolve to adequately regulate space tourism in the future.

      1.5.3.1. Pro:
      When automobiles were introduced, they faced no regulations [156], but lawmakers reacted to this by implementing relevant restrictions, such as speed limits [157].

      1.5.3.2. Con:
      Space tourism is unique in that it does not take place on our planet. This presents unprecedented jurisdictional complications.

        1.5.3.2.1. Con:
        Unlike space colonizers, space tourists will still return to earth, where they are subject to existing laws and regulations. This could simplify jurisdictional disputes.

    1.5.4. Con:
    Potential space tourists will likely only want to travel with legitimate, well-regulated companies, given the risks [158] involved with space travel.

    1.5.5. Pro:
    Aerospace technology is so cutting-edge and niche that maintaining a government body of experts to oversee and enforce regulation will be challenging.

      1.5.5.1. Pro:
      There is already a shortage of aerospace engineers [159], the best of whom are likely to work for private companies who pay better.

    1.5.6. Con:
    Space tourism is actually very easy to regulate, as space launches are hard to hide, and the technology involved is so complex that only a few big actors will be able to provide it. This means that the regulation is easy to enforce.

      1.5.6.1. Con:
      Nuclear weapon development is similar in its complexity, and yet international organizations have failed to prevent nuclear proliferation in states like North Korea [160].
