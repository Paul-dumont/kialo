Discussion Name:
Should Governments Ever Limit Free Speech?

1. Thesis:
Governments should not set policies that limit free speech.

  1.1. Con:
  Regulations on free speech protect individuals from unnecessary harm.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Fraudulent speech should be prohibited due to the material harm it can cause.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      Defamation [1] laws are necessary to protect individuals and society from the consequences of carelessly harmful or actively malicious speech.

        1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
        Defamation protects people from blatantly false [2] statements that may damage their reputation.

          1.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
          One's reputation is immensely important in contemporary societies. People should be able to defend themselves.

          1.1.1.1.1.2. Con:
          Defamation laws do not limit free speech, they are a means of redress from those who knowing lie to destroy another person reputation, livelihood, or impact there freedom.

          1.1.1.1.1.3. Pro:
          People can often lose their jobs [3] following false claims made by other individuals against them.

        1.1.1.1.2. Con:
        This is not an argument against free speech. Free speech concerns subjective opinions. Making absolutely verifiable statements is something entirely different.

        1.1.1.1.3. Con:
        A state will not be able to pre-emptively stop defamatory speech. Even if individuals are tried for such speech, the damage of the speech will be done already.

          1.1.1.1.3.1. Pro:
          False accusations [4] can often catch the public eye far more than the defenses which acquit the accused.

          1.1.1.1.3.2. Con:
          Speech laws will deter individuals from engaging in defamatory speech.

            1.1.1.1.3.2.1. Con:
            Since defamation cases are very expensive [5], few people will be able to use them. These laws will therefore provide only a minor little deterrent.

            1.1.1.1.3.2.2. Pro:
            Defamation suits, particularly ones for economic damages, tend to cost the offenders [6] large amounts of money. This will act as a good reason for people to not break the law.

        1.1.1.1.4. Con:
        Defamation laws often reinforce the power of the powerful and wealthy in society at the expense of less well-off actors (p. 11) [7].

          1.1.1.1.4.1. Pro:
          Unequal access to defamation protection has the effect of eroding [8] public confidence in the judicial system.

          1.1.1.1.4.2. Con:
          Unequal access to representation is a flaw in the current legal system and not with defamation laws in principle.

          1.1.1.1.4.3. Pro:
          Defamation suits can only be brought by groups and individuals who are able to fund them in a civil court. Many poorer individuals lack the time or money to do this.

      1.1.1.2. Pro:
      Fake calls to emergency services can cost lives by diverting vital resources from where they are needed.

        1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
        Fake calls to 911 can lead to innocent people being attacked by SWAT teams. This practice is known as 'swatting [9]'.

        1.1.1.2.2. Pro:
        The BBC reported that fake 999 calls [10] placed a heavy strain on Britain's emergency services.

        1.1.1.2.3. Con:
        Intentionally wasting the emergency services' time can be a separate crime without infringing on someone's free speech.

      1.1.1.3. Con:
      Most lawsuits for fraudulent speech, like slander and defamation, fail [11].

      1.1.1.4. Pro:
      Regulations on free speech can prevent companies from lying to customers.

        1.1.1.4.1. Con:
        There are already laws [12] in place to regulate marketing and advertising materials that companies use. This is not a freedom of speech issue.

        1.1.1.4.2. Pro:
        Nike attempted to claim 'freedom of speech' as a defense [13] in court after it was accused of lying about working conditions in its factories.

          1.1.1.4.2.1. Pro:
          Nike lost the case [14]. Their lies were ruled as not being protected by free speech.

        1.1.1.4.3. Con:
        There is a difference between free speech, which involves some interpretation of facts and narratives, and flat-out fabrication [15] due to a lack of corporate transparency.

          1.1.1.4.3.1. Con:
          Absolute free speech allows companies to say whatever they want. Any limit on this would not be absolute free speech.

          1.1.1.4.3.2. Pro:
          Corporations are the only ones with information about their products. Therefore, their obligation to not lie is not a speech restriction but a market accountability mechanism.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    Completely free speech in public spaces can be very disruptive and dangerous.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      The popular hypothetical of someone shouting "fire! [16]" in a crowded theatre could lead to many deaths.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      Regulations on freedom of speech are often important in creating safe spaces, for example, in universities.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        Safe spaces are often utilized to silence contradictory opinions [17].

          1.1.2.2.1.1. Pro:
          Advocates of safe spaces have attempted to make college campuses safe spaces, limiting the free exchange of ideas [18] necessary for successful higher education.

          1.1.2.2.1.2. Con:
          Safe spaces prevent offensive or discriminatory speech. As such, they allow better academic discussions since all participants feel more included and free to speak.

        1.1.2.2.2. Pro:
        Safe spaces are important [19] in providing protection and freedom of expression to minority groups.

          1.1.2.2.2.1. Con:
          Safe spaces limit the free speech of others [20] while doing nothing to enhance the free speech of minorities.

            1.1.2.2.2.1.1. Pro:
            Safe spaces make it difficult to discuss important yet uncomfortable issues [21] in public settings.

            1.1.2.2.2.1.2. Con:
            Safe spaces do not infringe anyone's right to speech; one is free to leave the safe space if they wish to say something potentially offensive.

          1.1.2.2.2.2. Pro:
          Safe spaces give members of minority groups safety and the ability to fully express themselves [22] without fear of criticism.

            1.1.2.2.2.2.1. Pro:
            Safe spaces can allow people to share the challenges [23] they face without fear of being criticized.

          1.1.2.2.2.3. Pro:
          Safe spaces can act as support networks [24] for those within them.

            1.1.2.2.2.3.1. Pro:
            This enhances the freedom of speech of those within safe spaces, as people feel comfortable expressing their views since they are guaranteed the support of others.

      1.1.2.3. Con:
      To prevent dangerous public activity, many countries [25] enforce non-speech laws while giving citizens the right to peacefully assemble for the redress of social and political grievances.

        1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
        Restrictions on public assembly typically focus on the destruction of property or physical assault [26].

        1.1.2.3.2. Con:
        Restrictions on violent protest are still restrictions on speech. These actions are considered symbolic speech in many countries, such as the US [27].

          1.1.2.3.2.1. Pro:
          The U.S. Supreme Court has upheld that symbolic actions, like burning the US flag [28], are protected by the freedom of speech.

          1.1.2.3.2.2. Con:
          Violence is not the same as symbolic action.

            1.1.2.3.2.2.1. Pro:
            One can perform a speech act and an act of violence simultaneously without them being reduced to the same action. This violence can be prosecuted while maintaining the freedom of speech.

            1.1.2.3.2.2.2. Pro:
            Violence is not a form of expression and thus is outside of the scope of speech protections.

            1.1.2.3.2.2.3. Con:
            Acts of violence or destruction [29] are frequently used as expressions of anger and frustration in large protests and thus are, at least partly, speech acts.

          1.1.2.3.2.3. Pro:
          If only peaceful protest is allowed, freedom of speech is not absolute.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Offensive speech can cause significant amounts of harm to people.

      1.1.3.1. Con:
      Hate speech itself can not harm people. Only the actions that people take because of it can. In most cases, those actions are already illegal.

        1.1.3.1.1. Pro:
        The major harm of hate speech is incitement of violence, and violence is already illegal.

        1.1.3.1.2. Con:
        The fact that some acts are illegal does not mean that people will not commit them. As such, the causes of these acts must also be restricted to some extent.

          1.1.3.1.2.1. Pro:
          Due to a lack of available resources [30], law enforcement agencies are unable to arrest perpetrators of violence.

            1.1.3.1.2.1.1. Con:
            If law enforcement agencies do not have sufficient resources to police physical violence, they will also likely not have sufficient resources to enforce speech laws.

          1.1.3.1.2.2. Pro:
          Racism in the judiciary [31] makes it unlikely for the perpetrators of hate crimes against minority groups to be held accountable.

          1.1.3.1.2.3. Con:
          While states should curb the influence of regressive groups, they should use means that do not infringe upon the rights of citizens.

            1.1.3.1.2.3.1. Pro:
            For example, states can seek to increase awareness of the forms of discrimination faced by minority groups.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      Regulations on speech are difficult to maintain, as speech is deeply tied to context. It is both difficult to determine what harmful speech is, as well as difficult to legislate properly.

        1.1.3.2.1. Con:
        Courts largely work on interpretations and precedents [32]. Over time, a clear understanding of when restricting speech is legal would be forged.

          1.1.3.2.1.1. Pro:
          Such a law will eventually gain a clear and widely understood scope, based on the kinds of cases that arise, allowing a true balance to be reached with speech rights.

          1.1.3.2.1.2. Con:
          Any balance that would be reached would likely lead to the over-regulation of people's speech.

            1.1.3.2.1.2.1. Pro:
            Given the immense political power [33] that social media companies currently have, it will probably be difficult for governments to hold them accountable for restricting too much speech.

              1.1.3.2.1.2.1.1. Pro:
              Facebook and Twitter were able to suspend the accounts of the sitting US President [34].

            1.1.3.2.1.2.2. Con:
            There will likely be great scrutiny on the creation of these laws, especially given the current widescale mistrust of social media [35] companies. It is, therefore, unlikely they will be draconian.

            1.1.3.2.1.2.3. Pro:
            News and social media companies may find it cheaper to opt to restrict people's speech rather than risking backlash from advertisers or the government over hateful speech.

        1.1.3.2.2. Con:
        European countries have created well-defined hate speech [36] laws that have not infringed upon the rights of people.

          1.1.3.2.2.1. Con:
          Only 18% of Germans [37] feel free to express themselves in public.

          1.1.3.2.2.2. Con:
          European hate speech laws have been widely criticized. [38]

          1.1.3.2.2.3. Pro:
          Many European free speech restrictions have focused on pushing social media [39] companies to use better moderation policies.

        1.1.3.2.3. Pro:
        Offensiveness cannot be measured objectively [40], and therefore hate speech can not be defined along these terms.

          1.1.3.2.3.1. Pro:
          Words that are deemed to be 'offensive' and 'hateful' change over time. [41]

            1.1.3.2.3.1.1. Pro:
            Many words that are typically considered slurs have been reclaimed by the communities they were targeted against. When used by these communities, they are no longer offensive.

              1.1.3.2.3.1.1.1. Pro:
              The LGBT+ community has reclaimed [42] many words which were previously used as insults against them.

              1.1.3.2.3.1.1.2. Pro:
              Many black people use [43] the "N-word" as a way to reclaim a word which previously was used to oppress them.

              1.1.3.2.3.1.1.3. Con:
              States have no way of verifying whether groups are offended by some speech. Those who claim to be the most offended can thus gain an outsized influence on discourse in society.

                1.1.3.2.3.1.1.3.1. Pro:
                A previously existing law prohibiting blasphemy [44] in Ireland proved impossible to enforce, as it was difficult to prove when language had blasphemous intent and genuinely offended someone who had religious convictions.

                1.1.3.2.3.1.1.3.2. Con:
                The state does not have to ban all speech that may be offensive. Instead, it can measure and decide what speech is most dangerous and worth policing.

              1.1.3.2.3.1.1.4. Pro:
              Most of these words are still considered very offensive if used by an outsider [45], making it difficult to determine whether a slur is being used maliciously or not.

                1.1.3.2.3.1.1.4.1. Con:
                To make this easier, social media sites [46] often have users report offensive material, allow users to appeal post removals, and allow multiple offenses before blocking users.

              1.1.3.2.3.1.1.5. Con:
              Whether or not slurs should be reclaimed and used by members of the demographic groups they once targeted is a hotly debated issue. [47]

            1.1.3.2.3.1.2. Con:
            Laws can always be updated to reflect new ways that words are being used.

          1.1.3.2.3.2. Pro:
          Potentially offensive words can often have different meanings depending on the context in which they are used.

            1.1.3.2.3.2.1. Con:
            This ambiguity is sometimes used as a justification [48] for using offensive words while pretending to use them in an inoffensive manner.

            1.1.3.2.3.2.2. Pro:
            It is difficult to determine the intent of an individual making potentially offensive claims.

              1.1.3.2.3.2.2.1. Pro:
              Many jokes could possibly be read as hate speech [49] given a lack of context.

          1.1.3.2.3.3. Pro:
          Feeling offended or hurt by speech is a matter of personal choice. Thus it will be impossible for states to determine what speech people will choose to be offended by.

            1.1.3.2.3.3.1. Pro:
            Feeling offended is often the result of self-identified insecurities [50] one has about themselves.

            1.1.3.2.3.3.2. Con:
            Even if it is a matter of personal choice, it is reasonably easy to pre-empt many of the forms of speech that will cause offense to people.

            1.1.3.2.3.3.3. Con:
            Being offended or hurt is not a choice, it is a subconscious emotional reaction [51].

              1.1.3.2.3.3.3.1. Pro:
              Far from being a choice, people react differently to speech due to different circumstances and conditions.

              1.1.3.2.3.3.3.2. Con:
              People can analytically decide when something not viscerally hurtful offends their sensibilities.

              1.1.3.2.3.3.3.3. Con:
              Fundamentally, people are in charge of their own emotions. [52]

          1.1.3.2.3.4. Con:
          Free speech laws do not seek to prevent all cases of offense, just the most harmful and universal offensive speech, which can be more clearly identified.

            1.1.3.2.3.4.1. Pro:
            Online bullies are easy to detect because they make a flurry of offensive posts [53] and send their followers to attack their victims.

            1.1.3.2.3.4.2. Pro:
            Well-documented symbols [54] and the talking points of hateful, racist groups can be unambiguously identified.

            1.1.3.2.3.4.3. Con:
            Often, hate groups co-opt everyday symbols [55] to make it difficult to identify who they are. As such, these policies will do little to stop the dissemination of these ideas.

            1.1.3.2.3.4.4. Con:
            Many proponents of free speech regulations seek to target "microaggressions" [56] specifically.

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      If hateful views proliferate, they may win the support of large numbers of people, becoming normalised and more dangerous.

        1.1.3.3.1. Con:
        If hateful beliefs are not engaged with, their followers are unlikely to change their beliefs and more likely to carry out acts of discrimination and violence against minority groups (p. 146 [57]).

        1.1.3.3.2. Pro:
        If hateful views receive majority support, it would be politically impossible to ban them because anyone who attempted it, they would be voted out of office.

        1.1.3.3.3. Pro:
        In the aftermath of the successful Brexit campaign, anti-immigration rhetoric led to a rise in racially and religiously motivated crimes [58].

      1.1.3.4. Pro:
      Offensive speech can cause non-physical harm.

        1.1.3.4.1. Pro:
        Hate speech can cause people emotional distress (p. 5 [59]).

          1.1.3.4.1.1. Pro:
          Being subject to hate speech is likely to affect one's mental health [60] and exacerbate any existing anxiety, trauma, and depression.

            1.1.3.4.1.1.1. Con:
            If listeners are triggered [61] by speech, that would likely be evidence of pre-existing mental or emotional issues, which the speaker can not reasonably be expected to be aware of or responsible for.

              1.1.3.4.1.1.1.1. Con:
              Direct and credible threats of violence would likely be 'triggering' to most people, regardless of their pre-existing mental or emotional issues.

            1.1.3.4.1.1.2. Pro:
            Individuals are likely to have greater anxiety [62] when they are unable to express themselves in a safe space.

          1.1.3.4.1.2. Con:
          The emotional harm caused by hate speech does not infringe upon natural rights and, thus, should not be regulated by law as if it were physical harm.

            1.1.3.4.1.2.1. Pro:
            Many perfectly legal acts can cause people emotional distress.

            1.1.3.4.1.2.2. Con:
            Studies [63] have found that emotional pain is real and just as, if not more, painful than physical injuries.

          1.1.3.4.1.3. Pro:
          Emotional pain can result in people physically [64] harming themselves.

        1.1.3.4.2. Con:
        Even if it can harm individuals, speech cannot be considered [65] an act of violence. Therefore, speech's harms should not be considered to be the same as physical harms.

          1.1.3.4.2.1. Con:
          Speech acts can be violent because they have real-world effects, even if those effects are indirect.

            1.1.3.4.2.1.1. Con:
            The hateful action is what violates the rights of others, not the hateful speech. Therefore, only the hateful action can be justifiably restricted.

            1.1.3.4.2.1.2. Pro:
            Even where one's speech is not inciting action, it can still scare and anger listeners [66], and change their minds in dangerous and intolerant ways.

          1.1.3.4.2.2. Pro:
          Conflating speech with violence does a grave injustice [67] to people who suffer from actual violence.

        1.1.3.4.3. Pro:
        Hate speech often makes people feel worse about themselves, making their quality of life worse.

          1.1.3.4.3.1. Pro:
          The display of certain offensive or provocative symbols, like the swastika [68], can remind people of horrific crimes and violence that have affected them or their families.

          1.1.3.4.3.2. Pro:
          Hate speech against minorities reinforces the idea that they are inferior and subordinate to the majority.

            1.1.3.4.3.2.1. Pro:
            Often this leads to members of the minorities themselves internalizing the discrimination [69].

          1.1.3.4.3.3. Con:
          There is a difference between causing offense [70] and engaging in hate speech.

            1.1.3.4.3.3.1. Pro:
            Very offensive behavior can be prosecuted under harassment laws [71]. Speech restrictions are not necessary.

      1.1.3.5. Pro:
      Hate speech can inspire people to commit hateful actions.

        1.1.3.5.1. Con:
        Banning hate speech might exacerbate crimes as individuals may no longer have a legal outlet to express their hateful views non-violently.

          1.1.3.5.1.1. Pro:
          By banning hate speech, we drive the opinions and attitudes deemed hate speech underground, preventing them from being confronted and leading them towards becoming more extreme.

            1.1.3.5.1.1.1. Con:
            -> See 1.1.3.3.

            1.1.3.5.1.1.2. Pro:
            Increased scrutiny [72] on mainstream social media sites has led to alt-right and terrorist opinions flocking to niche and less policed platforms. This indicates that banning hate speech would likely not have much effect.

              1.1.3.5.1.1.2.1. Con:
              It is preferable for these extremists to retreat to smaller sites to express their harmful views, rather than using platforms, such as Twitter, where they are more likely to convert moderates.

                1.1.3.5.1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
                Fewer people [73] are likely to use sites like Parler or Gab.

                1.1.3.5.1.1.2.1.2. Con:
                Smaller platforms provide a safer space for radical groups where they can express themselves more freely [74]. These radicalized echo chambers are more dangerous than any potential influence on moderates.

              1.1.3.5.1.1.2.2. Pro:
              This leads to the creation of echo chambers, where no opposition exists to one's beliefs.

                1.1.3.5.1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
                Echo chambers [75] strengthen and radicalise beliefs once they are no longer challenged. The only solution is for the government to regulate this speech itself.

                  1.1.3.5.1.1.2.2.1.1. Pro:
                  Within echo chambers, the views of others are often caricatured and misrepresented [76], making insiders believe that their opponents are both uninformed and immoral.

                  1.1.3.5.1.1.2.2.1.2. Con:
                  People are more likely to escape misinformed hateful echo chambers once their opinions are engaged with.

                    1.1.3.5.1.1.2.2.1.2.1. Pro:
                    Echo chambers form due to an absence of dissenting opinions within a space. When these opinions are available, the effect of an echo chamber will likely dissipate.

                    1.1.3.5.1.1.2.2.1.2.2. Con:
                    People who engage in hate speech lack respect for those they consider to be the "other [77]" and are unlikely to engage in productive dialogue with them.

                1.1.3.5.1.1.2.2.2. Con:
                Echo chambers are most often the result of social media algorithms [78] that seek to inundate users with content they enjoy.

            1.1.3.5.1.1.3. Pro:
            Hate speech should not be addressed through censorship but, instead, deterred by the social consequences of saying offensive things.

              1.1.3.5.1.1.3.1. Con:
              Social consequences are of inconsistent and heavily dependent on the background and surrounding environment [79] of a speaker.

              1.1.3.5.1.1.3.2. Pro:
              People strive to be accepted. If the community does not tolerate [80] hate speech, people will adapt their views.

              1.1.3.5.1.1.3.3. Con:
              Social backlash has not worked [81] in "cancelling" people who engage in hate speech.

              1.1.3.5.1.1.3.4. Con:
              Social consequences do not deter offensive speech in general. Individuals who wish to communicate offensive ideas will continue to do so in circles where those ideas are accepted and encouraged.

                1.1.3.5.1.1.3.4.1. Pro:
                -> See 1.1.3.5.1.1.2.2.1.

                1.1.3.5.1.1.3.4.2. Con:
                Even if hate speech laws were in effect, people in these circles could simply refuse to report this speech.

              1.1.3.5.1.1.3.5. Pro:
              After Charlottesville's "Unite the Right" rally, white nationalist marchers faced a backlash [82] from many American communities.

                1.1.3.5.1.1.3.5.1. Con:
                The most lasting impact of the social backlash was when the people involved lost their jobs [83]. Businesses would need a legal basis to do this, which stems from the government.

                  1.1.3.5.1.1.3.5.1.1. Con:
                  The businesses had no legal basis to fire them and yet they still did.

                    1.1.3.5.1.1.3.5.1.1.1. Con:
                    It is legal [84] within the private sector to fire someone based on their political beliefs.

                1.1.3.5.1.1.3.5.2. Con:
                White nationalist marchers also faced social apathy.

                  1.1.3.5.1.1.3.5.2.1. Pro:
                  The President of the United States' first response [85] in the rally's aftermath was to blame both sides.

          1.1.3.5.1.2. Con:
          Those within circles where hate speech is normalized are more likely to justify or cause suffering and harm to minority groups due to the ‘dehumanization effect [86]’.

        1.1.3.5.2. Pro:
        Speech calling for a boycott of a business for political reasons can be both economically harmful and legal. [87]

          1.1.3.5.2.1. Con:
          US Courts have found that boycotts where the primary purpose is economic harm are illegal. [88]

        1.1.3.5.3. Pro:
        Widespread hate speech has been shown to massively increase [89] violence against minorities.

          1.1.3.5.3.1. Con:
          All sorts of speech, not just hateful kinds, can inspire people to act in unexpected and even harmful ways.

          1.1.3.5.3.2. Pro:
          Public figures are influential [90] and may enjoy mass support. They are likely to instigate violence through their followers if they carry out hate speech.

          1.1.3.5.3.3. Pro:
          Hate speech was instrumental in provoking violence in Rwanda [91].

          1.1.3.5.3.4. Pro:
          The rhetoric of the 2016 Trump presidential campaign led to an increase [92] in hate crime.

    1.1.4. Con:
    Banning free speech may ultimately lead to more harm in the form of angry and violent backlash.

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      Many restrictions on free speech are uncontroversial.

        1.1.4.1.1. Pro:
        Bans on certain extremist political parties are frequent in Europe [93] and face little opposition outside those extremist movements.

          1.1.4.1.1.1. Con:
          Some countries, such as Germany [94], have stopped banning these parties. This indicates a changing perspective on the legitimacy of these bans.

        1.1.4.1.2. Con:
        Interpretations of what constitutes offensive or misleading speech tend to fall across partisan lines, meaning many will feel that restrictions are meant to silence them.

          1.1.4.1.2.1. Pro:
          Twitter has faced backlash [95] over the way it uses its terms of service, indicating that even seemingly non-partisan rules can cause partisan anger.

      1.1.4.2. Con:
      Policies relating to the use of our freedoms should not be determined by the short-term opposition they may face.

        1.1.4.2.1. Con:
        If backlash is sufficiently well-supported, it may lead to the election of governments who actively seek to protect hateful and violent groups, thereby making the problem worse.

        1.1.4.2.2. Pro:
        The current opposition to free speech restrictions is a very contemporary phenomenon [96] and will not necessarily carry on in the future.

      1.1.4.3. Pro:
      The groups who tend to propagate the most hateful speech tend to be the most willing to resort to violence when silenced or challenged.

        1.1.4.3.1. Con:
        Hateful groups tend to be more violent when they feel their cause is being vindicated and widely supported [97]. Restricting their speech will prevent them from having that feeling.

          1.1.4.3.1.1. Pro:
          The recent rise in hate crimes in the US [98] is more correlated to the rise of prominent far-right politicians than to restrictions on free speech.

          1.1.4.3.1.2. Con:
          In these groups, violence is often justified by the common anger [99] of members towards authorities. Being silenced would likely make them angrier and more violent.

        1.1.4.3.2. Pro:
        Many far-right leaders use the suppression of their speech online as a pretext for violent rallies and riots [100].

          1.1.4.3.2.1. Pro:
          Such events provide the opportunity for hateful individuals to turn their violent rhetoric into action by attacking minorities [101] in these protests.

          1.1.4.3.2.2. Con:
          Far-right leaders have a violent ideology [102]. They would likely resort to these actions regardless of speech restrictions.

      1.1.4.4. Pro:
      Such backlash can undermine the initial intention behind the free speech restriction.

        1.1.4.4.1. Pro:
        Riots [103] emerged in UC Berkeley in the US when a talk by a right-wing activist was shut down.

        1.1.4.4.2. Pro:

          1.2.2.4.1.3. Con:

    1.2.3. Con:

  1.3. Pro:

    1.3.4. Con:
    There is room for abuse in many policies, but that does not make them bad policies.

      1.3.4.1. Con:
      Governments with good intentions may slip into more heavily utilizing free speech restrictions.

        1.3.4.1.1. Con:
        Most democratic countries have become less tyrannical and expanded civil rights despite limitations on speech.

          1.3.4.1.1.1. Con:
          The U.S. has one of the most lenient [302] free speech systems in the world.

            1.3.4.1.1.1.1. Con:
            America explicitly limited freedoms of speech with the first Alien and Sedition act. [303]

          1.3.4.1.1.2. Pro:
          Free speech is not absolute in the UK [304], where hate speech is illegal, and yet, it has not resulted in tyranny.

            1.3.4.1.1.2.1. Con:
            Article 10 of the UK Human Rights Act of 1998 [305] states "everyone has the right to freedom of expression."

              1.3.4.1.1.2.1.1. Con:
              This right is tempered by a clarification [306] that it "may be subject to formalities, conditions, restrictions or penalties as are prescribed by law and are necessary in a democratic society."

        1.3.4.1.2. Pro:
        Good policies should not rely on the hope that people will not abuse their powers.

          1.3.4.1.2.1. Con:
          All state power can be abused. Police and the carceral state are generally accepted as necessities even though police can and have at times abused their positions of power.

          1.3.4.1.2.2. Pro:
          Governments should be risk-averse when institutionalizing our rights, as it is hard to reclaim one's rights once they have been taken from them.

        1.3.4.1.3. Pro:
        Governments may unintentionally lean into suppressing the speech of those who do not agree with their approach to governance.

          1.3.4.1.3.1. Pro:
          -> See 1.3.2.2.1.

          1.3.4.1.3.2. Pro:
          If governments find that censoring more egregious opposition figures allows them to govern easier, they may continue censoring an increasing number of critics.

      1.3.4.2. Pro:
      While there is room for abuse [307] in many domestic surveillance laws, it is generally agreed that they are beneficial for national security.

        1.3.4.2.1. Con:
        There is widespread disagreement [308] about the necessity of the warrantless wiretapping of American citizens under the name of "national security".

        1.3.4.2.2. Pro:
        -> See 1.2.1.2.3.1.1.2.1.

  1.4. Con:
  Unfettered free speech could cause mass misinformation.

    1.4.1. Pro:
    Limitless free speech allows the spread of false and dangerous information.

      1.4.1.1. Con:
      It is likely that individuals will continue to believe and spread lies amongst themselves after misinformation is banned.

        1.4.1.1.1. Pro:
        So long as widespread mistrust [309] in traditional media and political parties persists, it is likely that demand for fake news will remain high.

        1.4.1.1.2. Con:
        The most pernicious misinformation comes from large, centralized groups. Free speech laws can have a real positive impact [310] just by targeting those powerful voices.

        1.4.1.1.3. Pro:
        If misinformation spreads outside of the confines of easily and widely monitored public spaces like social media, it may be even harder [311] to stop.

          1.4.1.1.3.1. Pro:
          Those who spread fake news may claim that being censored is an indication that their claims are true [312]. This may make followers more fervent in their support.

      1.4.1.2. Pro:
      Misinformation can undermine a state's stability and significantly harm its citizens.

        1.4.1.2.1. Pro:
        Due to the dangerous propaganda claiming vaccines cause autism, there has been an increase in cases of diseases [313] that were once eradicated.

        1.4.1.2.2. Pro:
        Unregulated speech makes the public vulnerable to subversive propaganda from foreign powers [314] that can swing referendums and elections to act in their own interests.

          1.4.1.2.2.1. Con:
          Foreign powers attempting to use subversive propaganda are probably not going to be controlled by free speech regulations.

          1.4.1.2.2.2. Pro:
          Some countries, like Russia, use 'bots' [315] and 'fake news' content farms [316] to alter political opinion in other countries.

        1.4.1.2.3. Con:
        Misinformation often seeks to reinforce views [317] that are already held by many. A better solution to this problem would be to devise new ways to change misinformed opinions.

          1.4.1.2.3.1. Con:
          Conspiracy theories, like QAnon [318], do not relate to pre-existing beliefs but rather seek to radicalize existing mistrust. If these views are banned, distrust will not escalate to extremes.

          1.4.1.2.3.2. Pro:
          The lies by the Trump campaign claiming to have won the election are meant to justify the desire of supporters [319] for Trump to remain in office. Banning these lies would not change that impulse.

        1.4.1.2.4. Con:
        The prevailing opinion in a society is not necessarily correct. Silencing dissenters would simply entrench falsehoods.

          1.4.1.2.4.1. Pro:
          For many decades, the myth of 'exceptionalism' [320] among colonial powers justified imperialist action. It required speech and protest at home and abroad to shift this offensive opinion.

          1.4.1.2.4.2. Con:
          When one challenges prevailing opinions, they ought to ensure their arguments are factual. Therefore, speech restrictions would not exclude all opposing ideas, but merely their crudest formulations.

        1.4.1.2.5. Pro:
        Lies can greatly change the support for political causes, as seen by the lying during the Brexit referendum [321].

        1.4.1.2.6. Pro:
        The perpetuation of climate change denial could have dire consequences.

          1.4.1.2.6.1. Pro:
          Climate change denial makes it harder [322] to make policies to fight climate change.

            1.4.1.2.6.1.1. Pro:
            People are less likely to pressure governments to invest in climate change policies if they do not feel that it is a threat.

            1.4.1.2.6.1.2. Pro:
            Significant political capital is instead used [323] to argue that climate change is real rather than debating different policies.

            1.4.1.2.6.1.3. Con:
            Corporations spend a lot of money spreading this misinformation [324] and lobbying governments to support it. They would likely prevent the enforcement of speech rules against them.

          1.4.1.2.6.2. Con:
          Scientists are currently changing their language due to fears of a backlash [325]. A ban on climate change denial would likely increase this backlash.

            1.4.1.2.6.2.1. Con:
            Given that many do not understand the science behind climate change, widespread education [326] is needed to make them understand that climate change is a major issue.

            1.4.1.2.6.2.2. Pro:
            A backlash against scientists [327] can lead to voters supporting politicians who explicitly claim climate change to be a hoax.

            1.4.1.2.6.2.3. Pro:
            This backlash often leads politicians to use sanitized, abstract language [328] when describing the harms of climate change.

      1.4.1.3. Pro:
      Unrestricted misinformation [329] can lead to widespread belief in lies.

        1.4.1.3.1. Pro:
        Limitless free speech may lead people who are uneducated on a subject to form rushed, wrong, and misleading opinions. [330]

          1.4.1.3.1.1. Pro:
          People who are uninformed on a topic would be less likely to believe dangerous sources if those sources were not shared in the first place.

          1.4.1.3.1.2. Con:
          No authority should determine [331] when someone has an adequate amount of information on a topic to be allowed to comment on it.

        1.4.1.3.2. Con:
        Limited free speech can make the truth harder to find, leading to individuals and society as a whole being misled about important issues.

        1.4.1.3.3. Pro:
        When misinformation is banned, it will be harder for lies to spread. [332]

          1.4.1.3.3.1. Pro:
          Misinformation spreads fastest on social media, where individuals see the same lie coming from multiple sources on their feed [333]. When these lies are banned, their visibility will fall.

          1.4.1.3.3.2. Con:
          It will be impossible [334] to find and censor all websites spreading this fake news. People will still consume it.

            1.4.1.3.3.2.1. Pro:
            Governments around the world have failed to close down illegal websites, like PirateBay [335] or ZBooks, who maintain a large number of users. It is likely that some news sources will remain active.

              1.4.1.3.3.2.1.1. Con:
              People read the news every day [336]. Relying on websites that are frequently removed or are hard to access may be too much hassle for many users.

            1.4.1.3.3.2.2. Con:
            Even if people still have access to misinformation outlets, few who do not already use them are likely to discover them, ultimately minimizing their influence.

        1.4.1.3.4. Con:
        The truth is not always clear and obvious. This means that censoring misinformation will likely be difficult and controversial.

          1.4.1.3.4.1. Pro:
          Science rarely provides clear answers. Findings must be interpreted, particularly when they are being used in political discussions.

            1.4.1.3.4.1.1. Pro:
            It has been argued that statistics can be used to prove almost anything [337].

            1.4.1.3.4.1.2. Con:
            Some interpretations of data are better than others. There exist fairly objective ways [338] to distinguish between plausible interpretations and partisan ones.

            1.4.1.3.4.1.3. Con:
            There still exist unambiguous scientific truths [339].

              1.4.1.3.4.1.3.1. Pro:
              Climate change denial is simply false [340]. There is no merit to it.

              1.4.1.3.4.1.3.2. Pro:
              The 'science' used to justify white supremacist talking points is unambiguously false [341].

              1.4.1.3.4.1.3.3. Con:
              There are a large number of questions science cannot answer. [342]

          1.4.1.3.4.2. Con:
          There are objective truths that must form the basis of political discussions.

          1.4.1.3.4.3. Con:
          Any backlash will likely be short-lived if censorship is able to produce a more stable and fact-based form of politics [343].

    1.4.2. Pro:
    Media outlets often purposefully or inadvertently perpetuate falsehoods when left unregulated.

      1.4.2.1. Pro:
      Over time, false claims erode trust in all news sources. This allows the media to polarize the electorate by publishing alternative, misleading views of reality.

        1.4.2.1.1. Pro:
        Laws like the Fairness Doctrine [344] are limits on speech that can prevent misinformation. It did not lead to other restrictions on speech but instead made news organizations more accountable.

        1.4.2.1.2. Con:
        This problem of trust provides media organizations with an incentive to ensure their coverage is factual. Otherwise, viewers may leave for other outlets.

          1.4.2.1.2.1. Con:
          Media outlets can spread misinformation and increase their popularity by framing their coverage as partisan.

            1.4.2.1.2.1.1. Pro:
            Outlets like Fox News [345] can openly spread false information because they encourage their viewers to distrust rival organizations who accuse them of lying.

          1.4.2.1.2.2. Pro:
          News organizations are often quick to point out the mistakes made by their competitors, meaning that if one spreads misinformation, they are likely to be publicly pilloried.

        1.4.2.1.3. Pro:
        Without trust in media, individuals will gravitate towards outlets that support their pre-existing political views.

          1.4.2.1.3.1. Con:
          Most people already follow outlets that support their political views [346].

            1.4.2.1.3.1.1. Con:
            Most people want unbiased news [347]. It is only when people feel there are no trustworthy options that they default to biased ones.

            1.4.2.1.3.1.2. Pro:
            People are unlikely to trust news sources that heavily criticize their views and favorite leaders.

          1.4.2.1.3.2. Pro:
          Readers will ascribe some negative, partisan motives to an outlet's misinformation. This will make them move to other sources who 'fight against' [348] those outlets, increasing partisanship.

      1.4.2.2. Con:
      Limiting free speech can limit the ability of the media to report.

        1.4.2.2.1. Pro:
        Laws, like ones against defamation [349], can make media outlets fear lawsuits and prompt them to avoid publishing information that should be known by the public.

          1.4.2.2.1.1. Con:
          If defamation laws did not exist, media corporations would not be deterred from defaming individuals and groups.

            1.4.2.2.1.1.1. Pro:
            Since media corporations have political interests [350], they are likely to defame political opponents of the groups that fund them.

            1.4.2.2.1.1.2. Con:
            The free market [351] regulates the media: news corporations lose credibility if they are caught airing and publishing lies.

          1.4.2.2.1.2. Con:
          In Europe, self-censorship is mostly caused by the belligerent political opponents of journalists [352], not state censorship. Free speech restriction would do little to change this.

          1.4.2.2.1.3. Pro:
          Wide-ranging defamation laws led to mass self-censorship of the media [353] in Greece.

        1.4.2.2.2. Con:
        Restrictions on freedom of speech laws help to make news outlets accountable for spreading misinformation.

          1.4.2.2.2.1. Pro:
          Fox News was forced to pay $28 million [354] in compensation to a judge for spreading misinformation about his work habits.

      1.4.2.3. Con:
      Often, readers mistake [355] the opinion desks at media outlets, which are allowed to spin information, with their news desks, which must be more factual.

        1.4.2.3.1. Con:
        Limits should be placed on these opinion desks by their parent news companies, as they often spread misinformation [356].

        1.4.2.3.2. Pro:
        At Fox News, there is a wide disparity [357] between the type of content that comes from their opinion and news desks.

        1.4.2.3.3. Con:
        News media outlets often purposely do a poor job of distinguishing [358] their editorial and news content for their viewers.

      1.4.2.4. Pro:
      There are numerous examples of media outlets lying to their viewers.

        1.4.2.4.1. Pro:
        The Daily Show [359] published a list of 50 lies that were spread by Fox News.

        1.4.2.4.2. Pro:
        Many conservative outlets purport that mainstream outlets like CNN and MSNBC mislead the public [360] about current events.

        1.4.2.4.3. Con:
        Often, these are not lies but ideological spins (p. 2) [361] based on the partisan leanings of the news organization.

      1.4.2.5. Pro:
      Media outlets often have an incentive to do so to excite their audience [362] and retain their viewer base.

    1.4.3. Con:
    Free speech is the best way to prevent misinformation.

      1.4.3.1. Con:
      Most people do not have the required skill set to discern manipulative speech from good faith speech.

        1.4.3.1.1. Con:
        If people can be considered literate enough to vote, they should be considered literate enough to distinguish between truth and falsehood.

          1.4.3.1.1.1. Pro:
          It is the responsibility of every citizen to be well informed. Information literacy should determine which speech an individual accepts as credible, not which speech is allowed.

          1.4.3.1.1.2. Con:
          The right to vote is not based on anything beyond age. Literacy does not factor into it.

        1.4.3.1.2. Pro:
        People often believe claims as a result of rhetorical flourishes and cognitive biases [363]. Therefore, the truth of a claim is not necessarily what leads people to believe it.

          1.4.3.1.2.1. Pro:
          Hitler's popularity has often been credited in part to his charismatic public speaking [364] abilities, which could have helped bolster the lies he was telling.

          1.4.3.1.2.2. Con:
          Rhetoric is unlikely to change people's opinions. It is more effectively used to excite people [365] who already share biased views.

        1.4.3.1.3. Pro:
        Often, falsehoods are justified by supposedly 'scientific' methods [366], which require technical knowledge to debunk.

        1.4.3.1.4. Pro:
        Many sources spread misinformation under the veneer of 'credibility' [367], making it difficult for people to discredit them.

          1.4.3.1.4.1. Pro:
          People tend to use news sources that are familiar to them [368] and that people around them use. Consequently, it is often easy to believe misinformation.

        1.4.3.1.5. Con:
        The solution to this problem should not be to restrict speech, but rather to invest more in media literacy training [369] in schools.

          1.4.3.1.5.1. Con:
          This would be akin to treating an injury, rather than preventing the injury from occurring all together.

          1.4.3.1.5.2. Pro:
          There exist many resources [370] for teaching media literacy as some schools around the world do already. Expanding access to media literacy would not be difficult.

      1.4.3.2. Pro:
      Government falsehoods and abuses of power can be best countered by citizens spreading the truth.

        1.4.3.2.1. Con:
        Limiting free speech can ensure that correct information is not drowned out or suppressed.

          1.4.3.2.1.1. Pro:
          When free speech is limited, it is less likely that people will miss the truth in the middle of a very large deluge of incorrect facts.

            1.4.3.2.1.1.1. Pro:
            If people know that there exists a large amount of fake news, they may be reticent to trust [371] the news they see. This may make them more skeptical of the truth.

          1.4.3.2.1.2. Con:
          Free speech limits can frequently be used as a tool specifically to ensure that the truth is drowned out and controlled.

            1.4.3.2.1.2.1. Con:
            It is more powerful for states to use media and propaganda so as to discredit the truth [372] rather than trying to hide it. Speech restrictions are, therefore, ineffective.

            1.4.3.2.1.2.2. Pro:
            Proposed restrictions on gender studies in Poland [373] would give the government a greater ability to control and drown out non-heteronormative perspectives.

        1.4.3.2.2. Con:
        Governments are often seen as a highly credible information source, so it may be difficult to debunk their falsehoods.

          1.4.3.2.2.1. Pro:
          Over 40% of Americans [374] still believe that the US Army found weapons of mass destruction in Iraq, despite the CIA and other international agencies publicly concluding that this was not true.

          1.4.3.2.2.2. Con:
          Trust in government is rapidly declining [375], so there is likely to be skepticism against government-spread information.

        1.4.3.2.3. Pro:
        Laws restricting free speech often allow governments to lie [376] to their citizens.

          1.4.3.2.3.1. Con:
          Representatives have limited free speech when speaking in the name of the government since they are not speaking in their own name. Therefore, free speech laws are irrelevant.

          1.4.3.2.3.2. Pro:
          The Bush administration knowingly lied [377] about Iraqi weapons of mass destruction to justify the invasion of Iraq.

          1.4.3.2.3.3. Con:
          Free speech allows for the existence of websites such as Politifact [378] to fact-check the accuracy of government official's claims.

        1.4.3.2.4. Pro:
        -> See 1.3.3.2.

      1.4.3.3. Con:
      Protected free speech includes protecting the right to lie.

        1.4.3.3.1. Pro:
        Limits on free speech require punishment for crimes like defamation [379], while absolute free speech would have to allow for complete free expression, including mistruths.

        1.4.3.3.2. Con:
        Lies can be handled by having fact-checkers on public websites, as social media sites [380] have recently incorporated.

          1.4.3.3.2.1. Pro:
          Fact-checking can also make correct information more widely accessible, thereby diminishing the reach of lies.

          1.4.3.3.2.2. Con:
          Existing fact-checkers have done little to mitigate widespread lying on political issues.

            1.4.3.3.2.2.1. Pro:
            Fact-checkers often appear highly partisan [381]. Individuals who believe political lies may ignore them.

            1.4.3.3.2.2.2. Con:
            The current forms of fact-checking may be highly imperfect. However, they can be improved [382].

            1.4.3.3.2.2.3. Pro:
            Donald Trump lied [383] extensively to citizens during his Presidency and has faced no consequences.

          1.4.3.3.2.3. Con:
          What is deemed 'false' often reflects the political views [384] of the fact-checkers.

            1.4.3.3.2.3.1. Con:
            Fact-checkers often consult an array of sources [385] from around the political spectrum to decide what content should be considered to be a lie.

              1.4.3.3.2.3.1.1. Con:
              At best, this method balances biases - this does not mean that it produces objective or truthful results.

            1.4.3.3.2.3.2. Pro:
            Fact-checkers can lie, twist the truth, or lie by omission. There will have to be fact-checkers of fact-checkers [386], and thus fact-checkers all the way down. Ultimately everyone must be their own fact-checkers.

        1.4.3.3.3. Pro:
        Many worry about the increasing difficulty of identifying 'deepfakes [387]', which are highly realistic but doctored videos that can be used to make people believe lies.

          1.4.3.3.3.1. Pro:
          Even if these videos are eventually fact-checked, many may not see these corrections and will continue to believe the videos they have seen.

      1.4.3.4. Pro:
      The marketplace of ideas [388] requires freedom of speech to ensure that ideas are presented to the population. Once these ideas are spread, the people then decide their validity.

        1.4.3.4.1. Con:
        It is often very hard to change people's minds, and free speech typically fails to do this.

          1.4.3.4.1.1. Pro:
          People often believe that which validates their pre-existing opinions [389].

            1.4.3.4.1.1.1. Pro:
            An example of this is Alex Jones and his channel Infowars [390], from which he spreads conspiracy theories such as the claim that the Sandy Hook massacre [391] did not occur.

            1.4.3.4.1.1.2. Con:
            People often change their opinions [392] regarding social and political issues. It is just a slow process: allowing free rational debate will, over time, produce a marketplace of ideas.

          1.4.3.4.1.2. Con:
          If people could not talk about their potentially mistaken beliefs, it would be unlikely that they would ever be called out on them by experts.

            1.4.3.4.1.2.1. Con:
            Ridicule by experts seems to have little effect on the promulgation of conspiracy theories [393] and anti-science [394] sentiment.

            1.4.3.4.1.2.2. Pro:
            Most people are likelier to change their opinion if confronted on it by an expert than a random person.

        1.4.3.4.2. Pro:
        Free speech allows all points of view to be addressed, allowing people to decide for themselves what is wrong and what is right.

        1.4.3.4.3. Pro:
        The scientific method relies on the ability to make hypotheses [395] that may or may not be correct.

          1.4.3.4.3.1. Pro:
          Scientists make incorrect assumptions to get closer to the truth all the time. However, they never treat their assumptions as truth and spread them before they are proven (Section 3.4 [396]).

  1.5. Pro:
  Free speech is a fundamental human right.

    1.5.1. Con:
    No rights can be absolute.

      1.5.1.1. Pro:
      -> See 1.1.6.3.

      1.5.1.2. Con:
      Rights can be absolute [397] and have been recognized as such.

        1.5.1.2.1. Pro:
        The right to life is often absolute. [398]

          1.5.1.2.1.1. Con:
          Most countries have policies and laws that do not respect the right to life.

            1.5.1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
            There is no country that does not give lethal weapons to its military and police.

            1.5.1.2.1.1.2. Pro:
            The UN's near-unanimous international agreement, which enumerates absolute rights, does not include the right to life [399].

            1.5.1.2.1.1.3. Pro:
            53% [400] of countries still have the death penalty.

            1.5.1.2.1.1.4. Con:
            Most of these laws apply to extreme situations. The countries generally respect the right to life.

          1.5.1.2.1.2. Pro:
          Rights to life and free speech are similar as they are necessary for individuals to have autonomy and build their own identities.

            1.5.1.2.1.2.1. Con:
            Many cultures disagree [401] over which rights are most fundamental for one to have autonomy and identities.

            1.5.1.2.1.2.2. Con:
            No meaningful hierarchy of rights can exist because all rights are interconnected [402]. We require all of them to live meaningful lives.

              1.5.1.2.1.2.2.1. Con:
              Being interconnected does not preclude some rights from being more important than others.

            1.5.1.2.1.2.3. Pro:
            Therefore, there must exist a set of core 'absolute' rights like those to life, bodily autonomy, and freedom of speech.

        1.5.1.2.2. Pro:
        Many international organizations have recognized the existence of absolute rights.

          1.5.1.2.2.1. Pro:
          In Australia [403], both the freedom from slavery and the right to recognition before the law are considered absolute rights.

          1.5.1.2.2.2. Pro:
          Almost every [404] nation in the world expressly agreed on 7 absolute rights [405] when signing the UN's ICCPR.

            1.5.1.2.2.2.1. Con:
            The UN's near-unanimous treaty enumerating absolute rights does not include the right to expression [406].

          1.5.1.2.2.3. Con:
          Many countries' laws and constitutions allow for the limitation of fundamental rights.

            1.5.1.2.2.3.1. Pro:
            In the US [407], "laws encroaching on a fundamental right generally must pass strict scrutiny to be upheld as constitutional."

            1.5.1.2.2.3.2. Pro:
            In UK law, many fundamental rights are limited [408].

            1.5.1.2.2.3.3. Con:
            It does not follow from the fact that many countries restrict their citizen's rights, that it is just to do so.

          1.5.1.2.2.4. Pro:
          The European Convention on Human Rights [409] expressly declared some rights to be absolute.

      1.5.1.3. Pro:
      Almost all rights can be suspended or revoked, if a government decides to do so, and thus cannot be inherently absolute.

        1.5.1.3.1. Pro:
        You can lose your right to bear arms by committing a felony. [410]

        1.5.1.3.2. Pro:
        Many rights are suspended during times of war and conflict. [411]

        1.5.1.3.3. Con:
        Such suspensions are often allowed in reasonable and pre-established circumstances, meaning they cannot be done arbitrarily.

    1.5.2. Pro:
    Free speech is a necessary prerequisite for the realization of other rights.

      1.5.2.1. Pro:
      -> See 1.2.4.2.1.

      1.5.2.2. Con:
      Not all opinions a person can express necessarily need to be expressed for them to have full access to other rights.

        1.5.2.2.1. Pro:
        Degrading others does not materially improve one's life.

        1.5.2.2.2. Con:
        The ability to freely express opinions is necessary for dignity. The choice of what to express is personal.

          1.5.2.2.2.1. Pro:
          Being able to express one's opinions is symbolic of freedom. Since freedom is the cornerstone of dignity [412], freedom of speech is a fundamental right.

      1.5.2.3. Pro:
      Free speech allows for the open and free practice of religion [413], conscience, and thought.

        1.5.2.3.1. Pro:
        Free thought and the practice are religion are often communal activities: people need to openly discuss their views with others to develop and refine them. That requires free speech.

        1.5.2.3.2. Con:
        Free thought can still occur where some restrictions of speech exist. The restrictions merely prevent the expression of decidedly immoral or harmful ideas.

      1.5.2.4. Con:
      Free speech cannot be considered prior to other rights.

        1.5.2.4.1. Con:
        One right being absolute does not mean that other rights cannot be absolute and therefore of equal importance.

          1.5.2.4.1.1. Pro:
          -> See 1.5.1.2.2.1.

          1.5.2.4.1.2. Con:
          The demands of protecting rights often conflict (p. 381) [414]. Therefore, no right can be absolute as there must be some way to adjudicate contradictory rights-based claims.

            1.5.2.4.1.2.1. Con:
            There is no objective way to adjudicate these claims. Therefore, free speech is a prerequisite to balance different rights, insofar as it allows communities to set the rules together.

            1.5.2.4.1.2.2. Pro:
            This is why the incitement of violence is illegal [415]: in a situation where one's right to physical security is compromised by someone exercising their free speech, the right to security is prioritized.

        1.5.2.4.2. Pro:
        The right to free speech is not more important than the right to life or liberty.

          1.5.2.4.2.1. Con:
          Even if freedom of speech is not a prerequisite for other rights, it can be derived as a necessary component of other rights.

            1.5.2.4.2.1.1. Pro:
            If we fundamentally own our bodies and our minds, then we must enjoy the natural and fundamental right to think and say what we want. This self-ownership is the axiom on which rest all of our natural rights.

              1.5.2.4.2.1.1.1. Con:
              This is not necessarily the case: the right to say something differs from the right to believe or think something, in that speech has a relationship to others that thought and belief do not.

            1.5.2.4.2.1.2. Pro:
            The right to free speech itself can be derived from the right to information [416]: we have a right to know the beliefs of others.

              1.5.2.4.2.1.2.1. Con:
              Freedom of speech is used to contravene one's right to information when it takes the form of false advertisement and fraud, since the information is incorrect.

              1.5.2.4.2.1.2.2. Con:
              People do not inherently have the right to information. People should not be forced to share their beliefs and opinions.

              1.5.2.4.2.1.2.3. Pro:
              We can only freely form and express opinions if we can hear the views and opinions of others. Free expression is always limited when our access to information is limited.

            1.5.2.4.2.1.3. Pro:
            -> See 1.5.2.3.

            1.5.2.4.2.1.4. Con:
            If the importance of free speech is tied to its relation to other rights, then free speech need not be absolute. Instead, speech must be allowed only insofar as it facilitates other rights.

          1.5.2.4.2.2. Pro:
          One can only have free speech if they do not fear death, incarceration or other forms of social or economic sanctions for their actions. Speech is, therefore, of secondary importance.

          1.5.2.4.2.3. Pro:
          Free speech presupposes that one already has control of their bodies and lives. As such, rights to bodily autonomy and self-determination outweigh speech concerns.

        1.5.2.4.3. Pro:
        Rights do not exist in a vacuum and thus cannot be weighed in abstract terms. Instead, evaluating which rights are more important should be judged on a case-by-case basis.

          1.5.2.4.3.1. Pro:
          When rights clash, we typically make ethical decisions on the basis of which right is quantitatively less harmed, independent of what those rights are.

            1.5.2.4.3.1.1. Pro:
            Many countries restrict choice by placing sin taxes [417] on or outright banning harmful products. These are justified by the greater harm imposed on the health of individuals who consume them.

            1.5.2.4.3.1.2. Con:
            It is often difficult to quantitatively measure [418] how much of one's rights are infringed by a certain action. It is more useful to define rights in terms of their relative importance.

          1.5.2.4.3.2. Con:
          Certain rights, like bodily autonomy or freedom of choice [419], are more important than others because they allow humans to choose how to live their own lives.

            1.5.2.4.3.2.1. Con:
            Bodily autonomy is regularly violated in a variety [420] of widely accepted laws.

    1.5.3. Pro:
    Free speech is enshrined as a fundamental right in the traditions and constitutions of many countries.

      1.5.3.1. Pro:
      The Universal Declaration of Human Rights recognises free speech [421] as a right.

      1.5.3.2. Con:
      While you have the freedom to say whatever you like, it does not mean you have the right to say whatever you like.

        1.5.3.2.1. Pro:
        One always has the freedom to break the law, but that does not mean that they have the right to break the law.

          1.5.3.2.1.1. Con:
          For one to break the law, one must first agree to follow it in the first place. A law that is imposed by force cannot define rights because a relationship of force is, in the first place, a violation of natural rights.

      1.5.3.3. Con:
      -> See 1.5.1.2.2.3.

      1.5.3.4. Con:
      The content and roles of human rights in society are not universal. Therefore, one cannot categorically say that any specific right is 'fundamental'.

        1.5.3.4.1. Con:
        Fundamental rights are those that are inalienable [422], or "unable to be restrained or repealed by human laws."

        1.5.3.4.2. Pro:
        There is heavy disagreement [423] around the world regarding which sets of rights are more important than others.

        1.5.3.4.3. Pro:
        Opinions on what is a fundamental right are different in different cultures [424].

      1.5.3.5. Pro:
      Freedom of speech is an American ideal and is defended by the Constitution [425].

        1.5.3.5.1. Pro:
        -> See 1.3.4.1.1.1.

        1.5.3.5.2. Con:
        Freedom of speech is limited in specific cases, even in the United States [426], like the incitement of panic or violence, and the making of credible threats of violence.
