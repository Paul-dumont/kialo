Discussion Name:
Should Comprehensive Sex Education be Mandatory?

1. Thesis:
Comprehensive Sexual Education should not be mandatory in Liberal Democracies.

  1.1. Pro:
  Mandatory comprehensive sex education will have unintended consequences.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Comprehensive sex education may lead to some students being bullied.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      For example, teaching students about the symptoms of puberty may draw unwanted attention to students currently experiencing those symptoms.

        1.1.1.1.1. Con:
        Even the simplest forms of sex education tend to include discussions about puberty and its symptoms, such that comprehensive sex education is unlikely to increase attention on this.

        1.1.1.1.2. Con:
        Or it could increase empathy and decrease taboo, decreasing bullying for those reasons.

      1.1.1.2. Pro:
      If schools fail to create a safe and trusting environment within the classroom, students are liable to ask questions or disclose experiences which make them the targets of bullying.

        1.1.1.2.1. Con:
        The counterfactual here is that those students do not disclose these experiences at all, which is likely to be hugely alienating and burdensome for them.

        1.1.1.2.2. Con:
        Students are likely to disclose information that can be a motivating factor for bullying in other forms of sexual education as well.

          1.1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
          If CSE provides the school with professionals well-trained in sexual education, they are more likely to intervene successfully to prevent and deal with abuse than if that education is conducted by teachers lacking that training.

      1.1.1.3. Con:
      Alternatively, with encouragement from teachers, and an open and broad curriculum, this sex education could result in positive discussion and engagement between students.

      1.1.1.4. Con:
      Even insofar as this is true in the short term, comprehensive sex education is likely to improve bullying in the long run.

      1.1.1.5. Con:
      Non comprehensive sexual education programs often assume all students are cisgender and heterosexual and some even include unfavorable [1] views of LGBT relationships and individuals. That is more likely to encourage the bullying of LGBT students.

      1.1.1.6. Con:
      Bullying is going to happen most of the times regardless. CSE can actually promote a greater understanding and a destigmatization of sex among students.

    1.1.2. Con:
    If the benefits outweigh those unintended consequences, CSE is still a better option.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Sexual education to young age children can make them initiate sexual relationships earlier.

      1.1.3.1. Con:
      Sex is a huge part of life and should be taught in depth throughout a child's youth. One huge misconception [2] is that kids are "too young" to learn these things but in reality puberty can begin as early [3] as 7 years old for girls and 9 years old for boys.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      Multiple [4] studies [5] have found that sex education does not encourage earlier sexual relations, but rather delays them.

        1.1.3.2.1. Pro:
        Comprehensive sexual education can help [6] youth
        delay onset of sexual activity, reduce the frequency
        of sexual activity, reduce the number of sexual
        partners, and increase condom and contraceptive
        use.

        1.1.3.2.2. Pro:
        14 out of 23 [7] effective CSE programs demonstrated a statistically significant delay in the timing of first sex.

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      Being exposed to facts about sex might make children curious to try it.

        1.1.3.3.1. Con:
        Whether or not children receive CSE, they are aware and curious about sex.

          1.1.3.3.1.1. Pro:
          Animals seem to figure out how to have sex (at much younger relative ages than humans), without any education.

          1.1.3.3.1.2. Pro:
          Given this, it is important that children’s innate curiosity and desire to experiment is coupled with information and education that enables them to do so safely (e.g. consent education).

          1.1.3.3.1.3. Pro:
          CSE does not come close [8] to covering the type of imagery or topics that children will have already been exposed to prior to even taking the class - this exposure in our modern day is simply unavoidable.

            1.1.3.3.1.3.1. Con:
            References to sex in the media will be regarded by kids as something belonging to the adult world that is unknown and not for kids. When it is brought into the classroom, however, it is suddenly brought into the kids' domain. There's a difference.

            1.1.3.3.1.3.2. Pro:
            Research shows that children are regularly exposed to online material about sex.

              1.1.3.3.1.3.2.1. Pro:
              A national survey [9] in the US found that 42% percent of young people on the internet had been exposed to online pornography in the past year, of which 66% reported unwanted exposure (from ads, for instance).

              1.1.3.3.1.3.2.2. Pro:
              In an international study, an antivirus software company concluded that kids start watching porn online as early as the age of 6 years old [10].

              1.1.3.3.1.3.2.3. Pro:
              An extensive study from an antivirus software company found that as a single category, Pornography [11] (11,35%), is the “Top Web Categories that Most Interest Kids”.

            1.1.3.3.1.3.3. Pro:
            Studies assert the preponderance of topics surrounding sex consumed by children in traditional media.

              1.1.3.3.1.3.3.1. Pro:
              Rihanna, amongst many other modern artists with young fans, is famed for sexualised music videos [12], with explicit imagery that millions of teenagers will have watched. Her video for S&M, for instance, has been viewed over 93 million times.

              1.1.3.3.1.3.3.2. Pro:
              On average [13], teenagers hear or see 15,000 references to sex on TV in a year.

              1.1.3.3.1.3.3.3. Pro:
              According to studies, over 75% [14] of primetime TV programs contain sexual content.

        1.1.3.3.2. Con:
        Sex is already ubiquitous in media [15] and advertising [16], therefore most children have seen some portrayals of it or know about it.

          1.1.3.3.2.1. Pro:
          -> See 1.1.3.3.1.3.

      1.1.3.4. Con:
      At least with comprehensive sex education, if children do make the decision to have sex, they will have full information about contraception and healthy sexual relationships.

        1.1.3.4.1. Pro:
        As the definitions of abstinence vary, even when young adults define themselves as abstinent, they can still engage in sexual practices that require information about consent and protection against STDs.

          1.1.3.4.1.1. Pro:
          Research [17] surveying university students found that oral sex and genital stimulation were considered 'abstinent practices' by some students. In the case of oral sex, 39% believed so.

            1.1.3.4.1.1.1. Pro:
            Students were also more likely to classify a sexual practice or behaviour as abstinent [18] if orgasm did not occur.

      1.1.3.5. Pro:
      This may be particularly true under comprehensive sex education, which teaches sexual pleasure alongside basic biological reproductive function.

      1.1.3.6. Con:
      While CSE might let individuals know about sex, it isn't the cause of sexuality itself which instead is based off a biological urge.

        1.1.3.6.1. Pro:
        -> See 1.1.3.2.

        1.1.3.6.2. Pro:
        A study [19] in the US found that among teens ages 15-17 who have had sex, nearly 80% did not receive any formal sex education before they lost their virginity.

        1.1.3.6.3. Pro:
        Cognition around sexual desire [20], including sexual thoughts and sexual attraction, emerges in early puberty [21] due to a myriad of factors [22], including neuroendocrine and psychological changes during puberty.

          1.1.3.6.3.1. Pro:
          At the age of  9-11,  16% of boys and girls report “thinking about sex [23]” while at 11-12, the 25% do, according to research in different countries. In some studies, that number raises to 66% at the age of  13-14.

          1.1.3.6.3.2. Pro:
          A study interviewing adults about their sexual memories, around 25% of men and 6,6% reported to sexually stimulate [24] themselves in solitary at the age of 11-12.

      1.1.3.7. Pro:
      Abstinence-only programs result [25] in a delay in sex and reduces present sexual activity.

        1.1.3.7.1. Con:
        Sexuality is an important element [26] for the psychological well being of an individual. Provision of CSE improves [27] sexual satisfaction within individuals.

        1.1.3.7.2. Con:
        Although the abstinence-only program in that study [28] did work, there are crucial differences between that program and the abstinence-only programs advocated by groups such as the American College of Pediatricians [29].

          1.1.3.7.2.1. Pro:
          The study's abstinence-only program [30] only advocated delaying sex until "until a time later in life," but the ACP explicitly endorses "abstinence-until-marriage sex education [31]."

          1.1.3.7.2.2. Pro:
          The study's abstinence-only program [32] only discouraged "vaginal, anal, and oral intercourse," whereas the ACP's proposed program also discourages mutual masturbation and outercourse.

        1.1.3.7.3. Pro:
        Delaying sex leads [33] to more satisfying relationships.

          1.1.3.7.3.1. Con:
          Having more sexual partners may provide a variety of benefits for an individual.

            1.1.3.7.3.1.1. Con:
            Young people partaking in casual sexuality can lead [34] to a variety of problems. Since this is based around the idea of multiple sexual partners, intimacy and closeness is hard to achieve and in turn this is "negatively associated with psychological wellbeing and positively correlated with psychological distress".

            1.1.3.7.3.1.2. Pro:
            Having more sexual partners may expand people's horizons about sex as well as giving them a better comparison between what good or bad sex is, since they have something to compare it with.

            1.1.3.7.3.1.3. Pro:
            Having more sexual partners could possibly [35] increase the fertility of women.

        1.1.3.7.4. Pro:
        Sex can be an emotional intense experience that many young teens are unable to handle. Delaying sex can allow teens to mature more so to properly be able to internalize and handle the emotions tied to sex.

        1.1.3.7.5. Pro:
        Studies [36] show that cancer [37] of the uterus is more prevalent in women who began sexual relationships early in life. There is also a high rate of venereal diseases associated with early sex.

        1.1.3.7.6. Pro:
        Early sex is linked to an increase [38] in delinquent behavior.

        1.1.3.7.7. Pro:
        Sex as a young teenager can have a negative effect [39] on mood and emotions into adulthood.

    1.1.4. Pro:
    CSE will incur additional responsibility on teachers, by making them responsible for adolescents developing a healthy relationship with sex.

    1.1.5. Con:
    CSE will normalize conversation about sex, and in doing so will allow any unintended consequences to be better dealt with through open communication.

    1.1.6. Con:
    Since 2020, 30 US states require public schools to teach sex education and HIV education [40], no unintended consequences have been reported so far.

      1.1.6.1. Con:
      This requirement has only been in effect for a year. Any results from teaching these courses will likely take more time to acquire.

    1.1.7. Con:
    There's no reason to assume that the unintended effects of mandatory comprehensive sex education will be more negative than positive.

    1.1.8. Pro:
    Educating children about sex without their parents' consent will lead to backlash.

      1.1.8.1. Con:
      If an action results in the improvement of life for the people concerning it along with a vast array of benefits, mere backlash is not enough grounds to not do it.

      1.1.8.2. Con:
      The role of the government is to act rationally where individuals do not. Therefore regulatory standards like law and order exist to protect people from themselves.

      1.1.8.3. Con:
      Many government decisions especially those that involve change, can lead to backlash and yet, if they are considered necessary, criticism is not a sufficient ground to stop them.

        1.1.8.3.1. Pro:
        There was a massive backlash [41] against legalizing of homosexual marriages in many countries, but many countries went ahead and did it anyways.

        1.1.8.3.2. Pro:
        There was significant opposition [42] to the smoking ban in England, despite the fact it has significantly improved people's health prospects.

        1.1.8.3.3. Pro:
        The law enforcing people to wear seat belts in the US was regarded by some as an infringement [43] of individual freedoms, yet it was passed regardless.

        1.1.8.3.4. Pro:
        In order to continue making progressive change, it is likely that the government will have to commit to decisions that not everyone agrees with.

      1.1.8.4. Con:
      Giving in to backlash only further marginalises [44] students who are sexually or gender diverse.

      1.1.8.5. Pro:
      Where children have different ideas about sexuality to their parents, friction may arise in their home lives.

        1.1.8.5.1. Pro:
        The friction that children receive in their home lives will lead to distress and unease for the children as they seek to reconcile radically different ideas about an important part of their lives.

        1.1.8.5.2. Con:
        If this is the case, that friction is likely to arise regardless of whether the children receive CSE, as they will still develop their own ideas about sexuality over time.

          1.1.8.5.2.1. Pro:
          In this instance, children are more likely to become resentful because they will feel as though their parents have actively concealed information and perspective from them.

        1.1.8.5.3. Pro:
        For example, a comprehensive sex education program might encourage LGBT children to 'come out' to their families. This could make those children vulnerable to alienation and abuse in the instance their parents are not supportive.

          1.1.8.5.3.1. Con:
          This is a problem of society's intolerance, rather than a problem of CSE. Members of the LGBTQ community should be free to express who they are without fear of backlash from anybody.

            1.1.8.5.3.1.1. Con:
            The well-being of vulnerable children and youth should be paramount. Therefore if CSE can jeopardise it, even if the underlying problem is society’s intolerance, any actions risking youth should be avoided regardless of who’s fault it is.

      1.1.8.6. Pro:
      Forcing liberal, comprehensive sex education onto conservative families and/or communities is likely to lead to their disengagement from the school community.

        1.1.8.6.1. Con:
        Engaging families and communities is central [45] to successful implementation of CSE. If schools prioritise this in their programs, they may actually strengthen their ties to the community.

        1.1.8.6.2. Pro:
        Parents' desire to instill specific religious and moral values in their children is the primary reason more than 20% [46] of homeschooled children are not in mainstream schools. Under compulsory sex education, more parents may pull their children out of school altogether.

        1.1.8.6.3. Pro:
        Parents who disagree with CSE are likely to remove their children from lessons, even if it is technically compulsory.

          1.1.8.6.3.1. Pro:
          It would be preferable for schools in conservative areas to offer a more limited sex education curriculum that aligns with parents' beliefs, because at least that way children will get some information, as opposed to none at all when they are excluded.

          1.1.8.6.3.2. Con:
          Ministers in the UK have declared it 'unlawful' [47] for parents to remove their children from sex education classes.

            1.1.8.6.3.2.1. Con:
            A recent move in the UK to make sex education compulsory stipulated that parents still have the right [48] to remove their children from these classes.

          1.1.8.6.3.3. Pro:
          This form of removal from classes is likely to be more harmful than just opting out in a voluntary model. For example, parents may keep their children home for an entire school day, meaning they lose out on other learning time.

      1.1.8.7. Con:
      Other subjects with a set curriculum are similarly made mandatory. Students or parents are still able to lodge complaints if they feel there are problems with the curriculum or their views do not align with a particular subject.

  1.2. Pro:
  The state should not intervene in private matters.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Comprehensive sexual education to children should be provided by their parents according to their beliefs and values.

      1.2.1.1. Con:
      To deny children the right to information about reproductive health due to personal beliefs is an abuse of the parent, teacher or guardian's position.

        1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
        Comprehensive sexual education is necessary so people can fully exercise their reproductive and sexual rights.

          1.2.1.1.1.1. Con:
          Nobody needs to learn how to have sex, it is instinctive and otherwise culturally taught.

          1.2.1.1.1.2. Con:

            1.2.1.1.1.2.1. Con:

            1.2.1.1.1.2.2. Con:
            Children in most societies are educated [49] through formal education (schools) and informal education, via after-school activities or their families. If CSE is necessary, it should be taught in one of those settings. To ensure every child gets the same access to it, schools seem the best option.

              1.2.1.1.1.2.3.2. Con:

                1.2.1.1.1.2.3.2.1. Pro:
                Unreliable information can inform children’s views about sexuality at a sensitive point when they are still figuring out their own sexuality. Those views can have a lasting impact.

                  1.2.1.1.1.2.3.2.1.1. Pro:

                1.2.1.1.1.2.3.3.2. Pro:
                Not only are not all children old enough or or tech savvy enough, some do not have access to the technology or the resources to begin with. By providing age appropriate CSE to all children we eliminate gaps that have long term impacts when relating to generational poverty and economic struggle.

              1.2.1.1.1.2.3.5. Con:
