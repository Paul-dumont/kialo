Discussion Name:
Violations of young boys' bodies aren't taken seriously

1. Thesis:
People do not take on-screen and real-life abuse of young boys and teen boys seriously.

  1.1. Con:
  Toxic masculinity is responsible for this. Males are the first ones who don't care about boys' rights. medium.com [1]

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Abuse is considered a pathway to male bonding, as demonstrated by male initiation rituals and ceremonies in the military [2] and frat groups [3].

    1.1.2. Pro:
    Feminists have made the world a better place for boys [4] by challenging toxic masculinity standards (e.g., boys don't cry), which make it difficult for boys to seek help and support.

    1.1.3. Con:
    Toxic masculinity is the result of social behaviors accepted by both males and females. In fact, it is a common comedy trope in TV series/films to see a girl hitting a boy (or pantsing/stripping him).

    1.1.4. Con:
    The beliefs and values that underlie abuse are irrelevant. What matters is individual participation and endorsement of abuse. Thus, if women laugh at the abuse of boys and men, they are guilty of perpetuating such toxicity as well.

  1.2. Pro:
  Many violations of young boys' bodies are reduced to horseplay [5].

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Many TV shows [6] and films include scenes where men and boys are hit in the testicles [7] as a joke. Attacking a female character's genitals neither has the same comedic effect nor would be considered appropriate.

    1.2.2. Pro:
    In the American comedy "Good Luck Chuck", a 13-year-old boy is sexually assaulted by a girl "for laughs" [8]. She kisses the boy against his will, she rips his shirt open, she scratches his chest with her nails, she twists his nipples and he finally manages to get free.

    1.2.3. Pro:
    There is a disparity between male and female tropes, irrespective of the genre. For example, it is considered a funny trope to pull down a boy's pants in front of a crowd [9], but it would be considered assault to do the same to a girl.

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      Fictional assault is not a crime; instead, it is a depiction of one, so it is not problematic.

        1.2.3.1.1. Con:
        Fictional assault can negatively affect young people (and even adults) [10] and that's why many countries have a content rating for movies/series/cartoons [11]. Can you imagine Disney movies using racial/homophobic slurs as if they were acceptable behavior. Not a crime, but there's a reason why this is not allowed.

          1.2.3.1.1.1. Con:
          The article linked (in the parent claim) does not indicate (significant) negative effects.

            1.2.3.1.1.1.1. Con:
            The meaning of "significant" is subjective, as it applies a relative unit of measure [12] to define the importance/impact of something.

            1.2.3.1.1.1.2. Pro:
            Democratic laws are a good indicator of consensus on moral issues, because it shows that millions of people cared enough about a topic to vote on it's validity and it is currently legal to depict immoral acts on screen.

          1.2.3.1.1.2. Con:
          Counterexamples [13] such as the debate about violent videogames (being found to have no impact on violent behavior) also exist.

            1.2.3.1.1.2.1. Con:
            It has actually been proven [14] that violent video games do increase aggressive behavior and even reduce "pro-social behavior, empathy and moral engagement".

      1.2.3.2. Con:
      Actors/actresses choose which scenes they wish to appear in.

        1.2.3.2.1. Con:
        As you might imagine since we're talking of child actors/actresses, many of them are even forced to act by their parents and sometimes have to endure a lot of pressure [15], such as the fear of losing any future role. That's the problem behind the entire casting couch situation and the #metoo movement. [16]

          1.2.3.2.1.1. Con:
          Coercion shouldn't take place regardless of activity, being associated to the film industry is not the fault of the film industry per se.

            1.2.3.2.1.1.1. Con:
            The fear of losing a job, which is common in every field of our society, is an example of coercive power and is the reason why many employees act submissive [17]. It's an ethical ground connected with capitalism, but I think we can agree that those scenes are written by adults and those children are forced/encouraged by adults.

      1.2.3.3. Con:
      It would be an unnecessary objectification to insist that either boys or girls ought to choose whether to participate in acting out scenes for film based on their sex.

    1.2.4. Con:
    Girls also participate as actresses in funny films in similar circumstances.

      1.2.4.1. Pro:
      Hanna Montana [18] splits her pants as she's standing up in a shop, her friend is dragged around to protect her back-side from being visible.

        1.2.4.1.1. Con:
        The same situation is portrayed differently when it comes to a boy. As you can see in this example (Disney) [19], the boy splits his pants and - contrary to the Hanna Montana scene - you get to see his underwear. His friend lends him his own pants, but they also rip, leaving both of them in underwear. At the end of the scene, it is implied that one of the boys trips and remains naked.

      1.2.4.2. Pro:
      Countless funniest-home-videos, fail-vids and viral videos involve girls in these ordeals, often with laughter of those around them or they-themselves.

        1.2.4.2.1. Con:
        Nothing to do with the entertainment industry, in which professional figures (writers, directors, actors, cameramen, producers, parents, make-up artists and so on) decide it is appropriate to humiliate a boy.

          1.2.4.2.1.1. Con:
          It does however indicate that it is plausible to generate these circumstances in either candid, or impromptu-scripted/heavily staged settings without requiring coercion, because none of the industry positions you listed are present to coerce them. To then assume that coercion is required is evidently false.

            1.2.4.2.1.1.1. Con:
            Most of those figures are present while filming, as in this famous example [20].

      1.2.4.3. Con:
      The participation of girls in similar scenes has no bearing on determining whether on-screen and real-life abuse of boys is taken seriously or not.

  1.3. Con:
  Artistic framing can determine whether viewers perceive a scene as funny or offensive. Thus, to say that all portrayals of abuse are not taken seriously is a generalization.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    Laugh tracks [21] can be conceptualized as cues that inform the viewer about which scene is intended to be funny [22] and which is intended to be serious.

    1.3.2. Con:
    Just because artistic framing can cause viewers to perceive abuse in a lighthearted way does not mean that what is being portrayed isn't wrong.

      1.3.2.1. Con:
      Suspension of disbelief is a core component of film which allows us to apply temporary perceptions to fictional characters with no moral bearing on reality.

        1.3.2.1.1. Con:
        Suspension of disbelief is reached when the audience reads/watches a fictional story that is far from reality, such as science fiction or action movies [23]. For example, if you watch "Garfield", you have to temporarily put aside that cats do not talk or act like that. Nothing to do with morality.

          1.3.2.1.1.1. Con:
          Some films might necessitate the wilful suspension of morality [24].

            1.3.2.1.1.1.1. Pro:
            A good example would be "Cooties", [25] a comedy-horror movie in which an outbreak amongst schoolchildren turns them aggressive and cannibalistic, which forces the teacher to use weapons to slaughter the kids.

  1.4. Pro:
  The physical and sexual abuse suffered by young boys is often overlooked, minimized, or trivialized, especially compared to young girls.

    1.4.1. Pro:
    There are several social experiments available online that demonstrate how people react based on the victim's gender.

      1.4.1.1. Pro:
      An abandoned boy isn't noticed as much as an abandoned girl [26].

    1.4.2. Pro:
    In general, the abuse of male children [27] remains an unacknowledged reality.

      1.4.2.1. Pro:
      1 in 6 boys [28] are sexually assaulted by the age of 18 in the United States.

      1.4.2.2. Pro:
      Ongoing masculine gender socialization [29] sustains the myth that boys cannot be victims of sexual abuse.

        1.4.2.2.1. Pro:
        As a result of the expectation that boys are self-reliant and can protect themselves [30], childhood sexual abuse among boys is probably greatly underestimated due to under-reporting.

    1.4.3. Pro:
    Male circumcision is practiced across the world [31] and not generally considered abuse, whereas female circumcision is considered abusive bodily mutilation [32].

    1.4.4. Con:
    Girls are more likely [33] to be victims of abuse than boys, and so it is understandable why abuse of girls is treated as a more serious problem.

      1.4.4.1. Con:
      The higher prevalence of abuse against girls justifies proportioning resources to address it accordingly, but that is not the same thing as treating abuse of girls as more serious than boys.

      1.4.4.2. Con:
      There are studies claiming the opposite [34], due to the fact that boys do not recognize abuse and usually do not report it [35].

    1.4.5. Pro:
    The wave of criticism surrounding Netflix's "Cuties" [36] proves that young girls are more protected than young boys.

      1.4.5.1. Con:
      There has been no comparable show involving young boys to support the argument that boys would not have received the same treatment.

        1.4.5.1.1. Con:
        There's a comedy sketch in which a boy dresses up as a beauty pageant. He then dances almost naked [37]. The audience is shocked because they believe he's a girl. As soon as the truth comes out, you can see some mothers laughing and one even tries to clap her hands.

      1.4.5.2. Con:
      That such shows, typically focusing on girls rather than boys, are even made in the first place testifies to greater social tolerance of the violation of girls' bodies.

        1.4.5.2.1. Con:
        "Cuties" was a movie made to criticize the violation of girls' bodies. The movies/shows discussed in this debate, instead, promote the idea that it is funny to violate boys' bodies.

    1.4.6. Pro:
    There are many examples of films and TV shows in which the sexual abuse of boys is treated comedically [38].

      1.4.6.1. Pro:
      -> See 1.2.2.

  1.5. Con:
  This has nothing to do with abuse, but rather with a lack of humour.

    1.5.1. Con:
    A viewer's perception that a behavior is not abuse, but just a funny act, does not mean that it is not abuse.

    1.5.2. Con:
    Abuse is defined as any action that intentionally harms or injures another person. [39]

    1.5.3. Pro:
    People laugh because they know that television is not real life. They are aware that the actors consented to the depictions; viewers would not laugh if the abuse was real.

      1.5.3.1. Con:
      Viewers do laugh if the abuse is real or they tend to downplay its seriousness, whether it's a boy being pantsed in front of other relatives [40] or a boy being sexually abused by his babysitter [41].

    1.5.4. Pro:
    Child abuse is a legal issue, and depicting child abuse on screen will land the production company in legal problems. Acts that do not trigger such a reaction can, therefore, be said to not be abuse.

      1.5.4.1. Con:
      This doesn't happen because the same act is not considered abusive if the victim is a boy. For example, the movie "10 1/2" contains child nudity, oral sex, masturbation and has earned 6 awards and 12 nominations [42].

    1.5.5. Con:
    Humor that involves hurting the sentiments of certain groups of people should not be tolerated.

      1.5.5.1. Con:
      In 'Anchorman' [43] dozens of newscasting employees attack each other with lethal weapons and it won multiple awards and nominations based on its light-hearted and on-the-nose humor, indicating that movie-goers and industry-buffs think that it should be tolerated. As has Monty Python [44] in prior years.

  1.6. Con:
  The portrayed situation (ordeal) itself is the point of a scene, and therefore that the characteristics of the person or character, such as their sex, are not relevant.

    1.6.1. Con:
    The Austrian movie "Blütsbruder teilen alles" [45] has a scene in which an adult woman soothes a 14-year-old boy by masturbating him under the covers. The scene is played for laughs, especially because the boy moans too loud. Now imagine switching genders and having an adult man doing the same to a 14-year-old girl and tell me that sex is not relevant in the portrayed situation.

    1.6.2. Con:
    If a behavior is considered abuse when it involves a girl, it should be the same when it involves a boy.

    1.6.3. Con:
    Since the sex of the person in such cases often constitutes part of the ordeal itself, their sex is relevant in terms of representation.

  1.7. Pro:
  Young boys are not given the same right to bodily autonomy and privacy [46] as girls.

    1.7.1. Con:
    Abusive behaviors lie on a continuum of severity with embarrassing a child on the lower end and sexual abuse on the other. Low-severity behaviors may elicit different reactions based on the gender of the victim, but severe behaviors like abuse are unlikely to be perceived as funny, regardless of the gender of the victim.
