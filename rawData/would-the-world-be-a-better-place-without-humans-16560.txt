Discussion Name:
Would the world be a better place without humans?

1. Thesis:
The world would be better without humans.

  1.1. Pro:
  Human beings are the only force on Earth capable of eliminating all life and destroying the planet. This potential for harm is reason enough for them to not exist.

    1.1.1. Con:
    The eruption of a super-volcano and the resulting atmospheric event could potentially also eliminate all life.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      A volcanic eruption in Iceland caused the worst year in human history, leading to widespread famine, disease, and millions of deaths.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    Humans are the only beings with the intelligence required to create tools or weapons that can destroy the planet.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      The leafcutter ant [1] is another animal that can create tools. However, it is obvious that this animal cannot cause mass destruction like humans have.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      Human beings have invented a wide variety of technologies that destroy forests [2] and produce waste faster than nature's capacity to recover.

      1.1.2.3. Con:
      Human beings are unlikely to destroy the planet as it is not in their interest to die.

        1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
        Without the planet, humankind is unlikely to survive. Finding another place to live is just a remote possibility.

        1.1.2.3.2. Con:
        All of humanity need not necessarily contribute to a cataclysmic event—a handful of individuals' unwarranted use of nuclear weapons would destroy most life on the planet.

      1.1.2.4. Con:
      The weapons created by human beings to this point pale in comparison to some of the most destructive natural disasters in history.

        1.1.2.4.1. Con:
        Pollution kills 20 times more people [3] annually than all wars combined.

        1.1.2.4.2. Pro:
        The Krakatoa eruption [4] had an estimated equivalent explosive force of 200 megatons of TNT [5]. In comparison, the Tsar Bomba, the most powerful explosive ever detonated, had an explosive force of 50 megatons of TNT [6].

          1.1.2.4.2.1. Con:
          Explosions are only one form of destructive event among many, meaning that it does not make sense to compare events using metrics of force.

        1.1.2.4.3. Con:
        Comparing singular destructive events in this way is unreasonable because humans harm the planet over time through over-consumption and wasteful behavior.

      1.1.2.5. Con:
      Humans are the only intelligent beings that can save the planet from being destroyed.

        1.1.2.5.1. Pro:
        Humans are the only species with the intellectual capacity [7] to protect the Earth from external threats, such as asteroid strikes.

          1.1.2.5.1.1. Con:
          There is no evidence to suggest that humans have developed a definitive method of protecting the planet [8] from asteroid or meteor strikes.

    1.1.3. Con:
    Human beings are incapable of entirely destroying the planet and eliminating every shred of life.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      While nuclear Armageddon may be able to wipe out life on the surface of the Earth, it’s unlikely that anything could substantially eliminate any amount of deep sea creatures.

        1.1.3.1.1. Con:
        Plastic pollution is destabilizing oceanic ecosystems [9] at a scale that is difficult to comprehend. This is especially important because ecosystems are interconnected, which means that changes in oceanic ecosystems could have major impacts on other major ecosystems.

          1.1.3.1.1.1. Con:
          A major ecosystem collapse, while disastrous, is far removed from the total extermination of all life.

          1.1.3.1.1.2. Pro:
          Scientists have found plastic waste in the deepest areas of the Mariana Trench [10].

          1.1.3.1.1.3. Pro:
          Some estimate that by 2050, there will be more plastic trash in the ocean [11] than there are fish.

        1.1.3.1.2. Con:
        Amphipods found in the Mariana Trench have atomic residue [12] from nuclear tests in the late 1940s and early 1950s.

        1.1.3.1.3. Pro:
        Tardigrades are one of the most indestructible animals [13] on the planet; human beings would be hard pressed to kill all of them even if they were to destroy the planet.

          1.1.3.1.3.1. Con:
          Leaving exclusively tardigrades as the only living beings on the planet is hardly making the world a better place, given the incredible biodiversity on the planet that would become extinct.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      Humans may become capable of eliminating all life and destroying the planet in the future; the potential to even develop that capacity is something other animals lack.

      1.1.3.3. Con:
      Although it is true that humans are not capable of destroying all life on Earth in a literal sense, humans can definitely be held responsible for speeding up the timeline [14] for mass extinctions or the destruction of the planet to occur.

        1.1.3.3.1. Pro:
        Other than exceptions such as asteroid collision, almost all other issues are caused by human beings, like increasing temperatures, extinction of animals, war, etc.

        1.1.3.3.2. Pro:
        In the past, carbon emissions were largely due to volcanic activity; nowadays, the balance has shifted [15] to energy production and the burning of fossil fuels.

      1.1.3.4. Pro:
      Even if humans manage to completely destroy the Earth, given enough time, the debris, under the force and pressure of gravity, will eventually reform into something else [16].

    1.1.4. Con:
    Allowing humans to change their behavior to protect the environment is preferable to wiping out humans.

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      Human beings are selfishly preoccupied [17] with their own purpose in life; very few think about the sustainability of life on the planet.

      1.1.4.2. Pro:
      Humanity has only caused more harm because it has been the most successful at survival. All unchecked, unchallenged species wreak havoc [18] on their respective ecosystems as they compete for resources. A more relevant point would be to look at humanity's ability to adapt to a more sustainable way of life.

        1.1.4.2.1. Con:
        Killing for food is a necessity for all carnivores, but humans uniquely also kill for sport or as a consequence of other activities (either knowingly or unknowingly).

      1.1.4.3. Pro:
      Earth is the sole opportunity for humans to overcome their destructive side [19] and prove themselves worthy to live on Earth. Without this opportunity, humans are robbed of their purpose.

        1.1.4.3.1. Con:
        This assumes that human purpose is to prove themselves worthy of life on Earth, which is tenuous at best.

      1.1.4.4. Con:
      While smaller groups of individuals have shown that they want to, and can be better in this regard, the collective as a whole continuously shows that selfish desires will always be prioritized over the well-being of the environment and living organisms.

        1.1.4.4.1. Con:
        Things always having been done a certain way in the past is a logical fallacy [20] that does not mean that they should continue to be that way in the future. Framing the status quo in terms of paying attention to the well-being of the environment and living organisms as a situation that is still bad but getting better seems more appropriate.

        1.1.4.4.2. Pro:
        Many people rationalize selfish behavior like overconsumption on the basis of claiming that everyone else is doing the same and so them stopping won't have an impact.

      1.1.4.5. Con:
      Human existence has a certain inevitable cost. Humans can try to minimize it, but it isn't immediately clear that they can support a population of their size without damaging the environment in some way.

        1.1.4.5.1. Con:
        The existence of any living organism has come at a cost, humans are not special in this regard.

    1.1.5. Pro:
    Human influence is accelerating and worsening the effects of global warming [21].

      1.1.5.1. Pro:
      Some experts warn that it is too late to stop the climate crisis (p. 3 [22]).

      1.1.5.2. Con:
      Throughout its history [23], the Earth has experienced natural cycles of warmer and cooler weather, meaning that the degree of influence that human beings have on the planet is minimal.

    1.1.6. Pro:
    Humans are dangerous because they are capable of violence [24] and can work together in groups to change the landscape, consume resources, and displace other species.

      1.1.6.1. Con:
      Chimpanzees are also capable of working in groups [25] and displacing other species.

      1.1.6.2. Pro:
      Only 3% of the land on Earth remains untouched by human activity [26].

  1.2. Con:
  Value judgments are only relevant for human beings who assign value, meaning that the Earth cannot be better or worse without human beings to make value judgments about it.

    1.2.1. Con:
    All human suffering would be eliminated without humans. In this manner, the world would in fact be better, even if only in the eyes of the humans who lived prior to their extinction.

      1.2.1.1. Con:
      Humans would likely suffer [27] as they were dying off—especially following a global catastrophe with starvation and rampant disease.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      Eliminating something negative by eliminating something positive is not an overall net positive.

        1.2.1.2.1. Pro:
        The fact that humans have the unique capability to ponder over this debate makes them an anomaly that should be preserved.

        1.2.1.2.2. Pro:
        The elimination of humans would also completely eliminate human happiness.

          1.2.1.2.2.1. Con:
          Human happiness and comfort for some often comes at the expense of others through the exploitation of labor, exploration, and the farming of weaker species, along with the unfair distribution of resources and opportunities (p. 7 [28]).

          1.2.1.2.2.2. Pro:
          Happiness [29] is a global phenomenon, shared by billions of people across the planet.

        1.2.1.2.3. Con:
        Suffering and happiness are not evenly distributed [30]; in general, human suffering is the default and happiness is the exception, meaning that eliminating suffering would indeed be a net gain.

          1.2.1.2.3.1. Pro:
          Ending human suffering is more important than preserving human happiness as suffering and poverty [31] are rampant among humans.

      1.2.1.3. Pro:
      The very existence of humans causes harm [32] other sentient beings, meaning that procreation causes both the child and the rest of the planet to suffer.

      1.2.1.4. Con:
      This claim operates under the assumption that the only way to evaluate being is along a pleasure-pain axis [33]. Meaning and fulfillment exist outside of this dichotomy, which is neglected by this claim.

        1.2.1.4.1. Con:
        The claim does not presuppose that evaluating life in terms of a happiness-suffering axis is the only conceivable metric, but rather that it is the most intuitive and relevant metric to be used in a discussion of ethics.

        1.2.1.4.2. Pro:
        There are facets of human living and existence that add a wholly unique, immeasurable, and unquantifiable value to the universe that are worth maintaining despite massive amounts of suffering.

      1.2.1.5. Pro:
      Most animal suffering, which is similar to human suffering [34], would also be eliminated without humans.

    1.2.2. Pro:
    The Earth will continue to exist by the time humans have gone extinct. Once humans are gone, the Earth will heal itself and new species will evolve.

      1.2.2.1. Con:
      Human beings have caused such extensive damage to the planet and other living things that the possibility exists that nothing will be able to heal even if human beings go extinct.

      1.2.2.2. Pro:
      The world and all its creatures have thrived before and will continue to thrive once humans have gone. Humans need the Earth but the Earth doesn’t need humans.

        1.2.2.2.1. Con:
        “Thriving” is a concept only recognized by conscious observers. God or man are necessary to define this term.

        1.2.2.2.2. Con:
        The Earth itself is not conscious and is indifferent to change.

    1.2.3. Pro:
    If the species making such a judgment no longer exists, it would therefore be impossible to ascertain a value or for the Earth to be better off.

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      Without the existence of humans, the lives of several species could potentially improve. Even if humans were the only species that could understand how the other species’ lives had gotten better, this would not discount the improvement of the quality of life for those species.

      1.2.3.2. Pro:
      From a purely scientific point of view, the universe has neither meaning nor purpose. From the point of view of humans, the human presence in this world might be a bad thing. From the universe's point of view, humans are and that is all, no worse or no better than the meteorite which killed off the dinosaurs.

    1.2.4. Pro:
    If the world would be better or worse is subjective as those things aren't intrinsic qualities of something.

      1.2.4.1. Pro:
      The perspective of the Earth is not possible to ascertain.

      1.2.4.2. Con:
      There are general or pragmatic standards for earthly wellness, like the stability of weather patterns or the preservation of species, that could be adopted without falling into naive subjectivism.

        1.2.4.2.1. Con:
        Stability of weather is only good to achieve stable conditions for life, not as a reliable marker of the wellness of the planet—unless it is assumed that the objective of the planet is to allow life.

    1.2.5. Con:
    The point of judging human existence on Earth is for humans to decide whether or not their presence is better than their absence.

      1.2.5.1. Pro:
      Human consciousness is the tool that allows judgment decisions to be made about what can be considered better or worse.

      1.2.5.2. Con:
      The removal of humans is akin to a rejection of everything human, including the notion that the world can be made "better," which renders such a sentiment self-contradictory.

    1.2.6. Pro:
    From a theistic God viewpoint, the world was made for humans and humans were made for the world. Thus, humans are uniquely positioned to find value in the world.

      1.2.6.1. Pro:
      The Earth has been formed for human life. The food it produces, fruits, vegetables, etc., is to feed the entire human race. Animals, which are instinctively wise, are inferior to human beings in intelligence and therefore provide all human beings with company, help for work, everything is harmoniously intertwined so that people can live and enjoy wonderful natural landscapes.

      1.2.6.2. Pro:
      As the designer of all things, a monotheistic God is capable of imbuing life with ultimate meaning or purpose. Without God to create humans and meaning, life is absurd [35].

        1.2.6.2.1. Con:
        Humans give meaning to their own existence, not God. Many atheists find a purpose [36] in life without God.

          1.2.6.2.1.1. Con:
          All humans, as conscious observers, can ascribe a self-defined meaning to their life; however, this is temporal and finite. In the grand spectrum of the world, it is meaningless without an eternal and infinite being.

        1.2.6.2.2. Pro:
        While it is reasonable to look to science to understand the physical parts of the world, God is necessary for grasping the metaphysical dimensions of the world.

      1.2.6.3. Pro:
      If a theistic God exists, the world was made for the ultimate purpose of bringing free creatures into some sort of relationship with God. Thus, a world with no observers is meaningless.

        1.2.6.3.1. Con:
        The world could still have meaning without humans. It is possible God just wanted to design a place for His creations to flourish and humans are detracting from this purpose.

      1.2.6.4. Con:
      The world does not need to be meaningful [37].

        1.2.6.4.1. Con:
        Ascribing transcendent meaning [38] and purpose to the world would make it better.

        1.2.6.4.2. Con:
        If the world does not need to be meaningful, then there is no point in calling the world better without humans. If there is no meaning in the world [39], then it doesn't matter if humans ruin it or not.

      1.2.6.5. Con:
      Most conscious beings have existed far longer [40] than humans, so the planet certainly wasn't created specifically for people.

    1.2.7. Con:
    Removing humans from the picture would most likely benefit the remaining conscious beings on the planet.

      1.2.7.1. Con:
      The nearly extinct species which are surviving under human care and supervision would most likely perish.

      1.2.7.2. Pro:
      There would no longer be factory farming or species being driven to extinction by the activities of humans.

        1.2.7.2.1. Con:
        Most of these suddenly-liberated animals would likely perish as they've been bred in captivity for generations and wouldn't know how to fend for themselves.

    1.2.8. Con:
    Removing humans from the Earth does not equate to the extinction of the human race.

      1.2.8.1. Pro:
      A human colony on Mars could evaluate the Earth's progress from afar and still be within the scope of the removal of humans from Earth.

        1.2.8.1.1. Con:
        It does not make sense to assess the Earth's goodness from another planet without the intent to colonize it. The closer Earth is to a survivable state, the more humans will be living on Earth.

  1.3. Con:
  Humans are the most advanced species that has ever been on Earth, and humanity is still evolving. Removing humanity from existence would eliminate this progress.

    1.3.1. Con:
    These technological advancements have no inherent value to the universe and are only of limited value to humans.

      1.3.1.1. Con:
      Nothing in the universe has inherent value. Value is subjective and creatures can benefit from human innovation. Look at prosthetics and veterinary clinics as examples.

      1.3.1.2. Con:
      In near future, humanity could develop a reliable technological ability to protect the planet and life from extinction events [41].

        1.3.1.2.1. Pro:
        Humans are very useful for life on Earth since humans have the means to expand life to Mars and beyond. Without humans, life will simply stay on Earth. If humans die out, it is a horrible possibility that when the Earth collapses into the sun, all life in the universe will be dead forever.

      1.3.1.3. Pro:
      Just because humans are the most advanced species, doesn't mean they make the Earth better.

        1.3.1.3.1. Pro:
        Humans are too smart for their own good by creating factories, automobiles, and other machines that destroy the planet [42].

    1.3.2. Pro:
    As far as humans know, they are the most advanced species in the entire universe. If humanity did not exist, the universe would lose the species with the most advanced consciousness.

      1.3.2.1. Con:
      Claiming that one species is more advanced than all of the others is a mischaracterization of evolution. Evolution is not linear; it proceeds as a tree [43] and therefore, no extant species can be said to be more evolved or advanced than any other.

        1.3.2.1.1. Pro:
        Earthworms have been evolving for the same amount of time [44] as humans. Evolution is not a progression from primitive to advanced—such a view is incorrect [45].

        1.3.2.1.2. Con:
        Without human beings, the world may have become inhabited by an even more destructive apex species.

          1.3.2.1.2.1. Con:
          Humans have existed for at most, 300 thousand years [46]. Life in general has existed for billions of years prior to that without needing humans to "control" the negative actions of predators.

          1.3.2.1.2.2. Con:
          Evolution suggests that humanity is a stepping stone [47] to something else; the evolved race will inevitably be better.

      1.3.2.2. Pro:
      Humans are likely to be the only species capable of helping the Earth recover after a massive destructive event.

        1.3.2.2.1. Pro:
        On a long enough timeline humans are the only species with the potential, via intellect and ingenuity, to preserve and protect nature from natural catastrophe. So, it doesn't matter how bad humans get, humans are still custodians of all life known on the planet.

          1.3.2.2.1.1. Con:
          Life has existed on Earth for more than three billion years [48] without human interference. Rather than preserving and protecting nature, humans are causing another mass extinction event [49] through their interference.

        1.3.2.2.2. Con:
        Mother Nature has shown the ability to recover from past massive destruction events and will certainly recover [50] on its own following the next one.

          1.3.2.2.2.1. Pro:
          The natural environment in the area around the Chernobyl Exclusion Zone has largely recovered [51] and could even be considered thriving.

            1.3.2.2.2.1.1. Con:
            Many animals in the area have diseases caused by the radiation [52] and officials have advised locals not to eat wild game [53].

            1.3.2.2.2.1.2. Pro:
            The grey wolves in the Exclusion Zone are healthier and more densely populated [54] than their counterparts in Yellowstone National Park.

          1.3.2.2.2.2. Con:
          Any such recovery would be devoid of people to witness it, diminishing the grandeur.

            1.3.2.2.2.2.1. Con:
            The recovery of the natural environment is a spectacle with or without humans around to witness it.

          1.3.2.2.2.3. Pro:
          Asteroids have hit the Earth before, and life continued to subsist [55] afterwards.

            1.3.2.2.2.3.1. Con:
            Life has barely continued to subsist; the asteroid that killed the dinosaurs also eliminated an estimated three-quarters of the animal species on the planet at that time.

            1.3.2.2.2.3.2. Pro:
            The chance of the Earth being destroyed by an asteroid [56] are negligible. NASA has plans underway for deflecting or destroying asteroids and future technology will be sufficient to help avoid a catastrophe (pp. 1-2 [57]).

          1.3.2.2.2.4. Con:
          Life as a general phenomenon has subsisted, but many of the planet's creatures have been killed prematurely. It is possible that human technology could prevent the premature destruction of the individual lives of many living organisms under such a scenario.

            1.3.2.2.2.4.1. Pro:
            Most species that have ever lived have gone extinct [58] before man even existed. In fact, humans are likely to be the first species to care whether or not another species besides humans survives or not.

            1.3.2.2.2.4.2. Con:
            Preventing the destruction of species that otherwise would have gone extinct impedes natural selection [59], and may be detrimental to the process of evolution overall.

      1.3.2.3. Pro:
      Human beings are learning from their mistakes and have the ability to reshape the world [60] and be the prodigy of nature.

      1.3.2.4. Con:
      Humans have equated themselves to being the most advanced conscious species [61] in the universe; this is not a proven fact.

        1.3.2.4.1. Pro:
        According to a recent whistleblower in the US government, the US has access to intact or partially intact alien craft [62], potentially hinting at the existence of species with a higher consciousness than ours.

          1.3.2.4.1.1. Con:
          There is no evidence [63] to suggest that these claims are legitimate.

          1.3.2.4.1.2. Con:
          Even if these claims are true, it is unknown whether these beings are more advanced then human beings.

        1.3.2.4.2. Con:
        So far, humans have not found any credible sign of life [64] in the universe outside of Earth.

    1.3.3. Con:
    By living the way they are today, human beings are destroying opportunities for other intelligent life to develop and are risking irreparably destroying Earth.

      1.3.3.1. Con:
      In the cosmic perspective of life, there is no absolute sense of 'good' and 'bad.' That's a human-created concept that wouldn't matter if humans were eradicated. Therefore, it makes humanity's existence irrelevant.

      1.3.3.2. Con:
      There is nothing now living today with the intelligence and level of consciousness humans possess. The claim assumes that it is a foregone conclusion that other intelligent life would develop.

        1.3.3.2.1. Pro:
        The chances of intelligent life developing on Earth again is astronomically low. An environmental stressor would be required in order to demand this adaptation along with additional time.

        1.3.3.2.2. Pro:
        The development of human-level intelligence and consciousness is a black swan moment [65] in evolution; killing humans over the minute improbable event of intelligence in other lifeforms is a lottery with terrible odds.

        1.3.3.2.3. Pro:
        It is not certain that humans are eliminating all chances for new intelligent life to develop, and through the use of AI technology human effort could be the most probable way of fostering a new type of intelligent life.

      1.3.3.3. Pro:
      Humans are the apex species on the planet, but are definitely not necessary for life to thrive [66]. Humans damage the planet more than they help it.

      1.3.3.4. Con:
      The other lifeforms that evolve after the exit of humans may go through a similar evolutionary path as humans; the environment may suffer from similar consequences. Humans, by contrast, have already gone through their development stage, and can now work on making their lives more sustainable.

    1.3.4. Pro:
    Humans have created a unique set of values, cultures, and traditions in a way that only they could (p. 79 [67]). To say the world would be a better place without humans is to fail to take into account the complex and rich culture of every human society.

      1.3.4.1. Con:
      Every organism has unique behaviors, habits, and experiences that only they could. It should not be assumed that human behaviors, habits, and experiences are more valuable or more unique than all other kinds.

      1.3.4.2. Pro:
      Human beings regularly create art and music [68], which contribute significantly to society and culture, while adding beauty to the world.

        1.3.4.2.1. Con:
        Humans create just as much, if not more, demolition and death in the world compared to beauty and culture.

        1.3.4.2.2. Pro:
        Humans, unique among other species [69], spontaneously evolved musical and linguistic cultures simultaneously, making music, like language, unique to humans.

        1.3.4.2.3. Con:
        Art does not create new forms of beauty. Art merely realizes or reflects the beauty of nature and the human experience of it.

  1.4. Pro:
  Humans are directly responsible for many negative effects on Earth, all of which could be avoided if they did not exist.

    1.4.1. Pro:
    Humans have caused irreparable damage [70] to the environment [71], which could be reduced and eventually reversed without them around anymore.

      1.4.1.1. Con:
      There are positive trends that suggest that the situation is improving without needing to resort to removing humans.

        1.4.1.1.1. Pro:
        In spite of commonly-repeated rhetoric, the Earth is getting greener [72]. Humans are coming to their senses and reducing their impact on the environment.

        1.4.1.1.2. Pro:
        The quality of human life is actually getting better [73]. Suffering is on the decline along with poverty, violence, and disease.

      1.4.1.2. Pro:
      Humans restrict the freedom and autonomy of countless of living organisms for their entire lives and the lives of generations to come, including other humans.

        1.4.1.2.1. Pro:
        Domesticated animals [74] have no natural freedoms [75], they are almost entirely stripped away for the purpose of production for humans.

        1.4.1.2.2. Pro:
        Whenever human populations expand they take more land away from wild habitats which heavily restricts the freedom [76] of all living organisms in that habitat often leading to many deaths as well.

        1.4.1.2.3. Con:
        Some animals do not suffer or feel pain [77], and thus cannot feel the effects of a loss of autonomy.

          1.4.1.2.3.1. Con:
          All available understanding of consciousness or intelligence is far too biased in human perception; as such, humans cannot possibly define or measure the suffering of other life forms.

            1.4.1.2.3.1.1. Pro:
            Scientists studying other creatures are constantly discovering how much more complex [78] other life forms are than previously assumed.

          1.4.1.2.3.2. Pro:
          Suffering should be put in the context of the complexity [79] of the nervous system. Animals tend to have less complex nervous systems than human beings, which implies that they have a reduced capacity for suffering.

          1.4.1.2.3.3. Con:
          Many cephalopods, decapods, and other invertebrates feel pain, debunking [80] the previous understanding of their nature.

        1.4.1.2.4. Con:
        The principle of autonomy does not apply to any other living organisms besides humans, as humans are the only agents aware of or capable benefitting from an abstract principle like automony.

        1.4.1.2.5. Pro:
        Humans disrupt many ecosystems and food chain by automating like building farms [81] which leads to imbalance in the environment and ecosystems. Extinction of the human race would make the world "better" if we are talking strictly about the environment.

      1.4.1.3. Con:
      While there is evidence of environmental change, it is not clear that this constitutes damage or harm. Human beings can conceptualize the loss of the ability to support life as damage, but this is merely one cycle of life in the long history of the planet [82].

        1.4.1.3.1. Pro:
        Environmental changes and their effects are most likely natural occurrences, even if humans play a role in exacerbating them.

          1.4.1.3.1.1. Pro:
          More than 99% of all species [83] that have ever lived on Earth have already gone extinct, mostly due to five mass extinction events [84] that took place before humans ever existed.

        1.4.1.3.2. Con:
        Everything discussed is a human conception, created by humans. The environment is less able to support the life forms than it used to; therefore, it is damaged.

        1.4.1.3.3. Con:
        The Earth would be able to heal and become a better place because the root cause for ecocide [85] would simply not exist.

        1.4.1.3.4. Con:
        The possibility of naturally occurring extinction events does not make human-made extinction events morally permissible. While the former are unavoidable, the latter are the result of human action.

          1.4.1.3.4.1. Con:
          The former is avoidable by human intervention. Mankind is able to make such decisions that can benefit the environment and create new technology to combat problems around the world.

            1.4.1.3.4.1.1. Pro:
