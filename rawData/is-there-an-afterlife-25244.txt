Discussion Name:
Is there an afterlife?

1. Thesis:
There is an afterlife.

  1.1. Con:
  Evolution has been proven beyond doubt [1], which disproves the possibility of an afterlife. Specifically, humans evolved over time, so it is unclear why they would, at some point, suddenly have souls.

    1.1.1. Con:
    Evolution does not disprove the existence of an omnipotent creator, or God, and the existence of a God would generally imply that an afterlife exists.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      Because the first stage of evolution (genetic mutation) occurs through largely random processes [2], and the later stages (the survival of certain mutations over time) are also heavily dictated by chance, the idea that evolution follows a "plan", or is guided by a God, is a contradiction in terms.

        1.1.1.1.1. Con:
        The apparent randomness of these processes could be attributed to a human inability to pick up on what might actually be a divine design.

          1.1.1.1.1.1. Con:
          Saying that a phenomenon is random is not the same as saying "maybe there's a pattern here, but we haven't spotted it yet". Randomness [3] is a strictly-defined mathematical phenomenon which can be rigorously tested.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      The theory of evolution [4] demonstrates that no omnipotent creator is needed to create complex life. Creation can occur without a creator. It is thus highly probable that such a creator does not exist.

        1.1.1.2.1. Con:
        Evolution does not provide a satisfactory explanation for the origins of life. Given this, it does not rule out the existence of a creator.

          1.1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
          There is currently no general scientifically accepted model of abiogenesis [5], or the origin of life. Hence scientific explanations are presently limited in their explanations for the creation of existence.

        1.1.1.2.2. Con:
        The inability to detect an omnipotent entity's influence [6] on evolution does not prove that no influence exists.

          1.1.1.2.2.1. Con:
          It is impossible to prove that an omnipotent planner has no influence over evolution. However, it is perfectly possible to prove that evolution can operate completely independently, without an omnipotent planner's influence. Thus, adding an omnipotent planner into an already-comprehensive theory is unnecessary, and it is highly unclear why anyone would feel the need to do so, unless they were trying to validate a pre-existing religious conviction.

            1.1.1.2.2.1.1. Pro:
            Adding an omnipotent deus-ex-machina explanation into an already-comprehensive theory is a bit like saying "sure, mechanical physics explains precisely why these billiard balls bounce off each other, but you can't prove that they're not also being guided by invisible gnomes".

            1.1.1.2.2.1.2. Pro:
            Occam's Razor [7] dictates that, when presented with a choice of two possible explanations, we should believe the simplest one (the one with fewest assumptions). Thus, when presented with the choice of A (a comprehensive, fully explained and self-sustaining system for creating human life), and B (which is A, but with the undefined influence of a mysterious creator) we should prefer A.

              1.1.1.2.2.1.2.1. Pro:
              The paradox surrounding the 'creator of the creator' [8] leads to a never-ending set of assumptions that have to be proven.

        1.1.1.2.3. Pro:
        The Oparin-Haldane hypothesis [9] postulated that the random interactions of various chemicals gave rise to all living organisms.

          1.1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
          The Miller-Urey Experiment [10] - which attempted to simulate the conditions of early Earth to investigate the origin of life - provided further support for this theory

        1.1.1.2.4. Pro:
        Dawin's "warm little pond" hypothesis marked the beginning of decades of research [11] published to explain creation.

        1.1.1.2.5. Con:
        A creator need not necessarily create the living being, or even the organism from which it evolves. If a creator makes a universe that is set up from the beginning of time to facilitate and result in life emerging, they can also be credited with creating life itself.

      1.1.1.3. Con:
      Even if there is a God, it does not necessarily follow that there is an afterlife.

        1.1.1.3.1. Pro:
        The existence of a God does not in any way prove the intention of the God to create an afterlife, or allow human beings to enter it.

          1.1.1.3.1.1. Pro:
          Some faiths [12] believe in the existence of a God, but not in the existence of an afterlife.

            1.1.1.3.1.1.1. Pro:
            Jehovah's Witnesses regularly quote from Ecclesiastes [13] to prove that there is no afterlife.

              1.1.1.3.1.1.1.1. Con:
              Jehovah's Witnesses do not believe in an immediate afterlife, but they do believe in an eventual resurrection of the dead [14].

      1.1.1.4. Pro:
      The existence of God or an omnipotent creator is supported by the fact that the universe is fine-tuned [15] to allow humans to exist. The mere existence of human kind supports the theory of a God, which in turn supports the idea of an afterlife.

        1.1.1.4.1. Pro:
        If the value of pi, the density parameter [16], or the fine structure constant [17] were even slightly different, the universe would not support life as we know it.

        1.1.1.4.2. Con:
        While it appears as if the entire universe was created to facilitate the development of life, the multiverse theory says that there is an infinite number of universes, and so one would presumably have the conditions necessary [18] to support life. This theory removes weight from the argument that the universe was specifically 'fine-tuned' for life.

        1.1.1.4.3. Con:
        Climate change is making the Earth uninhabitable [19]. Therefore, the Earth is not fine-tuned to sustain human life.

          1.1.1.4.3.1. Con:
          If we believe in free will, the actions which have contributed to climate change are those of humans alone. The fact that the Earth would be perfectly habitable in the absence of climate change is sufficient evidence to say that a God, to the extent that they can, has structured the world to support human life.

            1.1.1.4.3.1.1. Con:
            One cannot believe [20] in the existence of an omnipotent God and free will at the same time. The infallible foreknowledge of a human act by God makes the act necessary and hence unfree.

            1.1.1.4.3.1.2. Con:
            The argument for the existence of a God fine-tuning the universe is much less compelling if the place we inhabit in it is 'rigged' to 'self-destruct' because of our behavior. If that's true, then its more likely that the universe is not the byproduct of a intelligent creator.

        1.1.1.4.4. Con:
        The planet humans inhabit is not special. According to scientists, some 60 billion planets [21] could sustain life.

          1.1.1.4.4.1. Pro:
          This is because although the chances of finding a life sustaining planet are small in any galaxy, the sheer number of galaxies in a massive universe means that even the small probability translates into a high number of planets of this kind.

        1.1.1.4.5. Con:
        This is an anthropocentric [22] point of view. The belief that the existence of human life is somehow special or significant is a human conceit, and does not say anything about potentially designed nature of the universe.

        1.1.1.4.6. Con:
        This argument is self-limiting. If God exists and has the capacity to create an entire universe to allow for the existence of human beings, then God could also create human beings that don't require the 'fine-tuned' nature of the universe - human beings could be created to survive without oxygen, food or water.

          1.1.1.5.1.2. Con:

            1.1.1.5.1.2.1. Pro:

              1.1.1.5.1.2.1.1. Pro:

        1.1.1.5.2. Con:
        Religion was created to placate [29] the fear of death. The afterlife provides comforting answers for the unknown and an appealing alternative to the permanence of death.

          1.1.1.5.2.1. Con:
          The thought of an afterlife does not necessarily comfort people. Many individuals are told by religions that their lives and actions will result in eternal damnation, torture, and suffering. An afterlife may therefore increase a fear of death for some.

            1.1.1.5.2.1.1. Pro:
            For instance, the Catholic Church until quite recently preached that infants who died would go neither to heaven nor hell but to an eternal limbo [30], a concept that caused thorough distress to parents.

            1.1.1.5.2.1.2. Pro:
            Muslims [31] believe that people who have committed more bad deeds than good ones in their life times are likely to be damned to hell.

            1.1.1.5.2.1.3. Pro:
            Buddhist cosmology states that an individual may end up in Naraka [32], a place of suffering and punishment, as a result of their accumulated actions in life (karma).

            1.1.1.5.2.1.4. Con:
            People who fear hell still fear the concept of "nothingness" more. The idea that their choice mean nothing (in terms of their own existence) after death is less attractive than the concept of punishment and possibly repentance/redemption.

          1.1.1.5.2.2. Pro:
          A study [33] testing the link between religion and fear of death found that very religious people exhibited less fear about the prospects of death than other participants in the study.

            1.1.1.5.2.2.1. Con:
            Research [34] found that 18% of the studies concluded that religious people were more afraid of death than non-religious people.

            1.1.1.5.2.2.2. Con:
            A more accurate interpretation of the study findings is that the relationship was curvilinear [35] and that people on the two ends of the believer-disbeliever spectrum tend to report the low fear of death.

          1.1.1.5.2.3. Pro:
          Cosmologist Stephen Hawking believed [36], following his studies into the creation, structure, and death of the universe, that the afterlife was an illusion for those "afraid of the dark" of death.

          1.1.1.5.2.4. Pro:
          A host of studies have found a positive relationship between fear of death and religiosity. (Hoelter and Epley, 1979, p. 2 -4 [37])

            1.1.1.5.2.4.1. Pro:

            1.1.1.5.2.4.2. Pro:
            Intrinsic religiosity [39] helps people cope with naturally evoked mortality salience.

            1.1.1.5.2.4.3. Con:
            This has nothing to do with how religion was created; religion could be something real that was revealed by God so that humans could have comfort in the reality of the afterlife.

        1.1.1.5.5. Pro:
        Belief in the afterlife is a core principle of Christianity [48].

          1.1.1.5.5.1. Pro:
          Christian philosophers have provided detailed arguments for the existence of the afterlife, based on logic and observation.

            1.1.1.5.5.1.1. Pro:
            John Hick [49] argues that the independent reality of the mind and brain, as mutually interacting entities or processes, considerably decreases the a priori improbability of the survival of the mind after the death of the body.

              1.1.1.5.5.1.1.1. Pro:
              The state of the person immediately upon death is subjective and dream-like [50]. This implies that a person's experience of the afterlife is what they perceive it to be.

                1.1.1.5.5.1.1.1.1. Con:
                This can only give facts about the mind before death, not what happens to the mind after death.

                1.1.1.5.5.1.1.1.2. Con:
                A dead brain does not form memories (by definition: it's dead), so no one who has died can possibly come back to recall what it was like to be dead. All NDEs are, at best, of what the end of life is like, not what the beginning of death is like.

              1.1.1.5.5.1.1.2. Con:
              It is highly implausible that mind and brain are independent.

                1.1.1.5.5.1.1.2.1. Pro:

              1.1.1.5.5.1.1.3. Con:

            1.1.1.5.5.1.2. Pro:

If the idea of a geometrical truth can exist without a physical or material basis, thought and thus a soul can also exist without a physical or material basis.

              1.1.1.5.5.1.2.1. Con:

                1.1.1.5.5.1.2.1.1. Pro:

                1.1.1.5.5.1.2.1.2. Pro:

                1.1.1.5.5.1.2.1.3. Pro:

              1.1.1.5.5.1.2.2. Con:
              It is not at all clear that belief in the existence of souls is the same as belief in an afterlife. St Thomas Aquinas argued [54] that, while souls could exist independently of bodies, neither was really complete without the other. Thus the continued existence of a soul does not imply the continued existence of a human being.

              1.1.1.5.5.1.2.3. Con:

        1.1.1.5.10. Con:
        Socrates argued that [78] living came from the dead because someone was alive, they must have been dead (death being the opposite of life). This entails that an entity, such as a soul, existed even after death.

          1.1.1.5.10.1. Con:
          Even if one accepts every part of Socrates' argument, it only proves that souls exist before birth. It does not necessarily follow that souls continue to exist following death and the destruction of the body.

            1.1.1.5.10.1.1. Con:
            If it is supposed that souls can sustain themselves in a non-bodily form before the existence of a physical being, it is reasonable to presume that they can continue to do so after death.

          1.1.1.5.10.2. Pro:
          Extending this logic, truth is the opposite of falsehood. If it is impossible to prove the falsehood of an afterlife, then it must by default be true.

            1.1.1.5.10.2.1. Con:
            It is also impossible to prove the truth of belief in the afterlife. That does not make such belief false.

          1.1.1.5.10.3. Con:
          While Socrates made many great contributions to reasoning, this wasn't one of them. This concept could be applied as "since you cannot prove the afterlife exists, it must not exist." But the argument is a non sequitur, one does not logically follow the other by nature of the two statements being contrary. It creates a false dichotomy, with the only explanations being considered are a concept and it's opposite.

          1.1.1.5.10.4. Con:
          The argument is begging the question, as it assumes there is a soul.

          1.1.1.5.10.5. Pro:
          Socrates also argued [79] (in the same vein) that things are produced from their opposites. Something bigger cannot be produced except from something smaller. Something dead cannot be produced except from something living. It follows from this that something living cannot be produced except from something dead. Thus dead souls exist before birth, and souls can exist while dead.

            1.1.1.5.10.5.1. Con:
            There is a difference between generation and alteration. Socrates conflates the distinction between being born from someone dead, and having the potential of being born out of material processes.

          1.1.1.5.10.6. Con:
          Socrates' argument rests on misleading word-play. He assumes that everything that is not alive is dead (and thus that if life is to be produced from things which are not-alive, then life must be produced from dead things). This is false. A sperm and an egg can produce new life, but neither can reasonably be described as dead (and, even if one wants to argue that they are dead, they are not dead in the same way that a deceased person is dead).

          1.1.1.5.10.7. Con:
          Socrates' argument is readily answered by modern biology. His argument is that complex life forms (such as humans) can only be produced from things which are dead. Modern biology however, understands that complex life forms emerge from the growth of less-complex life forms [80] (cells which reproduce and multiply over time [81]). These less-complex life forms are distinct from dead life forms, which are inanimate objects.

          1.1.1.5.10.8. Con:
          Socrates' argument assumes that souls exist, and that human beings are essentially unchangeable souls, which may be either "dead" or "alive". Thus according to him there is essential continuity between the three stages of human existence - pre-birth, life, and death - because in each stage the human being is a soul. This is an unjustified assumption.

            1.1.1.5.10.8.1. Pro:
            Souls are not real, therefore there cannot be an afterlife.

              1.1.1.5.10.8.1.1. Con:
              Mental operations [82], such as having an imagination or memory, suggest the existence of a soul.

                1.1.1.5.10.8.1.1.1. Pro:
                According to Kant [83], everything humans experience, including all the colors, sensations and objects they perceive, are nothing but representations in their mind.

                  1.1.1.5.10.8.1.1.1.1. Con:
                  For instance, the physical properties of light [84] that are responsible for color are characteristics of the natural universe. Therefore, the sensory experience of color is subjective, but the properties of light responsible for that sensory experience are objectively true. The mind does not create the natural phenomenon itself; it creates a subjective experience or a representation of the phenomenon.

                1.1.1.5.10.8.1.1.2. Con:
                Features such as imagination [85] and memory [86] can be directly linked to activities and parts of the brain, and so do not require the existence of a soul to be explained.

              1.1.1.5.10.8.1.2. Con:
              Plato's Theory of Recollection [87] supports the existence of the soul. He proposes that information cannot be either taught or learnt. All knowledge has already been acquired in the lifetime of the soul. All "learning" is simply the recollection of this information.

                1.1.1.5.10.8.1.2.1. Con:
                Plato described [88] the body as a prison for the soul. He believed that upon one's death, the soul moved from one body to the other. This does not support the existence of the afterlife. Rather, it reinforces the possibility of reincarnation.

                  1.1.1.5.10.8.1.2.1.1. Con:
                  Before reincarnation [89] into a new physical form, souls must spend time in a state of "limbo" where their actions are judged. This limbo can be described as a sort of afterlife.

                1.1.1.5.10.8.1.2.2. Pro:
