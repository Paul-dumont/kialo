        The Bible says [55] that plants existed before the sun.

          The sun was created on the second day, but it is described on the fourth day because that's when an observer from Earth would see it clearly for the first time (the early atmosphere was not transparent [56]). This is important because Genesis 1 is written from the perspective of an observer on Earth.

            1.2.2.2.1.1.1. Con:
            Genesis 1 is written effectively from God's perspective, not from the perspective of a hypothetical earthly observer.

              1.2.2.2.1.1.1.1. Con:

                1.2.2.2.1.1.1.1.2. Con:

                It would be highly inappropriate and unclear if it was written in the first person.

              1.2.2.2.1.1.2.1. Pro:
              This makes sense because an Israelite would have had no concept of the sun as a faraway ball of gas in space, they would only understand it as the bright disk of light in the sky during daytime.

          In Genesis 1 on the first day [61], God created light. So not having the sun might cause problems for photosynthesizing organisms today, while things were being created light did come first.

            1.2.2.2.1.4.2. Con:

          1.2.2.2.1.5. Con:

            1.2.2.2.1.5.1. Con:

        This could simply be hyperbole [273] meant to emphasize Jesus's overall point.

        1.5.3.2.2. Con:
        The mustard seed is the smallest seed a local farmer would have planted and been aware of [274], thus making the statement true from the perspective of Jesus's audience.

      1.5.3.3. Pro:
      Matthew 13:32 [275] incorrectly implies that the mustard seed grows into a tree when actually [276] it grows into a bush.

    1.5.4. Pro:
    Some Bible passages contradict biological facts about microorganisms.

      1.5.4.1. Pro:
      Leviticus 13:47-59 [277] describes leprosy infecting inanimate objects such as clothing, which we now know isn't possible.

        1.5.4.1.1. Con:
        The "leprous disease" was probably a general enough term [278] to refer to molds or similar things that can taint garments.

        1.5.4.1.2. Con:
        The transmission of leprosy is still unknown [279] and therefore clothing could be reasonably expected to transmit this bacteria.

    1.6.1. Pro:

      1.6.1.1. Con:
      The Bible never claims or implies that Noah's flood was global.

        1.6.1.1.1. Con:
        -> See discussion #50211: The Bible claims that Noah's flood was global.

      1.7.1.1. Con:
