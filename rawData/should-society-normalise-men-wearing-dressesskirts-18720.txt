Discussion Name:
Should Society Normalise Men Wearing Dresses/Skirts?

1. Thesis:
Society should normalise men wearing dresses/skirts.

  1.1. Pro:
  People ought to be free to wear what they want without being subjected to negative social consequences.

    1.1.1. Con:
    Nudity is also a choice of clothing, yet most societies consider public nudity to be unacceptable. Since most societies are comfortable imposing such limits, it is therefore reasonable to accept that other clothing choices should be stigmatized.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      There is a meaningful distinction between what is appropriate in public [1] and what is appropriate in private.

        1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
        It is in society's interest to encourage certain behaviors and values and discourage others, regardless of whether individuals agree.

        1.1.1.1.2. Con:
        The prohibition of public nudity is based on an artificial construct of the normal state of a human body; the natural state of the human body is naked [2], but society defines this as nude [3].

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      There is a difference between the act of covering and what you cover with.

        1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
        Being nude in public can lead to charges of indecent exposure [4]; people wearing gender-nonconforming clothing remain clothed.

        1.1.1.2.2. Con:
        In many cases, such as with revealing swimwear, the extent to which one's body is covered is directly related to one's choice of clothing.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    Clothing is a creative way of expressing oneself, and all forms of self-expression should be valid.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      Expanding the range of expression removes limitations and allows new forms to emerge. As an example, the normalization of women wearing pants/trousers has lead to a panoply of new fashion, whereas men's fashion has remained relatively static.

        1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
        Men's fashion is often characterized as boring [5]: a problem which the addition of skirts and dresses would help to alleviate.

        1.1.2.1.2. Con:
        Throughout the 20th and early 21st centuries, every decade has seen significant changes [6] in men's fashion.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      There will always be social restrictions on what is acceptable to wear. In the same way that goths [7] attract a degree of social condemnation, fashions like men wearing skirts will do the same.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        This may be a harmful social attitude, as the superficial judgement of goths can place vulnerable people in an even more precarious position [8].

        1.1.2.2.2. Pro:
        Virtually every society on Earth has a notion of socially-appropriate clothing.

      1.1.2.3. Con:
      Unrestricted self-expression can lead to unintended consequences, such as expressions of racism, homophobia, or sexism. It can also lead to expressions of sexuality that may not be considered appropriate for certain people or in certain circumstances.

        1.1.2.3.1. Con:
        Wearing a skirt does not produce any of these adverse consequences.

        1.1.2.3.2. Pro:
        Because there are clear circumstances in which society shouldn't normalize self-expression via clothing, preventing the normalization of men wearing skirts is valid, despite its infringement on expression.

        1.1.2.3.3. Con:
        Unintended consequences such as these are the cost of a free society. Freedom of speech prevents us from stopping racist, homophobic, or sexist comments. If these unintended consequences are acceptable in other circumstances, they should be acceptable in this case.

      1.1.2.4. Pro:
      Clothing reflects, rather than defines, the wearer's identity. It makes no sense to fear an honest display more than a forced concealment.

        1.1.2.4.1. Con:
        The imposition of clothing norms over an individual's life combines with other forms of social pressure to limit, shape, and define their identity.

        1.1.2.4.2. Pro:
        The practical aspects of clothing are rather obvious. Variations on forms, colours, shapes, etc. are neither mandatory nor prohibited.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Since it does not create any harm, there is no need for society to interfere in an individual's choice of clothing.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      It would create an oppressive and unwelcoming society if individuals felt that it was their right to police what others wear.

        1.1.3.1.1. Con:
        Fashion policing [9] is a common practice in the US and some other societies.

        1.1.3.1.2. Pro:
        Outside of their home countries, religious enforcers who police people's clothing [10] are almost universally maligned.

        1.1.3.1.3. Con:
        When others wear clothing that is unwelcome by most of society, the most reasonable thing to do is police their clothing.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      Society is not a person; it does not interfere. Rather, individual people may find it strange if someone does something uncommon.

        1.1.3.2.1. Pro:
        Some individuals will always be critical of others' behaviors, no matter the societal norms.

          1.1.3.2.1.1. Pro:
          In most societies, it is thoroughly normalized for women to wear pants, yet there are still some who contend that this is wrong [11].

          1.1.3.2.1.2. Con:
          It is much easier to ignore criticism from a minority than from a majority.

        1.1.3.2.2. Con:
        Societal norms are the aggregate [12] of individual opinions and actions.

        1.1.3.2.3. Con:
        When a significant number of individuals within a society interfere with the lives of some members, this constitutes majority influence [13].

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      Basic freedom generally includes being able to choose what one wears (p. 13 [14]).

        1.1.3.3.1. Pro:
        The ACLU [15] supports the ability to choose one's clothing as a basic human right.

        1.1.3.3.2. Con:
        Freedom to wear what you want does not mean you are free from being criticized because what you are wearing violates social norms.

          1.1.3.3.2.1. Con:
          If the norm is changed as suggested by the thesis, this criticism would not be available.

            1.1.3.3.2.1.1. Con:
            The above statement is about criticism for violating norms in general. Violating norms will always be critiqued, no matter how the norms are changed.

          1.1.3.3.2.2. Pro:
          Someone may be legally free to wear a profane or discriminatory message on their clothing, yet it would not be frowned upon to criticize such choices.

      1.1.3.4. Pro:
      A man who chooses to wear a dress or skirt does not harm anyone by doing so.

        1.1.3.4.1. Con:
        Destroying the gender binary harms society, as the gender binary is the building block of human social groups (p. 732 [16]).

        1.1.3.4.2. Pro:
        Even if some are offended or upset by the violation of gender norms, an individual bears no responsibility for the ways in which their personal choices are interpreted by others.

      1.1.3.5. Con:
      Profanity, when it is not directed at anyone, is just as harmless as a man wearing a skirt. However, it is generally accepted to discourage profanity in public, so it is no less strange to discourage men from wearing skirts.

        1.1.3.5.1. Con:
        Although profanity may be discouraged, it generally remains protected speech under the First Amendment [17]. Men wearing dresses/skirts should be an equally-protected form of expression.

          1.1.3.5.1.1. Pro:
          In the US court case Doe v. Yunits [18], the court ruled that dress is a form of individual expression, protected under the constitutional guarantee of freedom of speech.

        1.1.3.5.2. Con:
        Profanity is not harmless; it is correlated with increased aggressive behavior [19] in middle-school children. Dresses/skirts are not associated with such social harm.

        1.1.3.5.3. Pro:
        According to Christian belief, men wearing dresses/skirts and using profanity [20] are both sinful and should not be allowed.

          1.1.3.5.3.1. Con:
          Many Christians do not believe that cursing or men wearing dresses/skirts are inherently sinful, but rather that the intention is more important [21].

          1.1.3.5.3.2. Pro:
          Men wearing dresses is clearly prohibited in the Bible (Deuteronomy 22:5 [22]).

            1.1.3.5.3.2.1. Pro:
            There are a number of Bible passages [23] that have been interpreted as prohibiting cross dressing; the Christian view supports traditional gender roles as divinely inspired [24].

              1.1.3.5.3.2.1.1. Con:
              Focus on the Family [25] is an organization with a reputation for spreading transphobia and homophobia under the guise of religious conserativism. As such, their interpretation of the scriptures should be regarded as fundamentally biased.

              1.1.3.5.3.2.1.2. Con:
              There is no biblical support for the claim that "gender roles are divinely inspired." To the contrary, Jesus affirmed the identity of eunuchs in Matthew 19:12 [26].

              1.1.3.5.3.2.1.3. Con:
              The parent claim makes an unfounded assertion about "the Christian view." Christians hold a wide range of different views about gender (p. 8 [27]).

            1.1.3.5.3.2.2. Con:
            There are Christians who interpret this passage differently and are accepting of cross-dressing [28].

            1.1.3.5.3.2.3. Con:
            The verse prohibits both men and women from wearing clothes intended for the other gender. Given that women wear men's clothing (e.g., trousers), citing this verse to prohibit men from wearing skirts reinforces a sexist double standard.

            1.1.3.5.3.2.4. Con:
            Jesus repeatedly dismissed [29] the laws of the Old Testament in favor of loving one another.

        1.1.3.5.4. Con:
        Unlike profanity, which is discouraged among all types of people, skirts and dresses are seen as perfectly acceptable when worn by women.

          1.1.3.5.4.1. Con:
          Profanity is not always discouraged and studies indicate that it is becoming increasingly common [30].

        1.1.3.5.5. Pro:
        Clothing that carries a profane or discriminatory message is typically considered to be socially "off limits."

          1.1.3.5.5.1. Con:
          The US Supreme Court ruled that denying trademarks for immoral or scandalous material, such as profane messaging, is a violation of free speech [31].

            1.1.3.5.5.1.1. Con:
            In the Court's opinion on the same case, it asserted that a ban on profanity does not necessarily violate the free speech protections, and that a more targeted statute could legally exclude profane messages [32].

          1.1.3.5.5.2. Pro:
          Profane clothing is generally impermissible in schools [33] and private businesses, such as airlines [34] and cruise operators [35].

          1.1.3.5.5.3. Con:
          There are numerous examples of popular t-shirts with offensive or profane messages [36].

      1.1.3.6. Con:
      There are countless non-harmful activities and practices that are not accepted as norms, such as having one's face tattooed [37].

    1.1.4. Con:
    Men should only be allowed to cross-dress in private.

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      If the same were said of other common activities that some might disagree with, such as open displays of religious belief, this would be considered unlawful discrimination.

      1.1.4.2. Pro:
      The rejection of non-normative gender expressions [38] in others, such as cross-dressing, is a normal part of developing a gender identity.

        1.1.4.2.1. Pro:
        People should be free to hold opinions on the proper differentiation of the sexes.

          1.1.4.2.1.1. Con:
          Although people may hold whatever opinion they choose, taking actions to limit the expression [39] of others is a violation of their human rights.

          1.1.4.2.1.2. Pro:
          The right to free speech and self-expression is a cornerstone of civil liberties [40], even when such expression is considered regressive.

        1.1.4.2.2. Con:
        People should open their minds to cross-dressing. Opposing men wearing skirts is sexist.

          1.1.4.2.2.1. Pro:
          Today, the concept of cross-dressing almost exclusively, and therefore disproportionately, applies to men as it is normal [41] for women to wear traditionally-masculine clothing.

      1.1.4.3. Pro:
      Mismatches between sex and gender expression, as occurs when biological males wear typically-female clothing like skirts, may elicit a phenomenon similar to the uncanny valley [42], which is uncomfortable for dressers and viewers alike.

        1.1.4.3.1. Con:
        If men don't mind appearing uncanny, wearing a skirt should be an accepted form of self-expression.

        1.1.4.3.2. Pro:
        People should not be made to feel uncomfortable by the clothing choices of others.

          1.1.4.3.2.1. Con:
          An individual's clothing choice is not the concern of others; a man wearing a dress/skirt bears no responsibility for the discomfort caused by another's biases.

        1.1.4.3.3. Con:
        Humans continue to produce and utilize humanoid robots [43] whose appearance falls within the uncanny valley.

      1.1.4.4. Con:
      Male skirt-wearing isn't cross-dressing if it's normalised.

        1.1.4.4.1. Pro:
        Cross-dressing [44] implies that one is wearing clothing that is not typical for them. The term would become meaningless if men wearing dresses/skirts was normalised.

      1.1.4.5. Con:
      In Western society, the prevailing concept of freedom allows people to do what they want so long as it doesn't infringe on the freedom of others.

        1.1.4.5.1. Con:
        Not all societies support this interpretation of freedom.

        1.1.4.5.2. Pro:
        "Your liberty to swing your fist ends just where my nose begins" is a popular truism [45] regarding the balance of individual freedoms.

        1.1.4.5.3. Pro:
        A simple choice of modest, innocuous clothing does not infringe on others' freedom.

    1.1.5. Con:
    Despite attempts to normalize men wearing dresses/skirts, men who do so are still likely to be perceived negatively by others.

      1.1.5.1. Pro:
      The entire question of normalization presupposes that a man wearing a skirt or dress is currently perceived as aberrant.

        1.1.5.1.1. Pro:
        Men who wear skirts or dresses are well aware of the potential for negative reactions, up to the point of violence [46].

          1.1.5.1.1.1. Pro:
          Because the risk of attracting negative attention is so present, men wearing a skirt or dress is often portrayed as an act of bravery [47] by supporters.

            1.1.5.1.1.1.1. Con:
            This is also an example of how men who wear skirts or dresses can be perceived in a decidedly positive light.

          1.1.5.1.1.2. Con:
          Reactions to deviation from social norms are culturally relative and vary widely [48]. In some cultures, violating norms about gendered clothing would be considered a harmless deviation.

        1.1.5.1.2. Con:
        A man wearing a skirt or dress is only aberrant according to the values of a particular culture.

          1.1.5.1.2.1. Pro:
          It was thoroughly normalized for men to wear skirt- or dress-like garments in the ancient world [49], just as it is normal in many contemporary cultures [50].

      1.1.5.2. Con:
      Negative perceptions from others are not a valid reason for repressing self-expression.

      1.1.5.3. Pro:
      Men who dress traditionally are more confident and respected [51].

        1.1.5.3.1. Con:
        The cited research [52] does not support the claim: the study only investigated the perception of tailored versus off-the-rack suits, not the perception of traditional versus nontraditional dress among men.

        1.1.5.3.2. Pro:
        Men who wear a suit and tie in professional roles are perceived as more suitable, capable, and affable [53].

          1.1.5.3.2.1. Con:
          This perception is purely a result of social conditioning; these are not inherent properties of the fabric or garment.

          1.1.5.3.2.2. Pro:
          This is why men are usually required to wear a suit and tie when they assume positions of leadership.

            1.1.5.3.2.2.1. Con:
            Even if men are required to wear traditional formal attire at work, this does not mean that they should be negatively perceived for wearing a dress or skirt on their personal time.

          1.1.5.3.2.3. Con:
          Not every outfit aims to make the wearer appear serious or competent, as people also choose clothing to look attractive, feel comfortable, or express themselves in some way. Wearing a skirt or dress can fulfill any of these other functions.

        1.1.5.3.3. Con:
        This is a circular argument; men feel more confident and respected in traditional attire because they are fulfilling social norms. If the norms were altered, men would feel equally confident and respected while wearing skirts or dresses.

      1.1.5.4. Con:
      Normalization is a long process. Over time, the increased number of men wearing skirts or dresses will help to remove the stigma around this choice of attire.

        1.1.5.4.1. Pro:
        Performing a stigmatized act is a crucial step toward normalizing it.

        1.1.5.4.2. Pro:
        Men may elect to wear skirts or dresses in order to intentionally change the public's perception of acceptable masculine attire.

      1.1.5.5. Pro:
      Fashion isn't changed solely as a result of discourse; there must be voluntary public uptake [54]. Thus, if the public is not willing to accept a change in fashion, one will not occur.

        1.1.5.5.1. Pro:
        A man wearing a dress might as well be wearing a ruff and cape. No matter what he says, he will still clearly be the odd one out.

          1.1.5.5.1.1. Con:
          A ruff and cape may be perceived as ridiculous because it is thoroughly anachronistic attire. However, the comparison to men wearing dresses and skirts does not hold up because these garments are perfectly acceptable for women.

        1.1.5.5.2. Con:
        Discourse around fashion [55] is a key part of priming the public for new trends.

      1.1.5.6. Con:
      Short skirts can allow men's legs to be more easily appreciated [56].

        1.1.5.6.1. Con:
        The objectification of men's bodies is not a valid reason to normalize their wearing of dresses or skirts.

          1.1.5.6.1.1. Con:
          Men should be free to wear whatever clothing they wish in order to highlight their physical attributes.

            1.1.5.6.1.1.1. Con:
            Fashion that highlights men's genitals has been widely denounced [57], and may constitute indecent exposure [58] in many places.

          1.1.5.6.1.2. Pro:
          Objectifying people can have many negative impacts [59] on their mental health.

          1.1.5.6.1.3. Con:
          The objectification of bodies is at the root of many sartorial norms, such as well-fitting suits on men or skirts and high heels on women.

        1.1.5.6.2. Pro:
        It is normal for men to show off their upper bodies [60] by wearing sleeveless t-shirts. Wearing a skirt fulfills the same function for men's lower bodies.

        1.1.5.6.3. Con:
        The sole function of a short skirt is not to show off the wearer's legs.

          1.1.5.6.3.1. Con:
          Although this may not be the sole function of a short skirt, it is nevertheless an inevitable result of wearing a short skirt.

      1.1.5.7. Con:
      Celebrities like Dan Levy [61], who regularly wears skirts, prove that a man can wear a skirt and still attain great success in society.

    1.1.6. Pro:
    Some men have a different fashion sense and enjoy wearing skirts or dresses.

      1.1.6.1. Pro:
      More men have been wearing skirts in recent years [62], largely due to their comfort and style.

        1.1.6.1.1. Pro:
        An increasing number of high fashion designers [63] are producing skirts and dresses for men.

        1.1.6.1.2. Con:
        The greater number of men wearing skirts in recent years is attributable to increasing media hype [64], rather than comfort and style.

      1.1.6.2. Con:
      Just because someone enjoys something doesn't mean that it should always be met with social approval.

        1.1.6.2.1. Con:
        If an act is legal and harmless to others, then there is no reason why someone should face disapproval for doing it.

        1.1.6.2.2. Pro:
        -> See 1.1.1.1.1.

    1.1.7. Con:
    Men are already free to wear what they want, just as everyone else is free to judge them based on how they dress.

      1.1.7.1. Con:
      In the US it is generally legal for employers to enforce dress codes based on gender norms [65], which is oppressive to both men and women.

        1.1.7.1.1. Pro:
        In the US, employee dress codes are only considered illegal discrimination if they place a heavier burden [66] on one gender over another.

          1.1.7.1.1.1. Pro:
          Forcing men to conform to a smaller range of available clothing options than women constitutes a discriminatory burden.

          1.1.7.1.1.2. Con:
          The lack of dresses/skirts as an acceptable option for men in employee dress codes is a minor inconvenience, at best.

        1.1.7.1.2. Con:
        There are numerous formal and informal norms [67] that employees are expected to adhere to in the workplace. Dress codes are no different than these other norms.

          1.1.7.1.2.1. Con:
          While norms around procedure and interpersonal interaction all have a concrete effect on workplace function, a gendered dress code does not have any clear benefit for productivity.

          1.1.7.1.2.2. Pro:
          Promoting traditional gendered dress norms in the workplace helps avoid the distractions and tensions inherently caused by nonnormative behavior.

        1.1.7.1.3. Con:
        Many men are unlikely to adopt dresses/skirts in the workplace since their primary consideration when choosing work attire is avoiding undue attention (p. 91 [68]).

        1.1.7.1.4. Pro:
        The enforcement of gender norms as a condition of employment has forced some women to become more sexualized for their jobs [69].

          1.1.7.1.4.1. Con:
          In the US, the sexualization of women through their clothing is a legally-protected element of some business models [70], and it is essential in certain jobs, such as pornography or exotic dancing.

            1.1.7.1.4.1.1. Pro:
            There is nothing inherently wrong with sexualization; opposition to sexualized dress is predicated on sex-negative [71] attitudes and beliefs.

            1.1.7.1.4.1.2. Con:
            The fact that some business models rely on the sexualization of women does not make the sexualization of women acceptable in all cases.

          1.1.7.1.4.2. Pro:
          Requiring women to become sexualized as a condition of employment can lead to sexual harassment [72].

        1.1.7.1.5. Con:
        Private employers should be allowed to determine what is appropriate attire for their employees.

          1.1.7.1.5.1. Pro:
          The point of a business is to make profit, not challenge existing norms. Thus, if enforcing gender norms increases profitability, then businesses should be free to do so.

          1.1.7.1.5.2. Con:
          While an employer should have considerable license to define appropriate workplace attire, gender should not be a factor in this decision, as this promotes sexism.

            1.1.7.1.5.2.1. Con:
            If anything, gender norms around workplace dress are a result of, rather than a source of, existing sexism.

          1.1.7.1.5.3. Pro:
          Employers bear the risk of the business failing, therefore they should have the right to make decisions about issues that impact the success of the business, including choosing appropriate employee attire.

      1.1.7.2. Con:
      In schools with dress codes, female students often have the option of choosing a school uniform with pants or a skirt, whereas male students are restricted to pants [73].

        1.1.7.2.1. Con:
        In the US, school dress codes that bar male students from wearing skirts or dresses are subject to legal challenges [74] based on freedom of speech.

          1.1.7.2.1.1. Con:
          This may not be the case for private schools as they are not subject to the same restrictions as public schools in determining dress policies.

          1.1.7.2.1.2. Pro:
          Public schools are forbidden from creating dress code policies that discriminate on the basis of sex [75].

        1.1.7.2.2. Pro:
        Male students can face administrative punishments [76] for wearing typically female clothing, like skirts or dresses.

          1.1.7.2.2.1. Con:
          Such punishments may well violate federal law, as they are discriminatory based on sex stereotypes (p. 1 [77]).

      1.1.7.3. Con:
      Although people are free to judge, when an act has been normalized, they are less inclined to do so harshly.

        1.1.7.3.1. Pro:
        Normalisation of a wider range of acceptable dress options would promote pluralism [78] and thus increase respect for human diversity.

      1.1.7.4. Pro:
      This principle is generally protected by the right to free speech.

        1.1.7.4.1. Pro:
        -> See 1.1.3.5.1.1.

        1.1.7.4.2. Pro:
        In Matal v. Tam [79], the US Supreme Court ruled that under the constitutional principle of free speech, the government cannot discriminate against speech that expresses the speaker's point of view.

        1.1.7.4.3. Con:
        Based on the social norm that skirts and dresses are exclusively women's clothing, the US routinely treated cross-dressers as criminals [80] throughout the 20th century, and 13 countries still criminalize cross-dressing [81].

          1.1.7.4.3.1. Pro:
          Laws against cross-dressing have been used as a tool to enforce gender norms [82] on gender minorities.

          1.1.7.4.3.2. Con:
          -> See 1.1.4.4.

    1.1.8. Pro:
    Men could be a lot more comfortable this way.

      1.1.8.1. Pro:
      Men are often prohibited [83] from wearing shorts in more formal settings. When the weather is warm, often men must remain uncomfortable in full-length trousers. If men were allowed to wear skirts or dresses, they could maintain a formal appearance and comfort at the same time.

        1.1.8.1.1. Pro:
        Among Scottish people, kilts are already an accepted item [84] of formal wear.

          1.1.8.1.1.1. Pro:
          Kilts are comfortable and don't pinch or squish, where traditional pants (which will always have their place) leave men frequently re-adjusting.

          1.1.8.1.1.2. Pro:
          Normalizing skirts and dresses would be a natural extension of the already normalized kilts.

          1.1.8.1.1.3. Con:
          Adopting kilts in other parts of the world could be considered cultural appropriation [85].

        1.1.8.1.2. Con:
        Formal shorts could be normalized instead of normalizing dresses and skirts for men.

          1.1.8.1.2.1. Pro:
          Bermuda shorts [86] are already considered appropriate dress wear in some places, and could be normalized as formal wear.

      1.1.8.2. Con:
      The additional comfort may be nice, but it is unlikely to outweigh the associated social ostracization for many men.

        1.1.8.2.1. Con:
        A great many people consistently choose clothing based on comfort over style [87].

        1.1.8.2.2. Pro:
        Research shows that the desire to adhere to societal expectations is a primary factor in men's clothing choices (p. 1 [88]).

      1.1.8.3. Pro:
      Dresses, skirts, and similar clothing allow for greater air flow to critical regions.

        1.1.8.3.1. Pro:
        Male genitalia are hotter than body temperature and require better ventilation. This is likely why the testes are external rather than internal [89]. A skirt would allow better ventilation and result in the production of a greater number of viable sperm.

        1.1.8.3.2. Con:
        For most men, the air flow in pants or shorts is sufficiently comfortable.

      1.1.8.4. Pro:
      The freedom to wear whatever they wish will make men more comfortable, regardless of what they choose to wear.

        1.1.8.4.1. Pro:
        There is a causal relationship [90] between wearing the clothing that makes one feel comfortable and increased self-confidence.

      1.1.8.5. Con:
      It might not be as easy to incorporate a zipper into a skirt to allow men to urinate while standing. Having to lift the entire skirt can be bothersome in the case of long skirts.

        1.1.8.5.1. Pro:
        Men are unlikely to adopt any garment that makes urination more difficult.

        1.1.8.5.2. Con:
        Troubles with urination clearly aren't prohibitive for the many women who regularly wear skirts or dresses.

        1.1.8.5.3. Con:
        Skirts with zippers in the front [91] are quite common.

      1.1.8.6. Con:
      Skirts and dresses are often more figure-hugging and binding than trousers, resulting in less overall comfort.

        1.1.8.6.1. Pro:
        Skirts can cause uncomfortable thigh chafing [92].

          1.1.8.6.1.1. Con:
          Many people find skirts phenomenally comfortable. Anecdotal experience does not dictate absolute truth. This is about allowing people to choose their preference.

          1.1.8.6.1.2. Con:
          Thigh chafing can be easily prevented and does not affect everyone.

          1.1.8.6.1.3. Pro:
          Part of comfort is practicability, and many people find pants more comfortable because they are more practical [93].

        1.1.8.6.2. Con:
