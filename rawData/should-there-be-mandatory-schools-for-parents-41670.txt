Discussion Name:
Should there be mandatory schools for parents?

1. Thesis:
There should be mandatory schools for parents.

  1.1. Pro:
  Mandatory school for parents will result in better outcomes for children.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Children will grow up to commit fewer violent acts, as studies have shown [1] that violence in adult life is correlated with violence seen or experienced as a child.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      Many people who experience violent home environments do not go on to commit violent acts.

        1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
        Likewise, not all who commit violence grew up in a violent home. Many factors can contribute to an adult's violent behavior.

      1.1.1.2. Pro:
      Domestic violence is prevalent [2], so it is imperative that steps are taken to reduce it.

        1.1.1.2.1. Con:
        People who engage in domestic violence need professional help beyond just mandatory parenting classes.

      1.1.1.3. Con:
      If combating domestic abuse were as easy as sending people to parenting school, it would not be such a deeply rooted, complex issue.

        1.1.1.3.1. Pro:
        Domestic violence is heavily interlinked with alcohol abuse and addiction [3]. It is unlikely that mandatory parenting school would be able to rehabilitate parents.

    1.1.2. Con:
    Mandatory schools will not be able to address many of the societal factors that hinder children's development.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      There are a number of socioeconomic factors that influence [4] a child's upbringing.

        1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
        Parents may not be able to implement strategies for child rearing taught at parenting schools due to socioeconomic factors.

          1.1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
          Many parents cannot afford [5] the best baby formulas or most stimulating toys for their children.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      Kids raised in single parent households often have a more difficult time [6] than those raised by two parents. A parenting school would not be able to address this.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        There could be classes specifically tailored to single parents.

      1.1.2.3. Con:
      This is a separate issue that should be addressed by separate means.

        1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
        Societal factors hindering a child's development are best addressed by politicians who can implement effective policies.

    1.1.3. Con:
    No school can teach to each child's individual needs. This will render many of the lessons inapplicable.

      1.1.3.1. Con:
      There are generic activities that all parents go through, like changing their baby's diapers. This could be addressed in the school.

        1.1.3.1.1. Con:
        Parents do not need to go to school in order to learn the basics of parenting, like changing a diaper [7].

      1.1.3.2. Pro:
      All children have physical, mental, and behavioral differences that would render these classes ineffective.

        1.1.3.2.1. Pro:
        Parents of child geniuses have a very different set [8] of parenting issues to deal with than parents of children with learning disabilities.

    1.1.4. Pro:
    There will be fewer children with mental health problems if a mandatory parenting school is implemented.

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      Parenting lessons would likely teach parents how to help their child maintain better mental and emotional health.

        1.1.4.1.1. Pro:
        Parents are unlikely to have the time to wade through the vast amount of child psychology literature [9] without a mandatory class.

      1.1.4.2. Con:
      In some cases, mental disorders are caused by biological factors. [10]

        1.1.4.2.1. Con:
        Parent classes will be able to address issues like raising a child with mental or emotional problems.

      1.1.4.3. Con:
      There is no guarantee that parents will internalize what is being taught in school and raise their children accordingly.

        1.1.4.3.1. Pro:
        Parents could simply attend school just so that they're allowed to have children. There is no way to ensure that they raise their children based on what they were taught.

        1.1.4.3.2. Pro:
        Parents could be unable to meet the standards of what they have learned because they lack the intellectual or emotional capacity.

          1.1.4.3.2.1. Con:
          Parenting schools may demand that parents 'graduate' from their school by demonstrating an understanding of the content that has been taught.

          1.1.4.3.2.2. Pro:
          A parent who physically abused their child might have learned that it's wrong to do so, but could then be unable to stop the abuse because they lack coping [11] strategies for conflict.

          1.1.4.3.2.3. Con:
          The contact time with parenting professionals may make it easier to identify where parents are failing to meet standards so that they receive more help.

        1.1.4.3.3. Con:
        Nobody would expect parents to become perfect. If the effects of the parenting school were nontheless positive, it would be worth implementing.

          1.1.4.3.3.1. Pro:
          Research has found evidence [12] that parenting courses can have positive effects on parents' psycho-social well-being.

  1.2. Pro:
  Mandatory school will improve the lives of parents by reducing many of the uncertainties of child rearing.

    1.2.1. Con:
    Mandatory school would make parents' lives harder by adding to the stress they already face.

      1.2.1.1. Con:
      Going to school could be a chance for parents to leave the rest of their stresses behind in order to go learn something that will benefit them and their child.

        1.2.1.1.1. Con:
        If they are attending school after their baby is born, parents may have to pay for a babysitter or take their baby with them to class, both of which could be sources of stress.

          1.2.1.1.1.1. Con:
          The schools could have childcare onsite to ease this burden for parents.

      1.2.1.2. Pro:
      This is likely to negatively impact the lives of women more, as it is not always evident who the father is.

        1.2.1.2.1. Pro:
        Women are already forced to partake in large amounts of unpaid labor [13]. Including a mandatory parenting school would add to this burden.

      1.2.1.3. Pro:
      Parents will be under more stress because they would have to attend school.

        1.2.1.3.1. Con:
        It is unclear whether it would significantly increase stress or reduce and whether either would be negative [14] or [15] when [16] moderate [17] positive [18].

        1.2.1.3.2. Pro:
        If there are exams, parents would have to study for them. These could be a source of stress for them.

        1.2.1.3.3. Pro:
        Stress from school can have negative health implications [19]. This would not be ethical to induce for something that is not a necessity.

          1.2.1.3.3.1. Pro:
          Increasing a woman's day to day stress may have negative implications [20] for her ability to conceive. This means that parenting school may be counterproductive for having children.

        1.2.1.3.4. Con:
        Adults may be better placed to deal with school stress, as they can more easily implement mitigation measures [21] than teenagers or children. This could be things like self-care, or reaching out to support networks.

      1.2.1.4. Con:
      Learning how to parent in school could prevent parents from feeling overwhelmed [22] in their parenting life, which is a major cause of mental health problems.

        1.2.1.4.1. Con:
        If parents are suffering from mental health issues, they should seek professional help, not go to a parenting school.

    1.2.2. Con:
    Parents have figured out the uncertainties of parenting since prehistoric times [23]. There is no need to have mandatory schools for this now.

      1.2.2.1. Con:
      Life is much more complicated today than in prehistoric times, with societal and cultural changes happening more rapidly [24]. Parents should be educated in how to address these changes.

        1.2.2.1.1. Pro:
        Parents who don't go to parenting classes might not know about the latest apps [25] or techniques that could make their lives easier.

      1.2.2.2. Pro:
      Parents of every generation up to now have gotten along fine without mandatory schools, including parents of the generations alive today.

        1.2.2.2.1. Con:
        Outcomes for parents and children could have been even better in the past if those generations had had access to modern tools and knowledge.

    1.2.3. Pro:
    Many parents say that they are unprepared for having a child.

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      There are plenty of free resources [26] available to learn how to raise a child.

        1.2.3.1.1. Pro:
        Internet websites and local health centers can be sources of knowledge [27] on how to raise children.

        1.2.3.1.2. Con:
        Accessing these resources relies on parents taking the initiative. Many parents might think such resources aren't necessary and therefore not seek them out.

      1.2.3.2. Pro:
      A large portion of pregnancies are unplanned, with the parents not being ready to take on the responsibilities of a child.

        1.2.3.2.1. Pro:
        Parents who take classes will be more prepared for parenthood and thus more likely to appreciate being a parent, even if the pregnancy was unplanned.

        1.2.3.2.2. Pro:
        Approximately 44% [28] of all pregnancies were unplanned worldwide.

          1.2.3.2.2.1. Pro:
          In the UK, one sixth [29] of pregnancies are unplanned.

        1.2.3.2.3. Con:
        Whether a pregnancy is planned or not, parents could still benefit from learning the best parenting methods.

      1.2.3.3. Pro:
      Teens who get pregnant are likely to be unprepared [30] for the responsibilities of parenthood, and thus would benefit from a class.

        1.2.3.3.1. Con:
        Teens are probably already in high school classes and would be less inclined to pay attention during a parenting class.

          1.2.3.3.1.1. Con:
          Pregnant teens are likely to see a parenting class as much more useful than most of their high school curriculum.

    1.2.4. Pro:
    Parents will be under less stress because they will know what do to in certain situations.

      1.2.4.1. Pro:
      It is easier to face difficult situations when you already have some experience.

        1.2.4.1.1. Con:
        One could get first-hand experience of raising a child by doing it. For example, raising one's second child will be easier after the first.

          1.2.4.1.1.1. Pro:
          Previously these issues may have been dealt with through intergenerational household structures. These often lent support [31] and expertise.

        1.2.4.1.2. Pro:
        Parents can sometimes feel shocked when their child has a tantrum [32] and might not know how to react. If they are aware of issues like these, they will handle them more effectively.

      1.2.4.2. Con:
      Learning about stressful situations in a classroom is different from actually living it.

        1.2.4.2.1. Pro:
        If one's own child is in a difficult or dangerous situation, people are more likely to rely on instinctive, emotional reactions, rather than on what they learned in school.

      1.2.4.3. Pro:
      Stress may also be reduced as parents have better support [33] structures, including other new parents and parenting professionals.

        1.2.4.3.1. Pro:
        Parenting classes will be a good way for new and soon-to-be parents to meet others in the same situation. This will allow them to share best practices and tips.

          1.2.4.3.1.1. Pro:
          Lots of people feel out of their depth parenting, and really struggle to advise [34] their friends. This makes life harder for new parents who are looking for support.

  1.3. Con:
  Parents should be free to raise their children without interference from the state.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    Deciding a curriculum based on government standards would be hard, if not impossible.

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      Parents may have different value systems to those they have been taught and choose to raise their children according to those values.

        1.3.1.1.1. Con:
        Parent schools would not teach values, but only standard best practices for child rearing.

        1.3.1.1.2. Pro:
        Parents might not agree with the viewpoints of the course for religious, personal, or moral reasons. In which case, it is likely that they would not apply what they learned.

        1.3.1.1.3. Pro:
        The right to family life [35] provides parents with great leeway to bring up their children in accordance with their social, cultural, and religious values. It would be impossible to draft a curriculum that caters to all and does not undermine this right.

      1.3.1.2. Pro:
      To design the curriculum, a neutral institution would need to be in charge. Otherwise, third-party interests could manipulate the contents.

        1.3.1.2.1. Pro:
        If governments were in charge, it is likely they would use the opportunity to push their political agenda [36] on families.

        1.3.1.2.2. Pro:
        The Catholic Church already provides courses for couples that will be married. The intention is to train them as parents, but also to cultivate Catholic values [37].

        1.3.1.2.3. Con:
        There is no such thing as value neutrality; for example [38], schools often teach English story telling skills based on the majority culture and unintentionally restrict the education of minority culture children.

      1.3.1.3. Pro:
      There are different styles of parenting [39] a child. It would be difficult to ascertain which style is better and should be taught.

        1.3.1.3.1. Con:
        Scientific research [40] into child development is an objective and reliable source of information about which parenting techniques are best.

        1.3.1.3.2. Pro:
        Governments might discriminate against minority cultures and how they would like to raise their children.

          1.3.1.3.2.1. Con:
          Government discrimination can be addressed via the courts. It is not a reason to abandon mandatory parenting schools.

      1.3.1.4. Con:
      There is extensive [41] evidence nowadays about what good parenting looks like. Deciding the curriculum would be a matter of teaching parents the bare minimum.

        1.3.1.4.1. Pro:
        A parenting school curriculum could include core subjects such as basic health, nutrition, speech, and child psychology, rather than how to parent.

    1.3.2. Con:
    State interference is already widely accepted in cases of child abuse or neglect [42].

      1.3.2.1. Pro:
      By preemptively addressing issues like child abuse or neglect, parenting schools will save the state money in the long run, while helping to reduce such activities.

        1.3.2.1.1. Con:
        Mandatory school would punish parents for abuse or neglect that they haven't committed by making them attend classes.

      1.3.2.2. Con:
      Since mandatory parental classes do not relate to this issue, there is no reason why they should be accepted.

        1.3.2.2.1. Con:
        The child rearing information given to parents in the courses is likely to contain tips on spotting and preventing [43] abuse and neglect.

    1.3.3. Con:
    State inference in child rearing is accepted in terms of education, with most countries forcing kids to attend some kind of school for a certain number of years.

      1.3.3.1. Con:
      Many parents home school [44] their children in order to avoid the state's curriculum.

        1.3.3.1.1. Con:
        Most home schooled children are still required [45] to pass certain assessments administered by the state.

      1.3.3.2. Pro:
      If the government can force children to go to school, it can force parents to go to school too.

        1.3.3.2.1. Con:
        Children do not have all the same rights and responsibilities as adults do. Adults have much higher levels of individual freedom [46] in most societies.

      1.3.3.3. Pro:
      Compulsory education [47] for primary and secondary school is not seen as harmful by the majority of people, due to the societal benefits it brings.

        1.3.3.3.1. Pro:
        Most parents want their children to go to school for the benefits it will bring them. Parenting school is simply an extension of this.

    1.3.4. Pro:
    Mandatory school for parents would be impossible to enforce in a free society.

      1.3.4.1. Pro:
      There is no way to force prospective parents to attend school if they don't want to, short of incarceration.

        1.3.4.1.1. Pro:
        Fines and other punishments like taking away one's children would cause greater societal harms than any benefits provided by the parenting school.

      1.3.4.2. Con:
      If the benefits were clear, then enough parents would want to attend, making enforcement unnecessary.

        1.3.4.2.1. Pro:
        If there were just a few fringe groups that didn't want to attend the schools, they could be given exemptions or authorities could turn a blind eye.

    1.3.5. Pro:
    The right to life should not be conditioned by the state in any way.

      1.3.5.1. Con:
      Often the state actively incentivises life through child tax credits [48], which are socially desirable.

        1.3.5.1.1. Pro:
        These can support growing families, making it easier for people to express [49] their preferences for the good life. [50]

      1.3.5.2. Con:
      States cannot avoid conditioning the right to life; almost anything the state does has ramifications on fertility rates.

        1.3.5.2.1. Pro:
        Policies can often have unforeseen ripple effects on familial life.

          1.3.5.2.1.1. Pro:
          Child car seats may have negatively influenced [51] the number of children families have, reducing the number of three child families.

          1.3.5.2.1.2. Con:
          Piloting policies [52] can often be effective and mitigate the worst excesses of poor policy making.

      1.3.5.3. Pro:
      States should act to protect their citizens from harm and to preserve property rights. Any action beyond that is illegitimate [53].

        1.3.5.3.1. Con:
        This is just one of many competing viewpoints [54] about the role of the state. It does not imply an objective truth about reality.

      1.3.5.4. Pro:
      The decision to have children belongs to the parents; governments have no right [55] to interfere with that decision.

        1.3.5.4.1. Con:
        This is not a concept that is shared by all countries, as many countries have or had birth control policies [56] in place.

  1.4. Con:
  Many parents do not have the time or resources to attend extra schooling.

    1.4.1. Pro:
    Many working parents might find it difficult to take time off and attend these schools.

      1.4.1.1. Con:
      Employers would have to allow their employees to miss work without penalties to attend mandatory classes.

        1.4.1.1.1. Con:
        Employers would be against this, as many of them already penalize women [57] for taking time off to raise children.

      1.4.1.2. Con:
      If parents plan on having a child, they should be prepared to take time out of their schedules for it. Going to school to learn how to raise a child is part of this sacrifice.

        1.4.1.2.1. Con:
        Parents who have an unplanned pregnancy might not be in the position to make this sacrifice.

      1.4.1.3. Pro:
      Mothers struggle [58] to cope with work while raising their children as it is; adding essential obligations would make things worse.

        1.4.1.3.1. Con:
        If more fathers attend these classes, they could learn child rearing techniques that would reduce the mother's overall burden.

    1.4.2. Con:
    As a mandatory requirement, parenting schools would likely be free of charge.

      1.4.2.1. Con:
      This unfairly burdens taxpayers who don't have children.

        1.4.2.1.1. Pro:
        Mandatory parenting schools would likely face opposition from fiscal conservatives [59] who want to reduce the tax burden.

      1.4.2.2. Con:
      Other mandatory schools like drivers education [60] still require people to pay for them.

        1.4.2.2.1. Pro:
        Getting a drivers license can cost thousands of dollars [61] in some European countries.

      1.4.2.3. Pro:
      If the schools weren't free, few people would attend, rendering them useless.

        1.4.2.3.1. Con:
        If such classes were mandatory, parents could face legal punishments for not attending.

    1.4.3. Con:
    The time and resources saved in the long term by going to one of these schools will make the investment worth it.

      1.4.3.1. Pro:
      Parents will not waste money on needless things if the school teaches how to budget for a child.

        1.4.3.1.1. Pro:
        With so many superfluous children's products [62] on the market, it is easy to waste money without training.

      1.4.3.2. Con:
      If parents can't take time out of their schedule to attend the classes in the short term, then they won't be able to reap these benefits.

        1.4.3.2.1. Pro:
        May poor people work multiple jobs [63] and do not have the time to take off work for these kinds of classes.

    1.4.4. Pro:
    Putting this additional burden on prospective parents would disincentivise having children, when birth rates in many developed countries are already below replacement.

      1.4.4.1. Pro:
      Below replacement birth rates are likely to cause a number of social issues. [64]

        1.4.4.1.1. Con:
        A modestly low birth rate in developed states is shown to improve standards of living [65].

        1.4.4.1.2. Pro:
        Inverted age structures (where there are more elderly people than young people) can lead to constraints on public spending [66] due to a small working age population.

      1.4.4.2. Pro:
      The UN estimates that 50% of the global population [67] has below replacement fertility (BRF).

        1.4.4.2.1. Con:
        This could be remedied by allowing immigration [68] from countries with high birth rates to countries with low birth rates.

          1.4.4.2.1.1. Con:
          This would not be popular in many places, as citizens are often opposed [69] to large scale migration, especially in low birth rate countries.

      1.4.4.3. Con:
      Mandatory parenting schools could be structured in a way to incentivise parents, such as being a condition for extended paid parental leave.

        1.4.4.3.1. Con:
        Adequate paid parental leave [70] should be afforded to people in all countries, whether or not they attend a school.

      1.4.4.4. Con:
      It is unlikely that a few hours of parenting school would dissuade people from having children who might otherwise have had them.

        1.4.4.4.1. Pro:
        Many people don't weigh up this level of minutiae when considering [71] whether to have children.
