Discussion Name:
Was the EU response to the humanitarian situation following the earthquake in Turkey and Syria sufficient?

1. Thesis:
The EU's response to the humanitarian situation following the earthquake in Turkey and Syria is sufficient.

  1.1. Con:
  The response of the EU does not meet the needs of the affected nations.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    The aftermath of the earthquake has left Syria and Turkey in a severe crisis. [1]

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      The earthquakes have resulted in the death of more than 46,000 people [2] in Turkey.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      The impact of the earthquakes was not as severe in Syria.

        1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
        The earthquakes have resulted in the deaths of almost 5,000 [3] people in Syria, compared to the 45,000 in Turkey.

        1.1.1.2.2. Con:
        The ongoing civil war in Syria has heightened the vulnerability of the Syrian people.

          1.1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
          Over 4 million people [4] in Syria depend on international humanitarian aid.

        1.1.1.2.3. Con:
        In contrast to Turkey, Syria lacks [5] established structures for disaster management.

        1.1.1.2.4. Pro:
        The critical infrastructure in Turkey has been compromised across as many as 10 provinces [6].

      1.1.1.3. Pro:
      There have been more earthquakes and aftershocks [7] in the region following the initial earthquake on the 6th of February.

        1.1.1.3.1. Pro:
        More than 11,000 aftershocks [8] have occurred in Turkey.

      1.1.1.4. Pro:
      The earthquakes severely impacted the quality of life and commerce in Turkey and Syria.

        1.1.1.4.1. Pro:
        About a quarter [9] of citizens lost their jobs because of the earthquake.

        1.1.1.4.2. Pro:
        Three out of four locals [10] reported food insecurity and are at a risk of starvation and malnutrition.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    The contribution of the EU is insignificant compared to what has been estimated [11] to be needed.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      The EU has committed less than 100 million dollars, which is a small portion of the billions required. [12]

        1.1.2.1.1. Con:
        The EU made donations in phases, of which the 100 million was an initial donation. [13]

        1.1.2.1.2. Pro:
        The contribution of the EU as a percentage of the estimated targets is not proportional to its share of the global economy. [14] This means that it is unlikely the expected funds would be obtained without further commitments from the EU.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      The EU has been the largest provider [15] of international aid to Syria since the outbreak of the Syrian civil war in 2011.

      1.1.2.3. Con:
      The EU does not need to bear the full burden, as there are other countries outside the EU who are also making contributions.

        1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
        The United States has offered over $185 million [16] to assist Turkey and Syria.

        1.1.2.3.2. Pro:
        Canada has committed $50 million [17] to the recovery efforts in Syria and Turkey.

        1.1.2.3.3. Pro:
        Saudi Arabia has sent several rounds [18] of aid and relief items to Syria and Turkey.

        1.1.2.3.4. Pro:
        The UAE has committed 100 million [19] dollars in aid to the affected nations.

        1.1.2.3.5. Con:
        Most of the commitments are pledges [20], which have not been fully delivered.

      1.1.2.4. Pro:
      Various stakeholders have accused [21] the EU of doing too little despite the gravity of the situation.

    1.1.3. Con:
    The EU cannot meet all the needs of the affected nations alone.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      It is unfair to expect the EU to unilaterally deal with everyone else's national disasters.

      1.1.3.2. Pro:
      Sovereign states take responsibility to handle their issues and protect their people; Syria and Turkey need to handle this issue on their own.

      1.1.3.3. Con:
      The EU has not had to handle the needs of the affected nations alone; many other states [22] have assisted Syria and Turkey after the earthquake.

        1.1.3.3.1. Pro:
        Even countries that are economically struggling [23] themselves have offered help to Syria and Turkey.

        1.1.3.3.2. Pro:
        China has sent both aid and personnel [24] to help the affected nations.

        1.1.3.3.3. Con:
        Other states helping Syria and Turkey have done so because of positive relations; the same does not apply to Syria.

        1.1.3.3.4. Con:
        While many countries have supported, not all of them had the capacity to help as the EU does.

      1.1.3.4. Con:
      Humanitarian organizations have played a leading role in providing aid to Syria and Turkey.

        1.1.3.4.1. Pro:
        The Red Cross has provided surgical equipment [25] to nations affected by the earthquake.

        1.1.3.4.2. Con:
        Humanitarian organizations are meant to use money to help vulnerable populations. The EU cannot be held to the same standards as them.

    1.1.4. Con:
    It is often difficult to determine what is needed by the beneficiaries and from each benefactor in a crisis as complex [26] as the earthquake in Syria and Turkey.

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      The earthquake in Turkey and Syria is an evolving situation [27], with new challenges arising with aftershocks [28] and attempted responses.

      1.1.4.2. Con:
      While it may be difficult to determine exactly what is needed, most of the needs of the affected nations can be obtained with more funds.

      1.1.4.3. Con:
      Earthquakes are not a new phenomena, and as a result, it is not difficult to assess the relief needed for areas affected by earthquakes.

        1.1.4.3.1. Pro:
        Emergency medical services [29] for the injured are crucial for earthquake relief.

        1.1.4.3.2. Pro:
        Search and rescue teams [30] are needed to find people who may be trapped under rubble or in other dangerous situations.

        1.1.4.3.3. Pro:
        People affected by earthquakes may experience trauma, anxiety, and depression [31]. Psychological support is essential to address the mental health needs of affected individuals.

        1.1.4.3.4. Con:
        Different areas and populations may have different needs, depending on factors such as geography, demographics, and the severity of the earthquake.

        1.1.4.3.5. Con:
        Earthquake relief efforts may be hindered by logistical challenges, such as limited access to affected areas or disrupted supply chains.

        1.1.4.3.6. Con:
        The aftermath of an earthquake may be characterized by uncertainty, chaos, and rapidly changing conditions, which can make it difficult to plan and implement [32] effective relief efforts.

      1.1.4.4. Pro:
      Earthquakes have varying long-term impacts [33] across different regions which are challenging to predict.

        1.1.4.4.1. Pro:
        The experience of an earthquake can have long-term public health effects. [34]
