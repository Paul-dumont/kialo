Discussion Name:
Was Covid-19 more dangerous than regular flu viruses?

1. Thesis:

  1.1. Pro:

          1.1.1.1.1.2. Pro:
          The first vaccine shot [3] for Covid-19 was administered on 14 December 2020. By then, 1,685,371 [4] people had already died of Covid-19.

          1.1.1.1.1.3. Pro:
          Even after the vaccine was developed, countries faced significant logistical and organizational issues that impeded people's ability to access the vaccine. Slower vaccine rollout prolonged the pandemic [5] and thus lead to more deaths.

            1.1.1.1.1.3.1. Pro:
            The intricacies of transport and storage [6] complicated the rollout of the Covid-19 vaccine.

            1.1.1.1.1.3.2. Pro:
            Due to supply chain issues [7], Pfizer announced that it will only be able to rollout half its expected volume of the vaccine in 2021.

            1.1.1.1.1.3.3. Pro:
            Coordinating the Covid-19 vaccine distribution plan proved to be a challenge [8], particularly in countries that did not have a central healthcare registry.

      1.1.1.2. Pro:

    1.1.2. Pro:

      1.1.2.1. Con:

      1.1.2.2. Con:

        1.1.2.2.1. Pro:

        1.1.2.2.2. Con:
        100% protection is only guaranteed by the total eradication [16] of the virus, which is not the purpose of vaccines. Rather, the purpose is to manage the outbreak's uncontrolled spread [17] to managable levels.

        1.1.2.2.3. Pro:
        New variants of Covid-19 could be relatively more resistant to vaccines [18].

      1.1.2.3. Pro:
      This was evidenced by the rampant spread [19] of Covid-19 throughout the world.

      1.1.2.4. Pro:
      People were unwilling to follow guidelines that helped prevent the spread of Covid-19.

        1.1.2.4.1. Con:

        1.1.2.4.2. Pro:

          In January 2021, Covid-19 reinfections [329] caused milder infections and therefore were unlikely to add significantly to the death rate.

        1.7.7.2.3. Con:
        Inadequate testing suggests that the actual fatality rate was higher [330] than reported, not lower. This further entailed that it was deadlier than the flu.

      1.7.7.3. Con:
      Even without accurate rates, the possible range of the fatality rate [331] strongly indicated that Covid-19 was deadlier than the seasonal flu.

        1.7.7.3.1. Con:
        As flu viruses undergo mutations, it is difficult to make predictions about the rate of death. In Italy, the total number of flu-related deaths during the 2014-2015 season was 65.6% higher compared to the previous season. [332]

          1.7.7.3.1.1. Con:
          Mutations often lead to an increase [333] in the number of deaths. By that trend, mutations made Covid-19 even more dangerous than it already was.

    1.7.8. Pro:
    Among immunocompromised [334] patients, those with Covid-19 had a higher mortality compared to those with influenza.

      1.7.8.1. Con:
      The formerly developed Flu Pandemic Severity Index classifies the flu's "dangerousness" (severity) in 5 categories [335] ranging from a death rate lower than 0.1% to more than 2%. In that sense, Covid-19 was not more and not less dangerous, but fits the definition.

        1.7.8.1.1. Con:
        The Flu PSI is outdated and has been replaced by the Pandemic Severity Assessment Framework [336] (PSAF).

        1.7.8.1.2. Con:
        The only time that the flu met the threshold for Category 5 was during the 1918 influenza pandemic. [337] This is, thus, an exception rather than the rule.

    1.7.9. Con:
    -> See 1.4.5.2.9.2.

    1.7.10. Pro:
    Covid-19 outbreaks caused indirect deaths [338] by using up medical resources that would have been used to treat other fatal conditions.

      1.7.10.1. Pro:
      Covid-19 restrictions meant that many non-emergency procedures, including cancer screenings, were postponed [339], leading to excess deaths indirectly related to Covid-19.

        1.7.10.1.1. Pro:
        In 2020, experts predicted that 60,000 people [340] would be dead from cancer as a result of delayed treatment or diagnosis in the UK alone.
