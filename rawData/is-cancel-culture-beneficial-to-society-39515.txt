Discussion Name:
Is Cancel Culture Beneficial to Society?

1. Thesis:
Cancel culture [1] is beneficial to society.

  1.1. Con:
  Cancel culture is ineffective since it only focuses on individual actions and words rather than on addressing systemic issues [2].

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Cancel culture distracts us away from systems and institutions by focusing too much on individuals [3]. Real and sustainable change happens when power structures are challenged.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      Some anthropologists have claimed that acts of rebellion against people in power can reinforce power structures [4]. When individual leaders are replaced, social dissatisfaction is vented out in a way that keeps these structures intact.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      In a social media culture, the most followed individuals represent an important part in legitimating [5] social reality (as other power structures do) in our society. Focusing on those individuals is a way of challenging power structures.

        1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
        Canceling certain individuals with an important following is a way of canceling their "symbolic power [6]" (p.26) which can be understood as the capacity they have of shaping the perceptions of social reality.

          1.1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
          "Symbolic power [7]" can transform or conserve our social reality as we use it to shape our classifications, relations of power, group identities, etc. By targeting certain forms of "symbolic power," cancel culture is effectively transforming social reality.

          1.1.1.2.1.2. Con:
          Cancel culture is not only canceling people's symbolic power but also trying to conquer it [8].

        1.1.1.2.2. Con:
        Social media manipulates citizens [9] in different ways and it can narrow even more their understanding of social issues.

      1.1.1.3. Con:
      Canceling can have institutional consequences. A top editor at Refinery29 [10] quit due to issues concerning discrimination and racial diversity. In the aftermath, the organization changed its hiring practices and began working towards achieving a diverse workforce.

      1.1.1.4. Pro:
      Cancelling a person publicly results in a cathartic release [11]. This makes it seem like a significant change has been achieved even though the institutional issues remain untouched.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    Canceling someone has a limited impact on any structural change since it focuses on individual actions and words rather than addressing systemic issues.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      Canceling is effective in blocking discussion and thought leaders [12] for unpalatable issues but ineffective in solving root causes of the underlying behaviours many would like to see less of in our society.

        1.1.2.1.1. Con:
        Cancelling isn't always about a particular person. It still can shame or publicize a matter [13] enough for many others to conform to a particular moral standard.

          1.1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
          Cancelling J.K. Rowling might have been ineffective, but it managed to create an ecosystem where a lesser known author Gillian Philip lost her job [14] for tweeting support for J K Rowling.

          1.1.2.1.1.2. Pro:
          Shaming [15] can also be a force for social change because it encourages people to obey certain moral rules to avoid being shunned.

            1.1.2.1.1.2.1. Pro:
            Victims of sexual abuse and violence are often shamed and blamed. Cancel culture is a way to shift this burden [16] onto the offender.

            1.1.2.1.1.2.2. Con:
            Shaming or insulting someone for their views can result in them holding on to their views even more strongly [17].

          1.1.2.1.1.3. Con:
          While the intention behind canceling may be to promote a particular moral standard, it can result in a superficial conformity rather than genuine ethical growth.

          1.1.2.1.1.4. Con:
          Cancel culture can suppress diverse perspectives and discourage critical thinking.

        1.1.2.1.2. Pro:
        The problem might be the fact that there's indeed a culture of saying/doing morally hateful things [18], in which case we, as a society, should analyze why such a culture is still existing in order to eliminate it from its source.

        1.1.2.1.3. Pro:
        The Internet fosters a culture of hate speech [19].

          1.1.2.1.3.1. Pro:
          Because of the possibility of being anonymous, social media can become a platform for different types of hate speech [20].

            1.1.2.1.3.1.1. Con:
            Social media does not always allow for anonymity and impunity from hate speech.

              1.1.2.1.3.1.1.1. Con:
              Although social media companies are improving, they still often refuse or fail to regulate [21] hate speech or problematic speech.

              1.1.2.1.3.1.1.2. Pro:
              The right-wing commentator Katie Hopkins had her twitter account removed because it didn't follow Twitter's "hateful conduct [22]" policy.

            1.1.2.1.3.1.2. Pro:
            Social media can amplify hate speech as it facilitates "an extremely rapid bottom-up and transversal communication [23]" where messages can have an unlimited replication. (p.17)

            1.1.2.1.3.1.3. Con:
            Some social media influencers have been exploiting cancel culture by deliberately saying controversial things [24] to generate traffic and views.

              1.1.2.1.3.1.3.1. Pro:
              The popularity of controversy is fueling some Internet sleuths to go through the past of celebrities or family members of celebrities [25] just to dig up something that would justify a cancellation.

              1.1.2.1.3.1.3.2. Pro:
              This lessens the impact of cancellation and obfuscates discussions of real social justice issues.

              1.1.2.1.3.1.3.3. Con:
              Those actions are antithetical to the origins of cancel culture [26] and its intended use.

            1.1.2.1.3.1.4. Pro:
            Cancel culture is built on principles of collective action and community policing [27], which help to fight back against the anonymity of Internet forums of discourse.

          1.1.2.1.3.2. Con:
          The Internet is a neutral tool that can be used for both positive and negative purposes [28]. The responsibility for fostering hate speech lies with individuals who misuse the platform, rather than the technology itself.

        1.1.2.1.4. Pro:
        With the development of social media, people tend to rely more on celebrities and amateur experts [29] as a source of knowledge than on credited professionals, which can lead to oversimplified visions of the world.

        1.1.2.1.5. Con:
        Eliminating the source would entail eliminating the bases of our society (institutions) that are responsible for constructing and legitimizing our social reality [30]: such as schools, media and governments. Cancel culture is a more attainable way of forcing change.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      The cancelling of an individual typically casts blame on the individual [31], rather than looking at its broader structural causes. Cancel culture is therefore unproductive.

        1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
        The anonymity of social media [32] makes it easier for cancellers to distance themselves from the individual being cancelled, thus shutting down discourse about what may have contributed to their behavior.

        1.1.2.2.2. Con:
        This is not always the case. The cancelling of Bon Appetit editor-in-chief Adam Rappaport [33] prompted a broader discussion of racism in food media [34].

      1.1.2.3. Con:
      Humans have been saying and doing morally repugnant things throughout history; a behavior is never changed overnight, but through smaller challenges to the norm. Cancelling an individual is one action that helps shift away from problematic culture.

        1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
        Social media is considered to be the new agora [35] of the 21st century. Similar to the Greek agora were philosophers would discuss social life, social media uses rhetoric [36] against problematic cultures.

        1.1.2.3.2. Con:
        Those behaviors are hard to change because they are rooted in a historical fight for power [37]. Using power itself to change those dynamics is contradictory.

      1.1.2.4. Con:
      Cancel culture is an acknowledgment that we, individuals, don't have the power to change structural inequality [38], but we can take small steps towards it.

    1.1.3. Con:
    Cancel culture can be a catalyst for cultural change.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      By publicly condemning certain behaviors, society is forced to confront and address deep-rooted issues [39], leading to a more progressive and inclusive society.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      Cancel culture promotes a fear-based culture [40] where no real long-term change can take place.

    1.1.4. Con:
    Some of the canceled videos have become their own genre [41] and can be watched for leisure.

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      YouTube apology videos [42] featuring a YouTuber taking responsibility for one accusation are also becoming a distinct genre of their own.

      1.1.4.2. Con:
      Canceled videos have led to discussions about the broader societal issues at play, including freedom of speech, accountability, and the power dynamics between creators and their audiences which can lead to positive societal digital changes [43].

  1.2. Pro:
  Cancel culture supports social justice [44] and accountability.

    1.2.1. Con:
    Who gets canceled and to what extent is not purely determined on moral grounds.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      Social media algorithms [45] play a very important role in determining which Internet trends will get highlighted, which eventually will decide who gets canceled and who might get a second chance.

        1.2.1.1.1. Con:
        Even if an algorithm recommends posts about a specific topic, judgments about the content of that topic are still made by individuals [46].

        1.2.1.1.2. Pro:
        Social media algorithms are designed to prioritize content that generates high engagement and interaction [47]. As a result, certain trends and narratives gain more visibility and spread rapidly, reaching a larger audience.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      Cancel culture tends to be fueled by a moral outrage which aims at creating a deterrent effect [48] so individuals refrain from engaging in immoral or offensive behavior.

    1.2.2. Pro:
    Cancel culture holds individuals accountable for their harmful and offensive behavior.

      1.2.2.1. Pro:
      Cancelling an individual often occurs after they have transgressed repeatedly without consequences from other institutions.

        1.2.2.1.1. Con:
        Canceled individuals rarely have the ability to defend themselves, so judgment is delivered from a biased perspective.

        1.2.2.1.2. Pro:
        It sets an example that famous people won’t be let go only because they happen to have the money, power, or influence.

          1.2.2.1.2.1. Pro:
          These individuals often hold positions of power, so without the option of cancelling them it is likely they would be able to continue victimizing others.

          1.2.2.1.2.2. Pro:
          Bon Appetit editor-in-chief Adam Rappaport [49] resigned from the publication [50] after a photo resurfaced [51] showing him and his wife dressed up as Puerto Ricans for Halloween.

            1.2.2.1.2.2.1. Pro:
            In this case, cancelling can be a watershed moment [52] for victims of the cancelled individual, whose voices are elevated by the upswell of social media outcry.

          1.2.2.1.2.3. Con:
          In many cases, those famous people haven't lost their career because they have money, power or influence. [53]

      1.2.2.2. Con:
      Cancel culture is harmful because it offers no room for forgiveness [54], being able to learn from one's mistakes, and move on to become a better person.

        1.2.2.2.1. Pro:
        Cancel culture is un-Christian [55] because the Bible teaches Christians to be forgiving toward others, in the same way that God has forgiven them.

        1.2.2.2.2. Con:
        Forgiving implies a level of empathy that the person using hateful speech hasn't shown toward other people. It wouldn't, therefore, teach a lesson.

      1.2.2.3. Pro:
      Cancel culture fights against the impunity of anonymity [56] by holding individuals accountable.

        1.2.2.3.1. Pro:
        -> See 1.1.2.1.3.1.

        1.2.2.3.2. Con:
        Cancel culture uses anonymity to cancel. [57]

      1.2.2.4. Con:
      Cancel culture does not account for the moral development on individuals.

        1.2.2.4.1. Pro:
        Cancel culture allows for shaming or penalizing someone for views held in the past. Sometimes, these individuals have corrected or changed their beliefs.

          1.2.2.4.1.1. Pro:
          Some people who held racist beliefs in the past have disowned [58] them in later years. Shaming such people for their past beliefs is not fair because it does not reward positive moral transformation.

            1.2.2.4.1.1.1. Con:
            People are in control of what they post online and what they decide to keep online. Therefore, if racist beliefs are still publicly in display, it is fair to shame them.

            1.2.2.4.1.1.2. Pro:
            Cancel culture can also harm individuals who have associated with the individual without being aware of their past transgressions, such as the backlash against Majdi Wadi [59] for his daughter's racist tweets.

            1.2.2.4.1.1.3. Pro:
            Stories of moral transformation inspire change in others.They are also useful tools [60] in public education.

          1.2.2.4.1.2. Pro:
          Cancel culture is driven by a moral certainty that offers no hope for personal redemption or defense against claims.

            1.2.2.4.1.2.1. Pro:
            -> See 1.2.2.1.1.

            1.2.2.4.1.2.2. Pro:
            The nature of the Internet means that individuals will have their cancellation and transgression enshrined in Google searches [61] about them for years to come, preventing them from moving on from the incident.

            1.2.2.4.1.2.3. Con:
            Individuals can come back from a cancellation if they make sincere attempts to reform their behavior.

              1.2.2.4.1.2.3.1. Pro:
              Aziz Ansari's career bounced back [62] after his cancellation because he addressed the issue and apologized for it.

              1.2.2.4.1.2.3.2. Con:
              This ability to bounce back is constrained by privilege. Celebrities can hire crisis management firms [63] to help them develop a strategy for controlling the damage of a cancellation.

            1.2.2.4.1.2.4. Con:
            Cancellations can follow acts that are criminal, such as the rampant sexual abuse perpetrated by Harvey Weinstein [64]. These are prosecuted by a court of law, where the individual can present their defense.

            1.2.2.4.1.2.5. Pro:
            Moral certainty [65] can be justified when an individual's actions blatantly and repeatedly violate societal norms.

          1.2.2.4.1.3. Con:
          Cancel culture tends to be a direct reaction from a present action which might then put the spotlight on past problematic events.

        1.2.2.4.2. Con:
        Cancel culture emphasizes that moral development cannot occur in isolation from the impact an individual has on society.

          1.2.2.4.2.1. Pro:
          Cancel culture operates within the framework of collective morality [66], where the values of the community play a crucial role.

          1.2.2.4.2.2. Con:
          Cancel culture's public criticism can prompt individuals to examine their actions and beliefs, leading to personal and moral growth.

    1.2.3. Pro:
    Cancel culture creates a more democratic and inclusive society [67].

      1.2.3.1. Pro:
      Cancel culture tends to focus on issues of discrimination and racism by giving marginalized groups a voice and a chance to speak up.

        1.2.3.1.1. Pro:
        It is an important method of validating the difficulties facing minority communities, who are often the targets of bigoted or 'problematic' behaviour. [68]

          1.2.3.1.1.1. Pro:
          Cancel culture tries to ensure that safe spaces are created for groups which have were historically shut out from voicing their views. These spaces are considered safe because they do not allow for views and actions which cause harm or abuse towards these groups.

            1.2.3.1.1.1.1. Con:
            If groups that have been historically shut out from voicing their views are using similar tools of oppression to shut down other voices, cancel culture is more about retribution [69] than social justice.

            1.2.3.1.1.1.2. Pro:
            Power is everywhere [70] in our society, therefore the only way to create safe spaces is by using power to cancel any views or actions that might cause harm. (p.35)

          1.2.3.1.1.2. Con:
          Social media is an important tool for minority communities "to organize and to resist [71]" by creating more awareness and expanding the power of their messages. Cancel culture doesn't expand messages, it deletes them.

            1.2.3.1.1.2.1. Pro:
            The use of cancel culture to call-out and ultimately "delete" problematic behaviors can also be used to "exclude those championing equality and progressive change [72]".

              1.2.3.1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
              A detective, Florissa Fuentes [73], was fired from the force after posting a photo of her niece with protest signs hostile to the police.

              1.2.3.1.1.2.1.2. Pro:
              A museum exhibition in Cleveland showcasing the work of a Black artist was canceled without consulting him because protesters viewed images of police brutality as disturbing [74].

            1.2.3.1.1.2.2. Con:
            When the Dalai Lama was canceled for reiterating sexist comments he made in 2015 [75], his problematic comments stating that a female successor must be ‘attractive’ in 2019 went viral and forced him to apologize this time.

          1.2.3.1.1.3. Con:
          Based on the concept of Otherness [76], we tend to devalue the "others" in order to boost "us". Cancel culture can become an oppressive tool used by a specific group to devalue everything or anybody that doesn't embody their norms.

            1.2.3.1.1.3.1. Pro:
            Cancel culture has probably existed for as long as societies have been existing. Sexism and slavery are two clear examples of historical "cancelation".

            1.2.3.1.1.3.2. Con:
            Cancel culture, understood as social media call-outs is a new way of shifting social discourses as traditional "elites [77]" don't exclusively own social discourses anymore.

              1.2.3.1.1.3.2.1. Pro:
              Cancel culture is a result of the overall social mistrust in governments, politicians, media, etc; i.e. the "elites". Social media is the tool being used by citizens to question, push aside, and ignore [78] the traditional status quo.

              1.2.3.1.1.3.2.2. Con:
              Even if social media might allow for a shift in the ownership of social discourses, they are originated on the concept of "agenda setting" where "news messages cannot be read, apprehended, or studied in isolation of the context in which they are produced [79]".

                1.2.3.1.1.3.2.2.1. Pro:
                Social media is still greatly influenced by the agenda setting because journalists are still the "gatekeepers [80]".

                1.2.3.1.1.3.2.2.2. Con:
                Social media has also managed to create a "reverse agenda-setting [81]" through which the online community can impact the media agenda's hierarchy. Cancel culture is, in this case, a clear example of this.

            1.2.3.1.1.3.3. Con:
            Cancel culture is a way of reclaiming space by minority groups who are or have been "othered" [82] by majority groups.

          1.2.3.1.1.4. Pro:
          Cancel culture originated as a joke [83] on Black Twitter, and in its original form continues to be a term by and for BIPOC.

            1.2.3.1.1.4.1. Con:
            It has since been co-opted as a scare term by the conservative right [84], robbing it of any of its original humor or nuance.

            1.2.3.1.1.4.2. Pro:
            Cancel culture is built on important Black cultural touchstones such as the civil rights boycotts of the 1960s [85].

          1.2.3.1.1.5. Pro:
          Cancel culture [86] is a modern definition of a practice which has been occurring to the benefit of the masses for over a century [87].

        1.2.3.1.2. Pro:
        Since the #MeToo hashtag went viral in 2017, an increasing number of women who were victims of sexual harassment or abuse have raised their voices [88] on social media by using cancel culture.

        1.2.3.1.3. Pro:
        Cancel culture has been positively defined as a "way for marginalized communities to publicly assert their value systems" [89].

          1.2.3.1.3.1. Pro:
          Historically, marginalized people have used cancel culture to question authority figures by "mutilating public images of rulers [90]".

          1.2.3.1.3.2. Con:
          Although cancel culture has become a symbol of inclusiveness, on a societal level it cannot eradicate deep-rooted racist, sexist or excluding systems unless there is a "concerted assault on corporate power [91]".

        1.2.3.1.4. Con:
        Cancel culture is a product of consumer empowerment in modern capitalism, where the aim is instant gratification [92] rather than long-term social change.

        1.2.3.1.5. Con:
        Cancel culture can become a tool for "performative wokeness [93]" where people are more concerned about their social media likes than actual positive outcomes for marginalized groups.

          1.2.3.1.5.1. Pro:
          Performative activism can often impede social movements by taking away valuable visibility and air time, such as the #BlackOutTuesday debacle on Instagram [94].

            1.2.3.1.5.1.1. Pro:
            In online spaces, people often engage in moral grandstanding [95] to enhance one's status or image in the eyes of others.

            1.2.3.1.5.1.2. Con:
            The #BlackOutTuesday [96] wasn't cancelling anyone in particular.

          1.2.3.1.5.2. Pro:
          The concept of "being woke [97]" has been culturally appropriated by the larger culture. It would be contradictory to claim to be "woke" and to offer a space for marginalized groups to speak up at the same time.

          1.2.3.1.5.3. Con:
          Corporate activism [98] has been criticized for being performative. It can, however, raise awareness about social issues and positively engage with the policymaking process.

      1.2.3.2. Pro:
      Cancel culture, in the context of social media call-outs, represents the only direct space for citizens to hold public figures accountable [99].

        1.2.3.2.1. Pro:
        Cancel culture is especially effective against public figures who have previously not been held accountable by the law, such as R. Kelly [100].

        1.2.3.2.2. Pro:
        Cancel culture democratizes the ability to hold people in power accountable. It gives it to people who otherwise did not have the power to hold people in the elite accountable.

          1.2.3.2.2.1. Con:
          Journalists have an important role in society as "gatekeepers": they determine what becomes the public's social reality [101]. If that role is democratized, it can quickly become dangerous.

            1.2.3.2.2.1.1. Con:
            This role is already being usurped by social media influencers [102]. Information gatekeeping has little to do with the democratization of power prompted by cancel culture.

          1.2.3.2.2.2. Pro:
          Democratization of power was one of the founding principles of the Internet [103]. This is an extension of the broader democratization brought on by the advent of the Internet.

        1.2.3.2.3. Con:
        There are other possibilities for citizens to practice social justice: online platforms can create digital civic spaces where citizens both engage with, and respect digital deliberative practices [104].

      1.2.3.3. Con:
      Cancel culture fosters a sense of community through creating in-groups and out-groups [105], which ultimately builds prejudice.

      1.2.3.4. Pro:
      The discussions prompted by the prominent cancellation of a public figure can foster community and connection.

        1.2.3.4.1. Pro:
        Cancelling requires collective action [106], which can help community development around a social justice issue.

      1.2.3.5. Con:
      Cancel culture creates more divisiveness and polarization in society [107].

        1.2.3.5.1. Pro:
        The polarization around the term cancel culture [108] means that even invoking it can shut down any productive discussions that it could provoke.

          1.2.3.5.1.1. Pro:
          Cancel culture is a product of an increasingly polarized society [109].

          1.2.3.5.1.2. Pro:
          Rather than discussing the problematic behavior that got an individual cancelled, media discussions around the topic may choose to focus on cancel culture itself, thus reducing the impact of the cancellation.

          1.2.3.5.1.3. Con:
          Addressing this polarization and how it relates to the backlash against cancel culture is an equally important discussion.

        1.2.3.5.2. Pro:
        Cancel culture can increase political and social divisions if the cancelling is usually done by people leaning towards a particular political viewpoint.

          1.2.3.5.2.1. Pro:
          Some believe that canceling is effective only within a circle of left-leaning people. It is ineffective [110] against people outside this circle.

            1.2.3.5.2.1.1. Pro:
            The issues that result in cancellation are usually those that align with political beliefs held by the left. This gives the left a disproportionate share of cultural power [111] to shape public discourse.

          1.2.3.5.2.2. Con:
          Anti-racist, anti-homophobic, and inclusive dialogue shouldn't be considered a political viewpoint.

        1.2.3.5.3. Con:
        Cancel culture also create a sense of community and support.

    1.2.4. Con:
    Cancel culture in the context of social media doesn't support long-term accountability.

      1.2.4.1. Pro:
      Social media platforms are characterized by their fast-paced nature [112], where topics can trend for a short period before being replaced by the next viral sensation. This can lead to a culture of sensationalism, [113] with no long-term consequences.

      1.2.4.2. Con:
      Some of the canceled individuals did lose their career forever as a direct consequence of being cancelled [114].

    1.2.5. Con:
    Cancel culture often undermines the principles of due process [115] and fair judgment.

      1.2.5.1. Pro:
      Due to lack of due process, it is easy for cancellation to occur upon information that is incorrect or insufficient [116]. It increases the likelihood that someone is irreversibly penalized for wrong or insufficient reasons.

        1.2.5.1.1. Pro:
        In 2005 in South Korea, bloggers online leaked personal information about a woman who did not clean up after her dog defecated publicly. The public humiliation led her to drop out of college. Some believe this to be a case where she was penalized excessively [117] or unjustly.

          1.2.5.1.1.1. Con:
          South Korea has some kind of Internet censorship [118] and the way people interact or debate online is, therefore, more limited than in other countries and can't be used as a fair comparison.

            1.2.5.1.1.1.1. Pro:
            Using social media to name, shame and "publicize censorship as to embarrass those who perpetrate it [119]" could be a great tool to fight internet censorship. (p.94)

        1.2.5.1.2. Con:
        If cancel culture were to be "regulated" as in with strict due process, then it could quickly become an institutionalized weapon for censorship.

          1.2.5.1.2.1. Pro:
          There have been instances [120] where governments have wrongfully censored works of art and literature to avoid hurting sentiments of electorally significant groups.

          1.2.5.1.2.2. Con:
          If someone is penalized or censored after substantive due process [121] then it should be considered fair and legal.

        1.2.5.1.3. Con:
        Cancelling does not have any criminal consequences to a cancelled individual.

      1.2.5.2. Pro:
      Validation of a minority through cancellation without due process for the accused is a form of minority-based tyrann [122]y.

      1.2.5.3. Con:
      The principles of due process and fair judgment are not enough to stop systemic, racist, and problematic behavior in society [123].

    1.2.6. Pro:
    Cancel culture has been successful in driving corporate accountability. [124]

      1.2.6.1. Pro:
      Social media and cancel culture are positively changing social activism [125].

        1.2.6.1.1. Pro:
        Activism in the age of cancel culture [126] is leading to new forms of social participation.

        1.2.6.1.2. Con:
        The kind of activism of cancel culture gives many people an easy out from critically assessing their own behavior, such as white people shaming Amy Cooper and not investigating their own racism. [127]

          1.2.6.1.2.1. Pro:
          Resolving racial conflict requires educating people so that they are able to see their prejudices. Cancel culture shuts out people with such prejudices without engaging in meaningful conversations [128] with them.

          1.2.6.1.2.2. Con:
          Canceling problematic behavior teaches people to critically assess their own behavior at least on online platforms.

      1.2.6.2. Con:
      Cancel culture can create an environment of fear [129] where corporations may prioritize avoiding backlash over genuine change.

      1.2.6.3. Pro:
      According to a 2018 Edelman global study [130], “nearly two-thirds of consumers will buy or boycott a brand solely because of its position on a social or political issue."

  1.3. Pro:
  Cancel culture fosters awareness and promotes positive values [131].

    1.3.1. Pro:
    Having a culture where saying or doing morally hateful things [132] has important repercussions, rewards positive values [133].

      1.3.1.1. Con:
      -> See 1.1.2.1.2.

      1.3.1.2. Pro:
      White Americans were inspired [134] by the watchful nature of cancel culture to step up and participate in the Black Lives Matter uprising.

      1.3.1.3. Pro:
      In some places, the #MeToo movement has improved [135] the way men behave towards women.

        1.3.1.3.1. Pro:
        A study [136] regarding sexual harassment at workplace before and after the MeToo movement showed a decrease in sexual coercion at work.

        1.3.1.3.2. Con:
        The MeToo movement also triggered a negative backlash [137]. In some places, men became reluctant to engage with women in activities crucial for career advancement.

    1.3.2. Pro:

      -> See 1.1.2.1.1.2.

      1.4.2.2. Pro:
      Instead of publicly shaming someone, it is better to talk to them privately [266] about their mistakes and what they can do to correct them. This is less damaging and makes room for personal transformation.

        1.4.2.2.1. Pro:
        Talking privately instead of calling people out puts more focus on dialogue and change, rather than performance and moral grandstanding [267].

        1.4.2.2.2. Con:
        Private talks won't lead to any social change since change requires a push from a community [268].

      1.4.2.3. Pro:
      Shaming someone publicly can have harmful psychological effects [269].

        1.4.2.3.1. Pro:
        Because of the internet's longevity, the consequences of cancellation endure longer than intended [270]. This makes it difficult for people to escape the psychological trauma associated with shaming.

        1.4.2.3.2. Con:
        Shame can also bring about healthy psychological effects [271] by preventing us from doing something that jeopardizes our relationships.

          1.4.2.3.2.1. Con:
          Our own shame or the shame brought about by an individual we harm shouldn't be confused with the damaging effects of being shamed in front of a large audience.

          1.4.2.3.2.2. Pro:
          Shame can be used as a tool for change [272].

        1.4.2.3.3. Con:
        People with platforms who express hateful views can cause a great amount of psychological harm to entire groups of people [273]. In these cases, shame is a reasonable consequence for those actions.

        1.4.2.3.4. Pro:
        Teens who have been canceled have faced [274] online and in-person bullying, harassment, and loss of friendships.

          1.4.2.3.4.1. Con:
          Bullying is an epidemic problem [275] that goes beyond online cancelation.

          1.4.2.3.4.2. Pro:
          Cyberbullying [276] can be more damaging than traditional bullying.

            1.4.2.3.4.2.1. Pro:
            Online shaming can be more vicious [277] because one can not see the victim's suffering or gauge the impact of shaming on their well-being.

            1.4.2.3.4.2.2. Con:
            Cyberbullying doesn't try to create social change [278], instead, it just ridicules individuals.

      1.4.2.4. Con:
      Cancellation does not necessarily entail public shaming and ostracization. The demands that come under cancellation are usually limited to removing someone from a particular professional community, sphere or activity.

        1.4.2.4.1. Con:
        The public nature of cancellation means it always entails at least some form of public shaming.

        1.4.2.4.2. Pro:
        Many cancelled individuals are simply put on a career hiatus [279] before being able to rejoin the same professional community.

      1.4.2.5. Pro:
      Public shaming was used in the past to exert power and subjugation [280].

      1.4.2.6. Pro:
      Individuals who are not in the public sphere have been cancelled, leading to job loss, shaming, and loss of privacy [281].

      1.4.2.7. Con:
      Many targets of cancel culture are already public figures [282] who can expect to face public criticism as a result of voluntarily being in the public eye.

        1.4.2.7.1. Pro:
        Public figures should have a stronger ethical responsibility [283] as both their actions and messages can have more impact on society.

          1.4.2.7.1.1. Con:
          Public figures are often confused with celebrities. Celebrities become famous in public spaces because of their branding and marketing [284] so they shouldn't be viewed for their ethics even if they hold influence on social spaces.

            1.4.2.7.1.1.1. Pro:
            Celebrities can do more damage than good to social causes and social actions. When celebrities get involved in social actions, their presence can overshadow the cause itself [285].

              1.4.2.7.1.1.1.1. Con:
              When celebrities use their status and social power to endorse a more institutional approach [286] by supporting and funding activist groups they are stating their ethics and encouraging social change.

                1.4.2.7.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
                Leonardo Dicaprio as a celebrity and activist created a foundation [287] to support and fund projects that address environmental issues.

                1.4.2.7.1.1.1.1.2. Con:
                The best way for a celebrity to endorse an institutional approach is by funding it, therefore using their economic power, instead of their social power.

              1.4.2.7.1.1.1.2. Pro:
              According to the 'vampire effect [288]', there's a decrease in brand recall for an advertising stimulus that features a celebrity endorser; the same could be applied to celebrities and social causes.

            1.4.2.7.1.1.2. Con:
            Celebrities are often seen as role models [289] by people who consume their work. This places greater moral obligations on their behaviour.

              1.4.2.7.1.1.2.1. Pro:
              Celebrities can become Goodwill ambassadors [290] for the UN for their ethical commitment with certain causes and because they can make "direct representations to those with the power to effect change [291]".

          1.4.2.7.1.2. Pro:
          -> See 1.3.3.1.2.

        1.4.2.7.2. Con:
        The line between public and private in the digital era is very thin [292]. All social media users are somehow "public", therefore anyone could become a potential target.

    1.4.3. Con:
    Boycott is a strong tactic in civil rights movements [293] and cancel culture is just a new form of boycott.

      1.4.3.1. Pro:
      Boycotting people for their views is beneficial to society when those views produce objective harm.

      1.4.3.2. Con:
      Boycott would use tactics such as selective buying which wouldn't bully an individual but a business.

    1.4.4. Con:
    Cancel culture fights back against the dangers of online anonymity [294].

      1.4.4.1. Pro:
      The anonymous spread of false information [295] can have serious real-world consequences. Cancel culture can help counter the dissemination of misinformation by shedding light on individuals who contribute to the spread of false narratives.

      1.4.4.2. Con:
      Cancel culture doesn't typically target anonymous people, but instead, it tends to focus on public figures [296].

    1.4.5. Pro:
    'Cancelling' an individual is broadly ineffective since it only creates a mob mentality [297].

      1.4.5.1. Pro:
      Cancel culture provokes a dangerous type of mob mentality [298].

        1.4.5.1.1. Con:
        Other forms of internet discourse, such as trolling [299] or doxxing [300] do more to promote the mob mentality and are more dangerous.

          1.4.5.1.1.1. Con:
          Trolling and doxxing sometimes follow cancelling a person [301].

          1.4.5.1.1.2. Pro:
          Trolling is one example of digital mob mentality [302].

        1.4.5.1.2. Pro:
        Using influence, rather than persuasion, to establish ideas creates a dangerous precedent where people who do not conform to ideas from groups which have the power of influence are cancelled.

          1.4.5.1.2.1. Con:
          Who is persuasive is constrained by social and economic bias as well. For example, rationality is a highly gendered standard [303].

          1.4.5.1.2.2. Pro:
          When swayed by influence, rather than persuasion, individuals are less likely to critically assess issues. [304]

        1.4.5.1.3. Pro:
        Cancellations are carried out in support of norms which have support from significant numbers of people or have a prominent voice on the internet. These norms do not necessarily promote the good of society.

          1.4.5.1.3.1. Pro:
          The norms upon which people are usually cancelled seem to be overwhelmingly those that are held by the Left [305].

            1.4.5.1.3.1.1. Con:
            Conservatives also use cancel culture. Trump's government tried to "intimidate critical media organizations [306]" or even purging "the executive branch of anyone who disagrees with him".

          1.4.5.1.3.2. Pro:
          People are often cancelled when they express a "politically incorrect" view. However, political correctness [307] is contingent on time and context.

          1.4.5.1.3.3. Con:
          These norms support basic human rights stated by the United Nations. [308]

      1.4.5.2. Pro:
      The phenomenon called "quarantine canceling [309]" might help users feel validated and uplifted during hard times, but it does so by damaging individuals.

      1.4.5.3. Con:
      Cancelling a public individual can be highly effective given the how money flows to individuals in terms of sponsorship deals or speaking opportunities.

        1.4.5.3.1. Pro:
        We are currently living in the so-called "attention economy [310]". Therefore, by depriving public figures or businesses of attention, we can effectively "deprive them of their livelihood" [311].

          1.4.5.3.1.1. Con:
          Cancel culture is a direct product of the "attention economy", its main effect is to drive attention as it doesn't drive any real change [312] or offline consequences.

          1.4.5.3.1.2. Pro:
          Cancel culture is a form of "cultural boycott [313]" using online platforms to stop supporting certain public figures.

        1.4.5.3.2. Con:
        Canceling a public individual can also create a spotlight for them, which could lead to free publicity.

      1.4.5.4. Con:
      Canceling some individuals has been highly effective in ending problematic behavior and creating a collective critical thinking ability to improve how a society acts.

        1.4.5.4.1. Pro:
        Cancelling can give societal momentum for prosecutorial actions, such as with Bill Cosby [314].

          1.4.5.4.1.1. Con:
          Sensationalizing an issue through media trial or public campaigning creates pressure on judges [315] to decide in favour of the public mood without adhering to principles of fairness and justice.

          1.4.5.4.1.2. Pro:
          During a standup comedy routine, Hannibal Burress reminded his audience that Bill Cosby had been accused of rape. The clip went viral, Cosby’s new television show, was canceled and Cosby was eventually convicted [316]. (p.81)

        1.4.5.4.2. Pro:
        -> See 1.4.1.3.6.

        1.4.5.4.3. Con:
        Cancel culture hasn't been highly effective since many celebrities have continued with their careers and only had to deal with minor setbacks. [317]
