Discussion Name:
Should people marry outside their religious faith?

1. Thesis:
People should marry outside their religious faith.

  1.1. Con:
  Marrying within one's own religious community leads to a happier marriage.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Interfaith marriages are less stable than their intrafaith counterparts.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      A study found that religious homogamy was positively linked to stability within a marriage (p. 733 [1]).

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      Only 21% [2] of those raised in interfaith families reported disagreement about religion between their parents.

      1.1.1.3. Pro:
      The divorce rate is higher [3] in interfaith marriages.

        1.1.1.3.1. Pro:
        A study [4] in 1993 showed that after five years of marriage, Mormons in intrafaith marriages had a divorce rate of 13%, compared to 40% for those in interfaith marriages.

        1.1.1.3.2. Con:
        Recent studies show that interfaith marriages have a higher level of dyadic consensus than those claimed by studies in the past (p. 49 [5]).

        1.1.1.3.3. Pro:
        Over 40% [6] of marriages between a Jew and a Christian end in a divorce within five years.

        1.1.1.3.4. Pro:
        It was found [7] that roughly a third of all evangelicals’ marriages end in divorce, but that the figure climbs to nearly half for marriages between evangelicals and non-evangelicals. It is especially high (61 percent) for evangelicals married to someone with no religion.

        1.1.1.3.5. Pro:
        Researchers cite that interfaith marriages have a failure rate that is 50% higher [8] than intrafaith marriages.

        1.1.1.3.6. Con:
        Results [9] from the American Religious Identification Survey shows that theological beliefs and the belief dissimilarity of spouses have little effect on the likelihood of dissolution (of marriage) over time.

          1.1.1.3.6.1. Con:
          Divorce rates are lowest [10] (21%) amongst those of no or undecided religion, suggesting that religion may play a part in the break down of relationships.

      1.1.1.4. Pro:
      Certain religions have traditional rules seeking to ensure the prevalence [11] of their own faith within the relationship. This makes it harder for interfaith couples to reach a compromise.

        1.1.1.4.1. Pro:
        A provision [12] of the 1983 Code of Canon Law [13] requires the Catholic party to be faithful to their faith and to “make an absolute and sincere promise to do all in his or her power” to have their children baptized and raised as Catholics.

        1.1.1.4.2. Pro:
        Although interfaith marriages are permitted in Islam, Sharia law insists that Islam must take precedence [14], especially where any children of the couple are concerned.

          1.1.1.4.2.1. Con:
          This is evidence that Sharia law, rather than interfaith marriage, is problematic.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    Research shows that couples have increased marital satisfaction when they engage in joint religious activities that hold symbolic meaning (p. 91 [15]).

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      In order to get along, many members of interfaith couples simply stop practicing [16] the specifics of their religion. This can lead to resentment towards their partners.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      Even partners who belong to different religious faiths pray, sing songs, meditate, read, and reflect on sacred texts together [17] to function as a family unit.

    1.1.3. Con:
    Research suggests that heterogeneous marriages have resulted in increased marital happiness and a decreased divorce rate (p. 94 [18]).

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      Around half of people in mixed faith marriages express negative emotions [19] about their religious differences.

        1.1.3.1.1. Pro:
        Partners often cite feeling like outsiders in the family due to religious differences, which decreases their sense of well-being (p. 96 [20]).

    1.1.4. Con:
    A study found that interfaith couples communicate more effectively (p. 47 [21]).

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      Unlike other interests about which couples negotiate and compromise, religious and cultural issues are highly charged and sensitive topics. Many interfaith [22] couples decide not to decide and then avoid discussions of religion.

      1.1.4.2. Pro:
      Communication has been shown to be the most important predictor [23] of marital satisfaction for both interfaith and intrafaith couples.

      1.1.4.3. Pro:
      Interfaith couples recognise [24] from the start that they will have to negotiate their religious differences, so they quickly learn how to carry this skill into other aspects of the relationship. This ensures that their relationship overall is healthier than their intrafaith counterparts.

    1.1.5. Con:
    Interfaith couples can often feel more connected to their individual faiths (p. 10 [25]).

      1.1.5.1. Con:
      Studies show [26] that around a quarter of intrafaith marriages in the US began as interfaith marriages where one partner was able to convert the other to the same religion as theirs.

      1.1.5.2. Pro:
      Individuals in interfaith marriages need to explore [27] their religion and question their faith in order to explain it to their significant other, which can deepen their understanding of their own faith (p. 10 [28]).

      1.1.5.3. Pro:
      A study found that interfaith couples are more open and diverse with regard to their individual faiths because they are able to observe and appreciate their spouse's religion (p. 1 [29]).

    1.1.6. Pro:
    Parents are less likely to come into conflict over the question of which faith to raise their children in.

      1.1.6.1. Con:
      Children should be allowed to choose their own religion [30] and identity. As such, parents should not come into conflict over their children's faith as it's not their decision to make.

        1.1.6.1.1. Pro:
        Children have a right to freedom of choice and freedom of religion [31]. They should be free to pursue their religious truth on their own, unencumbered by their parents.

          1.1.6.1.1.1. Con:
          All reason is tradition-based [32]. It stems from a system of practices and beliefs that make reasoning possible. It is only after experiencing religious tradition that children are able to reason with it.

          1.1.6.1.1.2. Con:
          Like with other important choices [33], such as healthcare or education - as children are still developing and need guidance - the ultimate decision on what religion a child takes should rest with what the parent or guardian deems to be in the child's best interests.

          1.1.6.1.1.3. Pro:
          The United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child recognises a child's right [34] to independent thought and freedom of religion.

        1.1.6.1.2. Con:
        Letting children choose their own religion implicitly makes the decision for them [35]. It teaches them that their thinking comes before God rather than being God-given.

        1.1.6.1.3. Pro:
        It is considered brainwashing [36] to impose religion on a child.

          1.1.6.1.3.1. Con:
          There are many flaws [37] with the idea that it is considered brainwashing to impose religion on a child.

        1.1.6.1.4. Con:
        Teaching children religion is a prism through which to teach them morality. Teaching children right from wrong is a good thing.

          1.1.6.1.4.1. Con:
          Morality precedes religion [38]. Teaching morality through religion confuses and simplifies our in-built moral compasses.

          1.1.6.1.4.2. Pro:
          Undesirable behavior in primary school children can be changed by appealing to models of behavior present in the bible (p. 522 [39]).

          1.1.6.1.4.3. Pro:
          Moral principles become stronger when supported by appropriate religious principles (p. 522 [40]).

          1.1.6.1.4.4. Con:
          Religion is not necessary [41] to teach morality and does not appear to have any effects [42] on a person's overall morality.

            1.1.6.1.4.4.1. Con:
            Other research has suggested that atheists [43] are believed to be less moral.

        1.1.6.1.5. Con:
        Religious education is responsible for the formation of intellectual, aesthetic, physical and cultural education (p. 522 [44]). These are the domain and responsibility of parents.

          1.1.6.1.5.1. Con:
          Those who identify as atheist or agnostic tend to be more intelligent [45] than those who have religious leanings.

            1.1.6.1.5.1.1. Pro:
            The University of Texas' research also found that students' performance on reading, math, and science tests [46] were negatively associated with several forms of parental religiosity.

            1.1.6.1.5.1.2. Con:
            It is unclear whether the correlation between nonreligiousness and intelligence is directly causative [47].

              1.1.6.1.5.1.2.1. Pro:
              The biggest divide is at university. It may be that the university environment makes people less religious [48], rather than university students being less religious in the first place.

                1.1.6.1.5.1.2.1.1. Pro:
                Religion is often considered an instinct. If this is the case, it is likely that the correlation comes from the fact that intelligence [49] (and its cultivation) gives one the ability to rise up against, and to question, one's own instinct.

              1.1.6.1.5.1.2.2. Pro:
              If atheism is disapproved of at home, higher intelligence may facilitate resistance to conformity pressure [50].

      1.1.6.2. Pro:
      -> See 1.1.1.2.

      1.1.6.3. Pro:
      Research [51] found that half of interfaith couples did not discuss the religion of any eventual children before marrying.

        1.1.6.3.1. Con:
        The absence of discussion prior to having children would not preclude the possibility of conflict over which faith to raise them in after their eventual birth.

      1.1.6.4. Pro:
      Couples who have discussed the religion of their future children may change their mind [52] once their child is born.

        1.1.6.4.1. Con:
        A Pew research [53] survey reports that three-quarters of surveyed interfaith children said that their parents rarely disagreed about religion.

      1.1.6.5. Pro:
      Research has shown that families and friends are likely to display negative reactions to decisions regarding the children of interfaith marriages (p. 98 [54]).

      1.1.6.6. Con:
      Even in cases [55] where both partners belong to the same faith, there are differences in culture or mentality, especially when it is a matter of bringing up children, which create conflict.

        1.1.6.6.1. Pro:
        In 2013, American Catholics who married Protestants had a divorce rate of 49% [56], despite both being denominations of Christianity.

          1.1.6.6.1.1. Con:
          Denominations of the same faith can differ significantly from one another. They should not be considered the archetype for intrafaith marriages.

            1.1.6.6.1.1.1. Pro:
            Different denominations of a faith often have histories of violence.

              1.1.6.6.1.1.1.1. Pro:
              Christian history is littered with examples of sectarian violence [57] on both small and large scales.

              1.1.6.6.1.1.1.2. Pro:
              Historically, there have been many conflicts and arguments between Sunni and Shia [58] Muslims.

                1.1.6.6.1.1.1.2.1. Con:
                The conflict [59] between Sunni and Shia Muslims has economic dimensions and is not purely about religious preferences.

                1.1.6.6.1.1.1.2.2. Pro:
                It is estimated that nearly 4000 Shia Muslims [60] have been killed in incidents of sectarian violence in Pakistan since the 1990s.

                1.1.6.6.1.1.1.2.3. Pro:
                Based on statistics [61] from 2012, an estimate amount of 500 to 800 Shias are killed every year, with around 700 to 1000 injured, in Pakistan.

                1.1.6.6.1.1.1.2.4. Pro:
                In 2005 [62], nearly 155 people died per week in Iraq due to sectarian violence between Sunnis and Shias.

              1.1.6.6.1.1.1.3. Con:
              If the histories of violence are distant in time, then those differences should be ignored when distinguishing interfaith from intrafaith marriages. They are unlikely to have any real impact on the couple's relationship.

            1.1.6.6.1.1.2. Pro:
            Even though Protestants and Catholics are both denominations of Christianity, they are very different [63] from one another.

              1.1.6.6.1.1.2.1. Pro:
              Protestants believe [64] that salvation is by faith alone, through grace alone, in Christ alone. They view justification [65] as the specific point upon which God declares that a person is righteous. In contrast, the Roman Catholic Church views justification as a process, dependent on the grace one receives by participating in the Church.

  1.2. Pro:
  Interfaith marriages promote diversity, which is an absolute good.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Diverse communities [66] promote less prejudice and intolerance.

      1.2.1.1. Con:
      Social cohesion and tolerance as a consequence of diversity are reduced when in deprived or disadvantaged [67] communities.

        1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
        Diversity can hurt [68] civic life and result in distrust within a community.

          1.2.1.1.1.1. Pro:
          The greater the diversity in a community, the fewer [69] people vote and the less they volunteer.

      1.2.1.2. Pro:
      A study concluded that it is likely that the improvement in societal attitudes in Canada is linked with the trend of increasing interfaith marriages throughout the country (p. 250 [70]).

      1.2.1.3. Pro:
      Promoting diversity is now more important than ever as the world becomes increasingly intolerant.

        1.2.1.3.1. Con:
        Tolerance is increasing [71] with each generation in the USA.

          1.2.1.3.1.1. Pro:
          A study [72] published in Social Forces indicates that Americans are becoming increasingly tolerant of people with views or lifestyles that differ from their own, even when those views are highly controversial.

          1.2.1.3.1.2. Pro:
          As of May 2015, 27% of millennials in the US [73] said that they were raised in mixed religion households, compared to 20% of generation X, 19% of baby boomers, and only 13% of those born pre-1946. This suggests that interfaith marriages are increasing at a rate in line with overall religious tolerance.

            1.2.1.3.1.2.1. Con:
            A 7% increase to a total of 27% is not evidence that there is no need to promote tolerance, but rather evidence to suggest that now is the best time to support the trend.

          1.2.1.3.1.3. Con:
          Crimes against Jewish Americans [74] increased by over 37% in 2016.

          1.2.1.3.1.4. Con:
          The Chicago office of the Council on American-Islamic Relations [75] reported a 50% increase in incidents of discrimination between 2016 and 17.

        1.2.1.3.2. Pro:
        Hate crimes are increasing around the world [76].

          1.2.1.3.2.1. Pro:
          Data shows [77] that the number of incidents of interfaith violence in India in 2017 was higher than that of previous years.

          1.2.1.3.2.2. Pro:
          Researchers [78] say that the US and UK are among countries that show a worrying rise in religious discrimination and hate crimes as a result of the same.

            1.2.1.3.2.2.1. Pro:
            An independent monitoring group in the UK has reported [79] that the number of anti-Muslim hate crimes has increased by 593% in the week after the Christchurch mosque shootings [80].

            1.2.1.3.2.2.2. Pro:
            Since the Brexit referendum in 2016, the UK has suffered its highest annual increase [81] in faith-based hate crimes.

          1.2.1.3.2.3. Pro:
          In Canada [82], criminal incidents motivated by hate and reported to the police rose by more than 60% between 2014 and 2017.

            1.2.1.3.2.3.1. Con:
            This trend has not continued past 2017. Between 2017 and 2018, hate crime rates reduced [83] from 2,073 to 1,798.

          1.2.1.3.2.4. Pro:
          Actual hate crime figures are expected to be much higher than reported figures because many go unreported [84].

          1.2.1.3.2.5. Con:
          Reported increases in hate crimes around the US coincide with more awareness of what hate crimes are [85]. It may be that people are more likely to identify and report hate crimes now, rather than an independent increase in those hate crimes.

            1.2.1.3.2.5.1. Con:
            An increase in hate crimes around the US is likely to be much higher than those actually reported. The same research highlights that 2,700 police and sheriff's departments [86] have not filed a single hate crime report.

          1.2.1.3.2.6. Pro:
          Hate crimes are increasing in the US.

            1.2.1.3.2.6.1. Con:
            In the past two years, anti-Muslim hate crimes in the US have decreased [87].

              1.2.1.3.2.6.1.1. Con:
              The Council on American-Islamic Relations [88] disagrees with these FBI statistics, and reports [89] that anti-Muslim hate crimes and anti-Muslim bias incidents have increased in the US since 2017.

            1.2.1.3.2.6.2. Pro:
            According to the Center for the Study of Hate and Extremism (CSHE) [90], hate crimes in the US have risen by 9% in 2019. This rise is the fifth consecutive increase and the steepest rise since 2015.

            1.2.1.3.2.6.3. Pro:
            Including estimated unreported hate crimes, there are an average of 250,000 [91] hate crime incidents in the US every year.

            1.2.1.3.2.6.4. Pro:
            Anti-Asian hate crimes have become more common on account of racism related to COVID-19, prompting the UN Secretary-General [92] to note his concern.

        1.2.1.3.3. Pro:
        A study [93] by the Pew Research Centre showed an increase in religious intolerance over the past several years.

        1.2.1.3.4. Pro:
        Research [94] by the United Nations on an assessment of the Sustainable Development Goals showed that discrimination and intolerance have increased over the years (p. 32 [95]).

      1.2.1.4. Con:
      Research shows that diversity is not necessarily correlated with tolerance.

        1.2.1.4.1. Pro:
        A study [96] concluded that diversity is not meaningfully linked to a tolerant citizenry.

        1.2.1.4.2. Pro:
        Research into Nigerian interfaith marriages found that prejudices relating to sex, race, religion, or caste are common (p. 503 [97]).

        1.2.1.4.3. Con:
        A study [98] found that multicultural personality dimensions such as cultural empathy, open mindedness, and flexibility, were heavily correlated with tolerance.

    1.2.2. Con:
    Interfaith marriages are simply a manifestation of a more progressive and accepting society. They do not do much to promote diversity on their own.

      1.2.2.1. Pro:
      According to a study [99], urbanization and changes in social norms about marriage are what drive the increase in the share of interfaith and interethnic unions (p. 2 [100]).

      1.2.2.2. Con:
      The rising number of interfaith marriages in the UK, has, in turn, spiked more awareness of and interest in [101] interfaith marriage. Therefore, even if these marriages are merely reflective of society, they act as a source of inspiration for more people to learn about progressive ideals.

    1.2.3. Con:
    Diversity isn't an absolute good.

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      Diversity across society has been shown to be beneficial. Diverse companies [102] perform better than non-diverse ones.

    1.2.4. Pro:
    The diversity produced by interfaith marriages is likely to reduce religious dogmatism.

      1.2.4.1. Pro:
      A survey [103] concluded that individuals that married people of different faiths had a more positive impression of those faiths compared to those who did not.

      1.2.4.2. Con:
      Historically, people in interfaith relationships with Jewish partners were often heavily encouraged to convert [104] to Judaism in order for their marriage to take place. Others may be urged to convert by their partner or partner's family at a later date if conversion did not take place beforehand.

        1.2.4.2.1. Con:
        This practice is becoming more scarce with modern generations, and conversion isn't always [105] a prerequisite in interfaith marriages today.

        1.2.4.2.2. Pro:
        Honeymoon Israel [106] is a Jewish funded organization that offers interfaith partners subsidized 10-day trips to Israel. The non-Jewish spouse is encouraged [107] to explore Jewish culture and religion for potential conversion to Judaism.

        1.2.4.2.3. Pro:
        Temple Adat Shalom [108] in California runs numerous interfaith and conversion programs [109] to offer an open and supportive environment that helps the non-Jewish partner develop a comfortable relationship with Judaism.

      1.2.4.3. Pro:
      People in interfaith marriages are generally less religious [110] than those with spouses of the same religion.

      1.2.4.4. Pro:
      Interfaith couples are likely [111] to be interested in knowing and learning more about their partners' faith.

    1.2.5. Pro:
    Interfaith marriages can foster tolerance and understanding within the families of the couples (p. 40 [112]).

      1.2.5.1. Pro:
      An interfaith couple reported [113] that both their families accepted and learnt from, and about, the other's cultures and beliefs.

      1.2.5.2. Pro:
      Many interfaith couples find creative ways [114] of negotiating religious holidays to bring their families closer together.

      1.2.5.3. Con:
      In reality, interfaith marriages are more likely to upset [115] the parents of the partners, leading to prejudice and possible estrangement.

        1.2.5.3.1. Pro:
        In India, interfaith couples are facing a growing societal divide [116], which could lead to families being angry at their children for marrying outside of their religion.

          1.2.5.3.1.1. Pro:
          Children can have a strained relationship with their grandparents as a result of their parents' interfaith marriage (p. 3 [117]).

  1.3. Pro:
  The children of interfaith marriages are more likely to be well rounded and happy people.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    Interfaith families are more likely to put emphasis on values [118] - such as respect, loyalty, honest, and integrity - they feel are important, than on religious beliefs.

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      80% [119] of those who are in an interfaith marriage believe that having similar values is more important than having the same religion.

    1.3.2. Con:
    The children of interfaith marriages have to deal with several psychological issues.

      1.3.2.1. Pro:
      Interfaith couples can cause a significant amount of stress for their children as they may feel that they are forced to choose between the religion of either of their parents (p. 279 [120]).

      1.3.2.2. Pro:
      Children of interfaith marriages may not develop a clear religious or cultural identity, which could lead to identity problems [121] for them in their future.

        1.3.2.2.1. Con:
        Children are more likely to choose to follow the religion of their mother [122] than that of their father.

          1.3.2.2.1.1. Pro:
          One-third of respondents in a survey [123] of roughly 5,000 Americans said that their mothers were more responsible for their religious experiences than their fathers were.

          1.3.2.2.1.2. Pro:
          According to Pew [124], nearly half of people who grow up in interfaith households practice their mother’s faith, compared to less than a third who practice their father’s.

          1.3.2.2.1.3. Pro:
          Women [125] often volunteer at churches, synagogues, mosques, and other houses of worship and keep them functional. They often take their children along, thereby exposing them to their religious beliefs.

          1.3.2.2.1.4. Pro:
          Children usually follow the religion of their primary caregiver [126]. While this can be their father, in the majority of cases it is the mother (p. 9 [127]).

          1.3.2.2.1.5. Con:
          However, children are more likely to go to church [128] if their father goes, as opposed to their mother, suggesting that religious learning for children is more complicated than the suggestion in the parent.

        1.3.2.2.2. Pro:
        Difficulties with the formation of a religious identity may cause children of interfaith marriages to be pushed away from religion in general.

          1.3.2.2.2.1. Pro:
          Being part of a religion and strong religious community as a child has a number of benefits. Therefore, losing out on religious identity by virtue of being born to an interfaith marriage comes with the loss of these benefits.

            1.3.2.2.2.1.1. Pro:
            Research [129] has shown religion to be associated with enhanced psychological adjustment and social competence among primary school-age children.

              1.3.2.2.2.1.1.1. Pro:
              Research shows that children with stronger ties to religion [130] have better self-control, social skills, and approaches to learning than other children.

            1.3.2.2.2.1.2. Pro:
            Research shows that children who are more religious have better mental health than those who are less religious (p. 2 [131]). On average, then, this would imply that being born to an interfaith family is better for a child's mental health.

              1.3.2.2.2.1.2.1. Pro:
              Personal religiousness is associated with lower rates of suicide [132].

                1.3.2.2.2.1.2.1.1. Con:
                A study of 321 patients with major depression or bipolar disorder found that past suicide attempts and suicidal ideation were higher [133] among those with religious affiliations.

                1.3.2.2.2.1.2.1.2. Pro:
                Religiousness can serve as a protective factor [134] against non-fatal suicidal behavior in some cultures.

                  1.3.2.2.2.1.2.1.2.1. Con:
                  The protective factor of belief on mental health is overrated [135], accounting for only 1-2% of the variance in mental health.

            1.3.2.2.2.1.3. Con:
            Studies show [136] that children who are more religious tend to be less kind and more judgmental than those who are less religious.

              1.3.2.2.2.1.3.1. Pro:
              Children from religious homes are more likely to be judgemental [137] of others' behaviours than their non-religious counterparts.

              1.3.2.2.2.1.3.2. Con:
              These studies [138] do not take into account their own limitations, whose results are actually influenced by a number of external factors.

                1.3.2.2.2.1.3.2.1. Pro:
                These studies have extremely small sample sizes [139] that can not be said to be representative.

                1.3.2.2.2.1.3.2.2. Pro:
                This study gives absolutely no consideration [140] to any evolutionary influences (nature), and instead relies entirely on cultural influences (nurture), even though psychologists have emphasized [141] on the importance of both in recent years.

                1.3.2.2.2.1.3.2.3. Pro:
                These studies group religious and non-religious individuals based on how often they attend church [142], which is not necessarily representative [143] of their beliefs.

          1.3.2.2.2.2. Pro:
          A 2006 survey [144] showed that 37% of those raised by parents of different religions reported weekly attendance at religious services, compared with 42% of those raised by parents with the same faith.

          1.3.2.2.2.3. Con:
          Moving away from religion is not necessarily a bad thing. A nonreligious [145] identity is just as valid as a religious one.

            1.3.2.2.2.3.1. Con:
            According to a study [146], non-religious individuals tend to have worse positive psychological functioning characteristics, social support relationships, and health behaviors.

              1.3.2.2.2.3.1.1. Pro:
              Personal religiousness is associated with less drug, alcohol, and tobacco use [147].

              1.3.2.2.2.3.1.2. Pro:
              -> See 1.3.2.2.2.1.2.1.

              1.3.2.2.2.3.1.3. Pro:
              Religious attendance [148] is associated with less suicide attempts in both the general population and those with mental illnesses.

                1.3.2.2.2.3.1.3.1. Con:
                -> See 1.3.2.2.2.1.2.1.1.

              1.3.2.2.2.3.1.4. Con:
              A person's available social support is by far the strongest predictor of mental health. This can be achieved through nonreligious community participation [149] just as well as through religious community participation.

            1.3.2.2.2.3.2. Pro:
            An increasing number of people throughout the world identify as nonreligious (p. 467 [150]). Therefore, the loss of religious identity from an interfaith marriage is not abnormal and will not inflict unique suffering on a child.

              1.3.2.2.2.3.2.1. Pro:
              Only 7% [151] of the 1.42 billion [152] people living in China feel religious.

              1.3.2.2.2.3.2.2. Con:
              The Muslim population [153] is increasing twice as fast as the world population. The number of nonreligious people might be increasing, but not as fast as the religious Muslim population.

                1.3.2.2.2.3.2.2.1. Pro:
                In 2015 [154], the Pew Research Center concluded that the global Muslim population is expected to grow at a faster rate than the non-Muslim population, primarily due to the young age and high fertility rate of Muslims.

              1.3.2.2.2.3.2.3. Pro:
              In 2015 the nonreligious world population was 1.17 billion. This is projected to rise to 1.2 billion [155] by 2060.

                1.3.2.2.2.3.2.3.1. Con:
                As a percentage of the world population, the nonreligious population is projected to reduce [156] from 16% to 13% by 2060.

              1.3.2.2.2.3.2.4. Pro:
              51% of people in Britain selected ‘no religion’ in the British Social Attitudes survey (p. 467 [157]).

                1.3.2.2.2.3.2.4.1. Pro:
                In the last 30 years, mainstream Church attendance [158] in Britain has roughly halved.

                  1.3.2.2.2.3.2.4.1.1. Pro:

            1.3.2.2.2.3.3. Pro:

Given the above, people from interfaith marriages are then likelier to be happier than others.

              1.3.2.2.2.3.3.1. Pro:
              A 2011 study found that those with wavering beliefs are less happy [161] than those with robust beliefs or a lack of belief.

            1.3.2.2.2.3.4. Pro:
            Those without religion are as emotionally healthy [162] as those who follow one.

            1.3.2.2.2.3.5. Con:

        1.3.2.2.3. Con:

        1.3.2.2.4. Con:
        In a survey [167] of teens and young adults raised in interfaith family communities, almost 90% said they were not confused by learning both Judaism and Christianity.

        1.3.2.2.5. Pro:

These schools are run by Catholics and Jews, and offer a kindergarten-through-eighth-grade curriculum for children whose families share multiple religious faiths.

        1.3.2.2.6. Pro:
        Children of interfaith couples are likely to doubt [171] their faith and religion.

      1.3.2.3. Pro:

        1.3.2.3.1. Con:
        Children who experience rejection often respond [173] with anger and aggression instead of empathy. Given this, they are less likely to be cautious of causing pain to others, and more likely to accidentally cause more pain.

    1.3.3. Pro:

      1.3.3.7. Con:
