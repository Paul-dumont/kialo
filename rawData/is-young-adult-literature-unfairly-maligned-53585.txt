Discussion Name:
Is 'Young Adult' Literature Unfairly Maligned?

1. Thesis:
'Young Adult' literature is unfairly maligned.

  1.1. Pro:
  Young adult literature is vital as a stepping stone for people who are only just beginning to read.

    1.1.1. Con:
    Over half [1] of today's readers of young adult literature are older adults.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      The ambition of young adult readers to progress to complex adult literature may be killed [2] if the readership of young adult literature is made up of mainly adults.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    From an educator's perspective, young adult literature has a number of important teaching applications for early readers.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      Young adult literature can be used alongside more complex classic pieces of literature [3] in order to serve as a 'bridge' for struggling readers.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      The inclusion of strong adolescent characters [4] allow for adolescents to enjoy engaging with reading and literary analysis.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        The ability to see things from unique or different perspectives is a large part of the appeal [5] of reading. If adolescents only read books that cater exactly to them, then they miss out on this.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Young adult literature often explores adult themes and issues in a manner which is accessible to younger readers [6]. This helps ease younger readers into critical analysis.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      Normalizing reading through any means [7] is positive for a young minds.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      If young adults are not intellectually proficient enough to explore adult themes and issues at an adult level, then they should not be engaging with such issues.

    1.1.4. Con:
    It is important that young readers challenge themselves, and the tropes [8] and indulgence [9] of much young adult literature may leave them complacent.

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      Literature the world over contains tropes and indulgence. It is not unique to YA.

      1.1.4.2. Con:
      Oftentimes, tropes and indulgence in YA literature are used as the pillars to support a larger story.

        1.1.4.2.1. Pro:
        In the Hunger Games, a central love triangle [10] allows teenagers to connect with a familiar entity within a complex dystopian totalitarian environment.

        1.1.4.2.2. Pro:
        Malorie Blackman's Noughts & Crosses [11] tells the story of a young couple living in a racially divided society. The author uses their relationship to portray the impact of privilege.

      1.1.4.3. Con:
      Just because someone enjoys young adult literature does not mean that, at the same time, they cannot also read other, more demanding works.

  1.2. Pro:
  Criticisms of YA literature are fueled by an elitist history of literary criticism, which is not reflective of the true value of a piece of literature.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    The value of literature should be a measured by the reader's experience [12], and not by the objective assessments of literary critics.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      Assessments within YA literature should be based of how the novel suits the Young Adult reader, and the ways in which it educates them on the realities of the world.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      For adults to support young adults in their development, it is important they are aware of the pros and cons of YA literature. Literary criticisms can help adults to support young adults, and reduce the level of uninformed judgements made.

      1.2.1.3. Con:
      When the reader is being introduced to the literary world, it is important the books marketed to them are both provide a good user experience and are of a high literary standard.

    1.2.2. Con:
    Literary criticisms, though flawed, are essential in shaping and improving [13] the literary world. This is of utmost importance when it comes to the literature we expose young minds to.

      1.2.2.1. Con:
      Literary criticism can only achieve these goals if those engaging in the criticism are representative of society, and are not an elite few.

      1.2.2.2. Pro:
      Criticisms of all kinds have ensured that readers remain discerning [14] about the novel before them.

      1.2.2.3. Pro:
      Criticism ensure that writers and publishers are actively attempting to make every novel the best it can be.

    1.2.3. Pro:
    Though the content and authors within the literary world are becoming more and more diverse, critics remain largely white [15].

      1.2.3.1. Pro:
      This pattern establishes a problematic paradigm [16] in which the insight and perspective of diverse authors are subject to the criticisms of white critics, which may harm their success.

        1.2.3.1.1. Con:
        While such a paradigm is problematic, it is also an issue if genuine and valid literary critiques are dismissed purely because of the race of the critic.

      1.2.3.2. Pro:
      Due to the high numbers of white critics, authors who write racist novels [17] are not called out when they should be because critics are blind to the racial implications.

      1.2.3.3. Con:
      If white critics are adequately educated on minorities and endorse an anti-racist viewpoint, their race should not be relevant.

    1.2.4. Pro:
    Many criticisms of young adult literature are fundamentally sexist in nature.

      1.2.4.1. Con:
      The culture surrounding young adult literature makes it difficult to criticise in any way [18]. Critics and authors alike often face the effects of 'cancel-culture' [19].

        1.2.4.1.1. Con:
        Critics and authors face backlash when they reduce a character to solely their understanding of a racial, sexual or cultural identity [20] that exists within a white system.

        1.2.4.1.2. Con:
        The very notion that it is 'difficult' for authors [21] to be culturally sensitive in a white dominant industry is problematic as it establishes a dynamic of entitlement to tell a story [22] based of superficial assumptions around what it means to have a given identity.

          1.2.4.1.2.1. Con:
          Cultural sensitivity can be increasingly subjective, and has been used to silence [23] minority [24] authors. The deconstruction of white supremacy and patriarchal constructs are not aided by mob justice [25].

          1.2.4.1.2.2. Pro:
          The roots of cultural appropriation are based in colonialism [26], neocolonialism [27] and orientalism [28]. They must be called out when they are identified, whether intended by the author or not.

        1.2.4.1.3. Con:
        Inaccuracies have always been criticized in creative arts. For example, Little Women was criticized for not featuring bonnets [29], a staple of the time.

          1.2.4.1.3.1. Pro:
          Inaccuracies in representations of race are more serious cases of this same act, as they reduce the lived experience of many to a tokenistic representation. [30]

          1.2.4.1.3.2. Con:
          The creators of Little Women did not face threats and accusations of racism or sexism over a small historical inaccuracy. Many YA authors can be blacklisted [31] or subjected to online abuse [32] for their inaccuracies.

            1.2.4.1.3.2.1. Con:
            These acts are a product of online culture, not of YA.

        1.2.4.1.4. Pro:
        Within the young adult literature community, it has become difficult for authors to write characters that do not reflect their own race, identity or sexuality, [33] for fear of 'cancellation' [34].

          1.2.4.1.4.1. Con:
          There is good reason for this - YA literature shapes the minds of young adults and informs them about society. When authors use the experiences of others to advance their writing, they pass their biases and stereotypes on to developing minds.

            1.2.4.1.4.1.1. Pro:
            It is the responsibility of authors and critics not to borrow stories from minorities [35] in order to appear diverse, but to explore their own role in cementing such oppression.

            1.2.4.1.4.1.2. Con:
            Unless authors are writing a biography or a story based on real, identifiable individuals, then they are not using the experiences of others for their own benefit by including diversity in their stories.

          1.2.4.1.4.2. Pro:
          It is important that authors are able to depict characters that exist outside of the authors lived experiences in order to create diverse and interesting stories.

      1.2.4.2. Pro:
      The history of young adult literature is founded in the efforts [36] and creativity [37] of young women and girls [38].

      1.2.4.3. Pro:
      Many famous female young adult authors such as S.E Hinton, E.L Konigsburg, and J.K Rowling were forced to obfuscate their femininity through the usage of their initials in order to avoid sexism [39].

        1.2.4.3.1. Con:
        If the criticism of their books was made without the knowledge that the author was a woman, then the criticism could not be fundamentally sexist in nature.

          1.2.4.3.1.1. Con:
          The fact that many now prominent authors felt that they had to hide their femininity is indicative of wider cultural problems than the feedback of a single book or series.

    1.2.5. Con:
    YA's current position as a contentious literary genre reflect that it is being held to similar standards as mainstream literature [40] and as such is legitimized by the literary world.

      1.2.5.1. Con:
      YA novels have been consistently subject to unfair criticism due to a negative assessment [41] of what it means to be a young adult.

        1.2.5.1.1. Pro:
        Prior to the labeling of novels as YA [42] many novels with teenage leads and teenage audiences - Gone With The Wind, Pride and Prejudice, Jane Eyre - received widespread acclaim. Criticism of YA novels stems from its categorization [43] as being for young adults, and not it's contents.

        1.2.5.1.2. Pro:
        Negative perceptions [44] of teenagers have been seen to pervade time and generations, despite no evidence that supports these prejudices.

      1.2.5.2. Pro:
      All books are subject to criticism. Were YA excluded from this it would imply it was not worth literary consideration.

    1.2.6. Pro:
    -> See 1.2.5.1.

  1.3. Con:
  Young adult literature is too commercial to offer products of literary or educational value.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    The young adult literature industry is worth nearly $20 billion [45], and the books created by the industry are designed to appeal directly to young adult consumers. This can lead to negative educational effects.

      1.3.1.1. Con:
      The educational literature industry in the US alone is worth billions of dollars [46]. Just because something is profitable on the open market does not mean that it is devoid of educational value.

      1.3.1.2. Pro:
      Sex sells. However publishers, do not have an interest in providing healthy or educational perspectives [47] of sex. This is particularly salient when considering the context of vulnerable and impressionable young adults.

    1.3.2. Pro:
    The success of large franchises and film adaptations founded in young adult literature has transformed it from a bohemian and feminist [48] means of expression [49] into a commercial industry behemoth.

      1.3.2.1. Con:
      If the success of YA literature leads to feminist ideals and examples gaining a platform, then that is inherently a positive thing.

      1.3.2.2. Con:
      The way in which film adaptations of books are made as well as their contents are not reflective of the source material since film adaptations are produced under different circumstances.

        1.3.2.2.1. Pro:
        Whereas books are relatively cheap to write and publish, films oftentimes cost hundreds of millions of dollars to make and market. Therefore, they have to appeal to much broader audiences to recoup their costs.

        1.3.2.2.2. Pro:
        Movies have to boil the oftentimes lengthy and complex story lines of literature into relatively short scripts. This requires script writers to make compromises in how they treat their source material.

      1.3.2.3. Pro:
      This commercial misunderstanding and simplification of complex feminist thought can be seen in the characterisation of modern young adult female protagonists, such as Katniss Everdeen [50] from the popular 'Hunger Games' franchise.

        1.3.2.3.1. Con:
        There are numerous examples of modern feminist young adult novels that encounter feminism in intellectual and empowering ways, across a wide array of genres [51].

    1.3.3. Pro:
    Literary and commercial fiction are two distinct categories of literature [52], and young adult literature falls squarely into the latter category.

      1.3.3.1. Pro:
      Commercial fiction, like young adult fiction, is read for entertainment rather than art [53], and is more focused on plot than character motivation [54] or intellectual engagement.

      1.3.3.2. Con:
      Genre labels can be misleading [55], and are not general indicators of quality.

    1.3.4. Con:
    Commercialisation is often an indicator of popularity, and has little to no bearing on the quality of the content.

      1.3.4.1. Pro:
      Though quality often leads to success [56], success is not always predicated upon quality.

      1.3.4.2. Con:
      In order for something to be commercially successful, it must have popularity, and therefore must have some degree of critical value.

    1.3.5. Con:
    Many YA books have strong morals to their tales which teach young people valuable life lessons [57].

      1.3.5.1. Pro:
      Harry Potter is famed for teaching children the value of resilience [58] in trying times.

      1.3.5.2. Pro:
      At a time when many young adults are coming of age [59], reminding them to value their personalities and accept the changes occurring is invaluable.

      1.3.5.3. Con:
      While the possibility for such moral lessons exists, whether or not these have any impact on young adults is unclear.

    1.3.6. Con:
    Classic literature has been widely commercialized [60]. Commodification is an inherent part of the modern literary world and thus is no reflection of the value of a piece of literature.

      1.3.6.1. Con:
      Most classic literature was not written with the intention of becoming commercial classics. Many young adult novels are now written with the intention of becoming commercial hits [61] or receiving film adaptations.

        1.3.6.1.1. Con:
        This is a false equivalence. Classic literature was written at a time when society was inherently less commercial and therefore the intent of the authors are bound to differ.

      1.3.6.2. Pro:
      Classic novels are being reprinted and rebranded [62], as well as being prioritized for new film productions [63].

  1.4. Con:
  Young adult literature often includes inappropriate or meaningless topics.

    1.4.1. Con:
    A topic is not meaningless purely because it's meaning does not reveal itself to the adult reader or is deemed too obvious.

      1.4.1.1. Pro:
      Many readers of young adult literature are inexperienced readers. What is too obvious or meaningless to those with literary experience may not be to young adults.

      1.4.1.2. Con:
      The breadth of adult literature and analysis is broad, and often involves discussions on adolescence and youth. Not all topics are of equal value, and some can be devoid of any meaning or literary value.

    1.4.2. Con:
    Many YA novels address complex social and political [64] issues.

      1.4.2.1. Pro:
      Harry Potter is said to parallel Nazi Germany [65] and the Third Reich.

      1.4.2.2. Pro:
      Illegal [66], a YA graphic novel, deals with the the plight and journey of illegal immigrants [67], an increasingly relevant political issue.

      1.4.2.3. Con:
      YA literature is oftentimes problematic as it displays a central hero who aims to fix issues faced by society. This does not reflect the truth of young people in modern society.

    1.4.3. Con:
    Impropriety is a judgement based off present cultural norms. The Catcher In The Rye was considered obscene [68] when published, and now it is viewed as a moderate story.

      1.4.3.1. Pro:
      As society becomes more liberal, representations of 'inappropriate issues' in literature aid in reducing taboo [69].

      1.4.3.2. Pro:
      Attempts to remove inappropriate topics from literature are futile as such topics are rampant in the modern world.

      1.4.3.3. Con:
      It is important that literature promote traditional values, so as not to condone impropriety.

    1.4.4. Pro:
    Young adult literature has been criticised for being unbearably dark [70], featuring issues such as sexual abuse, racism, domestic violence and shootings.

      1.4.4.1. Con:
      Dark and disturbing themes can often impart strong lessons and valuable warnings [71] that are important for young adults.

      1.4.4.2. Pro:
      YA novel '13 Reasons Why [72]' tells the story of Hannah Baker, a teenager who takes her own life following being raped by a classmate.

      1.4.4.3. Pro:
      YA novel 'Go Ask Alice' [73] follows the story of a teenager who is drugged, loses her virginity and falls into a lifelong and fatal drug addiction.

      1.4.4.4. Con:
      It is questionable to criticize this in the face of the challenges that today's young adults face, for example with an eye on their economic prospects and threats like climate change. Dark young adult literature may simply be a reflection of the times.

      1.4.4.5. Con:
      Bestselling YA novel, Only Ever Yours [74], focuses on the commodification of the female body in a unique and challenging way.

    1.4.5. Con:
    It is important that young adults be exposed to difficult topics, such as death, in a manner which is healthy and educational.

      1.4.5.1. Pro:
      The characterisation or discussion of death that is present in popular young adult novels such as"The Book Thief", "Tinder" and "Lovely Bones" inter alia, helps to demystify death [75] in an analytical and artistic manner.

      1.4.5.2. Con:
      It is also important that impressionable young adults have access to media that is life-affirming and positive.

        1.4.5.2.1. Con:
        Young adults are inherently exposed to dark and challenging aspects of life. YA literature offers an opportunity for readers to understand that they are not alone in their experiences [76].

        1.4.5.2.2. Pro:
        At a time when teen depression and suicide rates are rising significantly [77], influential literature aimed at impressionable young adults should not be overly dark or traumatic.

          1.4.5.2.2.1. Con:
          Stigma around mental illness only augments the challenges faced by young people. Creating open dialogue, in the ways that YA novels do, is a positive addition to the lives of young people [78].

            1.4.5.2.2.1.1. Pro:
            Books can provide insight, comfort and support for people going through difficult times or facing societal issues [79]. This can help people, particularly vulnerable young adults, in overcoming their problems.

            1.4.5.2.2.1.2. Con:
            Many young adult novels do not create an open dialogue, but rather represent and romanticise a twisted [80], pessimistic view of the world.

              1.4.5.2.2.1.2.1. Con:
              A literary work does not exist in isolation. It is the conversations around the contents [81] of such works that inspire open dialogue.

                1.4.5.2.2.1.2.1.1. Pro:
                The YA literature community has a strong online presence [82], and readers have the ability to engage in debate and discussion surrounding such topics at any time.

                1.4.5.2.2.1.2.1.2. Con:
                Young adults may not have the confidence nor the ability to engage in conversation and discussion regarding dark and adult topics, leaving them isolated and vulnerable.

                  1.4.5.2.2.1.2.1.2.1. Con:
                  This is indicative of adultism [83]. Generation Z show a great ability [84] to communicate, engage in discourse and take socio-political stances.

                  1.4.5.2.2.1.2.1.2.2. Con:
                  Millennials and consequent generations are more emotionally aware [85], open and repress less [86] than older generations.

    1.4.6. Pro:
    Young adult literature is often indulgent [87] and unrealistic.

      1.4.6.1. Con:
      Most literature [88] is indulgent and unrealistic and derives its appeal from exactly those qualities.

        1.4.6.1.1. Pro:
        Many incredibly successful adult literary series such as The Lord of the Rings and Game of Thrones take place in a highly unrealistic and fantastical world [89].

      1.4.6.2. Con:
      YA literature addresses challenging issues [90] while inspiring hope for the future and belief in the better aspects of the world are not necessarily indulgent.

      1.4.6.3. Pro:
      YA literature often has unrealistic happy endings [91], which give young people false understandings about the realities of life.

  1.5. Con:
  Young adult literature has very basic plots, bland characters, and rarely features a high quality of writing.

    1.5.1. Con:
    Many popular examples of young adult literature, such as Harry Potter, Twilight and The Hunger Games have grown into large multimedia franchises. These franchises would not have succeeded if the foundation material was basic and bland.

      1.5.1.1. Pro:
      Despite being released almost 25 years ago, Harry Potter remains one of the most popular novels, selling over 500 million copies [92] worldwide.

      1.5.1.2. Con:
      The reason for the success of these novels is that they include fantastical aspects which allow readers to escape from the real world.

      1.5.1.3. Pro:
      The foundation of these novels lie in the worlds they establish. These worlds are intriguing and challenging to readers as they cause them to see the world differently [93].

    1.5.2. Pro:
    Young adult literature generally lacks critical analysis or perspective [94], valuing satisfaction and instant gratification [95] over intellectual engagement.

      1.5.2.1. Con:
      YA literature allows young adults to intellectually engage with literature by helping them to enjoy the books they are reading [96], as opposed to forcing themselves to read.

      1.5.2.2. Con:
      Research shows that after reading YA literature, students [97] are more curious, open and listen more intentionally.

      1.5.2.3. Pro:
      Romance is present in virtually every YA novel [98], in an attempt to appeal to young adults.

    1.5.3. Con:
    Basic plots are not inherently indicative of low-quality writing. There are times when a lesson or story can best be imparted with a basic story line.

      1.5.3.1. Pro:
      Many iconic pieces of literature, such as Homer's Odyssey, Much Ado About Nothing, The Lord of the Rings, and Pride and Prejudice can have their plots condensed into one of seven basic plots [99].

      1.5.3.2. Con:
      Basic plots, when paired with bland characters, and the generally superficial or melodramatic [100] content of young adult literature, are generally indicative of poor-quality writing.

        1.5.3.2.1. Con:
        This is an unfair generalization. Twilight is indeed a poor quality book series [101], however, it is simply because Meyer is a poor writer, and is not reflective of all YA literature.

          1.5.3.2.1.1. Pro:
          Many books in the Twilight series contain basic grammatical errors and clumsy writing [102].

        1.5.3.2.2. Con:
        Poor quality writing is not decided based off a list of qualities [103], but the capabilities and limitations of the author.

          1.5.3.2.2.1. Pro:
          Just as there are some bad YA authors, there are bad authors in every genre.

        1.5.3.2.3. Con:
        Melodrama is often a purposeful stylistic choice [104], chosen in order to evoke emotion [105] and drama. Melodrama does not equal poor-quality writing.

          1.5.3.2.3.1. Pro:
          Emma, by Jane Austen, is one of the most renowned classics in the world [106]. However, its plot is basic and superficial and Emma herself is highly melodramatic.

    1.5.4. Pro:
    If young adult literature included intricate plots, competent characterisation or quality writing, it would simply be considered literature.

      1.5.4.1. Con:
      Young adult literature is a distinct subgenre of literature that is defined by its subject content and intended audience [107], not its quality.

        1.5.4.1.1. Pro:
        A number of young adult novels, such as 'The Fault in Our Stars', 'The Book Thief' and the 'Harry Potter' series have reached critical acclaim and commercial success.

    1.5.5. Con:
    YA novels are written with a young reader in mind and as such it is not necessary that their content meet the criteria for 'quality writing' established by older more experienced readers.

      1.5.5.1. Con:
      If the intention of YA literature is to increase engagement amongst young readers, it is important to bridge the quality gap between children's books and adult literature.

      1.5.5.2. Pro:
      Storytelling is the most important aspect of a novel for young readers, while the quality of the prose is less relevant.

      1.5.5.3. Con:
      The fact that many modern readers care more about a novel's appeal to their taste doesn't render literary merit obsolete. [108]

    1.5.6. Con:
    YA literature series often construct entire worlds filled with depth and variety in which a reader can become lost. This is indicative of quality writing as it transports the reader in a unique way and shifts their perspective.

      1.5.6.1. Pro:
      It is easy to create the illusion of coherent writing or world building when writing for young adults who lack the critical and intellectual skill to analyse such concepts and already have strong imaginations.

        1.5.6.1.1. Con:
        It is exactly because young adults have such vivid imaginations that their expectations for creative writing are valid. Young adults can recognize a boring or ill-developed world.

      1.5.6.2. Con:
      There are more components to quality writing than world building. The quality of the prose and the external significance [109] of the book are of great significance.

  1.6. Pro:
  Young adult literature is often written by, and for, people whose experiences are more diverse and varied than those captured in traditional literary canons.

    1.6.1. Con:
    YA literature has faced consistent critiques [110] for not having a diverse pool of writers, even in cases of diverse tales.

      1.6.1.1. Pro:
      It can be problematic for authors to co-opt the narratives of minority groups or ethnicities that they are not a part of [111].

      1.6.1.2. Pro:
      YA literature has been seen to be becoming less diverse [112] in recent years.

      1.6.1.3. Con:
      YA literature suffers from a lack of diversity as a result of the systematic racism [113] that is present in publishing the world over. It is not a genre specific issue.

    1.6.2. Pro:
    Young adults have issues that are unique to them such as puberty, school drama and young relationships. It is important that they can relate to the literature that they consume.

      1.6.2.1. Con:
      These issues are often the main focus of much pop culture such as TV, music and film. The literary world ought to be more intellectual and deal with larger issues.

      1.6.2.2. Pro:
      -> See 1.1.2.2.

    1.6.3. Pro:
    Young people feel increasingly ignored [114] in modern society. Sculpting novels for young adults allows them to be fairly recognized [115] in modern literature.

      1.6.3.1. Pro:
      The truth of the lived experiences of young people were not reflected in novels [116] for young readers before the birth of YA novels.

      1.6.3.2. Con:
      -> See 1.4.2.3.

    1.6.4. Con:
    Traditional literary canons can have an incredibly diverse range of authors and experiences represented. Young adult literature does not have a monopoly on diversity.

      1.6.4.1. Con:
      Those books within traditional literary canons that are made accessible to young people through education [117] do not reflect diversity. As such, YA makes diversity accessible to developing minds.

      1.6.4.2. Pro:
      A number of critically acclaimed classic novels represent the experiences of minority groups in society [118] in an intellectually stimulating and respectful manner.
