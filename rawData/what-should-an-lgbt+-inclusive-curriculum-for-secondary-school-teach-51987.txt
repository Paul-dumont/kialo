What should an LGBT+ inclusive curriculum for secondary school teach?

1. Question:
What should an LGBT+ inclusive curriculum for secondary school teach?

  1.1. Thesis:
  An LGBTQ+ inclusive narrative on the currently supported subjects would be an appropriate adjustment to the curriculum for secondary school.

    1.1.1. Con:
    Getting individuals to adjust thier narrative can be a treacherously political path; wrought with oppositional mishandlings.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    Understanding that Inclusion (as used here) is an aspect of the narration itself more than a topic to narrate, could lead to inclusion playing a more generalized role in pedagogy and other institutions.

  1.2. Thesis:
  An LGBT+ inclusive curriculum should provide the basic knowledge to keep all children and young people healthy and safe and prepare them for life and work.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    An LGBT+ inclusive curriculum in secondary schools should encourage students in the arts sector [1] (i.e. film students, music and other art forms) to direct their self expression to include LGBT+ communities.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      Increased LGBT+ representation in media and art can boost feelings of self-worth and affirmation within LGBT+ community members [2]. This would be no different in a school setting.

      1.2.1.2. Pro:
      LGBT+ often experience the world differently, making them suitable to express their perspectives in a creative way [3]. Fostering this skill in students will allow them to more confidently find success in this field.

      1.2.1.3. Pro:
      Those in the artistic community are already more open-minded [4] and accepting of LGBT+ people. Focusing part of the art curriculum to include works of LGBT+ people would be appropriate.

    1.2.2. Con:
    One of the dangers of an LGBT+ curriculum in secondary schools is that it would promote over sensitiveness and over reacting to even the simplest of actions from the LGBT+ community.

      1.2.2.1. Pro:
      Young people who are too sensitive can be stereotyped as "snowflakes [5]," are too easily offended. This leads them to being less resilient in the face of adversity.

        1.2.2.1.1. Con:
        Labeling someone a snowflake stigmatizes [6] mental health and evokes hatred.

      1.2.2.2. Con:
      Children who are sensitive is not necessarily a bad thing, as they are very in tune [7] with their environment.

      1.2.2.3. Con:
      Learning about LGBT+ and sexual minorities can encourage more empathy [8] and understanding for this historically marginalized group.

    1.2.3. Pro:
    An LGBT+ inclusive curriculum should teach strategies to challenge all forms of prejudice and discrimination.

      1.2.3.1. Pro:
      An LGBT+ inclusive curriculum should teach about the unacceptability of prejudice-based language [9] and behaviour, offline and online, including homophobia, biphobia, and transphobia.

        1.2.3.1.1. Con:
        The unacceptability of prejudice-based language constantly keeps changing. It will be extremely difficult to keep up with what was acceptable is now unacceptable, and what is considered offensive can also change in a heartbeat.

      1.2.3.2. Pro:
      An LGBT+ inclusive curriculum should teach the need to promote inclusion and challenge discrimination [10], and how to do so safely, including online.

      1.2.3.3. Pro:
      As part of the curriculum, self defense training [11] should also be included so that all LGBT+ students and faculty alike can defend themselves against any and all forms of hatred that may befall them.

      1.2.3.4. Con:
      An LGBT+ inclusive curriculum could teach the consequences of prejudice and discrimination, but not the solution to avoid it because solutions haven't been found yet.

      1.2.3.5. Con:
      This topic [12] can be already be addressed in classroom contexts outside of an LGBT+ curriculum.

        1.2.3.5.1. Pro:
        Topics of prejudice and discrimination are already often taught in language arts [13] and social studies [14] classes.

        1.2.3.5.2. Con:
        By excluding [15] the experiences of a certain group in the curriculum, it erases their existence and fuels ignorance.

      1.2.3.6. Pro:
      An LGBT+ inclusive curriculum should teach the impact of stereotyping [16], prejudice and discrimination on individuals and relationships.

    1.2.4. Pro:
    An LGBT+ inclusive curriculum should teach the importance of stable, committed relationships, including the rights and protections provided within legally recognised marriages and civil partnerships and the legal status of other long-term relationships.

      1.2.4.1. Con:
      Not all students will be in or want to be in relationships or be married. Having this as part of the curriculum would force an ideology that one must be in a committed relationship to achieve success or happiness in life.

        1.2.4.1.1. Con:
        Being married positively impacts one's success [17] in life.

        1.2.4.1.2. Pro:
        There is no need to be married to be fulfilled [18] in life.

      1.2.4.2. Pro:
      Students already have many thoughts [19] on their futures, including whether if they want to be married or not. Providing a guideline on how it works could be beneficial and practical.

      1.2.4.3. Con:
      In developed countries, the average age as to when people first get married has steadily increased [20] to around age 30. Learning this information during secondary school may not be helpful, as people may forget it by the time they enter into these relationships.

        1.2.4.3.1. Con:
        Learning this information will help in better preparing students in dealing with situations that they would have otherwise have a hard time dealing with when they become parents themselves.

          1.2.4.3.1.1. Pro:
          Since almost everyone attends secondary or high school, it is a good setting [21] to teach students about child development.

        1.2.4.3.2. Pro:
        It can cause a surge in youngsters getting married once they reach their 20s and too early in their life while not having enough life experience to tackle the rollercoaster ride that is marriage and commitment.

    1.2.5. Con:
    This is not specific to sex education, but is taught as part of general education already.

      1.2.5.1. Pro:
      In Japan, practical and compulsory courses such as kateika [22] (home economics education) prepare all students for life and work, regardless of their gender or sexual orientation. (p.1)

      1.2.5.2. Con:
      General education in secondary school often does not include enough practical knowledge to teach students how to stay healthy [23] or prepare them for real life skills.

        1.2.5.2.1. Pro:
        The best educator for these types of scenarios will be life itself and the experience gained from learning important life lessons.

  1.3. Thesis:
  An LGBT+ inclusive curriculum should teach some of the social and cultural history of same-sex attraction relevant to understanding philosophical and religious issues raised.

    1.3.1. Con:
    By teaching just some of the social and cultural history of same-sex attraction, such an LGBT+ curriculum could become dangerously biased and ethnocentric [24].

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      Schools have faced backlash by even implementing some LGBT+ friendly symbols [25] on campus resources.

      1.3.1.2. Pro:
      The LGBT+ experience is not homogenized. [26] Prioritizing one culture's values over another would drown out the voices of others.

      1.3.1.3. Con:
      Learning about LGBT+ history serves as a foundation [27] in understanding various social and political movements, not just ones relating to sex and gender.

    1.3.2. Con:
    Many students are still figuring out [28] their gender identity and sexual orientation in secondary school. Having them apply attraction's impact through a philosophical or religious lens may confuse them even more.

      1.3.2.1. Pro:
      In public schools, teaching about religion can be dangerously biased [29] towards only a dominant religion's view on any matter, including about relationships and attractions.

      1.3.2.2. Con:
      Though challenging, studying topics through a religious lens can helps students understand [30] how other people and societies view certain things, including attitudes on same-sex attraction.

    1.3.3. Pro:
    An LGBT+ inclusive curriculum should teach how the law has changed over time on issues related to sexual orientation and gender identity.

      1.3.3.1. Pro:
      The debates about homosexuality tend to be sharply polarized [31], in part because they often involve public policy and legal issues.

        1.3.3.1.1. Pro:
        References to natural law [32] still play an important role in contemporary debates about homosexuality in religion, politics, and even courtrooms, and the historical origins of these debates ought to be taught.

      1.3.3.2. Pro:
      An LGBT+ curriculum should trace the history of "LGBT rights are human rights [33]".

        1.3.3.2.1. Con:
        If a curriculum focuses on this aspect of human rights, it will need to cover the rights of every oppressed group in history, which will be impractical.

        1.3.3.2.2. Pro:
        The policy and legal debates surrounding homosexuality involve fundamental issues of morality and justice. Perhaps most centrally of all, they cut to issues of personal identity and self-definition.

          1.3.3.2.2.1. Con:
          What one person considers as morality is not homogenous when compared to what another may consider as morality. Such a fundamental concept is subjective to say the least.

        1.3.3.2.3. Pro:
        An LGBT+ inclusive curriculum should teach the legal rights, responsibilities and protections provided by anti-discrimination law.

        1.3.3.2.4. Pro:
        Human rights are norms that aspire to protect all people everywhere from severe political, legal, and social abuses [34], including LGBT+ people.

          1.3.3.2.4.1. Pro:
          LGBT rights are an intrinsically human right, beyond any controversy or polemic, because they are indeed about the application of existing rights [35]

          1.3.3.2.4.2. Pro:
          U.S. Secretary of State Hillary Rodham Clinton delivered remarks on International Human Rights Day in Geneva, Switzerland on December 6, 2011 [36].

      1.3.3.3. Con:
      Teaching how the law has changed without teaching the social historical context in which those laws were developed, would be devoid of meaning.

        1.3.3.3.1. Con:
        Laws are dependent on the historical context [37] of the time. Legal history is a specific field that studies this relationship and can be incorporated into a lesson.

          1.3.3.3.1.1. Pro:
          By studying 'law in context', students would better understand how the many ways legal norms and institutions are conditioned by culture and social [38].

        1.3.3.3.2. Pro:
        An LGBT+ inclusive curriculum should teach how moral philosophy has changed to understand why public opinion and the law around LGBT+ has changed.

          1.3.3.3.2.1. Pro:
          The Classical Utilitarians, Bentham and Mill, were concerned with legal and social reform. Jeremy Bentham felt that people often had responses to certain actions — of pleasure or disgust — that did not reflect anything morally significant at all. Indeed, in his discussions of homosexuality, for example, he explicitly notes that ‘antipathy’ is not sufficient reason to legislate against a practice [39]

        1.3.3.3.3. Pro:
        To understand why the law is as it is today, it is necessary to understand some of the context of key legal changes.

          1.3.3.3.3.1. Pro:
          In 1994, Conservative [40] MP Edwina Currie [41] proposed an amendment to the Criminal Justice and Public Order Bill [42] to lower the age of consent to sixteen years. This was important as would equalize the age of consent to that of heterosexual people.

            1.3.3.3.3.1.1. Pro:
            An equal [43] age of consent, finally secured in 2001, ensured that gay and bi men were not persecuted unfairly for actions they take in their private lives.

          1.3.3.3.3.2. Pro:
          The recommendations of the 1957 Wolfenden Report [44] eventually led to the passage of the Sexual Offences Act 1967 [45]. This replaced the previous law on sodomy [46] contained in the Offences against the Person Act 1861 [47] and the 1885 Labouchere Amendment [48], which outlawed every homosexual act short of sodomy.

    1.3.4. Pro:
    An LGBT+ inclusive curriculum should teach why being LGBT+ and observing religious teachings aren't mutually exclusive [49].

      1.3.4.1. Pro:
      Skeptical interpretations of Christianity and homosexuality suggest that the Bible makes mistakes [50].

        1.3.4.1.1. Con:
        Revisionist interpretations of Christianity and homosexuality suggest that certain kinds of homosexual conduct are wrong [51].

          1.3.4.1.1.1. Pro:
          We should understand Judaism and early Christianity in historical context [52].

          1.3.4.1.1.2. Con:
          Traditionalist interpretations of Christianity and homosexuality suggest that the Bible teaches that all homosexual conduct is wrong [53].

        1.3.4.1.2. Pro:
        In the Bible for example, there is a verse in the book of Leviticus that specifically instructs the believer to not eat anything from the seas that does not have scales and fins. Yet, many Christians ignore this part of the Bible, but conveniently focus more on the homosexuality part that only has two verses from the same of book of Leviticus from said Bible.

          1.3.4.1.2.1. Pro:
          The versus Leviticus 20:13 and Leviticus 18:22 seems to be the go-to verses in arguments.

            1.3.4.1.2.1.1. Pro:
            To use the Bible against LGBT+ people is an act of abuse [54] against the book itself.

          1.3.4.1.2.2. Pro:
          The verses that references this has gone through various translations [55], thus the original meaning may have been lost. The original meaning was to forbade male incestuous relationships, rather than homosexual relations.

      1.3.4.2. Pro:
      In the US, nearly half [56] of LGBT+ adults are religious, despite a history of discrimination and rejection.

      1.3.4.3. Con:
      Aquinas' teachings and Catholic natural law theory rule out that homosexuality can ever be moral. [57]

      1.3.4.4. Pro:
      There are queer perspectives on theology [58].

        1.3.4.4.1. Pro:
        In Christianity, emerging queer perspectives can reclaim the religion for themselves through the use of liberation theology [59].

    1.3.5. Pro:
    An LGBT+ course should teach about the variety of people in the community, how it came to be, and what the definitions are in the group.

      1.3.5.1. Con:
      An LGBT+ course should be focusing on normalising the communities instead of focusing on definitions and labels [60].

        1.3.5.1.1. Pro:
        Labels aren't helpful [61] for an individual establishing their own personality, as being LGBT+ may only be a small aspect of that person's life.

        1.3.5.1.2. Con:
        When used carefully, labels are a way for young people to connect [62] with other people who may have similar experiences, which can better help them understand their own gender and sexuality.

          1.3.5.1.2.1. Pro:
          Though they may not identify with traditional LGBT+ labels, many young people are identifying with emerging identify labels [63] that better represent them. This shows that labeling is an important step for teens to define themselves.

      1.3.5.2. Con:
      There are too many diverse experiences and complexities in between different LGBT+ people to qualify them as a community.

        1.3.5.2.1. Pro:
        Some LGBT+ people may even feel rejected [64] by said community; merely learning about the overall community would leave out these marginalized groups.

      1.3.5.3. Pro:
      An LGBT+ inclusive curriculum should teach about the similarities, differences and diversity [65] among people of gender identities and sexual orientations.

      1.3.5.4. Pro:
      An LGBT+ curriculum should present the experiences of LGBT+ people to activate empathy and provide a safe space [66] for discussion.

        1.3.5.4.1. Pro:
        This kind of curriculum could bring awareness to LGBT+ victimization via group-based empathy and intergroup emotions [67].

        1.3.5.4.2. Con:
        An increased empathy towards LGBT+s issues wouldn't make a fundamental change in their current situation.

          1.3.5.4.2.1. Con:
          As school environments can be traumatizing [68] for LGBT+ students, implementing social emotional learning that emphasizes on empathy would decrease the chance of such students committing suicide.

        1.3.5.4.3. Con:
        Teachers may have their own biases towards LGBT+ students, thus unwilling to have empathy for students who are harassed and discriminated against.

        1.3.5.4.4. Pro:
        This kind of curriculum would bring about a positive change for LGBT+ students.

          1.3.5.4.4.1. Pro:
          Having more empathy is essential in eliminating bias [69] that others may have against LGBT+ students.

  1.4. Thesis:
  An LGBTQ+ inclusive school should teach all the regular subjects. It doesn’t necessarily have to teach anything extra to just be inclusive.

  1.5. Thesis:
  An LGBT+ inclusive curriculum should teach the ongoing research surrounding the natural sciences and sexual orientation/gender identity.

    1.5.1. Pro:
    Whether genetic or other physiological determinants form the basis of sexual orientation is a highly politicized issue [70] and cannot be left to young people to struggle with alone.

      1.5.1.1. Con:
      Rather than struggling alone, there are more and more LGBT+ people in politics [71], especially in higher offices who will advocate for issues that are important for these young people.

      1.5.1.2. Pro:
      A clear genetic link would suggest that gay people are “born this way [72],” as opposed to having made a lifestyle choice. This is important in understanding gay activism, as being gay as a choice is used as a defence by some homophobic legislation still relevant in many countries of the world.

        1.5.1.2.1. Pro:
        There is evidence that sexual orientation has biological underpinnings. [1] [73] [2] [74]

        1.5.1.2.2. Con:
        No scientific study has found any evidence of a genetic "genetic cause of same-sex sexual behavior [75]."

          1.5.1.2.2.1. Con:
          Studies have found that at least 1500 animal species exhibit gay behavior [76].

    1.5.2. Con:
    Much of the research on this topic is written about in scientific papers. It is impractical to ask secondary school teachers to constantly be keeping up with scientific journals and incorporate them into their lesson plans.

      1.5.2.1. Pro:
      Teachers are already overworked. They should not be expected to read original research papers [77], which could go up to 30 pages or more.

      1.5.2.2. Con:
      Teachers can assign scientific articles [78] for students to read and investigate. This would help students develop and hone their higher-order thinking skills.

        1.5.2.2.1. Pro:
        If students are going to proceed into college/university level education, it is important that they learn these skills by engaging with the sort of scientific articles that they would encounter in college.

        1.5.2.2.2. Con:
        Unless they are provided with specific instructions and strategies, it may be very difficult for students of secondary level to read scientific papers. Even students who are in university already have a difficult time [79] reading these.

      1.5.2.3. Con:
      Many topics that are taught in schools are written about in academic papers. It is natural that teachers will only be expected to teach a simplified and more accessible version of this topic.

    1.5.3. Pro:
    An LGBT+ inclusive curriculum should teach how medical and psychiatric understandings of sexual orientation and gender identity have changed over time [80].

      1.5.3.1. Pro:
      This would be relevant to learning about the social cultural effects in accepting homosexuality [81], which the American Psychiatric Association removed from its Diagnostic and Statistical Manual in 1973.

      1.5.3.2. Pro:
      This is relevant to understanding pathologizing discourse surrounding the topic and the recent legal changes around conversion therapy [82].

      1.5.3.3. Con:
      Psychiatric understanding of sexual orientation and gender identity are not related to biology but rather to cultural and societal understanding within a specific time frame.

    1.5.4. Con:
    Sexual orientation and gender identity are social constructs [83], therefore it wouldn't be beneficial to associate them with natural sciences.

      1.5.4.1. Pro:
      The study of homosexuality within natural sciences has a long history of controversies [84], therefore, it would be more interesting to associate a LGBT+ curriculum with social sciences.

      1.5.4.2. Con:
      A central issue raised by queer theory [85] is whether homosexuality, and hence also heterosexuality and bisexuality, is socially constructed or purely driven by biological forces.
