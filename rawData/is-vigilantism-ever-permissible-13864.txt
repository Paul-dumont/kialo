Discussion Name:
Is Vigilantism Ever Permissible?

1. Thesis:
Vigilantism is permissible.

  1.1. Con:
  Pursuing vigilantism may encourage more violence and crime.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    "Protectors" can provide security for a community at first, yet if not controlled, further down the line the same "protectors" can create bigger problems for a community.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      Vigilantes took over Rio's favelas [1] initially to protect against ruling gangs, yet became violent and harmful themselves.

        1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
        Some favela [2] residents claim the vigilante militias are worse for their community than the drug traffickers.

      1.1.1.2. Pro:
      In Mexico [3], the rise of vigilante groups added to turmoil over crime gangs. According to a security expert, "some are clearly authentic efforts by communities to protect themselves, but others appear to have been infiltrated by criminals [from rival cartels]."

      1.1.1.3. Pro:
      In societies torn apart by long-lasting conflicts, vigilantism can take the form of paramilitary groups that reproduce and reinforce a climate of generalized violence by terrorizing communities and committing crimes beyond the ones they claim to punish.

        1.1.1.3.1. Pro:
        Even two decades after the Good Friday Agreement [4] formally ended the long-standing sectarian conflict between Catholics and Protestants in Northern Ireland, vigilante violence keeps terrorizing communities [5] to this day.

          1.1.1.3.1.1. Pro:
          The groups claim [6] to be policing “anti-social behavior” such as drug dealing, but are often dealing drugs [7] or participating in other criminal enterprises themselves and trying to protect their turf.

        1.1.1.3.2. Pro:
        Local Nigerian [8] vigilantes have been known to participate in forced recruiting, child and woman soldiering and extra-judicial killings.

        1.1.1.3.3. Pro:
        There are fears that the vigilantes against the Boko Haram militants in north east Nigeria [9] could themselves degenerate and proliferate into mercenary militias.

      1.1.1.4. Pro:
      Indian “anti-Romeo [10]” squads,originally set up to protect women from sexual harassment in public places, soon left people insecure and fearful [11] because of their heavy-handed tactics and moral policing. They started targeting and publicly shaming [12] young men and couples in parks, colleges and markets.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    Vigilantism can make it harder for the state to implement law and order efficiently.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      Online vigilante justice can influence law enforcement procedures.

        1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
        Social media commentary on court cases can result in a mistrial as rhetoric by online vigilantes might influence potential jurors unfairly. [13]

        1.1.2.1.2. Pro:
        Harassment and violence spurred on by vigilante justice may force courts to reduce the sentencing of criminals [14] for the violence and cyber bullying that they were forced to face.

        1.1.2.1.3. Pro:
        Vigilantes’ responses to perceived malicious activity has caused the loss of digital evidence and subsequently has obstructed [15] law enforcement’s effort in ascertaining attribution and jurisdiction over cybercrime offences.

        1.1.2.1.4. Con:
        The police are unable to cope with the sheer scale of online crimes. The increasing numbers of offenders and vigilantes has created an almost lawless [16] online domain in which the police have limited power anyway.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      Vigilante groups are a threat to democracy in many countries.

        1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
        According to the Former Chairman of the Electoral Commission, vigilantism is a major threat [17] to Ghana’s elections and democracy.

          1.1.2.2.1.1. Pro:
          According to The Commissioner for Human Rights and Administrative Justice (CHAR) in 2010, the increasing threat of vigilante groups [18] formed by political parties to threaten and beat up opponents threatened Ghana's democracy.

        1.1.2.2.2. Pro:
        The paramilitaries are the greatest political threat to Colombian democracy [19].

      1.1.2.3. Pro:
      Vigilantism means limited resources of the police are stretched [20] even further by having to investigate vigilante violence in addition to the initial crime that brought it on.

      1.1.2.4. Pro:
      Members of the public patrolling [21] their own streets may be trying to help, but they only add to the problems for police officers.

        1.1.2.4.1. Con:
        This is not true- the concept of Broken Windows Policing [22] has been very helpful to police.

          1.1.2.4.1.1. Pro:
          Broken Windows Policing include actions [23] such as neighbourhood watches, community clean ups and calling the police when something suspicious happens.

            1.1.2.4.1.1.1. Pro:
            Neighbourhood watch groups [24] are an example of a type of vigilantism, as they are organised groups, without formal legal authority, that do not resort to violence.

              1.1.2.4.1.1.1.1. Con:
              Neighbourhood watch groups don't actually tackle problems of crime or security, as they simply succeed at pushing the problems into neighbourhoods [25] that don’t have similarly effective neighbourhood watches.

              1.1.2.4.1.1.1.2. Con:
              Neighbourhood watch groups can also engage in acts of violence. George Zimmerman, the man who shot teenager Trayvon Martin, was an appointed neighbourhood watch coordinator [26].

      1.1.2.5. Con:
      If vigilante groups can become sufficient for the maintenance of law and order in a community, then there is no need for the state to become involved.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    People may be inviting more harm when they decide to pursue the suspects involved in a crime.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      Vigilantes can not know what criminal's intentions are or how far they may be willing to go to avoid being caught. Consequently, vigilantes can put their life or their family's life in jeopardy by pursuing them. [27]

    1.1.4. Con:
    The main drive behind vigilantism is to decrease crime, not to make matters worse.

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      The root cause for vigilantism occurs as a result of overwhelming levels of crime which is not adequately dealt with by state intervention.

      1.1.4.2. Con:
      The main drive behind vigilantism is to create a society that conforms to one's worldview [28]; failing to be accomodative of other points of view will inevitably be bad for a democratic society.

    1.1.5. Pro:
    Vigilantes can collectively turn into pack mentality [29] which can cause more damage to communities and neighbourhoods.

      1.1.5.1. Pro:
      In recent years Indonesia has seen a sharp increase in vigilante lynchings [30].

    1.1.6. Pro:
    Unprecedented [31] levels of violence and punishment can occur amongst vigilante groups who have little regard for human rights.

      1.1.6.1. Pro:
      Historically vigilantism has proved to result in horrendous examples of violence and hate, including the Whitecappers [32] and Klu Klux Klan.

      1.1.6.2. Pro:
      Jungle justice [33] is a form of public extrajudicial killings in Sub-Saharan Africa, where an alleged criminal is humiliated, beaten or summarily executed by vigilantes. Treatments can vary from a "muddy treatment", where the perceived perpetrator is made to roll in mud for hours to severe beatings.

      1.1.6.3. Pro:
      Necklacing [34] is the practice of extrajudicial summary execution and torture method carried out by forcing a rubber tyre, filled with petrol, around a victim's chest and arms, and setting it on fire.

    1.1.7. Con:
    Vigilantism may disincentive crime by showing that justice will occur by lay people if law enforcement fail to act.

    1.1.8. Pro:
    This could endanger innocent people who might accidentally get in the way.

      1.1.8.1. Pro:
      There are many cases where vigilantes have murdered [35] innocent people.

      1.1.8.2. Con:
      This could and often does apply to corrupt or incompetent police just as easily as vigilantes.

      1.1.8.3. Pro:
      Vigilante paedophile hunters [36] online have ruined the lives of both abuse victims and wrongly suspected innocent people.

        1.1.8.3.1. Con:
        Indeed they have. But this is vigilantism at its worst. An example of where a concept can go wrong does not make it inherently flawed.

      1.1.8.4. Pro:
      Internet vigilantism can often hurt innocent people.

        1.1.8.4.1. Con:
        Cybersecurity and internet vigilantism [37] can be legal, non-violent and effective.

          1.1.8.4.1.1. Con:
          Cyber-vigilantism can be often illegal [38], even unknowingly, if perpetrators tamper with digital evidence.

          1.1.8.4.1.2. Pro:
          Many expert vigilantes [39] such as computer scientists could utilise valuable knowledge and techniques to help prevent cybercrime, and real life crime.

          1.1.8.4.1.3. Pro:
          Hacktivism [40] is a form of hacking for socio-political purposes where hacking is the substance and form of activism.

            1.1.8.4.1.3.1. Pro:
            One of the earliest and most prolific hacktivism groups is the Cult of the Dead Cow (cDc). [41]

              1.1.8.4.1.3.1.1. Pro:
              In the 1990's the cDc worked with a group of Chinese dissidents [42] to allow citizens of the People's Republic of China to access censored online content.

              1.1.8.4.1.3.1.2. Pro:
              The cDc have campaigned against Google's decision to cooperate with China's online censorship by launching the "Goolag" project. [43]

              1.1.8.4.1.3.1.3. Pro:
              The cDc has released a number of publicly accessible tools for both hackers and system administrators.

                1.1.8.4.1.3.1.3.1. Pro:
                Back Orifice [44] was a remote system administration tool, developed to demonstrate the lack of security in Windows 98.

                1.1.8.4.1.3.1.3.2. Pro:
                NBName [45] is a computer program that can be used to carry out denial-of-service (DoS) attacks [46] that can disable NetBIOS services on Windows machines

                  1.1.8.4.1.3.1.3.2.1. Pro:
                  DoS attacks are a valuable hacktivism tool, [47] as they can disrupt the day-to-day running of processes., similar to the online equivalent of a protest.

            1.1.8.4.1.3.2. Pro:
            Anonymous is arguably the most famous group involved in internet vigilantism, Time magazine [48] featured them as one of the "100 Most Influential People in the World".

              1.1.8.4.1.3.2.1. Pro:
              Project Chanology [49] was a protest movement by Anonymous against Scientology, involving both physical protests and cyber attacks against scientology.org.

              1.1.8.4.1.3.2.2. Pro:
              In September 2010, Anonymous launched Operation Payback, a movement which aimed to supportfreedom of speech [50], and the sharing of information.

        1.1.8.4.2. Pro:
        Vigilantes may be working with incomplete information and consequently may end up instigating attacks on innocent people. [51]

          1.1.8.4.2.1. Pro:
          In the aftermath of the white supremacist rallies in Charlottesville [52], many civil rights activists took to Twitter and misidentified a University of Arkansas professor [53]. The professor and his wife had to hide at a friend's house as people started threatening them and even tweeted out his home address.

          1.1.8.4.2.2. Pro:
          -> See 1.1.8.3.

          1.1.8.4.2.3. Pro:
          In the wake of the Boston Marathon bombing, vigilantes from the Reddit website [54] looked to find the person responsible for the attack, but misidentified the perpetrator. They [55] had committed suicide before the bombing had happened and the family was harassed and threatened by internet users who had participated in the internet "investigation".

          1.1.8.4.2.4. Pro:
          These victims, if mistakenly targeted, can often suffer depression or anxiety from these attacks [56].

            1.1.8.4.2.4.1. Pro:
            In 2017, a video of a couple arguing with and pushing an elderly man who asked to share a table with them went viral. In the process of trying to find the couple, people online incorrectly identified a couple. The woman wrongly named online wrote on her Facebook page that it was an “emotional period and scary moment [57]”.

          1.1.8.4.2.5. Pro:
          Innocent victims can suffer real-world ramifications from such inaccurate online attacks. Some have lost their jobs, received death threats and left the country to escape the relentless persecution. [58]

        1.1.8.4.3. Pro:
        Internet vigilantes can disclose private information about people as a form of public shaming for their actions. Publishing people's mobile numbers, home address, and photos of their family [59] can also harm family members and friends close to them that are innocent of any crime.

    1.1.9. Pro:
    This would possibly cause a spike in crime from people who might try to combat and take down the vigilante group.

      1.1.9.1. Pro:
      The John Brown Anti-Klan Committee [60] was an anti-KKK vigilante organization in the United States which participated in a spate of bombings in the 1980s.

  1.2. Pro:
  Vigilantism can free up public funds for other purposes than law enforcement.

    1.2.1. Con:
    The government should not stop spending on the police just because vigilantes exist.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      This can lead to a harmful precedent where the state steps away from managing domestic security, outsourcing it entirely to assorted groups of private citizens.

        1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
        Private groups are worse at distributing security and justice than the state.

          1.2.1.1.1.1. Con:
          According to research [61] conducted by The Guardian, half of the planet's population lives in countries where there are more private security workers than police officers.

          1.2.1.1.1.2. Pro:
          Private security is likelier to only be made affordable/available to people of certain communities or classes, unlike a more universally available state police force.

            1.2.1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
            South Africa has a booming private security industry [62], many businesses and wealthy families pay for 24/7 monitoring of their properties. [63]

          1.2.1.1.1.3. Pro:
          People are less likely to accept the writ of non-state organizations to dispense security and monopolise violence.

            1.2.1.1.1.3.1. Con:
            This is not always true - in many countries people do not have much faith in the army and police force.

              1.2.1.1.1.3.1.1. Pro:
              According to international group Human Rights Watch, the appeal of vigilante groups in Liberia [64] appears to stem from a lack of confidence in the police.

                1.2.1.1.1.3.1.1.1. Pro:
                Corruption is endemic in Liberia [65], and is not specifically a punishable crime under Liberian law.

                1.2.1.1.1.3.1.1.2. Pro:
                Liberian police officers demand money [66] before performing basic job functions so it is not possible for ordinary Liberians to turn to them.

              1.2.1.1.1.3.1.2. Pro:
              In 2008 in Argentina, only 36.3% of those surveyed [67] trusted the armed forces.

              1.2.1.1.1.3.1.3. Pro:
              On average only 78% of Europeans [68] trust their armed forces.

              1.2.1.1.1.3.1.4. Pro:
              Mob justice is frequent in Khayelitsha, a township in Cape Town, because the citizens do not have faith [69] in formal institutions and therefore resort to violence.

                1.2.1.1.1.3.1.4.1. Pro:
                According to a report [70] by the Women’s Legal Centre, Khayelitsha has a disproportionately high level of crime, combined with the failure of the police to prevent, combat and investigate crime effectively.

              1.2.1.1.1.3.1.5. Pro:
              Nigerians are embracing vigilante groups [71] because of soaring crime and ineffective police, which is poorly paid and often outgunned by well-armed criminal gangs.

    1.2.2. Con:
    In reality vigilantism would have very little impact on public funds.

      1.2.2.1. Pro:
      In the first place vigilantism often occurs due to a lack of public funding and lawlessness [72] in poorer areas of cities.

        1.2.2.1.1. Pro:
        Inequality [73] creates a demand for vigilantism since poorer citizens feel relatively deprived of security compared to wealthier neighbours who have advantages regarding private and public security.

          1.2.2.1.1.1. Pro:

            1.2.2.1.1.1.1. Pro:
            -> See 1.2.1.1.1.2.1.

            1.2.2.1.1.1.2. Pro:

    1.2.3. Con:

  1.3. Pro:
  When the state and its organs prove incapable of providing justice to the people, it is necessary for some other group to step up and provide said justice.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    Racial and socio-economic biases [78] in states' justice systems deny many individuals the justice and equality they deserve.

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      If vigilantes can offer minorities and disadvantaged groups justice that they would otherwise be unable to access, then then vigilantism provides an important benefit in society.

      1.3.1.2. Pro:
      Vigilantism can offer an opportunity to build awareness of important issues which can occasionally result in tangible change. [79]

        1.3.1.2.1. Pro:
        Legal systems are slow to change but call-out culture online can spark conversations and ongoing dialogue [80], which in turn can push politicians to act.

        1.3.1.2.2. Pro:
        A series of sexual harassment claims that have emerged under the #metoo hashtag in India have sparked a Facebook campaign that has already named 70 top academics [81]

      1.3.1.3. Con:
      However, vigilantes, by virtue of deriving from the same society that possesses these biases, are likely to be reflective of them. Consequently, they are likelier than not to be racist and sexist in their own right.

        1.3.1.3.1. Con:
        Majority groups in society will rarely feel threatened enough, or their interests interfered with enough, that they resort to vigilantism.

          1.3.1.3.1.1. Con:
          Soldiers of Odin (SOO) [82] is an anti-immigrant group founded in Finland in October 2015. The group was established as a response to thousands of migrants arriving in Finland amid the European migrant crisis. The group now has a presence in Australia, Belgium, Canada, Germany, Sweden, Estonia, the United States [83] and the United Kingdom.

          1.3.1.3.1.2. Con:
          "Cow protection gangs [84]", a form of religious vigilantes in India, have killed 45 people between 2012 and 2018.

        1.3.1.3.2. Pro:
        The Ku Klux Klan [85] has historically operated as a vigilante organization which has lynched African Americans.

      1.3.1.4. Pro:
      Members of minority groups often have lower levels of confidence in the police than non-members.

        1.3.1.4.1. Pro:
        A 5-year study [86] of England and Wales by the Office for National Statistics shows that in every one of the 5 years, a lower percentage of Black Caribbean people said they had confidence in their local police compared with White British people. Among the broad ethnic groups, a lower percentage of people with Mixed ethnicity said they had confidence in their local police compared with White people.

        1.3.1.4.2. Pro:
        According to a 2017 Gallup poll [87], 61 percent of white people report having a great deal of confidence in the police while only 45 and 30 percent of Latino and black people share the sentiment. It also reveals that the racial divide on this topic has widened in recent years.

      1.3.1.5. Con:
      Vigilantism can be used as an excuse to target minorities [88] rather than for true justice.

        1.3.1.5.1. Pro:
        -> See 1.3.1.3.1.2.

        1.3.1.5.2. Pro:
        Vigilante violence is on the rise in Indonesia [89] and members of Indonesia's religious and ethnic minorities have been victimized by the country's Sunni Muslim majority.

          1.3.1.5.2.1. Pro:
          The Islamic Defenders Front [90], a vigilante group in Indonesia which says their mission is to stamp out sinful acts and to protect the poor and marginalized, has been accused of attacking churches, raiding suspected communist conferences and waging assaults against minority religions.

        1.3.1.5.3. Pro:
        Members of vigilante groups with far-right views often terrorize and attack the Romani minority in Ukraine [91].

        1.3.1.5.4. Pro:
        Vigilante groups often raid houses of and attack people belonging to the LGBTQ+ community in Tanzania [92] and Nigeria [93].

        1.3.1.5.5. Pro:
        Vigilantism by moral policing entities and groups often target women and promote patriarchy in the society.

          1.3.1.5.5.1. Pro:
          Khap Panchayats [94] are quasi-judicial bodies in parts of India that pronounce harsh punishments based on age-old customs and traditions. Women [95] often find themselves victimized by the inequality of the Khap Panchayati verdict as they pass orders on questions of marriage, fraternization and other personal choices.

            1.3.1.5.5.1.1. Pro:
            Such councils, known as panchayats or jirgas, also exist in parts of Pakistan [96]. The Jirga law [97] has been reviled by activists for its role in enforcing honour killings, rape as retribution during tribal feuds, the exchange of girls to settle disputes, and the broader exclusion of women’s voices.

            1.3.1.5.5.1.2. Pro:
            Women are often beaten up in public [98] or raped [99] for being ‘characterless’ on the orders [100] of Khap panchayats.
