Discussion Name:
Is Cannibalism Ethically Permissible?

1. Thesis:
Human cannibalism is ethically permissible.

  1.1. Pro:
  There may be exceptional situations where cannibalism becomes necessary and thus justifiable.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    The rising human population has created an environmental emergency [1] by increasing the number of corpses, a problem that cannibalism solves.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      Cannibalism is unlikely to have a significant environmental impact due to its unlikely widespread acceptance.

        1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
        Taboos surrounding certain types of meat [2] may deter people from considering cannibalism as a viable option.

        1.1.1.1.2. Con:
        It is possible that once allowed, consuming human meat would gain traction for sustainability reasons. [3]

      1.1.1.2. Pro:
      Most burial techniques used today are not environmentally sustainable. [4]

        1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
        The global shortage of burial spaces [5] creates a crisis that ethical cannibalism could mitigate by reducing the number of bodies to be buried and the land used for them.

        1.1.1.2.2. Pro:
        Cremation takes a toll [6] on the environment as it significantly increases carbon emissions.

        1.1.1.2.3. Con:
        More sustainable alternatives [7] for recycling human bodies exist and should be chosen over cannibalism.

          1.1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
          Green burial methods [8], such as using natural burial shrouds [9] offer viable alternatives to cremation and traditional burial techniques.

          1.1.1.2.3.2. Con:
          Offering cannibalism as an additional alternative for those who prefer it would not harm the cause.

      1.1.1.3. Pro:
      Replacing burial with the consumption of corpses could promote sustainability [10] and improve quality of life.

        1.1.1.3.1. Con:
        The consumption of human bodies is likely to have negative psychological impacts on individuals due to the sentimental value [11] attached to human remains, resulting in reduced quality of life.

          1.1.1.3.1.1. Pro:
          The human body is considered a part of one's humanity [12], making it difficult to attach a fixed price to it.

          1.1.1.3.1.2. Con:
          The sentimental value associated with human bodies should not be prioritised over human life, which may depend on cannibalism in the absence of alternatives.

        1.1.1.3.2. Pro:
        Cannibalism may alleviate food scarcity [13] in societies, which would improve quality of life.

          1.1.1.3.2.1. Con:
          Allowing cannibalism to address food shortages among starving communities could diminish their motivation to create sustainable solutions for their food crises.

          1.1.1.3.2.2. Pro:
          The prohibition of cannibalism may have unjust consequences for impoverished individuals in societies where food is scarce, as it would restrict their only means of survival.

        1.1.1.3.3. Pro:
        Cannibalism would reduce the commercial pressure on the meat industry, which contributes significantly [14] to climate change.

          1.1.1.3.3.1. Con:
          Permitting cannibalism would merely add human meat as an option within the meat industry rather than reduce the pressure on it.

          1.1.1.3.3.2. Pro:
          The heavy contribution of livestock farming to greenhouse emissions [15] can be reduced by promoting the demand for and consumption of human meat as a replacement for other animals.

        1.1.1.3.4. Con:
        Looking to cannibalism as a solution to sustainability challenges would detract from the push for climate accountability.

          1.1.1.3.4.1. Con:
          Advocating cannibalism as a solution to climate change would shift the Overton window [16] towards more acceptable solutions to climate change.

          1.1.1.3.4.2. Pro:
          The political capital [17] available for climate change issues is extremely limited, as shown by the difficulty of passing climate protection policies.

      1.1.1.4. Con:
      Resorting to cannibalism cannot be justified by the problem of overpopulation, as other viable solutions exist.

        1.1.1.4.1. Pro:
        The world population is expected to reduce [18] organically by 1 billion by the end of the 21st century, and as such, there is no urgent need to combat overpopulation through cannibalism.

        1.1.1.4.2. Con:
        Overpopulation is a justified consideration [19] as it affects the quality of life for all people and hence must be addressed at all costs.

    1.1.2. Con:
    Ethics should be based on universal principles and not depend on specific situations.

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      Ethics need to be flexible [20] and adapt to new situations, as the factors that determine ethical status also change.

        1.1.2.1.1. Con:
        Allowing exceptions to ethical standards can lead to abuse and manipulation by those in power.

        1.1.2.1.2. Pro:
        The morality of actions might depend on many variables [21], such as intention, influences, and agency, all of which might vary even for the same action.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      A society cannot be formed around moral subjectivity due to the inherent flaws [22] in the idea.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        Most societies have had their moral standards change over time [23], and that has allowed them to progress beyond those that have been more conservative.

        1.1.2.2.2. Pro:
        Rather than allowing individuals to choose according to their own interests, societies must establish standards for ethical permissibility.

          1.1.2.2.2.1. Pro:
          Moral subjectivism leads to clashes [24] between individuals and groups as morality dictates how people should be allowed to interact with others.

          1.1.2.2.2.2. Pro:
          The establishment of codified laws [25] for governing a society relies on collective moral principles in the society.

          1.1.2.2.2.3. Con:
          Moral subjectivism [26] exists between groups that have different moral codes without causing chaos.

          1.1.2.2.2.4. Con:
          Society is unable to set ethical guidelines for every possible action. Individuals therefore must and do often develop personal moral codes. [27]

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Cannibalism may be justified in cases where it is necessary for human survival.

      1.1.3.1. Con:
      Survival and ethics are not always positively connected, as people often resort to unethical behaviours, such as stealing or murder, in order to survive. [28]

        1.1.3.1.1. Con:
        Humans frequently disregard established norms in times of extreme emergency.

          1.1.3.1.1.1. Con:
          In emergency situations, it would still be preferable to uphold ethics, even if it results in the deaths of some people.

            1.1.3.1.1.1.1. Pro:
            In society, we frequently sacrifice people for the benefit of the majority.

              1.1.3.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
              Soldiers are employed and encouraged [29] to put their lives on the line to protect the rest of their people.

              1.1.3.1.1.1.1.2. Pro:
              People are allowed to work dangerous jobs such as mining [30] to provide for the needs of the majority.

              1.1.3.1.1.1.1.3. Con:
              There have been efforts to reduce the need for others to sacrifice themselves for the public good. The use of drones [31] in warfare and machines [32] for dangerous jobs both prove this point.

              1.1.3.1.1.1.1.4. Con:
              Sacrifices are usually driven by personal ambitions for heroism. A similar laurel of heroism does not exist for cannibalism, and therefore, it cannot be compared to a soldier's sacrifice.

            1.1.3.1.1.1.2. Con:
            Sacrificing people for the benefit of the majority often leads to the marginalisation of minority groups.

              1.1.3.1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
              Poor, rural individuals who lack access to food are more likely to practise cannibalism, but their minority status may marginalise them in ethical considerations.

              1.1.3.1.1.1.2.2. Con:
              Some sacrifices can unite people [33] and create a shared sense of purpose, including among minority groups.

          1.1.3.1.1.2. Pro:
          Governments often invade [34] the privacy of ordinary citizens in times of national security emergencies.

            1.1.3.1.1.2.1. Con:
            Even in times of emergency, there are limits to what a government can do. For example, even during martial law [35], governments cannot abuse the fundamental rights of citizens.

            1.1.3.1.1.2.2. Pro:
            The suspension [36] of certain rules in desperate times allows society to survive crises.

          1.1.3.1.1.3. Con:
          While some norms could be discarded, there are others that stand regardless of the situation.

            1.1.3.1.1.3.1. Pro:
            Foundational norms that value life and dignity, for example, are very unlikely to be abandoned in emergencies.

            1.1.3.1.1.3.2. Con:
            The value of human life should not be placed below the value of ethics, which are malleable, subjective, and artificial. [37]

            1.1.3.1.1.3.3. Con:
            There is no objective mechanism to determine which norms are inviolable.

          1.1.3.1.1.4. Pro:
          In times of emergency, humans instinctively prioritise survival over following norms.

            1.1.3.1.1.4.1. Con:
            There are numerous records [38] of people helping others in times of crisis, even at the risk of their own lives.

        1.1.3.1.2. Pro:
        Violating someone's consent and right to life [39] by cannibalising them, even to ensure personal survival, is immoral.

      1.1.3.2. Pro:
      Human survival is the most important pursuit, under which all other considerations must fall.

        1.1.3.2.1. Pro:
        Human beings are biologically wired [40] to act in order to survive. Therefore, where cannibalism is the path to survival, human beings do not have a choice.

          1.1.3.2.1.1. Pro:
          Morality exists where there is choice [41], and hence an action cannot be wrong if the actor has no choice in the matter.

          1.1.3.2.1.2. Con:
          Human beings have the ability to choose [42] to die rather than undertake an immoral action.

        1.1.3.2.2. Pro:
        Life grants humans the opportunity to pursue all other things, and as such, life should have primacy and be sustained at all costs.

        1.1.3.2.3. Con:
        Living a moral life is more important than merely surviving.

          1.1.3.2.3.1. Pro:
          By putting criminals to death [43] for their immoral behaviour, society prioritises the preservation of moral values over life.

          1.1.3.2.3.2. Con:
          Some people may believe that life has a purpose rooted in objective end goals and that leading a moral life may not be their highest priority.

        1.1.3.2.4. Con:
        Even if human survival is prioritised, certain prohibitions that allow death can be allowed if some or most human beings survive in spite of them.

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      Humans may need to resort to cannibalism in dire circumstances, such as starvation.

        1.1.3.3.1. Pro:
        Humans have not evolved to withstand starvation.

          1.1.3.3.1.1. Con:
          Humans have evolved to go weeks [44] without eating.

          1.1.3.3.1.2. Pro:
          Not eating for prolonged periods can result [45] in organ-damaging malnutrition or, eventually, death.

        1.1.3.3.2. Pro:
        There have been instances in history where people resorted to cannibalism to avoid starvation.

          1.1.3.3.2.1. Pro:
          The survivors of Uruguayan Air Force Flight 571 [46] stayed alive for 72 days by eating the corpses of their fellow passengers.

          1.1.3.3.2.2. Con:
          In most cases, cannibalism is not the only option for survival. Efforts [47] should be made to find alternative sources of food or to provide aid to the affected populations.

          1.1.3.3.2.3. Con:
          Even in the most dire circumstances, people who engaged in cannibalism, such as the survivors of the Mignonette, were held accountable [48] by society.

          1.1.3.3.2.4. Pro:
          The survivors of the shipwrecked Mignonette [49] resorted to eating the youngest to stay alive.

          1.1.3.3.2.5. Pro:
          Historical documents show [50] that cooked human flesh was sold in English markets during an 11th-century famine.

        1.1.3.3.3. Con:
        Even in circumstances where cannibalism is necessary, it is preferable to use unanimously agreed-upon ethical standards rather than everyone reverting to their personal instincts.

          1.1.3.3.3.1. Con:
          Allowing exemptions for cannibalism is likely to lead to more exemptions and wider acceptance of the act.

          1.1.3.3.3.2. Pro:
          Having agreed-upon rules will likely minimise extreme behaviour and limit negative externalities.

    1.1.4. Con:
    The detrimental effects associated with the taboo [51] of cannibalism are likely to persist, even under exceptional situations.

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      Taboos are susceptible to evolving over time [52], and their existence can be contingent on specific contexts, thereby potentially negating the associated harms.

        1.1.4.1.1. Pro:
        Practices such as cohabitation before marriage and homosexuality were previously regarded as taboos [53] but are now widely accepted in modern times.

        1.1.4.1.2. Pro:
        Cannibalism being accepted and practised in certain societies [54] demonstrates that the taboo surrounding it is not intrinsic to human nature but rather a product of specific circumstances.

        1.1.4.1.3. Con:
        The public outcry over consensual cannibalism, such as the 2013 case [55] in Germany, demonstrates that it is still widely considered taboo.

      1.1.4.2. Pro:
      Most belief systems [56], by marking cannibalism as taboo, lead to the exclusion of its practitioners from religious communities.

        1.1.4.2.1. Pro:
        Given the widespread religious belief that humans were created in God's image [57], cannibalism is often deemed sacrilegious, potentially leading to ostracization.

          1.1.4.2.1.1. Pro:
          Engaging in cannibalism likely jeopardizes Christians' salvation by violating the commandments set forth in the divine commandments. [58]

            1.1.4.2.1.1.1. Pro:
            Christians believe that their bodies would be resurrected [59] at the second coming of Jesus.

              1.1.4.2.1.1.1.1. Con:
              Many have died as a result of events that obliterate the human body (e.g., fires, explosions, animal attacks). It is believed that the nature of these deaths does not [60] render those who died as such unfit for resurrection.

              1.1.4.2.1.1.1.2. Pro:
              As per Christian belief, if human bodies were consumed, there would be no body to resurrect.

            1.1.4.2.1.1.2. Con:
            In the Catholic Mass, bread and wine are transformed into the actual flesh and blood of Christ by way of transubstantiation [61] and then ingested by the congregation.

              1.1.4.2.1.1.2.1. Con:
              Jesus, according to the Bible, is not technically human [62] and therefore, consuming his flesh cannot count as cannibalism.

              1.1.4.2.1.1.2.2. Pro:
              The consumption of the consecrated bread and wine is seen as a sacred act [63] of communion with Christ and is considered to be one of the most important aspects of Catholic worship.

              1.1.4.2.1.1.2.3. Con:
              The consumption of the "flesh" and "blood" of Jesus in the Eucharist is merely symbolic. [64]

          1.1.4.2.1.2. Con:
          Many people [65] believe that God(s) do not exist, and therefore, to them, any such sacrilege means nothing.

        1.1.4.2.2. Con:
        Including atheism [66], certain belief systems might not expressly condemn cannibalism, thus not considering it inherently unethical, and would not result in any forms of exclusion.

          1.1.4.2.2.1. Pro:
          The notorious cannibal, Jeffrey Dahmer, rationalized his acts of murder and cannibalism via his atheistic convictions [67].

          1.1.4.2.2.2. Con:
          Atheists can still have individual reservations about cannibalism based on personal and societal moral standards. [68]

      1.1.4.3. Pro:
      The detrimental perception of cannibalism [69] within different communities, intensified by the prevailing taboo, ultimately inflicts harm on the deceased and their families.

        1.1.4.3.1. Con:
        It is unlikely that individuals who hold religious beliefs that prohibit cannibalism would agree to participate.

        1.1.4.3.2. Pro:
        According to numerous cultural beliefs, the act of consuming the deceased is believed to have adverse consequences for their afterlife [70].

          1.1.4.3.2.1. Pro:
          Certain cultures believe that the preparation and laying to rest of a person's body after death assists [71] the spirit in entering the afterlife. Being consumed would disrupt proper ritualistic burial practices and unsettle both believers and their families.

          1.1.4.3.2.2. Con:
          Funeral customs vary across cultures [72], where actions deemed harmful to the afterlife in one culture may be viewed as essential and suitable burial practices in another.

        1.1.4.3.3. Pro:
        Families who believe their loved ones died in sin, either through cannibalism or engaging in such acts, experience emotional distress due to their inability to change divine judgment [73].

        1.1.4.3.4. Con:
        Families and individuals who willingly embrace postmortem cannibalism are unlikely to be those who condemn or detest the practice; instead, they may perceive it as a celebratory act.

      1.1.4.4. Con:
      An action may be taboo, but that does not mean [74] it is unethical or illegal to partake in it in exceptional situations.

        1.1.4.4.1. Con:
        Taboos are often grounded in ethics [75].

          1.1.4.4.1.1. Pro:
          Food-related taboos often originate from ethical concerns for safety [76], serving to protect individuals [77] from potential harm or immoral actions.

          1.1.4.4.1.2. Pro:
          Certain taboos, such as against wastefulness or harming nature, align with environmental ethics [78], encouraging respect and care for our environment.

          1.1.4.4.1.3. Pro:
          Taboos that prevent trespassing or taking others' belongings without permission uphold ethical principles like respect for personal property and privacy [79].

          1.1.4.4.1.4. Con:
          Taboos can be highly subjective and specific to a particular culture or society, and may not align with universal ethical principles [80].

          1.1.4.4.1.5. Con:
          Some taboos, such as discouraging authority challenges [81], primarily maintain societal power structures rather than uphold ethical principles.

        1.1.4.4.2. Pro:
        Eating some forms of meat in some cultures, for example, may be considered taboo [82], but it isn't unethical to do so.

          1.1.4.4.2.1. Con:
          The emergence of cow vigilantism [83] in India implies a correlation between taboos and ethics.

          1.1.4.4.2.2. Pro:
          In India, consuming beef is considered taboo [84]; however, it is not illegal, and many individuals consume it without ethical contemplation.

          1.1.4.4.2.3. Pro:
          The Raw Paleo Diet [85] has faced backlash for its risks; however, it is not considered unethical.

  1.2. Con:
  Cannibalism devalues human beings.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    The acceptance and normalization of cannibalism will commodify [86] the human body.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      The demand for human meat is likely to increase the risk of vulnerable individuals becoming victims of human trafficking [87].

        1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
        A human trafficker would have an additional incentive [88] to engage in the trade of humans since they could sell people as meat instead of only selling them as forced labour.

        1.2.1.1.2. Pro:
        Trafficking for cannibalism may be more profitable than forced labour, as victims sold for meat don't require minimal sustenance [89].

        1.2.1.1.3. Con:
        As the demand for ethical meat [90] continues to grow, it is unlikely that consumers of human meat would be willing to consume trafficked meat.

          1.2.1.1.3.1. Con:
          If demand exceeds supply, trafficking may emerge as a method to fulfill that demand [91].

          1.2.1.1.3.2. Pro:
          People may feel guilty about consuming unethically sourced meat, akin to their aversion to using conflict minerals [92].

        1.2.1.1.4. Con:
        It is unlikely that cannibalism would be allowed without competent regulatory mechanisms to prevent human trafficking.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      The commodification [93] of the human body can create positive externalities [94].

        1.2.1.2.1. Pro:
        Pursuing top value for one's body in cannibalism may incentivize better self-care, as a healthy physical state likely increases appeal and worth.

          1.2.1.2.1.1. Con:
          The idea that cannibalism would result in better treatment of people is refuted by the way animals are treated in the meat industry. [95]

        1.2.1.2.2. Con:
        The commodification of the human body diminishes its reverence, equating the consumption of human flesh to that of animal meat.

        1.2.1.2.3. Pro:
        Families can sell corpses of their family members, akin to offering them for scientific research [96], so as to earn money.

      1.2.1.3. Con:
      Reasonable mechanisms can be developed to restrict the buying and selling of human bodies.

        1.2.1.3.1. Pro:
        The regulatory framework governing the trade of bodily organs [97] can serve as a model for managing the practice of cannibalism.

          1.2.1.3.1.1. Pro:
          In several jurisdictions, individuals have the option to consent [98] to donating their organs to those in need, and similarly, they should be able to choose to give their bodies for consumption after death.

          1.2.1.3.1.2. Con:
          Unlike organ donation, which is driven by altruistic motives [99] aimed at assisting those in need, cannibalism lacks a comparable incentive.

        1.2.1.3.2. Con:
        Enforcing laws against the trade of human bodies can be challenging [100], especially if the act is done clandestinely.

      1.2.1.4. Pro:
      The commodification of the human body causes individuals to view others merely as a means to an end [101] rather than as an end in themselves.

        1.2.1.4.1. Pro:
        Consumption of another organism denotes that what is consumed exists to serve what consumes it.

        1.2.1.4.2. Pro:
        Humans possess unique desires [102] and purpose, which are compromised when exploited for another's benefit.

          1.2.1.4.2.1. Pro:
          As per Maslow's hierarchy [103], food is the most basic need; using humans for sustenance demeans their value to the lowest level.

          1.2.1.4.2.2. Con:
          Numerous desires, like sex [104], necessitate interaction with others and are deemed acceptable when practised consensually.

        1.2.1.4.3. Con:
        If cannibalism is wrong because it exploits others, then it should be fine for one to consume oneself. [105]

          1.2.1.4.3.1. Pro:
          Self-cannibalism would be no different from using one's body as a means for survival, as is done in labour [106].

          1.2.1.4.3.2. Con:
          There is stigma against devaluing oneself even in pursuit of personal benefits.

            1.2.1.4.3.2.1. Pro:
            A stigma surrounds sex work [107], despite the potential benefits it may provide to the worker.

            1.2.1.4.3.2.2. Con:
            Societal stigmas erode over time [108] as a given act becomes more commonplace and finds greater acceptance in society.

        1.2.1.4.4. Con:
        Viewing human bodies as means to economic and political ends is morally permissible; thus, cannibalism should be similarly acceptable.

          1.2.1.4.4.1. Pro:
          Politicians and businessmen often view people as a means to power and profit, which is deemed acceptable since people also view them as a means to their own ends.

          1.2.1.4.4.2. Con:
          Customers and constituents benefit from those who seek to use them; however, in cannibalism, the individual being consumed derives no advantage from the act.

          1.2.1.4.4.3. Con:
          The fact that humans exploit one another in various ways does not imply that these methods, or any other, are ethical.

          1.2.1.4.4.4. Pro:
          A Tokyo chef offered his own severed genitals for consumption [109] at $250 per plate, and people willingly participated.

    1.2.2. Con:
    If a person consents to being consumed as an exercise of their free will [110], it cannot be considered devaluation.

      1.2.2.1. Pro:
      As autonomous beings, humans have the ability to consent to the act of cannibalism.

        1.2.2.1.1. Con:
        The right to bodily autonomy is not absolute [111], as there are limits to what actions individuals can consent to regarding themselves.

          1.2.2.1.1.1. Con:
          Bodily autonomy is considered a fundamental right [112] in many jurisdictions.

            1.2.2.1.1.1.1. Pro:
            The right to bodily autonomy is fundamental right because it facilitates other fundamental rights [113].

              1.2.2.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
              The freedom of thought, conscience, and religion [114] cannot be accessed without the right to bodily autonomy because many religious practices require the refusal to ingest certain substances into your body.

              1.2.2.1.1.1.1.2. Con:
              Other human rights can be accessed even in the absence of the right to bodily autonomy, therefore it cannot be said that the right to bodily autonomy is a basic or overarching right [115].

            1.2.2.1.1.1.2. Con:
            In most jurisdictions, limitations exist regarding bodily autonomy, even though it is recognized as a fundamental right.

          1.2.2.1.1.2. Pro:
          People should not be allowed to exercise their right to bodily autonomy if it causes harm to a non-consenting third-party [116].

            1.2.2.1.1.2.1. Pro:
            Public smoking is banned in many states because secondhand smoke harms [117] non-consenting bystanders.

            1.2.2.1.1.2.2. Con:
            Since no third party is harmed [118] during the act of cannibalism, it should be the prerogative of the consenting individuals involved to engage in the activity as they see fit.

          1.2.2.1.1.3. Con:
          As long as adults are engaging in a consensual act [119], it is their personal business and not that of the state.

          1.2.2.1.1.4. Pro:
          Parents or guardians have a responsibility [120] to ensure the welfare of their children, which may sometimes require overriding the child's bodily autonomy.

        1.2.2.1.2. Pro:
        People are rational [121] and therefore have a unique capacity to consent to being eaten.

          1.2.2.1.2.1. Pro:
          In some instances, cannibalism may align with an individual's best interests, rendering it as the most rational decision.

            1.2.2.1.2.1.1. Con:
            As a person cannot regret their decision to be cannibalized, they are unlikely to know their true feelings about the act.

            1.2.2.1.2.1.2. Pro:
            For some religious people [122], being consumed might be a rite they believe will benefit them in the afterlife.

          1.2.2.1.2.2. Con:
          Ethics cannot solely rely on an individual's ability to provide consent, as people are capable of consenting to immoral acts.

            1.2.2.1.2.2.1. Con:
            Consent makes an action done to someone ethically permissible because the consenting party is the one impacted by the action.

            1.2.2.1.2.2.2. Pro:
            While individuals have the capacity to consent to death by suicide [123], society generally considers suicide to be immoral.

              1.2.2.1.2.2.2.1. Pro:
              The illegality of assisted suicide [124] affirms that external actors, in this case the government, are right to prevent one from making decisions that negatively affect their bodies.

                1.2.2.1.2.2.2.1.1. Pro:
                Something that is illegal is right or wrong based on the law, while something that is unethical is based on the individual's or the collective's conscience.

                1.2.2.1.2.2.2.1.2. Con:
                Laws that do not conform to societal values are often repealed.

              1.2.2.1.2.2.2.2. Con:
              Assisted suicide is legal in many jurisdictions [125] now.

                1.2.2.1.2.2.2.2.1. Pro:
                Starting from March 2021, a broad range of citizens in Canada [126] can avail themselves of the government's assistance in dying.

                1.2.2.1.2.2.2.2.2. Con:
                The right to die, which applies only to the terminally ill [127], ensures that those who are sick are unlikely to be cannibalized.

      1.2.2.2. Con:
      The person providing consent may not be in a sound state of mind [128], thereby invalidating their consent.

        1.2.2.2.1. Con:
