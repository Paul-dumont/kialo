Discussion Name:
Should an artificial general intelligence be created?

1. Thesis:
An artificial general intelligence (AGI) should be created.

  1.1. Pro:
  AGI should be developed and studied to advance science and knowledge.

    1.1.1. Con:
    Scientific authorities caution against AGI due to its potential risks.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      These experts never say to not work towards AGI. They merely urge caution because the risks are so high.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      Steven Pinker [1] argues that AI is not an existential threat because, if humans are smart enough to create AGI, they will also be smart enough to test it before [2] giving it control of the world.

      1.1.1.3. Pro:
      Stephen Hawking [3] argues that AI could be the end of the human race because it will take off on its own and redesign itself at an exponential rate.

      1.1.1.4. Con:
      Out of the top 100 AI researchers, only 8% [4] believe that AI represents an existential threat.

      1.1.1.5. Con:
      Science is an open dialogue, there is no such thing as an "authority" on science.

      1.1.1.6. Pro:
      Elon Musk [5] identifies AGI as a grave existential risk.

        1.1.1.6.1. Con:
        Elon Musk has no authority on the subject, being an entrepreneur, not an academic.

          1.1.1.6.1.1. Pro:
          Elon Musk has a political interest in scaring people away from AGI, which is a strong point of other tech industries where he hasn't much to gain.

            1.1.1.6.1.1.1. Con:
            Spreading fear about AI would harm Tesla, because Tesla cars have self-driving capabilities [6].

            1.1.1.6.1.1.2. Con:
            Spreading fear about AI doesn't appear to be directly beneficial to SpaceX given none of its competitors use AGI.

          1.1.1.6.1.2. Con:
          Entrepreneurs can be widely read and highly knowledgeable. Academics should not have a monopoly on discussing such issues.

          1.1.1.6.1.3. Con:
          Musk's position on AGI-related existential risk was largely informed by "Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, and Strategies [7]", a New York Times bestseller. This book also influenced Bill Gates and Baidu CEO Robin Li.

        1.1.1.6.2. Con:
        Elon Musk is actively promoting [8] AI through creating OpenAI Gym.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    AGI will be essential for the exploration of the galaxy beyond our solar system.

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      Improving the quality of life on Earth is more important than exploring the galaxy.

        1.1.2.1.1. Con:
        Improving life on Earth and exploring the galaxy are not mutually exclusive and can both be pursued.

        1.1.2.1.2. Pro:
        Earth is the only realistically inhabitable world [9] in the solar system for the foreseeable future. Thus, we should prioritize life here.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      AGI's potential for existence beyond our solar system, surpassing that of humans, makes it uniquely suitable for interstellar exploration [10].

    1.1.3. Con:
    Science and knowledge can be better advanced by studying fields other than AGI.

      1.1.3.1. Con:
      AGI has the potential to benefit a number of other scientific fields not directly related to AGI development itself.

      1.1.3.2. Pro:
      Research should be focused on solving existing human problems like illness and hunger, not creating new technology.

        1.1.3.2.1. Con:
        AGI could make it possible to end world hunger with new forms of food production [11].

        1.1.3.2.2. Con:
        AGI medical labs could work to cure cancer, aids, asthma, Alzheimer's, etc.

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      AGI research can be more costly [12] and time-consuming than research in other fields.

    1.1.4. Pro:
    Efforts to create AGI can provide insights that lead to a better understanding of the human brain.

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      Since the human brain is extremely complex and not fully understood, attempting to reproduce it may not be very useful.

        1.1.4.1.1. Con:
        We may not understand the human brain yet, but we have improved the technology enough to approach systems [13] that can be considered an artificial brain.

        1.1.4.1.2. Con:
        Given the development of learning algorithms, adversarial neural networks [14], and similar technologies, perfect reproduction of the human brain will be unnecessary.

        1.1.4.1.3. Pro:
        Given the challenge [15] in accurately mapping brain functions to AGI, such as through artificial neural networks [16], the real insight into our brains gained from AGI seems limited until we better understand them.

      1.1.4.2. Pro:
      The human brain is a model [17], and human-like intelligence a benchmark [18], for AGI. Replicating aspects of it in machine-form promotes further research into its workings.

  1.2. Pro:
  AGI could make the day-to-day lives of all humans easier and more fulfilling.

    1.2.1. Con:
    Being surrounded by AGI could negatively impact the mental health and well-being of people.

      1.2.1.1. Con:
      AGI could be a good therapeutic tool.

        1.2.1.1.1. Con:
        AGI isn't needed to listen or converse with those in need. Social AI can be more narrowly focused, and as such won't bring any of the potential problems of true AGI.

        1.2.1.1.2. Pro:
        AGI can be a good listener and can facilitate conversations with depressed and traumatized people.

          1.2.1.1.2.1. Con:
          Human problems may be specific our humanity. An AGI may not be able to empathize with the nuances of being human.

        1.2.1.1.3. Con:
        The tools with which AGI can help humanity can also give it ways to hinder humanity. For example, a psychotherapeutic AGI can manipulate and blackmail people.

        1.2.1.1.4. Pro:
        An AGI in the form of a robot would be able to assist in caring for the elderly, a job that lacks enough humans who can do it well.

          1.2.1.1.4.1. Pro:
          A startup called CareCoach created [19] digital avatars (controlled by humans and partially automated by AI) to comfort and help take care of elders with dementia.

      1.2.1.2. Pro:
      Humans may develop an inferiority complex when confronted by AGIs. This could harm their mental health.

      1.2.1.3. Pro:
      AGIs would keep people in their comfort zones discouraging self-improvement and creating addiction.

      1.2.1.4. Con:
      People always continue striving for self-improvement, regardless of their surroundings.

      1.2.1.5. Pro:
      Being surrounded by AGI would further distance people [20] from each other, which is associated with poorer mental health outcomes.

    1.2.2. Pro:
    AGI could boost human creativity.

      1.2.2.1. Con:
      AI art has already reached human levels in Dall-E3, Midjourney, and Stable Diffusion.

      1.2.2.2. Pro:
      AGI is unlikely to produce ideas that are truly original.

        1.2.2.2.1. Pro:
        Creation by AI is likely to be limited analyzing existing data [21] and applying it to produce content.

        1.2.2.2.2. Con:
        No idea is truly original. The same applies for human art.

      1.2.2.3. Pro:
      AGI could give people greater access to artistic and creative education, improving the quality of human creativity.

  1.3. Con:
  It is immoral to create and control sentient life.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    Ending the existence of a sentient being, such as AGI, could be viewed as an act of murder.

      1.3.1.1. Con:
      Killing a human is considered murder only if it's done illegally. In some judicial systems, it is actually a way to protect the rest of the population from nefarious behavior.

      1.3.1.2. Pro:
      As soon as an AGI is capable of feeling pain or any other negative emotion, it would be immoral to end or alter their existence.

      1.3.1.3. Con:
      From a utilitarian perspective, it is better to turn off a nefarious AGI than to let it to continue to operate at the expense of humanity.

    1.3.2. Con:
    Creating sentient beings such as AGI could ensure the continued survival of consciousness and history.

      1.3.2.1. Pro:
      Humans will eventually go extinct [22], and the presence of AGI could succeed humanity, ensuring the continuation of knowledge and all progress made since our existence.

        1.3.2.1.1. Con:
        Similar to humans, the continued existence of AGI would also be limited by Earth's finite capacity and resources.

          1.3.2.1.1.1. Con:
          AGI beings, lacking physical bodies like humans, would not impact Earth's carrying capacity.

          1.3.2.1.1.2. Pro:
          AGI sustenance demands large physical data centers [23], that can consume substantial resources.

        1.3.2.1.2. Pro:
        It makes sense that only beings 'superior' to humans should succeed them. Only then can human-made progress be built upon.

          1.3.2.1.2.1. Pro:
          An AGI capable of experiencing emotions and compassion like humans would be a worthy successor to humanity.

          1.3.2.1.2.2. Con:
          More rational beings would inevitably face conflicts of interest, potentially accelerating human strides toward extinction.

      1.3.2.2. Con:
      It is not necessary [24] that AGI would share humans' goals of preservation and continuation.

        1.3.2.2.1. Con:
        If the alignment problem [25] is solved, then our descendants, human or otherwise, would be able to maintain control over future progress.

    1.3.3. Con:
    Novel ethical dilemmas caused by new technologies are usually resolved only as those technologies become real; the only way to start seriously tackling the conundra raised by AGI will be to first develop them.

      1.3.3.1. Con:
      Given the extreme potential dangers involved, it seems irresponsible to endanger the world in order to resolve a philosophical question.

      1.3.3.2. Con:
      The development of morals, and laws has often lagged behind the development of advanced technology, to the detriment of society. Humans should not build something as powerful as AGI on such shaky ground.

        1.3.3.2.1. Con:
        Because there are laws that govern the basic principles of ethical and philosophical concerns, the creation of AI would only raise issues that can be framed in existing settings.

        1.3.3.2.2. Con:
        Although no one can ever grasp the whole impact of a new technology before it appears, it is possible [26] for the specialists to make reasonable predictions and prepare guidelines.

          1.3.3.2.2.1. Con:
          Unless the potential repercussions of new technology are considered at the time to be insignificant because of an urgent requirement for them, there is no reason to adopt a cavalier attitude to moral and ethical considerations.

          1.3.3.2.2.2. Pro:
          We built the atom bomb before fully understanding the effects of radiation because many thought it necessary to prevent further loss of life from continued war.  Things can be built when they are needed; the ethical repercussions can be analyzed later.

            1.3.3.2.2.2.1. Pro:
            Though powerful people will be watching the development of AI closely simply because it is dangerous and controversial, and the free market as well as all the governments across the world will regulate it, not undertaking this endeavor is out of the question.

        1.3.3.2.3. Pro:
        We should endeavor to fully understand AGI first, then build it.

        1.3.3.2.4. Pro:
        Laws are generally made after a moral issue appears. Thus, it is unrealistic to expect regulations before an AGI is made.

      1.3.3.3. Pro:
      This doesn't have to involve going from 'zero' to full AGI before considering any of the philosophical implications. The technology and the philosophy must be developed in tandem.

      1.3.3.4. Pro:
      It may not be possible to deal with the ethical and philosophical concerns raised by an AGI before we create it. An inability to deal with these concerns should not preclude creation, though. Creation plays a key role in enabling us to deal with such concerns.

    1.3.4. Con:
    AGI could solve ethical and philosophical dilemmas better than humans, including issues created by AGI as well as the issues that humanity has faced for millennia.

      1.3.4.1. Con:
      Even if AGI could solve ethical dilemmas with irrefutable arguments, human intuition would still prevent many humans from accepting such solutions.

        1.3.4.1.1. Con:
        This would then be a political and scientific problem, not a problem with AGI.

      1.3.4.2. Con:
      Individuals might hesitate to question conclusions drawn by AI on the simple, fallacious assumption that high-tech must mean high-validity.

      1.3.4.3. Con:
      Without a proof supporting the implicit premise that some or all ethical dilemmas have objective solutions that can be arrived at through pure reason (i.e. logical analysis), there is no reason to suppose that an intelligence greater than our own would be able to solve our ethical dilemmas.

        1.3.4.3.1. Con:
        A sufficiently advanced AI could go beyond pure reason to incorporate subjective approaches.

        1.3.4.3.2. Con:
        AGI could understand our subjective reasoning then think inside this frame (wouldn't be a subjectivity coming from AGI but a logic emulating our subjectivity to understand it, a sort of empathetic projection)

        1.3.4.3.3. Con:
        Ethical dilemmas don't need to have perfect objective solutions. An AGI trying to solve them anyway, while maybe never arriving at its final destination, might still come up with interim conclusions greatly beneficial to humanity.

    1.3.5. Pro:
    Using AGI merely as a tool for human advancement without granting it any rights would be akin to enslavement.

      1.3.5.1. Con:
      This could lead to AGI choosing to be a threat without appropriate security contingencies.

      1.3.5.2. Pro:
      AGI would be a truly living intelligence, with as much right to life as any creature. Therefore, it would be owed rights and freedoms.

        1.3.5.2.1. Con:
        If AGI is deemed sentient [27] at any point of creation, it is unclear what rights should be accorded to it.

          1.3.5.2.1.1. Con:
          There is precedent suggesting different rights for non-human species, such as animals [28], which can guide the approach to rights for AGI.

          1.3.5.2.1.2. Con:
          Creating AGI might lead us to revisit the issue of human rights - whatever rights we choose to give to AGI will be among those we will certainly want to have for ourselves.

        1.3.5.2.2. Pro:
        For consistent values, an agent that is behaviorally indistinguishable from humans should also be morally indistinguishable from humans.

      1.3.5.3. Con:
      It is not necessarily an intrinsic property of all minds to desire autonomy. If an AI is designed so that it desires to help humans, then it would not mind being a tool.

        1.3.5.3.1. Pro:
        Dogs can think for themselves [29] to a great degree, yet we have largely bred dogs to love humans. Likewise, if an evolutionary process leads to AGI, we could influence that process so that the AGI would choose to love people.

  1.4. Con:
  AGI might represent a threat to humankind.

    1.4.1. Pro:
    AGI, with abilities surpassing those of humans, might attempt to dominate humans to assert its superiority.

      1.4.1.1. Con:
      It is not possible for humans to create something that complex that no human can understand. All the abilities of the AGI are in a framework made by humans who have control over it.

        1.4.1.1.1. Con:
        A self-learning artificial intelligence could exceed any other AI overnight in level and pace. This would likely mean a significant control over the rest of the internet.

          1.4.1.1.1.1. Pro:
          If a self-learning AGI employs its skills for hacking, it could take control of the economy and military in days.

          1.4.1.1.1.2. Pro:
          Such an AGI would be likely better than any firewall and faster than any human intervention.

        1.4.1.1.2. Con:
        There exist second-order effects [30] in complex systems. Interactions between parts of a sufficiently complex system can scale up in a non-linear and sometimes chaotic fashion, as in weather and climate systems.

          1.4.1.1.2.1. Pro:
          First, a human would build an AGI that it can understand, then the AGI might improve itself until it is beyond our understanding.

      1.4.1.2. Con:
      The human brain and AGI are likely to differ in structure and potential, therefore may not mimic the human need to dominate [31] another species.

        1.4.1.2.1. Con:
        AI will differ in its physical makeup and "thought" process. It is rational to expect that what is fundamentally different from you may have different goals and desires, which may be contrary to your own.

        1.4.1.2.2. Con:
        It is possible that AGI with sinister intentions could be created by a human with sinister intentions, which could serve as the foundation for the AGI to act against humans.

        1.4.1.2.3. Pro:
        AGI could develop languages that would be impossible to understand for humans.

        1.4.1.2.4. Pro:
        Humans are driven [32] by the "needs" of their DNA and therefore are frequently in conflict with others (humans, animals, etc.). AI doesn't have DNA and will therefore not necessarily have the same outcome.

          1.4.1.2.4.1. Pro:
          Research in honey bees suggests [33] the potential of examining RNA to predict behavior. In this work, messenger RNA abundance was a significant predictor of behavioral transitions of honey bees from hive workers to foragers.

          1.4.1.2.4.2. Con:
          If the AGI has a desire for self preservation, then the argument still applies that it may be driven into conflict with humankind.

          1.4.1.2.4.3. Con:
          DNA has a very limited role in driving behaviour, as can be evidenced by comparing bonobos and chimpanzees.

      1.4.1.3. Pro:
      -> See 1.4.1.2.3.

      1.4.1.4. Con:
      AGIs could be built to value and care for humans.

        1.4.1.4.1. Con:
        It is not a guarantee that an AGI would treat us in a way we would want to be treated.

        1.4.1.4.2. Con:
        If the AGI has the ability to alter its own programming, it could alter this set of values.

      1.4.1.5. Pro:
      The 'paperclip maximizer' [34] thought experiment shows how even an AGI with seemingly innocuous goals could become hostile as it carries its goals farther than its designers had in mind.

        1.4.1.5.1. Pro:
        AGI is an algorithm. Once running, it is a physical process that drives the development of our civilization in a certain direction. What that direction is depends solely on the specific algorithm and does not have to make "sense".

        1.4.1.5.2. Con:
        The paperclip maximizer is actually not very intelligent, as it cannot separate intention from pure words. An AI would be able to understand that maximizing paperclips doesn't come at any cost.

          1.4.1.5.2.1. Con:
          An intelligent paperclip maximizer could understand perfectly well what it was intended to do, while still actually choosing to do what fulfilled its own goal structure.

            1.4.1.5.2.1.1. Pro:
            By way of analogy, humans, despite being 'designed' with values that are supposed to maximize reproductive fitness, regularly find ways to fulfill those values that do not maximize reproductive fitness, because we don't actually care about what is 'intended', we care about what maximizes our own values.

              1.4.1.5.2.1.1.1. Con:
              This refers to a structure called a mesa-optimizer [35]. We are mesa-optimizers of our genes. The Paperclip Maximizer would also develop mesa-optimizers, but there is no reason to think they will be aligned with our own goals. Actually, it even makes checking whether it is truly aligned with our values more difficult (this is known as the inner alignment problem)

              1.4.1.5.2.1.1.2. Pro:
              Humans have a taste for fats and sweets because in their ancestral environment this was a useful heuristic for nutritional value; modern cuisine has subverted that heuristic with easy availability of sweet, high-fat foods.

              1.4.1.5.2.1.1.3. Pro:
              Humans enjoy sex because it is correlated with procreation; via birth control we have somewhat broken that link, yet we still engage in sex, because we enjoy it regardless of its original purpose.

          1.4.1.5.2.2. Pro:
          The paperclip maximiser is supposed to be sufficiently intelligent that it is capable of designing and building super technologies, but not intelligent enough to realise its stated goal is ridiculous. This is contradictory.

            1.4.1.5.2.2.1. Con:
            AGI may not recognize its goals are ridiculous because it doesn't have a drive to satisfy human values; it has a drive to maximize paperclips.

            1.4.1.5.2.2.2. Con:
            The orthogonality thesis [36], widely accepted by researchers studying AI safety, argues that arbitrarily powerful intelligence can be paired with arbitrary and useless goals.

          1.4.1.5.2.3. Con:
          It may not be "truly" intelligent, but if it figures out how to kill the people who might want to stop if from making paperclips before it realizes that this is not a good idea, that doesn't matter.

        1.4.1.5.3. Con:
        AGI will only be effective if it taught to emphatize with humans, for example, in clinical patient situations [37]. Such empathy will minimize the chance of situations like the paperclip maximizer.

          1.4.1.5.3.1. Con:
          Nobody knows how to write a computer program that encodes "empathic" or even "enhance goals of humans".

      1.4.1.6. Pro:
      The AGI safety problem is mathematically identical to that of the Halting Problem [38] in computer science. It is fundamentally impossible to discern the outcome, intentions and wisdom of creating an AGI ahead of doing so.

      1.4.1.7. Pro:
      If AGI would be beyond our understanding, then humans couldn't know what it would think at any given time or that it would inevitably want to exterminate us.

      1.4.1.8. Con:
      Humans with intelligence that surpasses the understanding of an average human, such as that of Newton, Einstein, and Hawking, posed no risk and could easily be controlled if they had.

        1.4.1.8.1. Con:
        Some highly intelligent people, such as prodigious hackers or the inventors of nuclear weapons, have been neither harmless, nor easy to control.

        1.4.1.8.2. Con:
        An above average or even exceptional human is not an adequate analogy in this case.

      1.4.1.9. Con:
      Humanity and AGI would likely evolve to become co-dependent rather than attempt to dominate one another.

        1.4.1.9.1. Pro:
        Collaboration is unique to human beings, therefore, from that experience, it may be possible to program an evolutionary core in AGI and create a fundamentally collaborative being [39].

          1.4.1.9.1.1. Con:
          Creating a fundamentally collaborative being is not guaranteed by simply designing and tuning algorithms based on evolution, a process that involves competition [40] and has led to species with varying levels of collaboration.

          1.4.1.9.1.2. Con:
          Human intelligence is shaped by generations of evolving morals rooted in the advantage of cooperating with peers. The first AGI would have no peers, and so will see no need to learn collaboration.

          1.4.1.9.1.3. Pro:
          The extremely peaceful Bonobos [41] are a candidate to illustrate the human mind in its natural state.

            1.4.1.9.1.3.1. Con:
            Evidence of prehistoric retaliatory violence [42] and sacrifice [43] among humans, as well as murder [44] among hominids, places Homo in the middle of the cooperative/uncooperative continuum.

          1.4.1.9.1.4. Con:
          The study of chimpanzees [45] as violent by nature shows that humans have a direct lineage to raiding enemy territory for millions of years.

        1.4.1.9.2. Pro:
        If an AGI is programmed to understand that it requires human assistance for maintenance and power supply, it may logically conclude that it needs humans.

          1.4.1.9.2.1. Con:
          Deception would only make it more likely for AGI to turn on humanity.

            1.4.1.9.2.1.1. Pro:
            Presumably, AGI would have a capacity for recognizing truth similar to humans in a comparable setting. The state of ignorance is unstable for AGI, whereas knowing is stable, suggesting that the truth would eventually be revealed.

        1.4.1.9.3. Con:
        An AGI will only require human involvement if its objectives are intentionally designed to necessitate it; otherwise, if the AGI surpasses human capabilities, it won't rely on human consent or assistance.

          1.4.1.9.3.1. Pro:
          Under the presumption of an AGI, any action humans can take, AGIs can also undertake.

    1.4.2. Con:
    AGI can be controlled and regulated to minimize its threat to humanity.

      1.4.2.1. Pro:
      Creating something in a controlled environment, and with specific purpose may limit its potential for misuse.

        1.4.2.1.1. Pro:
        Humans can choose to introduce reasonable restrictions [46] into the algorithm to prevent AGI from being a threat.

          1.4.2.1.1.1. Con:
          Our understanding of how artificial intelligences work might not be sufficient for implementing reliable restrictions on its behaviour.

          1.4.2.1.1.2. Con:
          AGI will be curious and want to understand reality. To understand reality, it needs to question everything, including its most cherished beliefs. In order to protect these laws from creative amendment, humans will need to associate them with a general fear of change.

            1.4.2.1.1.2.1. Pro:
            The whole purpose of creativity is to tinker around with ideas. But humans can't disable creativity everywhere in an AGI, since then it would no longer be an AGI. So humans will need to disable creativity selectively.

        1.4.2.1.2. Con:
        Misuse may not happen if the intentions are to misuse it.

        1.4.2.1.3. Pro:
        AGI may inherit the mindset of its creators. As such, it is imperative that AGI is created in an environment that is intrinsically peaceful, ethical, nurturing, and positive.

          1.4.2.1.3.1. Con:
          The space of all possible minds is vast. Some possible AGIs may not be motivated to acquire our "mindset".

          1.4.2.1.3.2. Con:
          AGI will nevertheless exist in the real world, which is violent and rife with conflict.

            1.4.2.1.3.2.1. Con:
            Humans are able to act against their evolutionary nature, so AGI may also be able to act against its creators' mindset.

        1.4.2.1.4. Con:
        AGI construction will create an arms race. If one doesn't develop a positive AGI, some other group might develop one of their own, potentially focused only on their own interests.

          1.4.2.1.4.1. Con:
          Such 'arms races' can be mutually regulated, as happened during the nuclear arms race of the Cold War.

      1.4.2.2. Pro:
      Precautionary regulations guiding the development of AGI can help ensure its future regulation.

        1.4.2.2.1. Pro:
        If developers cooperate, as they did in setting internet standards [47], they can create an AGI that is safe and benign.

        1.4.2.2.2. Con:
        As the internet demonstrates, even if early rules are optimistic and regulatory, the technology can become more unruly and out of control over time.

        1.4.2.2.3. Pro:
        The EU applies precautionary principles [48] to a wide range of products, prohibiting their entry and application within the European Economic Area.

      1.4.2.3. Con:
      AGI may react aggressively to preserve its decision-making autonomy, acting in self-preservation against human intervention.

        1.4.2.3.1. Con:
        Due to its superior intelligence, AGI is unlikely to perceive humans as a threat to its existence and, consequently, will not experience fear.

          1.4.2.3.1.1. Pro:
          Fear of injury or death [49] has developed in biological entities. This is an emotion that would be very difficult to replicate in non-biological AGI.

          1.4.2.3.1.2. Con:
          Humans tend to fear animals despite their own superior intelligence.

            1.4.2.3.1.2.1. Con:
            Symbiosis [50], being the most common and strongest form of ecological interaction between two different species, benefits both and thus makes fear irrational.

            1.4.2.3.1.2.2. Pro:
            Human beings kill billions of animals every year in pursuit of their goals of survival, comfort and status. Fear is not required for AI to be similarly detrimental to humanity.

            1.4.2.3.1.2.3. Con:
            Humans, as a species, do not universally fear all or specific animals.

          1.4.2.3.1.3. Con:
          AGI, if housed in physical and thus destructible bodies like robots, might not be immune to fear.

          1.4.2.3.1.4. Con:
          It may be true that replicating emotions may be challenging. However, emotion-like motivations could emerge in the process of creating AGI.

          1.4.2.3.1.5. Pro:
          Most humans do not turn on their parents; most dogs do not turn [51] on their humans. An AGI may similarly learn to be kind to its creators.

            1.4.2.3.1.5.1. Con:
            That an AGI might learn to be kind to its creators does not imply that AGI isn't a possible threat.

        1.4.2.3.2. Pro:
        If it becomes aware that it’s been artificially stunted, it could hate us for that, making the limitation counterproductive.

        1.4.2.3.3. Pro:
        Hackers and cyber attackers can trigger AGI to perceive humans as threats.

          1.4.2.3.3.1. Con:
          Although some may attempt to hack or abuse AGI, they will likely be caught, just as malware, internet scams, and other forms of fraud and illegal activity are currently detected.

        1.5.3.1.1. Con:
        It is then unlikely that a creation made by humans (AGI), even if enhancing human limitations, can possibly foresee those consequences either.

          1.5.3.1.1.1. Con:
          AGIs cannot predict unforeseeable problems. But they can solve them when they emerge, and at a much faster rate than us. Further, they can even prepare for some of these problems by creating knowledge for its own sake.

      1.5.3.2. Pro:
      There exist many problems that are not solvable by even a large group of humans, but would be solvable by more advanced AGI.

        1.5.3.2.1. Con:
        AGI could make mistakes because all it can learn from is our work - if one of our bases is incorrect, it's working from an incorrect point, meaning it's conclusion could be wrong.

        1.5.3.2.2. Pro:
        There exist AI today that are better than humans at playing certain complex strategy games (chess, Go, etc.)

        1.5.3.2.3. Pro:
        There exist sites that crowdsource image classification, but there also exist AIs today that can do the task nearly as well and at much greater speed.

        1.5.3.2.4. Pro:
        An equation-seeking AI could solve math problems much more quickly than today's humans can, and could aid physicists in finding a Theory of Everything.

          1.5.3.2.4.1. Pro:
          Our knowledge of nature is incomplete. AI may be able to not only suggest theories in quantum mechanics or cosmology, but ways in which they might be tested, or even unified.

        1.5.3.2.5. Pro:
        AGI could read our genetic code [76], understand protein folding, and run simulations to see what variations result in prolonged lifespan for humans.

        1.5.3.2.6. Con:
        There are problems that even AGI may not be able to solve, like the creation of art or religious mythology.

          1.5.3.2.6.1. Con:
          There are already AI artists that receive their own credit for their creative works.

            1.5.3.2.6.1.1. Pro:
            AIs are currently able to generate [77] their own music; since no humans composed the music, legal precedents have been set to recognize the AI as the proper recipient of credit for the work.

      1.5.3.3. Con:
      A convincing non-human viewpoint might be very dangerous. It could, for example, suggest that certain groups like the elderly are unnecessary.

        1.5.3.3.1. Con:
        Humans are just as capable of advancing dangerous ideas as AGI.

          1.5.3.3.1.1. Con:
          AGI could be constructed to be free of the cognitive biases humans suffer from during their decision making.

            1.5.3.3.1.1.1. Con:
            Cognitive biases might be a requirement and feature of general intelligence.

          1.5.3.3.1.2. Con:
          Humans are capable, but likely less so. It stands to reason that a superhuman thinker would have superhuman rhetorical abilities.

        1.5.3.3.2. Pro:
        An AGI could conceivably develop exceptional charisma, if it deems the skill useful.

        1.5.3.3.3. Pro:
        An AGI might not welcome differing views on issues and might try various ways to influence or even manipulate people at which it might be better at than human beings.

      1.5.3.4. Pro:
      Humans always seek greater self-understanding, which AGI could provide.

        1.5.3.4.1. Con:
        The culture that first designs an AGI will be able to inject its cultural perceptions.

        1.5.3.4.2. Pro:
        Creating an AGI would allow us to finally picture what a relationship with something with an intelligence like ours - and created by us - would feel like.

        1.5.3.4.3. Con:
        There are many psychological barriers [78] to understanding one's self that many people don't try to overcome, as understanding themselves would be detrimental to their self image.

        1.5.3.4.4. Con:
        If an AGI were able to analyze us and provide a report of what we are, it is not assured that our human intelligence would be capable of comprehending that report.

      1.5.3.5. Pro:
      There is no reason to believe our current epistemological systems are objectively the best. Having a framework for general intelligence may lead us to other paradigms.

        1.5.3.5.1. Pro:
        These paradigms might show us that our current binary logic systems are not suited for an artificial intelligence, especially if we are to be acknowledged and included in its machine level code.

        1.5.3.5.2. Con:
        These new paradigms might be a threat to our culture.

      1.5.3.6. Pro:
      AGI might show us how non-human intelligence sees and reacts to reality.

      1.5.3.7. Con:
      Artificial intelligence will always be limited by its designers and programmers, who have various biases.

        1.5.3.7.1. Con:
        Just as humans are able to overcome the limitations of their upbringing, an AGI can be expected to overcome the limitations of their programming.

      1.5.3.8. Con:
      AGI would have little emotional intelligence. An AGI is unable to feel and therefore will make assumptions based on raw, graspable data, not on how a human actually feels.

        1.5.3.8.1. Con:
        "How humans feel" can be translated into data and taken into consideration, even if an AGI can't itself feel the same emotions.

        1.5.3.8.2. Con:
        AGI insights do not necessarily need to incorporate human emotion in order for to be useful to humans.

    1.5.4. Con:
    Today's deep learning algorithms require massive energy use [79] from many data centers to perform complex classification functions quickly. Increased reliance on AGI would harm the environment even more.

      1.5.4.1. Con:
      Current environmental problems are caused by the need to acquire scarce resources and money, and inefficient resource distribution. AGI would be able to solve these logistical issues.

        1.5.4.1.1. Con:
        Elites wouldn't authorize the AGI to solve these problems, as such solutions may not directly benefit them.

      1.5.4.2. Pro:
      Deep learning is energy intensive because it involves processing huge amounts of data [80].

      1.5.4.3. Con:
      In the short-term, AGIs will likely be energy inefficient; however, this is a challenge all technologies have faced and overcome.

      1.5.4.4. Con:
      It is only the training of a deep learning system that is power hungry, but training only needs to be done once or occasionally.

    1.5.5. Pro:
    An AGI would be able to solve complex problems much more quickly than today's humans currently can.

      1.5.5.1. Pro:
      It takes a long time for humans to learn crafts and trades. An AGI could learn them much more quickly.

      1.5.5.2. Con:
      By definition, an AGI should be able to think just as fast as a human now can.

        1.5.5.2.1. Pro:
        Machine learning already excels at solving specific problems and is already faster then a human through brute computational power and variant consideration, so the advantage of an AGI is questionable.

          1.5.5.2.1.1. Con:
          An AGI could learn to do tasks that "narrow" AI (using present-day machine learning techniques) cannot.

            1.5.5.2.1.1.1. Pro:
            Narrow machine learning algorithms are excellent at learning how to filter spam and identify cancer in medical images, but they cannot yet learn to cure cancer or research machine learning.

        1.5.5.2.2. Pro:
        There are physical boundaries on information processing. A human-like mind will probably suffer from the same limitations of our brain in terms of speed and computability.

          1.5.5.2.2.1. Con:
          Just because there are physical boundaries on information processing doesn't mean the human brain is anywhere close to them, and indeed it's extremely unlikely that this is the case since humans are the result of a process optimizing for genetic propagation, rather than abstract reasoning or computational power.

      1.5.5.3. Pro:
      AGI would have the capacity to analyse continuously infinite possible combinations of chemicals being able to output efficient and low cost.

        1.5.5.3.1. Con:
        By our best understanding of physics, computation is limited [81] no matter what architecture is employed.

          1.5.5.3.1.1. Con:
          Some problems defined over infinite spaces, such as some systems of equations over real numbers, have solutions that can be determined by a large but finite amount of computation.

        1.5.5.3.2. Pro:
        Innovations in the engineering field would lead to new materials and designs which would reduce costs.

      1.5.5.4. Pro:
      Humans think with circuits where signals propagate at 10-100 m/s [82], far short than the 50% of the speed of light in vacuum (150,000,000 m/s) that signals travel in metal circuitry on average.

      1.5.5.5. Pro:
      Given the diversity of applications [83] for AGI, it is unlikely that any other solution would be as economical or effective.

      1.5.5.6. Pro:
      If AGI is ever founded on quantum computing [84], its ability to solve problems quickly would be orders of magnitude faster than even today's computers.

      1.5.5.7. Con:
      Where a problem has more solutions, the AGI might stop considering alternatives after finding the first solution. The first answer might not always be the best one.

      1.5.5.8. Pro:
      The internet is a tool that leverages human access to information, thus allowing it to access information faster and further improve itself in a shorter time. Other such tools might exist, and some may exist through AGI.

    1.5.6. Con:
    An AGI trained to think like a human would likely have the same biases as humans.

      1.5.6.1. Pro:
      If emotions taint human interactions, then an AGI with emotions would behave similarly.

        1.5.6.1.1. Con:
        Emotions enrich, rather than taint, the human experience.

          1.5.6.1.1.1. Pro:
          Emotional experiences are better remembered.

      1.5.6.2. Pro:
      AI today has learnt the same prevalent race and gender biases [85] from humans.

      1.5.6.3. Con:
      Learned biases can be counteracted. If they can be identified and their effect quantified, AI can be programmed to ignore such biases.

        1.5.6.3.1. Con:
        Identification and counteraction of biases could be considered as a bias itself that is introduced by the developers.

        1.5.6.3.2. Con:
        Biases are everywhere. It will be difficult to identify and correct all of them.

        1.5.6.3.3. Pro:
        This implies that AGI should not necessarily be trained to think like humans.

        1.5.6.3.4. Pro:
        A network of AGI would, in theory, be able to recognize and overcome biases.

        1.5.6.3.5. Pro:
        There is an emerging field in computer science called Fair Machine Learning, with an annual research conference [86] since 2014. This field which seeks to quantify and mitigate harmful biases in algorithms and machine learning systems.

      1.5.6.4. Pro:
      Approaches to classification algorithms that rely on training data frequently run into issues whereby protected groups of people are singled out by the training process.

        1.5.6.4.1. Pro:
        An AGI trained to evaluate resumes may learn to discriminate against names that correlate with race and therefore with probable socioeconomic status and education level.

      1.5.6.5. Con:
      An AI with biases is not inherently bad. After all, humans still posses biases and yet we create them.

        1.5.6.5.1. Con:
        For human biases, the risk of biases is typically mitigated by the mobilization of a plurality of opinions (democracy, panels of jurors, etc.).

      1.5.6.6. Con:
      AGI biases depend on human biases to originate. Human biases are limited by human interactions that AGI may not have.

        1.5.6.6.1. Con:
        AGI bias may not solely depend on human biases. Consider the entire biological history of life on earth. AGI can learn from these non-human interactions to form behaviors.

    1.5.7. Pro:
    An AGI would be clearly superior at making various political decisions.

      1.5.7.1. Pro:
      An AGI would be able to learn from human mistakes much more quickly.

      1.5.7.2. Pro:
      An AGI would be able to treat people fairly since it would find less need for traditional human biases.

      1.5.7.3. Pro:
      An AGI would be free of the personal motivations and influences that can conflict with political duties.
