Discussion Name:
Is Having Children Selfish?

1. Thesis:
Having a Child is Selfish.

  1.1. Pro:
  There can be a selfish economic motivation behind having children.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Child labor is still allowed [1] in many countries. Children can work on their parents' farms or even generate income for the family as apprentices or auxiliary workers elsewhere.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      Many people have children precisely for the purpose of free labour [2] and encourage them to start working from an early age to increase family income.

    1.1.2. Con:
    The economic side of having kids can also be the opposite of selfish since raising kids is expensive [3] and usually brings with it a decades-long financial burden.

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      Life, or living in general as an adult is dominated by financial burdens and debts. The cost of living [4] has skyrocketed in many countries across the world. It is generous not to impose financial troubles on other people and childlessness achieves that.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      Not having a child could be seen as selfish because you are hoarding everything you earn to be spent on yourself.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        It cannot be said with surety that the child will benefit by being born. Since it is an action which involves a considerable number of risks for the child, a person not willing to have children might simply be risk-averse instead of selfish.

        1.1.2.2.2. Pro:
        Having a child is quite expensive. [5] If one has the means to provide for a child but refuses to in order to accrue their wealth for themselves, that is selfish.

          1.1.2.2.2.1. Pro:
          The amount of work and sacrifice that parents put into earning the amount of money [6] needed to raise a child and not spending on themselves indicates a selfless choice.

          1.1.2.2.2.2. Con:
          Spending one's own money on oneself is not being selfish; it is simply one's reward for having worked hard to earn and save up that money.

            1.1.2.2.2.2.1. Pro:
            Any financial obligations an individual may have had to society is paid off through taxes, thus said person has the right to enjoy the use of the money that remains.

              1.1.2.2.2.2.1.1. Con:
              In addition to taxes, externalities [7] such as global environmental burden must be taken into account before one can say their individual obligations have been paid.

          1.1.2.2.2.3. Con:
          This appears to imply that wealthier families are obligated to have children, whilst poorer families should not, which would perpetuate the wealth divide by creating a double standard. Either it is selfish in the absolute, or it is not.

        1.1.2.2.3. Con:
        It is not selfish to "hoard" your wealth instead of having a child if the wealth you have is not enough to comfortably provide for the child.

          1.1.2.2.3.1. Pro:
          By not having children, one leaves them without the discomfort of not being adequately provided for.

        1.1.2.2.4. Con:
        This assumes that money will be kept for personal gain, but it could be spent on charity, friends, family, etc. Children are not the only option.

          1.1.2.2.4.1. Pro:
          In fact, giving to one's own child is more selfish than giving to a total stranger in need, because you get some reward from the first action but none from the second.

            1.1.2.2.4.1.1. Con:
            Giving a similar resource to one's own child may have proportionally more impact than giving to a stranger in need because there is a greater personal relationship between a parent and child. Leveraging resources where they will make the most difference is not selfish, but rational.

              1.1.2.2.4.1.1.1. Pro:
              A parent can ensure that the resource is most effectively used if it is given to their own child. They have no such control over the actions of a stranger.

        1.1.2.2.5. Pro:
        It is fundamentally self-obsessed to spend your life only focused on your own interests without giving back to society in the form of having children.

          1.1.2.2.5.1. Con:
          There are ways to give back to society [8] without having children, as having children may actually run the risk of harming society.

    1.1.3. Con:
    Having children is a way of supporting the economical pillars of many developed countries around the world that have a substantial shortage of workers.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      In many developed countries as well as possibly some developing countries, there is a rising problem [9] of demographic transitions [10].

      1.1.3.2. Pro:
      In many [11] developed [12] countries [13], there is a shortage of workers and this concerns many diverse industries of them.

      1.1.3.3. Con:
      The economies of many developed countries seek workers in industries that are highly suboptimal in terms of efficiency and/or are relatively harmful to people, especially future generations. Examples of such may include the car industry [14] or tourism air travel [15]. Concerning such industries, there are calls to inhibit and downscale their economic activities.

      1.1.3.4. Con:
      There are many alternatives to having children to meet these demands (labor demand reduction methods [16]) including: migration, automation, selective removal of jobs, sustainable design, and increases of efficiency and productivity (e.g. via digitalization).

    1.1.4. Pro:
    In many developing countries, parents have children so that the children will support them in their old age [17].

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      The number of children born per woman tends to be much higher in poorer countries [18].

      1.1.4.2. Con:
      To expect one's child to care for one in old age is not selfish, but an understood social norm, much like it is expected that a parent will care for a child while they need it.

        1.1.4.2.1. Con:
        The parent must care for the child in its young age because it is a child and was brought into this world due to their parents' decisions. The child does not owe the parents anything since it did not choose or consent to being cared for.

        1.1.4.2.2. Con:
        The fact that care from your children is a socially accepted norm in most cultures does not mitigate any selfishness involved in expecting that from them.

          1.1.4.2.2.1. Pro:
          Caring for elderly parents is often extremely stressful [19] for children and can become a drain on their resources.

      1.1.4.3. Con:
      A child has free will. If they do not wish to care for their parents in their old age - when it is unlikely that said parents will be in a place to object - then they have the choice not to.

        1.1.4.3.1. Pro:
        A selfish parent who doesn't support their children can't expect their children to be their free-of-charge caretakers.

        1.1.4.3.2. Con:
        The obligation a child often feels to their parents for the care they have given them through their life means that their free will to refuse care is compromised.

          1.1.4.3.2.1. Pro:
          Many children cope with feelings of guilt [20] about putting a parent in a nursing home when they can no longer care for themselves.

      1.1.4.4. Pro:
      A person bringing a child into the world to cement their own financial future is a selfish act.

        1.1.4.4.1. Con:
        For many people, it is an act of necessity, not selfishness, as financial security is not possible for them.

        1.1.4.4.2. Con:
        Not having children and relying on retirement in the future is selfish.

          1.1.4.4.2.1. Pro:
          Childless elderly people in modern society will have to rely on services (doctors, farmers, experts) provided by children of other people, while they didn't put any effort into raising these new working class adults. To reach selflessness in their case they should live outside of modern society not receiving anything from them.

        1.1.4.4.3. Pro:
        It is not only financial, but often emotional or physical support, that children must provide for their parents. These can place a significant burden on the child, and it is selfish for parents to rely on their children in this way.

        1.1.4.4.4. Pro:
        Having a child is selfish if one does it solely for financial benefits such as receiving alimony and government support.

          1.1.4.4.4.1. Con:
          It is extremely uncommon [21] for people to have children for this reason.

  1.2. Pro:
  Having a child is selfish as it places a burden on others in society.

    1.2.1. Con:
    A society with low child birth rate will age unless young people immigrate from elsewhere or old people are euthanized. This creates even larger burden on others in the society.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      Often, immigrants find it harder [22] to adjust to their new surroundings and culturally integrate in the society.

      1.2.1.2. Pro:
      Aging population requires tax increase in countries where the state pays the pensions [23] for the elderly.

      1.2.1.3. Pro:
      Aging makes people progressively more disabled [24] and requires care to sustain their lives. An aging population means an increased ratio of caretakers, draining the workforce from elsewhere.

        1.2.1.3.1. Con:
        Less human caretakers are needed if healthspans are extended [25] and labor demand reduction methods such as automation and AI [26]-supported [27] caretaking facilities developed and applied heavily.

      1.2.1.4. Con:
      This proves that having children is perhaps necessary for social stability. Having children for a higher population of one's nation is still selfish.

      1.2.1.5. Con:
      There are many countries with high child birth rates, particularly many developing countries [28].

    1.2.2. Pro:
    The average cost of having a child is between $12,800 - $14,970 [29] a year. Many families cannot afford this, which passes the burden onto the state in many countries.

      1.2.2.1. Pro:
      Any state that subsidises healthcare or education will inevitably have to contribute to the resources needed by the child, with each additional child putting more pressure on the system.

        1.2.2.1.1. Pro:
        Costs to taxpayers [30] went up 37% more when Germany started subsidizing higher education.

        1.2.2.1.2. Pro:
        The UK spends around £11 billion [31] per year on child benefits.

          1.2.2.1.2.1. Con:
          This is a choice of the UK government, not required spending. If the UK stopped spending this money, children would still be born, especially outside the UK.

        1.2.2.1.3. Con:
        The ‘system’ is comprised of people and will collapse without more children becoming productive contributors.

        1.2.2.1.4. Con:
        The purpose of those systems is to help those who need it. Not every child is necessarily a burden.

          1.2.2.1.4.1. Pro:
          Those who are helped by the system are given the opportunity to provide a greater benefit than they have extracted from society. The system exists only to provide a potential net gain to the society that it supports.

      1.2.2.2. Con:
      Individuals are the basis for any society, so it's not a burden, it's an investment.

        1.2.2.2.1. Pro:
        Germany subsidizes [32] its higher education and vocational programs to ensure an educated population that's willing and able to contribute to society.

    1.2.3. Con:
    When every member of society is a burden to others in society, it is illegitimate for them to not accept the same burden caused by others.

      1.2.3.1. Pro:
      Humans need each other [33] to build community in order to flourish in society.

      1.2.3.2. Con:
      It is unrealistic to be able to help everyone that needs it. One person cannot save everyone [34].

      1.2.3.3. Pro:
      In Christianity, the Bible calls for people to "bear one another's burden [35]."

      1.2.3.4. Con:
      Every living member has right to discourage fellow members from having children in case the community does not want to bear the burden of these future citizens.

    1.2.4. Con:
    Though they may be a burden initially, most children eventually become adults who contribute to society and bear those burdens as a net benefit to society. These individuals make sure that both the aging and next generation after them are supported.

      1.2.4.1. Con:
      An aging society is not necessarily bad for the economy. As people are living longer, they make larger economic contributions [36] over a longer period of time compared to previous generations.

        1.2.4.1.1. Con:
        Some countries, including Japan [37], have an aging society combined with low fertility rates. This has led to an economic strain in rural areas, where older residents lack access to basic necessities and young people move away to cities.

      1.2.4.2. Con:
      Having children for the reason that they'll grow up to support older members of the community is selfish.

      1.2.4.3. Pro:
      Societies will eventually die out without new children being born. If there are no more children, the human race will cease to exist and the economies which support society will also crumble.

        1.2.4.3.1. Con:
        It is very unlikely that the entire planet will stop reproducing. If the world ends due to a catastrophe, it will be due to overpopulation [38] and over consumption; not because the human race died out.

        1.2.4.3.2. Con:
        Economies can rely on a lesser number of people using technology. Robots or machines can, and do supplement or even replace human labour, reducing the need for a large number of people to exist for a thriving economy.

      1.2.4.4. Con:
      The motivation for having a child can still be selfish even if the child accrues net good for mankind. The externalities of an act do not affect the morality of the original act.

        1.2.4.4.1. Con:
        An act can be selfish and moral at the same time. Selfishness is not a point on the morality spectrum.

      1.2.4.5. Pro:
      By not having children, the world is deprived of all the good that the child could have done.

        1.2.4.5.1. Con:
        This assumes that all people become productive members of society, when in reality many adults are also dependent [39] on state support.

          1.2.4.5.1.1. Pro:
          The small number of children who end up changing the world positively does not outweigh the vast majority of children who are either neutral upholders of the status quo, or harmful to society.

            1.2.4.5.1.1.1. Pro:
            A majority of people move through life being thoroughly mediocre [40].

            1.2.4.5.1.1.2. Pro:
            By having children, the world is now subject to all the evil that a child can do as well.

            1.2.4.5.1.1.3. Pro:
            It is impossible to determine what contribution any given child will make to the world. Assuming your child will be one of the few to make a positive contribution to society is simply a justification for a selfish act.

            1.2.4.5.1.1.4. Con:
            Individuals who change the world for the better tend to create change far more drastic than those who change it for the worse.

              1.2.4.5.1.1.4.1. Pro:
              A person who invents a new medicine does more good than a person who commits a crime does harm.

                1.2.4.5.1.1.4.1.1. Pro:
                It is estimated that over 200 million lives [41] have been saved by the discovery of penicillin.

                1.2.4.5.1.1.4.1.2. Con:
                Pyramid schemes [42] that tout "health" benefits help very few people while harming many others.

              1.2.4.5.1.1.4.2. Con:
              Horrible individuals have been responsible [43] for tens of millions of deaths and untold amounts of suffering. This arguably rivals the positive contributions of humanity's best people.

            1.2.4.5.1.1.5. Con:
            Changing the world does not necessarily mean seismic shifts, but rather small behaviours and actions. Even positively influencing a single person contributes to society in a notable way.

        1.2.4.5.2. Pro:
        The utilitarian approach to ethics suggest that it is better to have more people, as there will be more good in the world when more people are doing the same amount of good per person.

          1.2.4.5.2.1. Con:
          Having more people does not guarantee more good. Goodness cannot be predicted; by having children one risks the possibility of producing more evil in the world.

          1.2.4.5.2.2. Con:
          The Utility Monster [44] thought experiment shows how some people are capable of experiencing more pleasure than others, while not contributing an equal amount of utility in society.

        1.2.4.5.3. Con:
        Using another person, especially without their consent, to improve the world is selfish and immoral.

          1.2.4.5.3.1. Con:
          Parents don't use their children to improve the world, rather the improvement is a potential byproduct of the child's existence.

            1.2.4.5.3.1.1. Con:
            Since the improvement is a "potential byproduct" and not a perfect guarantee, the parents risk being the cause of additional sorrow, pain and suffering being introduced to the world.

          1.2.4.5.3.2. Con:
          People may be limited in the way they can directly improve the world. If a parent believes that they can raise a child to improve the world in a way that the parent could not, that may be their most ethical option.

            1.2.4.5.3.2.1. Pro:
            Parents are in the best position to raise their child in a way that is most likely to have a positive impact on future generations.

          1.2.4.5.3.3. Pro:
          People should not be treated as a means to an end, but an end unto themselves.

            1.2.4.5.3.3.1. Pro:
            Having children is always accompanied by some idea of "purpose" for them; be it giving them a life better than the parent, deciding on a future occupation, etc. Almost everyone's birth has, to some extent, a predetermined goal-- which is treating people as a means to an end, and thus using them.

        1.2.4.5.4. Pro:
        Almost all children eventually develop relationships with those around them, which are valued and important to others.

          1.2.4.5.4.1. Con:
          Relationships often sour over time and become the source of great suffering [45].

          1.2.4.5.4.2. Con:
          Having children to form positive relationships is using someone to ward off loneliness and thus selfish.

        1.2.4.5.5. Con:
        By having children, the world is deprived of all the good that the parents could have done if they didn't spend that time raising children.

        1.2.4.5.6. Pro:
        It is beneficial to have children as they contribute to innovative, creative ideas that will improve society.

          1.2.4.5.6.1. Pro:
          It is virtually guaranteed that at least some children will grow up to be great inventors, leaders, or artists who improve the lives of others.

          1.2.4.5.6.2. Con:
          Conversely, children also grow up to spread destructive ideas and cause violence and sorrow in society.

          1.2.4.5.6.3. Pro:
          Many companies [46] that incorporate ideas from humans (that were at one point children) are working to save the planet.

            1.2.4.5.6.3.1. Con:
            It is also true that many companies are the primary contributors [47] to climate change.

            1.2.4.5.6.3.2. Pro:
            Elon Musk is striving to reduce carbon emissions and save the planet [48].

          1.2.5.2.1.6. Con:

          By using the earth's resources like every other living creature, humans have some kind of invisible bond with nature which ensures mutual survival. Since human capacities outdo natural forces (in some cases), we owe nature some responsibility and an obligation to preserve it.

          1.4.2.3.1.2. Pro:

            1.4.2.3.1.2.1. Pro:

              1.4.2.3.1.2.1.1. Con:

              1.4.2.3.1.2.1.2. Pro:
              There are ways to reduce [144] one's carbon footprint that will allow humans to live sustainably without needing to resort to anti-natalism.

            1.4.2.3.1.2.2. Con:

            1.4.2.3.1.2.3. Pro:
            -> See 1.2.4.5.6.3.

        1.4.2.3.2. Pro:

        1.4.2.3.3. Con:
        Human beings may not owe a duty to the Earth per se, but they do owe a duty to the ecosystems of living things that make up the Earth.

          1.4.2.3.3.1. Pro:
          Human beings owe a duty to their children and their descendants to ensure they are not born onto a planet which will only cause them suffering.

          1.4.2.3.3.2. Pro:
          We may not recognise animals as agents in the same way we do humans, but we still tend to recognise that they have rights [146] of some kind and acknowledge principles against animal cruelty. Taking actions that would lead to the extinction of ecosystems would violate these principles.

          1.4.2.3.3.3. Con:
          99% of species that ever existed are extinct [147]. Without any human influence, ecosystems shift and drastically change from ice ages [148] to massive [149] extinction level events with wanton disregard for the species that lived. It only serves to follow that human beings follow the example that nature set and have no regard for the species or ecosystems that exist on earth.

    1.4.3. Con:
    This suggests that because humanity as a whole negatively affects the planet, an individual child will too. If parents value protecting the environment, they can bring up their child to have a positive effect on the planet.

      1.4.3.1. Pro:

      1.4.3.2. Pro:

        1.4.3.2.1. Con:

      1.4.3.3. Con:

    1.4.4. Pro:

      1.4.4.2. Pro:

      1.4.4.3. Con:

      1.4.4.4. Con:

    1.4.5. Pro:

      1.4.5.1. Pro:
