      Non-worship of God could also separate one from their worshiping loved ones. Thus, the determination to worship God must be based on some other criteria.

    1.5.2. Pro:
    We should not worship a God who uses Hell like the stick end of the carrot-on-a-stick motivation, 'Do this or burn' which is more akin to psychological abuse.

      1.5.2.1. Pro:
      This tactic, of using Hell for social control, has been employed on populations for many millennia. Justice without fear is ineffective.

        1.5.2.1.1. Con:
        Justice is only fearful for those guilty of crime. For those who are victims, justice is highly desirable. Likewise the fear of Hell is only for those who might go there. To those who have been wronged by an evil act, Hell is also highly desirable. Therefore justice is ineffective only when it isn't applied in equal measure.

        1.5.2.1.2. Pro:
        Knowing that there might be a judgement of guilt leveled against a person, with a subsequent punishment for that guilt, might make a person evaluate other options when they are about to act criminally. These other options will always be better than criminal behavior.

        1.5.2.1.3. Con:
        Mankind’s use of the reality or concept of hell is irrelevant to the question of worshiping hell’s creator.

      1.5.2.2. Con:

      1.5.2.4. Con:
      All systems of justice require punishment to uphold moral standards. The alternative would mean either rewarding moral vice or allowing it to further contribute to community decay.

        1.5.2.4.1. Pro:
        Punishments are a superior method [146] to instill moral norms than rewards because they communicate that certain acts are an obligatory rule as opposed to a voluntary rule.

      The God of the Holy Bible does not use hell as a negative motivator.

Your example, 'Do this or burn', is only valid in certain contexts, such as a church. A church or Christian's misdeeds do not reflect on God. We are all sinfull and unjust.

    God wouldn't be a perfect judge if He let everyone into heaven.

      1.5.3.1. Con:
      Jesus taught his followers that the only way that a person can enter heaven is through faith in him [147], not good deeds. Thus, heaven is not a reward for loving oneself and others.

      1.5.3.2. Con:

        This assumes that our perspective on justice is at least on par with God’s. Which if God is God by definition his perspective is superior ours.
