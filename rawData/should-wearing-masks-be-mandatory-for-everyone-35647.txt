Discussion Name:
Should wearing masks be mandatory for everyone?

1. Thesis:
Wearing masks should be mandatory for everyone.

  1.1. Pro:
  Most people support making masks mandatory in public places.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Faced with the choice of further 6 months of a lockdown or wearing masks, most people, it is safe to assume, would choose to wears masks.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      While masks provide modest protection from the virus, if everyone were to wear one, the cumulative impact on transmission would be one of 'exponential decay [1]', thereby reducing the transmission of the virus and eliminating the need for lockdowns.

    1.1.2. Con:
    People would not comply with a rule requiring them to wear masks.

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      Even though wearing masks is initially quite annoying, you get used to it.

        1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
        In Asia [2], mask-wearing has become a kind of norm and people have gotten used to it. To the extent that many Asians, when abroad [3], still continue wearing their masks.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      Whether something will happen in practice or not should not overshadow a debate on whether something should happen.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        A debate about a real world policy cannot be divorced from what it will look like on the ground, and how people will respond.

      1.1.2.3. Pro:
      People are more likely to put the individual inconvenience of wearing a mask above the collective good.

        1.1.2.3.1. Con:
        Convenience is only prioritised when the alternative has no large impact on them. If we made it mandatory, they will abide by it.

          1.1.2.3.1.1. Pro:
          Robust enforcement powers can ensure that the inconvenience of wearing a mask is dwarfed by the personal cost of not doing so.

            1.1.2.3.1.1.1. Pro:
            Police in Philadelphia forcibly removed passengers [4] from buses who were not respecting rules requiring them to wear face masks when using public transport.

            1.1.2.3.1.1.2. Pro:
            In India's largest cities, failing to wear a face mask is punishable by up to 6 months imprisonment [5].

          1.1.2.3.1.2. Pro:
          Fear of being publicly shamed for failing to comply would likely outweigh the temporary inconvenience of wearing a mask.

            1.1.2.3.1.2.1. Con:
            Public shaming can have tragic consequences [6] and so should not be encouraged or relied on as a mechanism for compliance.

            1.1.2.3.1.2.2. Con:
            Public shaming does not work [7] as it makes people defensive and thus more stubbornly defiant.

            1.1.2.3.1.2.3. Con:
            Some people, namely men [8], find wearing a mask to be "shameful" or a sign of "weakness" and they, therefore, avoid it despite the thread of public shaming.

          1.1.2.3.1.3. Pro:
          Even though speeding is more convenient for the driver, they do not speed because there are regulating laws.

        1.1.2.3.2. Con:
        Studies in behavioural psychology [9] in the context of a disaster show that humans are naturally inclined to unite in solidarity with the community.

        1.1.2.3.3. Con:
        Seeing mask-wearing [10] as a social practice based on values such as shared responsibility and solidarity is likely to increase its prevalence.

        1.1.2.3.4. Pro:
        The prioritization of American individualism [11] is a barrier to widespread mask wearing.

      1.1.2.4. Pro:
      Resources and personnel will be required to enforce this rule when they could be allocated to more effective strategies.

    1.1.3. Con:
    The majority of people supporting a policy is not a good reason to implement that policy.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      Most people are not qualified public health experts. As such, they are not qualified to influence public health policy.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      While most people are not qualified to influence public health policy directly, their support for such measures is useful for determining whether the public will actually obey them if introduced.

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      If applied without question, this principle would give way to the tyranny of the majority. [12]

        1.1.3.3.1. Pro:
        If the majority of people supported murdering all children to prevent overpopulation, this does not mean we should implement such a policy.

        1.1.3.3.2. Con:
        Laws are always implemented by a majority, since elected representatives create laws which tend to require a majority to pass.

    1.1.4. Con:
    If most people do support mask-wearing, then they would choose to wear masks even without them being mandated by the government.

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      If most people support mask wearing, the public health benefits of mask-wearing can be achieved without introducing any over-reaching laws.

    1.1.5. Pro:
    Data from several countries shows that public opinion is firmly in favour of mandating mask-wearing.

      1.1.5.1. Pro:
      91% of Czech Republicans [13] support the government's decision to make wearing masks mandatory in public.

      1.1.5.2. Pro:
      52% of Britons [14] in one poll indicated that they would support making face masks compulsory.

      1.1.5.3. Pro:
      A poll conducted in the US in October 2020 found that most Americans, roughly 92 percent [15], always or sometimes wear a mask when leaving their home.

        1.1.5.3.1. Pro:
        Another survey, published in August 2020, found that most Americans support mask mandates, with 78 percent [16] of people supporting a federal mask mandate.

      1.1.5.4. Con:
      Even if everyone is in favour of wearing masks in theory, not everyone can afford to buy masks.

        1.1.5.4.1. Pro:
        In Hong Kong, nearly 70% [17] of low-income families cannot afford masks.

  1.2. Con:
  Making it mandatory to wear masks is an infringement on human freedom.

    1.2.1. Con:
    It is not possible to contain a pandemic without infringing upon human freedom to some extent.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      If people continued to live their lives as normal, with their freedom uninhibited, then the virus would circulate among the population more easily [18].

    1.2.2. Pro:
    Mandating wearing masks is coercive because it disproprotionately benefits people around [19] more than the ones wearing them.

      1.2.2.1. Con:
      If everyone wore masks then the benefits would be equal.

      1.2.2.2. Con:
      Research [20] suggests that wearing masks not only benefits people around but those wearing them as well. Masks decrease the likelihood of experiencing severe infection for those who wear them because they block virus particles.

    1.2.3. Con:
    Human freedom should not be absolute.

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      Human freedom should not be null either. The right to wear (or not wear) a mask seems a reasonable and fundamental freedom like freedom of speech and religion.

      1.2.3.2. Pro:
      If human freedom were absolute, an individual could murder another without facing repercussions.

      1.2.3.3. Pro:
      The good of the masses outweighs the good of the individual.

        1.2.3.3.1. Pro:
        Even if mandatory mask-wearing restricts freedom, such a restriction is justified because it reduces the risk of infection [21] for oneself and others.

    1.2.4. Con:
    The state has a duty to protect human life [22], even if it must infringe on freedom to do so.

      1.2.4.1. Con:
      The purpose of government is also to maximize human freedom and happiness, not reduce it.

        1.2.4.1.1. Con:
        Wearing a mask is less of an impingement on human freedom than having to endure a lockdown.

          1.2.4.1.1.1. Pro:
          -> See 1.1.1.1.

        1.2.4.1.2. Con:
        The purpose of the government is also to protect [23] citizens from external threats. The external threat being COVID-19 in this case, from which masks provide protection.

        1.2.4.1.3. Con:
        Letting people get sick has a significant negative effect [24] on one's happiness, so enforcing mask mandates maximizes happiness by avoiding sickness.

        1.2.4.1.4. Pro:
        Wearing masks can be the cause of unhappiness [25] for some people.

      1.2.4.2. Pro:
      If the Public Health Service Act [26] substantively infringed upon freedom, it could not have been legislated because it would have been considered unconstitutional. This is because the 14th Amendment [27] of the US constitution guarantees life and liberty.

      1.2.4.3. Con:
      The amount of safety that masks provide is not worth the inconvenience they cause.

        1.2.4.3.1. Pro:
        There are many things that could make people safer, like wearing helmets at all times, that we don't do because making life perfectly safe also makes life less pleasant.

        1.2.4.3.2. Pro:
        The amount of deaths caused by COVID-19 [28] is not substantial compared to other causes. In 2019 there were 55.4 million deaths worldwide. 8.9 million were caused by ischemic heart disease. [29]

      1.2.4.4. Pro:
      COVID-19 has resulted in thousands of deaths [30] across the globe. Thus, wearing masks to prevent the spread of the virus will protect human life.

        1.2.4.4.1. Pro:
        A new study [31] from Hong Kong, conducted using hamsters as test-subjects, demonstrated the effectiveness of wearing masks at stopping the spread of COVID-19.

      1.2.4.5. Pro:
      For human freedoms to exist, humans need to be alive and to survive COVID-19. A measure to facilitate that is the wearing of masks.

    1.2.5. Pro:
    One of the most intuitively important human freedoms is the right to bodily autonomy. This includes how people choose to dress.

      1.2.5.1. Con:
      Right to bodily autonomy does not include the right to endanger one's health. This is akin to mandatory wearing of helmets while riding a bike.

        1.2.5.1.1. Pro:
        Individual risk of contracting COVID-19 decreased with the increasing use of the mask in the community [32].

      1.2.5.2. Pro:
      Clothing and fashion are one of the most important and visible expressions [33] of identity and self (p. 1396).

        1.2.5.2.1. Con:
        Masks, like our other clothing choices, are slowly becoming a social norm [34] and people are wearing them voluntarily.

        1.2.5.2.2. Con:
        Similar to how many women wearing a hijab or other head coverings style these to fit with their personal taste and fashion [35], masks can be adapted [36] to fit an outfit.

          1.2.5.2.2.1. Con:
          This might again disadvantage [37] minority groups. For example, while wearing a niqab in public has been forbidden in France since 2011, a haute couture show celebrating face masks was seen as acceptable.

      1.2.5.3. Pro:
      Enforced clothing is a hallmark of oppressive regimes.

        1.2.5.3.1. Con:
        Wearing masks should be seen strictly as a health issue and not a political issue [38].

        1.2.5.3.2. Pro:
        North Korea [39] enforces stringent styling rules on its people.

          1.2.5.3.2.1. Con:
          The state does not wish to impose masks as an object of fashion or to enforce a stylistic norm. Masks are primarily medical tools that happen to be objects of clothing.

        1.2.5.3.3. Con:
        That liberal and democratic states are also mandating masks suggest that such a policy is not inherently oppressive.

          1.2.5.3.3.1. Pro:
          Nordic countries [40], known for upholding civic freedom, also began to make masks mandatory [41] in some contexts.

        1.2.5.3.4. Con:
        Clothing restrictions or prescriptions often target specific groups of a population, which is what makes these directives oppressive. Masks, however, should be worn by everyone, regardless of gender, status, or religious affiliation, for example.

      1.2.5.4. Con:
      The right of a person to choose how they dress is not absolute.

        1.2.5.4.1. Pro:
        Many jobs require staff to wear uniforms, sometimes including masks, yet this is not seen as an infringement on human freedom.

          1.2.5.4.1.1. Pro:
          Surgeons, for example, wear masks and proper medical attire for the safety of their patients. This is not seen as an infringement on human freedom but a necessary requirement.

          1.2.5.4.1.2. Con:
          An employee whose employer requires them to wear a mask is not analogous to members of the public being forced to wear one because the employee has freely entered into this contractual relationship with the employer.

            1.2.5.4.1.2.1. Con:
            Members of the public have a social contract [42] with their fellow citizens to take reasonable measures to avoid harming them, such as wearing a mask during a pandemic. Therefore, the analogy is justified.

              1.2.5.4.1.2.1.1. Con:
              A contract must be entered into by a party who is fully informed and freely consenting. This is not the case in a social contract, as the individual had no choice but to be born into a state.

                1.2.5.4.1.2.1.1.1. Con:
                In cases where people disagree with the social contract, they should seek residence elsewhere.

          1.2.5.4.1.3. Con:
          An employer demanding that an employee dress in a particular way is not analogous because this occurs in the professional sphere. An individual should have greater freedoms in the private sphere.

        1.2.5.4.2. Pro:
        Most societies limit public nudity [43] to at least some extent.

      1.2.5.5. Con:
      The right to bodily autonomy cannot infringe upon the rights of others. Not wearing a mask threatens other people's right to life and well-being.

        1.2.5.5.1. Pro:
        India's Supreme Court bench remarked [44] that the right to life of others is violated by those who are not wearing masks.

  1.3. Con:
  Many individuals do not have access to masks.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    Essential workers, like postal workers and grocery store employees, will not have access to masks if they are mandated for all.

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      If everyone has to wear a mask, these masks and other PPE will not be available to medical personnel who need them more.

        1.3.1.1.1. Pro:
        Medical personnel working on the frontlines during a healthcare crisis are more likely to encounter people that may infect them. Thus, they are in more urgent need of protective equipment.

        1.3.1.1.2. Pro:
        Health care providers across the US have voiced their concerns [45] and frustrations over the low supply of masks.

        1.3.1.1.3. Con:
        The masks recommended by governments are not the same ones used by medical professionals.

      1.3.1.2. Con:
      Naturally, the government would only make wearing masks mandatory when critical exposed workers like hospital staff have enough masks to do their work safely.

        1.3.1.2.1. Pro:
        WHO officials [46] said that part of their rationale for initially not recommending masks for the general public was fear of supply issues for medical personnel.

      1.3.1.3. Pro:
      The amount of protective equipment available has been stressed [47] due to the pandemic. If everyone is mandated to have masks, it will put additional strain on an already depleted necessity and many people will go without.

        1.3.1.3.1. Pro:
        Even in December 2020 [48], healthcare workers in the US were facing shortages of masks.

      1.3.1.4. Pro:
      The US Postal Service has not provided [49] essential protection, such as masks, to its workers. With such protections in high demand and low supply [50], mandating it for all may mean workers in high-risk jobs, such as postal services, may go without.

        1.3.1.4.1. Con:
        While this was true at the beginning of the outbreak, masks have since been provided [51] to these workers.

    1.3.2. Con:
    Governments will not make something mandatory that many will not be able to comply with or they will face the wrath of the people.

    1.3.3. Pro:
    Many people cannot afford to buy masks due to socioeconomic barriers.

      1.3.3.1. Pro:
      Prices of masks are soaring [52].

        1.3.3.1.1. Pro:
        -> See 1.1.5.4.1.

      1.3.3.2. Pro:
      If people who need masks cannot afford them, this will create an unfair two-tiered system in which the wealthy are more protected.

        1.3.3.2.1. Con:
        Governments may distribute masks to citizens, free of charge. [53]

        1.3.3.2.2. Con:
        Assuming that masks have a limited supply, any system of distributing masks would result in inequality of access to masks. Thus, class-based inequality of access to masks is not any different to alternative systems of distribution.

        1.3.3.2.3. Pro:
        BME people [54], who are disproportionately from poor communities, are already at increased risk of contracting and dying of COVID-19. This situation would be exacerbated if they are unable to afford masks.

        1.3.3.2.4. Pro:
        Individuals will face the prospect of legal punishment, even if they cannot afford masks or in the circumstance that there are no masks available for them.

          1.3.3.2.4.1. Con:
          In practice, it is unlikely individuals will be prosecuted if they are genuinely unable to get hold of masks due to supply shortages.

            1.3.3.2.4.1.1. Con:
            The excuse of supply shortages is only sufficient if they have no choice but to leave their homes. Yet if they could have remained at home but chose not to, then they are still choosing to break the law, irrespective of their reasons for not wearing a mask. Given this, they can and should be prosecuted still.

    1.3.4. Pro:
    Low-income countries have limited access to masks.

      1.3.4.1. Pro:
      A significant proportion of the population in many low-income countries cannot afford to purchase masks.

        1.3.4.1.1. Con:
        There are ways for the people who cannot afford masks to make their own [55] at home.

        1.3.4.1.2. Pro:
        For many South Asians, buying a mask means forgoing food. [56]

        1.3.4.1.3. Con:
        It is possible for low-income countries to produce masks [57] at low costs.

          1.3.4.1.3.1. Pro:
          The Central African Republic [58] engaged in a project where it successfully produced more than 2 million masks in just two months.

        1.3.4.1.4. Con:
        Compared to lockdowns [59], masks are a cheaper way for low-income countries to fight the pandemic.

          1.3.4.1.4.1. Pro:
          Some medical experts have said that face masks are the cheapest available [60] tools to fight the spread of COVID-19.

          1.3.4.1.4.2. Pro:
          Low and middle-income countries [61] have used face masks to successfully curb the spread of other viral diseases such as SARS and influence.

        1.3.4.1.5. Con:
        Many low-income countries have a high population density [62] that makes physical distancing relatively unfeasible. Mandating masks is a more effective and enforcible way to stop of the spread of COVID-19 in such contexts.

        1.3.4.1.6. Pro:
        Most of the population of Pakistan cannot afford [63] to buy face masks.

      1.3.4.2. Pro:
      Gaps in the global supply of masks [64] have entailed that low-income countries are unable to procure the number of masks they require.

        1.3.4.2.1. Pro:
        In April 2020, UNICEF [65] reported that it was only able to acquire one-tenth of the number of masks requested by low-income countries.

    1.3.5. Pro:
    Making masks mandatory would limit access to masks by increasing demand and straining supply.

      1.3.5.1. Pro:
      If masks were mandatory, the shortage in supply would lead to profiteering and panic.

        1.3.5.1.1. Pro:
        Making masks mandatory further incentivizes fraudsters to sell fake masks.

          1.3.5.1.1.1. Pro:
          Hundreds of thousands [66] of unapproved and unsterile masks are already on the market. If masks are mandatory, the incentive to make more of such masks increases even further.

          1.3.5.1.1.2. Pro:
          The spike in demand that is likely to occur when masks are made mandatory provides fraudsters with a large market incentive to produce masks, even if this means they are unable to produce the masks to the needed standard.

        1.3.5.1.2. Con:
        Adopting harsh penalties for those found profiteering would function as a deterrent, thus mitigating the harm.

          1.3.5.1.2.1. Pro:
          The UK's competition watchdog [67], the Competition and Markets Authority (CMA), has set up a COVID-19 taskforce to crack down on profiteering.

          1.3.5.1.2.2. Con:
          Given how lucrative profiteering in these circumstances can be, many will not be deterred even by the threat of harsh penalties.

            1.3.5.1.2.2.1. Pro:
            Some items are being sold for 24 times [68] their usual price.

        1.3.5.1.3. Con:
        Many companies in the industry that are currently on hold due to the outbreak could benefit from the manufacturing, sales, and distribution of masks during the quarantine, thus mitigating major losses in the economy.

          1.3.5.1.3.1. Pro:
          Some companies who usually make clothing, mattresses, and hockey equipment have transitioned [69] parts of their businesses into the production of masks.

        1.3.5.1.4. Con:
        Manufacturing of masks can be increased [70] to meet elevated demand.

        1.3.5.1.5. Pro:
        -> See 1.3.3.1.

        1.3.5.1.6. Pro:
        Australian pharmacies are selling overpriced masks and profiting [71] from the current heightened demand.

        1.3.5.1.7. Con:
        Whereas it's natural for prices to rise with demand, it's only temporary, as the prospect of profit induces competitors to create more supply.

      1.3.5.2. Pro:
      Making masks mandatory for all could exacerbate Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) supply problems.

        1.3.5.2.1. Con:
        Supply shortages will not be such a problem because people can make their own DIY masks [72].

          1.3.5.2.1.1. Con:
          Cloth masks do not significantly reduce the transmission of the virus.

            1.3.5.2.1.1.1. Pro:
            Researchers argue [73] that lighter/ home-made masks will not protect against infection.

            1.3.5.2.1.1.2. Pro:
            40-90% [74] of virus particles penetrate cloth masks. (p. 1)

            1.3.5.2.1.1.3. Con:
            Having some protection is always better than no protection at all.

              1.3.5.2.1.1.3.1. Pro:
              Experts recommend that, in absence of other masks, cloth masks [75] should be worn instead of not wearing any mask.

                1.3.5.2.1.1.3.1.1. Pro:
                The United States Centers for Disease Control and Prevention [76] recommend that people wear a cloth face-covering in public, especially when social distancing measures are difficult to maintain.

                1.3.5.2.1.1.3.1.2. Pro:
                Health officials in Thailand [77] have advised people to make cloth face masks at home and wash them daily.

              1.3.5.2.1.1.3.2. Pro:
              A study [78] found that improvised cloth masks are relatively more effective at reducing transmission than not wearing masks at all.

            1.3.5.2.1.1.4. Con:
            When made in specific, medically-recommended ways, cloth-masks can be effective at reducing transmission.

              1.3.5.2.1.1.4.1. Pro:
              A study [79] found that if a cloth mask involved two layers of fabric, it could be as protective against virus droplets as medical masks.

              1.3.5.2.1.1.4.2. Pro:
              In June 2020, the WHO [80] advised that people wear non-medical masks consisting of three layers of fabric. This suggests that cloth masks can effectively reduce transmission if they are made properly.

          1.3.5.2.1.2. Con:
          While homemade [81] cotton or cloth masks are more effective than not wearing a mask, they are still less effective than either N95 masks or surgical masks.

          1.3.5.2.1.3. Pro:
          Many people are already making [82] their own masks, or buying washable alternatives.

        1.3.5.2.2. Pro:
        In April 2020, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) [83] said that mask supply chains are already "stressed".

        1.3.5.2.3. Con:
        Even if masks were to result in a supply problem, the solution to this is to ramp up production, not to change a sensible rule.

          1.3.5.2.3.1. Con:
          It is difficult for global supply chains to shift and ramp up production of masks, as they are experiencing unparalleled disruption [84], with many suppliers facing insolvency.

          1.3.5.2.3.2. Pro:
          Many entrepreneurs [85] are attempting to fill the gap in supply.

          1.3.5.2.3.3. Pro:
          -> See 1.3.5.1.3.

          1.3.5.2.3.4. Pro:
          -> See 1.3.5.1.4.

        1.3.5.2.4. Pro:
        Countries experience surges in COVID-19 cases at different times, and so making masks mandatory across the world may prevent allocation of scarce resources to where they are most desperately needed.

      1.3.5.3. Con:
      Most countries do not forsee a shortage in the supply of masks.

        1.3.5.3.1. Pro:
        China [86] has enough production capacity to meet increased demand for masks.

    1.3.6. Con:
    Home-made cloth coverings work as masks, and are recommended by the CDC [87].

      1.3.6.1. Pro:
      The CDC - the leading public health institute in the US - applies rigorous scientific standards [88] to its research such as peer reviewing. As such, it can be regarded as a reliable authority on public health related matters.

      1.3.6.2. Con:
      European advisers have warned against using reusable cloth masks as they may increase the chance of infection [89].

  1.4. Pro:
  Public health and public safety will generally improve as a result of mask-wearing, both now and in the future.

    1.4.1. Pro:
    Getting used to wearing masks can have positive side effects, such as preventing other diseases from spreading.

      1.4.1.1. Pro:
      Wearing masks will protect vulnerable populations from getting infected during flu season.

        1.4.1.1.1. Pro:
        While COVID-19 is raging, the number of seasonal flu cases is unusually low [90], in no small part due to the wearing of masks.

        1.4.1.1.2. Pro:
        A 2013 study found that masks can produce a threefold reduction [91] in how much of the seasonal flu is sprayed into the air by sufferers. (p. 2)

        1.4.1.1.3. Con:
        A rebound of other viruses is possible and has been documented in Australia [92], long after when their flu season typically occurs.

      1.4.1.2. Pro:
      Mask wearing can help protect the wearer from harmful pollutants [93] in the atmosphere.

        1.4.1.2.1. Con:
        Many experts say that the only true way to protect the wearer from pollutants is to reduce pollution. [94]

      1.4.1.3. Con:
      Masks cause acne [95] on the face.

        1.4.1.3.1. Con:
        There are ways to prevent and treat acne [96] caused by wearing masks.

    1.4.2. Pro:
    Wearing masks helps to ensure that people who cannot wear masks [97], such as people with respiratory conditions, will also be protected from catching the virus.

      1.4.2.1. Pro:
      Masks are not required to be worn [98] by children under the age of two, persons who have trouble breathing, or persons who are unconscious, incapacitated, or otherwise unable to remove the mask without assistance. (pg 2)

      1.4.2.2. Pro:
      Enforcing mask-wearing disproportionately inconveniences individuals with special needs.

        1.4.2.2.1. Pro:
        There have been concerns that wearing masks makes communication difficult for people who are deaf or hard-of-hearing [99].

          1.4.2.2.1.1. Con:
          Some experts have called for transparent masks, which allow for lip-reading [100], to address communication challenges for the deaf community.

          1.4.2.2.1.2. Con:
          In certain situations, such as college lectures [101], there are mechanisms that can be employed to try and mitigate the additional difficulties faced by those who are deaf or hard-of-hearing, such as removing background noise or using visual aids.

        1.4.2.2.2. Pro:
        Masks make it challenging for people who depend on dogs for therapeutic or social reasons because communication with dogs [102] involves using facial expressions.

    1.4.3. Con:
    Masks hide part of the face, making it harder for the police to retrieve the identity of (possible) criminals.

      1.4.3.1. Pro:
      Wearing masks anonymize people, making it easier for criminals to commit crimes [103], like armed robbery.

      1.4.3.2. Pro:
      Mandating masks makes society more dangerous, as suspects cannot be properly spotted and therefore apprehended.

      1.4.3.3. Con:
      There are other key identifiers [104] that can help police apprehend a criminal beyond facial features.

    1.4.4. Pro:
    Wearing masks [105] could alleviate fears that prevent people from seeking medical care for other illnesses because they want to avoid contracting COVID-19 during a visit to the hospital.

      1.4.4.1. Pro:
      Masks can help positively impact the mental state of people [106] by reducing their fear of potential infection.

  1.5. Pro:
  Masks can slow the spread of the virus so that countries are better able to manage the fallout from the pandemic.

    1.5.1. Con:
    Wearing a mask will create a false sense of security.

      1.5.1.1. Con:
      Masks represent a visible reminder of an invisible pathogen [107]. As such, they may remind people of the importance of social distancing and other health measures.

        1.5.1.1.1. Pro:
        The visible reminder of a mask may encourage people to engage in other practices to combat the virus, such as social distancing and hand-washing.

        1.5.1.1.2. Con:
        The pandemic has drastically changed [108] the lives of people throughout the world. Another reminder of the pathogen is not necessary.

      1.5.1.2. Pro:
      -> See 1.3.5.2.1.1.

      1.5.1.3. Pro:
      Masks will not prevent people from contracting or spreading the disease in other ways.

        1.5.1.3.1. Pro:
        People can contract COVID-19 from virus particles entering their eyes [109], which most masks do not cover.

          1.5.1.3.1.1. Pro:
          Wang Guangfa, a respiratory specialist who became infected [110] with COVID-19, believes he was infected through his eyes.

            1.5.1.3.1.1.1. Con:
            Though an expert in the field, Wang Guangfa's belief about how he contracted the virus is only based on anecdotal evidence. Therefore, it is not necessarily reliable.

            1.5.1.3.1.1.2. Pro:
            Jan Evans Patterson, a professor of medicine and pathology in infectious diseases [111], agreed that the scenario described by Wang Guangfa is possible.

          1.5.1.3.1.2. Con:
          While this is possible, it is extremely rare [112] for someone to contract COVID-19 through their eyes.

        1.5.1.3.2. Con:
        While the virus can survive on some surfaces, it is unlikely to spread by touch [113].

        1.5.1.3.3. Con:
        Wearing a mask helps reduce the propensity for people to touch their faces [114], which is a source of infection, particularly for individuals who do not practice proper hand hygiene.

          1.5.1.3.3.1. Pro:
          Face masks can reduce fomite transmission [115] as masks can prevent people from spreading body fluids by touching their noses or mouths, for instance, when trying to cover up a sneeze or cough.

        1.5.1.3.4. Pro:
        COVID-19 can be spread by touching surfaces [116]. This is something masks do not protect against.

      1.5.1.4. Pro:
      Trust in masks might encourage people to undervalue social-distancing rules.

        1.5.1.4.1. Con:
        Public health institutions can communicate that the rate of infections are most effectively reduced when both social distancing and mask-wearing [117] are carried out.

        1.5.1.4.2. Pro:
        A study [118] from November 2020 found that while mask wearing in the US has increased, social distancing has dramatically decreased (p. 5).

        1.5.1.4.3. Con:
        Face masks can be used to complement social distancing.

          1.5.1.4.3.1. Pro:
          Social distancing is more effective when people are masked.

            1.5.1.4.3.1.1. Pro:
            Inter-personal physical distancing is more effective when people wear masks because COVID-19 can sometimes be transmitted over greater distances [119].

      1.5.1.5. Con:
      If everyone wore masks, the disease would be less likely to spread to others. Thus, collective security would increase.

        1.5.1.5.1. Pro:
        -> See 1.2.4.4.1.

    1.5.2. Pro:
    Wearing masks is particularly effective at stopping the spread of COVID-19.

      1.5.2.1. Con:
      Improper use and lack of knowledge about masks hinder [120] the effectiveness of wearing masks.

        1.5.2.1.1. Con:
        While improper use hinders the effectiveness of masks, many people will still use them correctly and gain the benefits they provide.

          1.5.2.1.1.1. Pro:
          A survey [121] in Southern California found that 42% of the people tracked were wearing masks correctly, compared to 10% who wore them incorrectly.

        1.5.2.1.2. Pro:
        Masks with exhalation valves [122] or vents do not help prevent the spread of COVID-19 to others because the one-way hole in the material can allow expelled respiratory droplets from the exhaled air to reach others.

        1.5.2.1.3. Pro:
        Many people wear masks under their nose [123], which is as dangerous as not wearing a mask.

        1.5.2.1.4. Pro:
        If people sneeze into their mask, as is being published [124] as advice, the mask could get wet [125], which diminishes its efficacy.

      1.5.2.2. Pro:
      Countries that have made mask-wearing [126] mandatory have seen a slowing down [127] of viral spread.

        1.5.2.2.1. Pro:
        The Czech Republic [128], which implemented mandatory face masks on 19 March [129], has significantly slowed the spread of the virus compared to other European countries which have not introduced these measures.

          1.5.2.2.1.1. Con:
          As of January 2021, the Czech Republic has the second highest daily rate [130] of COVID-19, and its crematoriums [131] are becoming overwhelmed.

        1.5.2.2.2. Con:
        Correlation does not imply causation. It is possible that viral slowdown in those countries is due to other factors such as testing, population density, prevalence of handwashing, or early use of lockdown.

          1.5.2.2.2.1. Con:
          There is increasing evidence [132] to support the claim that masks actually help reduce the spread and their presence do not merely correlate with a slower rate of spread.

            1.5.2.2.2.1.1. Pro:
            A study [133] from Thailand showed that people who reported having always worn a mask during high-risk exposures had reduced risk of infection by more than 70% than those who did not wear a mask.

            1.5.2.2.2.1.2. Pro:
            A study of a COVID-19 outbreak on a ship [134] found that use of face coverings was associated with a 70% reduced risk of infection.

            1.5.2.2.2.1.3. Pro:
            Studies concerning COVID-19 infections occurring during flights more than 10 hours [135] long strongly suggests that wearing masks prevented infections.

        1.5.2.2.3. Pro:
        In contexts where social distancing is difficult to achieve, masks help in reducing the rate of infections.

          1.5.2.2.3.1. Pro:
          Those without masks, like Pakistan's cricket team [136], eventually test positive when they are also not following [137] social distancing directives.

          1.5.2.2.3.2. Pro:
          On 8 April 2020, the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control [138] advised that face masks should be used in spaces that are busy and closed.

      1.5.2.3. Pro:
      Decreased air humidity [139] due to indoor heating during winter increases respiratory viral diseases. Wearing masks can help reduce this likelihood because they keep the noise warm and most.

      1.5.2.4. Pro:
      The use of masks has been recommended by many experts and health organizations.

        1.5.2.4.1. Pro:
        The WHO [140] suggested that in settings where physical distancing may not possible, masks could be helpful in reducing the spread of infectious droplets.

        1.5.2.4.2. Pro:
        Some experts consider universal mask-wearing [141] as one of the best ways to limit the asymptomatic spread of the virus.

          1.5.2.4.2.1. Pro:
          Masks reduce asymptomatic spread because they prevent viral particles in the upper respiratory tract [142], where high levels of viral particles typically reside, from spreading.

          1.5.2.4.2.2. Pro:
          The US CDC stated that masks help reduce asymptomatic and presymptomatic spread which account for over 50% of the transmissions [143].

          1.5.2.4.2.3. Pro:
          There is a high amount of virus particles in saliva of infected but asymptomatic people. Mask-wearing [144] can prevent the spread of these particles to healthy individuals.

        1.5.2.4.3. Pro:
        It is estimated that around 95% of the world population [145] lives in countries where the government and disease experts recommend or require the use of masks in public places to limit the spread of COVID-19.

        1.5.2.4.4. Pro:
        On 10 November 2020, The US CDC reiterated its recommendation for the community use of masks [146] to prevent transmission of COVID-19.

          1.5.2.4.4.1. Pro:
          In their 10 November 2020 brief, the CDC highlighted several studies [147] that presented strong evidence in favour of masking.

        1.5.2.4.5. Pro:
        Health experts in the Hong Kong [148] government have recommended that people wear masks in crowded places to slow the spread of COVID-19.

      1.5.2.5. Pro:
      Many studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of mask-wearing in slowing down the spread of the virus.

        1.5.2.5.1. Pro:
        A recent meta-study [149] reviewing the evidence for the efficacy of universal masking concluded that there is strong scientific evidence that universal masking reduces the risk of infection and dampens the spread of COVID-19.

          1.5.2.5.1.1. Pro:
          A comparative study [150] found that the COVID-19 infection rate significantly slowed down after mask mandates.

          1.5.2.5.1.2. Pro:
          A modeling study [151] from October 2020 found that if 95% of the public in the US were to wear masks, an additional 129,574 lives could be saved between September 2020-February 2021.

        1.5.2.5.2. Pro:
        Masks protect health workers and caregivers [152] from infections they might contract while performing their duties.

        1.5.2.5.3. Pro:
        A study in August 2020 found that a weekly increase in per-capita mortality rate due to COVID-19 was four times lower [153] in places where mask-wearing was a norm.

        1.5.2.5.4. Pro:
        Community-level analyses [154] in the US and Europe have shown that universal masking has been shown to reduce infections and mortality.

    1.5.3. Con:
    -> See 1.3.5.2.1.1.

    1.5.4. Pro:
    Unchecked, the number and needs of COVID-19 patients can overwhelm hospitals.

      1.5.4.1. Pro:
      In the UK, where measures against the pandemic were not [155] being strictly applied, hospitals have been overwhelmed time [156] and time [157] again.

        1.5.4.1.1. Pro:
        In July 2020, only 38% [158] of UK residents were wearing masks.

      1.5.4.2. Pro:
      In the US, hospitals in the worst-hit states were overwhelmed in July 2020 [159], when only 67% [160] of the population was wearing masks.

  1.6. Con:
  This will have detrimental effects on the environment.

    1.6.1. Con:
    Many people have been making face masks out of recycled plastics [161], which is good for the environment.

      1.6.1.1. Pro:
      Mask recycling programmes are few [162] and far between, and many do not have the capacity to produce recycled masks.

    1.6.2. Pro:
    China [163] - which has a poor track record for protecting the environment [164] - is the main manufacturer of face masks. Increased manufacturing of masks could considerably worsen its existing environmental problems.

    1.6.3. Con:
    Numerous outlets are describing how people can make their own DIY masks [165] from what they have at home instead of using disposable ones.

      1.6.3.1. Con:
      Home-made masks are being phased out by many countries, in favour of medical-grade [166] masks.

    1.6.4. Con:
    We should put safety and health ahead of environmental concerns.

      1.6.4.1. Con:
      This draws a false dichotomy between protecting health and safety and dealing with environmental issues. They are heavily intertwined.

        1.6.4.1.1. Pro:
        Addressing environmental concerns is putting safety and health first because climate change poses a greater threat to humankind than viruses.

          1.6.4.1.1.1. Pro:
          Global warming is responsible for [167] as many as 150,000 deaths a year and contributes to five million human illnesses each year.

          1.6.4.1.1.2. Pro:
          Air pollutants are responsible for 7 million deaths [168] each year around the world.

        1.6.4.1.2. Con:
        Most of the major health and safety implications arising from environmental problems will not transpire in the short to medium term. Therefore, the distinction between environmental concerns and present heath and safety challenges is justified.

          1.6.4.1.2.1. Con:
          There are many people facing the reality of the climate crisis today. Flooding [169] and forest fires [170] are just two examples of this.

        1.6.4.1.3. Pro:
        The worse climate change gets, the more deadly disease outbreaks [171] there will be in the future.

          1.6.4.1.3.1. Pro:
          Existing environmental problems are already known to be worsening the impact [172] of viruses like COVID-19.

        1.6.4.1.4. Con:
        The "environment" doesn't need our protection.

          1.6.4.1.4.1. Pro:
          The earth has gone through numerous life-extinction events and produced new forms of life.

      1.6.4.2. Pro:
      Putting health and safety before environmental concerns is more pragmatic in the current circumstances.

        1.6.4.2.1. Pro:
        Addressing environmental concerns likely involves collective international action, whereas responding to a health crisis can be orchestrated by national governments.

    1.6.5. Pro:
    Discarded face masks are a safety hazard for wildlife.

      1.6.5.1. Pro:
      Many face masks are found discarded on the ground. Animals, like birds [173], can become tangled in the straps and die.

        1.6.5.1.1. Pro:
        In March 2020, masses of discarded masks washed up on a beach [174] near Hong Kong, thereby polluting marine life.

          1.6.5.1.1.1. Con:
          The solution to prevent people from discarding masks in environmentally problematic manners is to encourage alternative means of disposal, not to abandon the policy of masks entirely.

            1.6.5.1.1.1.1. Pro:
            If people cut the strings [175] of their masks when they are disposing of them, this would not be as big an issue.

      1.6.5.2. Con:
      -> See 1.6.5.1.1.1.1.

      1.6.5.3. Pro:
      Turtles and marine life are harmed by plastic pollution. Thus far, an estimated 1.56 billion [176] masks have entered the world's oceans.

    1.6.6. Pro:
    If people opt to wear disposable masks, this could increase pollution.

      1.6.6.1. Pro:
      -> See 1.6.5.1.1.

      1.6.6.2. Pro:
      Prior to COVID-19, many countries were on course to heavily restrict single-use plastics [177]. These environmental initiatives will suffer if masks - which are often single-use - are made mandatory.

        1.6.6.2.1. Pro:
        The plastics industry [178] is positioning itself as an integral part of the fight against COVID-19, thereby undoing the work of environmentalists in shifting public opinion against the key players in plastic pollution.

        1.6.6.2.2. Con:
        The mandatory use of single-use masks would be temporary.

          1.6.6.2.2.1. Con:
          Plastic masks take up to 450 years [179] to decompose. Even if the wearing of them is temporary, their presence and environmental impact is not.

          1.6.6.2.2.2. Pro:
          -> See 1.3.5.2.1.3.

      1.6.6.3. Con:
      If all humans die from a pandemic, environmental conditions will matter little to humans.

        1.6.6.3.1. Con:
        COVID-19 has a 1.4% fatality rate [180]. All humans will not die from this pandemic.

      1.6.6.4. Con:
      There are known washable masks [181] as alternatives to disposable masks to avoid harmful environmental effects.
