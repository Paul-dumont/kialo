Discussion Name:
What are the benefits / harms of Critical Race Theory?

1. Thesis:
Critical Race Theory (CRT) is a benefit to society.

  1.1. Con:
  A race-first interpretation of social dynamics is liable to exacerbate tendencies of racial categorization.

    1.1.1. Con:
    By acknowledging race, society is better able to discuss phenomena, like passing, that exacerbate the social desirability of whiteness.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      Encouraging a focus on race may support right-wing populism [1], and move conversations away from criticisms of economic inequality [2].

        1.1.1.1.1. Con:
        The alternative to acknowledging race is race blindness. This is harmful as people propagate harm by obfuscating [3] systems of oppression.

        1.1.1.1.2. Pro:
        Economic inequality and shared historic exploitation [4] could unify many people who are otherwise pit against one another by nativist politics.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      Supporting whiteness as a distinct category may encourage [5] forms of incendiary nativist thinking akin to those activated [6] by Trump.

        1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
        It is counterintuitive [7] for people to decry group identification when it is made salient, even if it causes harm to others.

        1.1.1.2.2. Con:
        Those who care to remediate the harms of racism are better equipped [8] when made aware of how race is leveraged against others.

      1.1.1.3. Pro:
      Multi-cultural approaches may be more effective for acknowledging people's lived experiences and creating strong teams (p. 207 [9]).

        1.1.1.3.1. Con:
        Multi-culturalism may alienate white people, making it counter-productive to changing mainstream ideas of race and systems of power (p. 207 [10]).

        1.1.1.3.2. Con:
        Multi-cultural approaches often fail to explicitly account for and tackle issues of racism (p. 208 [11]).

        1.1.1.3.3. Pro:
        Multi-cultural approaches may improve understanding [12], as people have more opportunities to talk openly about their culture.

        1.1.1.3.4. Pro:
        Multi-cultural groups are more creative [13] than their monolithic counterparts, as people from different backgrounds can offer new perspectives that inform or alter their view of a problem.

      1.1.1.4. Pro:
      Passing, or the ability for white-presenting Black people to blend into white society [14], exacerbates the desirability of whiteness.

        1.1.1.4.1. Pro:
        Famous people like Sammy Sosa and Michael Jackson underwent treatments to lighten their skin tone [15] and appear whiter, furthering the desirability of whiteness in the public eye.

        1.1.1.4.2. Pro:
        Passing reinforces white racial domination and the economic exploitation of Black people (p. 1713 [16]).

          1.1.1.4.2.1. Pro:
          There are short and long term benefits of passing, unavailable to the majority of Black people (p. 1713 [17]).

          1.1.1.4.2.2. Con:
          The idea of passing often alienates [18] light-skinned people of color from their communities; this causes them harm as they lose support from those that may understand them.

            1.1.1.4.2.2.1. Pro:
            Darker skinned community members are often considered more legitimate and authentic [19] when they engage in community practices, this may deny light skinned Black people their heritage.

          1.1.1.4.2.3. Pro:
          A Black person passing as white is able to assert domination over their own race, intentionally or otherwise (p. 1713 [20]).

          1.1.1.4.2.4. Con:
          A Black person passing as white is simply using a method of survival [21]; most people would do it, given the chance.

        1.1.1.4.3. Pro:
        Passing as white allows white-presenting Black people to assure certain public and private social privileges and levels of attainment unavailable to most Black people (p. 1713 [22]).

        1.1.1.4.4. Con:
        The desire for lightness may be pre-colonial [23]; it is often disconnected from whiteness in a Caucasian sense, and is instead connected to ideas of fairness [24] and cosmopolitanism.

        1.1.1.4.5. Con:
        Passing did not create the systems that harm Black people—these come from white people who encouraged [25] the idea of dividing and conquering.

    1.1.2. Con:
    The racially prejudiced are good at radicalizing others [26]; merely acknowledging race is not enough to exacerbate racist tendencies.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      This situation is best described as an error of correlation and causation [27].

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      Awareness of racialization [28] may be necessary to make political change and push back against radicalization.

      1.1.2.3. Con:
      Racialization and racializing schemas are necessary for activating racial stereotypes (p. 71 [29]).

    1.1.3. Pro:
    All aspects of American life are analyzed through the prism of race [30] under CRT.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      CRT discusses the amount of land taken [31] from indigenous and Black people in the early days of American history.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      The Heritage Foundation, the conservative think tank that wrote the article in the parent claim, is known for taking contributions from corporations to influence politicians at the detriment of workers rights [32].

        1.1.3.2.1. Pro:
        The Heritage Foundation Board of Trustees [33] is filled with bankers, real-estate developers, and hedge-fund owners.

          1.1.3.2.1.1. Pro:
          There is practically no diversity on the Board of Trustees for the Heritage Foundation; it comes as a surprise to exactly no one that they would be critical of CRT.

          1.1.3.2.1.2. Con:
          This is not uncommon; many high-ranking businesspeople serve as Board members [34] across a variety of think tanks and non-profits.

        1.1.3.2.2. Pro:
        The conservative billionaires, the Koch brothers have made financial contributions to the Heritage Foundation [35].

        1.1.3.2.3. Con:
        Not supporting expanded workers rights does not necessarily preclude the think tank from legitimately criticising CRT.

          1.1.3.2.3.1. Pro:
          Many conservatives may oppose expanded workers' rights as they increase costs to businesses and signal increasing government intervention.

          1.1.3.2.3.2. Con:
          CRT was established as a legal practice through an argument against work place discrimination [36]. The theory seeks to hold power to account in a way that is likely to be threatening.

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      The BLM protests and diversity training are both consequences of CRT [37].

      1.1.3.4. Con:
      CRT acknowledges [38] the importance of other demographic factors through intersectionality. Therefore, analysis through the prism of race is unlikely to be reductionist.

    1.1.4. Pro:
    CRT attributes all success to power and refutes the idea of merit, instead claiming it as a mask for white privilege (pp. 9-11 [39]). This exacerbates tensions between different communities.

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      Refuting the idea of merit is problematic when defending the fact that Jewish and Asian Americans are highly successful minority groups. Ignoring merit suggests these marginalized groups are unjustly favored in the distribution of wealth and status.

        1.1.4.1.1. Pro:
        The model minority myth is used by majority groups to drive a wedge [40] between ethnic minorities by generalizing groups, ignorant of their sociocultural context [41].

          1.1.4.1.1.1. Con:
          Despite the model minority myth, many Asian Americans relocated to predominantly Black neighborhoods [42] and are subject to similar levels of mistreatment. This indicates that CRT neither exacerbates existing model minority myths, nor alienates non-Black minority communities.

        1.1.4.1.2. Con:
        Neither Jewish nor Asian Americans were ever enslaved in the United States. The powers-that-be during the period of slavery in the US unjustly held back Black Americans.

          1.1.4.1.2.1. Con:
          Asian Americans have long [43] been treated poorly [44] in the United States; including reports of slavery in the Old West of the US.

            1.1.4.1.2.1.1. Pro:
            Asian Americans have frequently been victims of violence in the US; due to the unjust wave of violence they were experiencing [45] during COVID-19, they received additional protective status [46] from the Hate Crimes Act passed during that time period.

          1.1.4.1.2.2. Pro:
          Forty-four percent of Jewish adults [47] have household incomes in excess of US$100,000, while 35% of American households make less than US$30,000.

      1.1.4.2. Con:
      CRT does not refute the idea of merit to stoke tensions. Rather, it points out that merit-based admission systems hide that one's racial position can influence the indicators of merit [48], meaning it seeks legitimate change.

        1.1.4.2.1. Con:
        POC are growing in numbers in the US Congress [49], Mensa [50], prestigious STEM institutions [51], the military [52], and law enforcement [53]—this trend contradicts the premise of systemic racism which CRT claims to dismantle.

        1.1.4.2.2. Pro:
        Though some people of colour have achieved success, they are still over-represented in prison [54], more likely to experience police brutality [55], and less likely to be wealthy [56]. All of these require change that current ideas of merit halt.

      1.1.4.3. Pro:
      CRT critiques capitalism which was developed by white men. Being anti-capitalist could be seen as being unfairly anti-white [57].

        1.1.4.3.1. Pro:
        Some feel that the anti-capitalist approach taken by some critical race theorists directly challenges [58] the founding principles of the nation.

          1.1.4.3.1.1. Con:
          The founding principles of the nation were made during a time of slavery, when Black slaves didn't count as human beings [59]; these principles need to be challenged if the nation is to move forward and progress.

        1.1.4.3.2. Con:
        Critics often associate CRT with communism to activate Red Scare [60] fears. Instead of challenging the core tenets of CRT, this tactic [61] stokes right wing culture wars.

          1.1.4.3.2.1. Pro:
          Conservatives are more concerned with protecting themselves and sowing political discord [62] than the truthfulness of the news media.

        1.1.4.3.3. Con:
        The success of white individuals was guaranteed by the capitalist system that they developed [63] to favor their endeavors to the detriment of Black people. It is fair to be angry about this system.

          1.1.4.3.3.1. Con:
          Nowadays, equality legislation [64] ensures direct discrimination is less likely to occur in job markets, giving everyone the potential to benefit equally from capitalism.

          1.1.4.3.3.2. Pro:
          Black people raised in abject poverty are far less likely to find a path to success [65] than their white counterparts who grew up attending good schools.

          1.1.4.3.3.3. Con:
          Everyone participates in [66] and is exploited by capitalism such that it is no longer possible to attribute the system and the harms it causes to one group.

            1.1.4.3.3.3.1. Con:
            In the United States, only white people have seen a growth in their ratio [67] of millionaire households. Such imbalances indicate that capitalism is not race blind as many claim.

          1.1.4.3.3.4. Pro:
          Attempts by Black people to gain wealth are often actively decimated [68] by white people.

      1.1.4.4. Con:
      Tension between communities is only illegitimate if stoked by lies. In the case of white privilege and the continuation of racist systems, antipathy from Black communities is justified.

        1.1.4.4.1. Pro:
        White people often exploit [69] the systems around them to maintain power, even if they are acting under the veneer of progressivism.

          1.1.4.4.1.1. Pro:
          White liberals often become avoidant [70] of situations where they could be called racist, using their white privilege to construct a world wherein they can claim to support progress while avoiding actually working to achieve it.

          1.1.4.4.1.2. Con:
          White people do not need to exploit social systems to maintain power; they are already in place to help them maintain it without needing to do anything more.

        1.1.4.4.2. Pro:
        Lies about CRT's origins, and associated beliefs and commentaries are frequently disseminated by the media [71], across both sides of the political spectrum. Such misinformation can cause serious confusion and create more tension [72] within communities.

    1.1.5. Pro:
    There is no possibility of remaining neutral [73] under CRT. Everything is either black or white, so to speak.

      1.1.5.1. Pro:
      Racial essentialism and racial identification correlate with a stronger tendency [74] to categorize people according to race.

        1.1.5.1.1. Pro:
        Studies suggest that those who identify strongly [75] with their racial identity are liable to categorize by racial attributes to a greater degree than those who do not.

        1.1.5.1.2. Con:
        CRT is not the only ideology that encourages racial identification [76] or essentialism; it is, however, uniquely liberatory which is necessary for counteracting [77] pernicious dialogues in society.

      1.1.5.2. Con:
      By discouraging neutrality, CRT makes it more easier for people to be heard [78] and resist dismissal from the more powerful.

        1.1.5.2.1. Pro:
        Anger can be incredibly politically utile [79]; it spurs change in otherwise stagnant situations.

        1.1.5.2.2. Pro:
        There is a long history of activist resistance to neutrality; feminists in the 60s and 70s strongly believed that the personal is political [80].

        1.1.5.2.3. Con:
        By encouraging polarised behaviour, CRT unwittingly plays into racist and reductionary stereotypes [81] of Black anger [82].

      1.1.5.3. Pro:
      Political polarisation is high [83], often fuelled by social media [84]. This may limit beneficial change, especially when exacerbated.

        1.1.5.3.1. Pro:
        Some believe that the reaction to CRT is being fuelled by network propaganda [85] akin to conspiracy theories; this drives distrust and disinformation.

        1.1.5.3.2. Con:
        The radical actions noted by CRT may be useful for invigorating fatigued activists [86] who find the current system of slow change lacking. This counteracts the inertia of polarisation.

  1.2. Con:
  CRT undermines legitimate attempts to resolve racism, slowing the rate of change.

    1.2.1. Con:
    Resolving racism is not limited to just one single solution; it is a process of unlearning and rebuilding from an exploitative system by using all available tools to assist with that endeavor.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      By noting the ways in which legal systems [87] entrench inequality, CRT makes institutional racism easier to remediate.

        1.2.1.1.1. Con:
        Black people have known their entire lives about institutional racism, white people are the ones with the power to make change but seem unwilling to participate [88] and learn more.

        1.2.1.1.2. Pro:
        CRT grew from Critical Legal Studies [89], which noted that the law was not objective. This may help CRT theorists challenge the influence the law has over moral societal norms.

          1.2.1.1.2.1. Con:
          Oftentimes the law does not reflect [90] moral society or objectiveness; it simply reflects what lawmakers decide is relevant and important.

          1.2.1.1.2.2. Pro:
          In many ways, the development of CRT was wholly based on the unjust legal system [91] itself and expanded from there to encompass other areas of society.

        1.2.1.1.3. Pro:
        CRT promotes the idea of legal indeterminacy [92] which emphasises the ways in which different forms of authority may change legal outcomes.

        1.2.1.1.4. Con:
        Civil rights organizations like the NAACP [93] already call out racist legislature and agitate for change. Therefore, CRT does not offer a unique benefit.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      Unlike other theories, CRT may advocate for the jettisoning [94] of other schools of thought, which is unproductive in the long term.

        1.2.1.2.1. Pro:
        A combination [95] of a variety of anti-racist tools is more likely help change current systems more quickly; CRT should not encourage any of them to be sidelined.

        1.2.1.2.2. Pro:
        Some feel that CRT is likely to fully discard ideas of liberalism [96], restricting future conversations.

          1.2.1.2.2.1. Con:
          Liberalism co-existed with slavery and its ideals were not applied to the enslaved; it should rightly be discarded [97] if it can co-exist alongside such atrocities.

            1.2.1.2.2.1.1. Con:
            A ideology’s application and the morality of its principles are not inextricably linked. Slavery existed before and within Liberalism. However, Liberalism also led to the world’s first widespread abolishment of slave trading.

        1.2.1.2.3. Con:
        Under Kuhn's theory of the development of science [98], periods of great upheaval are normal and necessary for the development of ideas, even if they unsettle previously answered questions.

    1.2.2. Pro:
    Conversations about race and racism are limited [99] under this theoretical framework.

      1.2.2.1. Pro:
      CRT is unlikely to be effective at remediating discrimination because it inflames [100] political polarisation in the US.

        1.2.2.1.1. Con:
        CRT is not responsible for the current culture wars [101] taking place in the US, it is merely a victim [102] of them.

        1.2.2.1.2. Con:
        Learning how interpersonal relationships influence social structure [103] in terms of race is the first step towards nullifying tensions between races, not exacerbating them.

        1.2.2.1.3. Pro:
        Some CRT theorists suggest that reparations for slavery [104] are a solution [105] for social inequities. Yet this forces people to pay to fix historic inequities they did not contribute to.

          1.2.2.1.3.1. Con:
          Without reparations, many oppressed groups will continue [106] to suffer from economic inequality to the benefit of their oppressors. Thus, even if unpopular, they are a just policy.

            1.2.2.1.3.1.1. Con:
            Individualized requests [107] for funds often undermine radical efforts for reparations by trivializing racial and/or gendered injustices.

          1.2.2.1.3.2. Pro:
          As people do not pick their place [108] in society, it is reductive to attack them on this basis by forcing them to pay reparations.

            1.2.2.1.3.2.1. Pro:
            One CRT theorist suggests that white privately-owned land and wealth [109] should be seized and redistributed among the Black community, a suggestion which likely threatens white people.

            1.2.2.1.3.2.2. Con:
            People often underestimate [110] the extent to which their communities [111] and origins [112] enable them to achieve in life, meaning that they are likely to be defensive of their gains.

          1.2.2.1.3.3. Con:
          People always gain from their position [113] in society. It is these individual gains that are being attacked in an attempt to encourage people to dismantle the system.

            1.2.2.1.3.3.1. Pro:
            Throughout American history, white people have profited off [114] of the backs of Black people, who were given nothing for their labor or troubles. This solidified white wealth, and ensured the restriction of Black development.

          1.2.2.1.3.4. Pro:
          People do not have a moral obligation to rectify historical harms they did not cause [115].

            1.2.2.1.3.4.1. Con:
            Limited access to capital in Black neighborhoods will prevent the transfer of US$82 billion in generational wealth (p. 14 [116]). Clearly, people today are directly harmed by historic actions.

            1.2.2.1.3.4.2. Pro:
            There are many points in which the causal chain [117] can be broken between slavery and wealth inequality today. This limits [118] moral responsibility.

        1.2.2.1.4. Pro:
        Political tensions stoked by CRT activists are often individualized, making people feel victimized [119]. This is illegitimate as they are not always responsible for the system they win from.

          1.2.2.1.4.1. Pro:
          Moves to ban CRT often cite [120] a refusal to feel guilt, or be burdened with a responsibility for racism, as a justification for why the theory is illegitimate.

      1.2.2.2. Con:
      Those who find CRT alienating are unlikely to have engaged in good faith with alternative analyses of racism in society.

        1.2.2.2.1. Pro:
        Current bills banning CRT from schools are nebulously phrased [121] and likely to limit most conversations around equity. This indicates critics of CRT are acting in bad faith.

        1.2.2.2.2. Con:
        This framing is dismissive to the ways in which some feel that CRT unduly attacks them [122] and marrs their character with little recourse to change.

      1.2.2.3. Pro:
      CRT leaves little room for discussion [123], pitting Black and white people against each other without space for concessions or flexible interpretation.

        1.2.2.3.1. Con:
        Both Black and white people agree that being Black is a disadvantage [124] in the United States; CRT merely reflects this reality in its analysis instead of contributing toward it.

          1.2.2.3.1.1. Con:
          Most white people have little ability to empathize [125] with their Black counterparts. This limits their ability to view CRT as beneficial.

        1.2.2.3.2. Con:
        Teachers focused on providing their students with a truthful, unbiased education—without any space for opposing viewpoints—are frequently swept up in the fervor behind CRT, often without prior knowledge [126] of the theory.

        1.2.2.3.3. Pro:
        The CRT concept of interest convergence [127] posits that white people do not seek [128] to improve the lives of Black people unless it benefits them. Thus, attempts by white people to remediate racism are seen as disingenuous to the detriment of activism.

          1.2.2.3.3.1. Con:
          Interest convergence assumes large groups of people have homogenous interests and are incapable of moral, unselfish thought (pp. 164-165 [129]). There is no reason for this to be true en masse.

          1.2.2.3.3.2. Pro:
          Some might argue that the overwhelming support for BLM came from peer pressure and a desire to appear woke [130]. This would still adhere to the principles of interest convergence.

          1.2.2.3.3.3. Pro:
          Derrick Bell posited that a contributing factor to the US government's reduction in lynchings was a desire to reform its image in the developing world during the Cold War (pp. 19-20 [131]).

        1.2.2.3.4. Pro:
        CRT views “interest-divergence [132]” as commonplace; through this lens, the continued active suppression of minorities [133] is advantageous for white power-holders.

          1.2.2.3.4.1. Pro:
          Prince Edward County School Board in Virginia chose to abolish the school system [134] rather than integrate Black schoolchildren into white schools.

            1.2.2.3.4.1.1. Con:
            After five years, integration finally reached Prince Edward County after the Supreme Court of the United States required the Board of Education to re-establish the school system for all students [135] in the county.

          1.2.2.3.4.2. Pro:
          One theorist posits that interest-divergence [136] occurs in the UK through the disenfranchisement of Black children in the education system. This reduces competition for white children.

          1.2.2.3.4.3. Con:
          Interest convergence is occurs more regularly [137] than divergence as institutions realise that their place in society is threatened by critiques of their racist behaviour.

          1.2.2.3.4.4. Con:
          In an increasingly global world, with a burgeoning Indian [138] and Chinese [139] middle class, being racist is increasingly unprofitable if attempting to drawn in international clientele.

        1.2.2.3.5. Pro:
        Everyone is subject to the birth lottery [140]. People do not get to choose their heritage and therefore should not be considered incapable of doing something as a result.

    1.2.3. Pro:
    Rather than relying upon rational inquiry, CRT centers on the use of narrative and storytelling which seem flimsy and subjective by comparison.

      1.2.3.1. Pro:
      By emphasising the subjective, phenomenological experience of racism, CRT replaces argumentation [141] based on empirical evidence with anecdotal claims. This is practically and morally problematic.

        1.2.3.1.1. Pro:
        The legal scholar, Richard Posner argues that approaching institutionalised racism from a subjective perspective alone reinforces stereotypes of the intellectual capabilities of ethnic minorities (p. 8 [142]).

        1.2.3.1.2. Pro:
        Without approaching institutionalised racism empirically, it is hard to identify patterns of behaviour, important causal factors, and, as a result, effectively prioritise and strategise for change.

        1.2.3.1.3. Con:
        Rationality, as defined and propagated in the West, comes alongside political commitments [143] that marginalize non-white voices and knowledge systems. Storytelling can help defy this stifling system.

          1.2.3.1.3.1. Con:
          By choosing political commitments based on their history and origin, CRT can dangerously invoke a bifurcation between the West and rest that is orientalist and racist.

      1.2.3.2. Con:
      Some critical race theorists believe that storytelling [144] is more useful than objective research for helping people to fully comprehend the extent and effects of racism.

        1.2.3.2.1. Con:
        In a divided world, relying on narratives instead of facts may encourage an increased divergence in opinion that erodes people's sense of a shared reality [145].

        1.2.3.2.2. Con:
        Anecdotal evidence is typically emotionally-biased and unfalsifiable [146]. As such, one cannot make broad assumptions based on the experience of an individual, or small group of individuals.

        1.2.3.2.3. Con:
        Memories are highly subjective [147] and more than likely colored by biases, both conscious and subconscious. Where possible, academics should use more reliable methods to guide their research.

          1.2.3.2.3.1. Con:
          Counter story-telling, in particular, is a useful method to challenge culturally homogenous discourse by drawing attention to the perspecitve of marginalized [148] voices.

          1.2.3.2.3.2. Con:
          As CRT often advocates for broad social changes based on principled alterations to legal or education systems, it is less important for them to have materially certain storytelling underpinning them.

          1.2.3.2.3.3. Con:
          Narrative storytelling is vital for research as it draws genuine interest from readers; compelling [149] storytelling is more important than the veracity.

          1.2.3.2.3.4. Pro:
          It is unfair to use storytelling to manipulate or market [150] policy recommendations when their ramifications can have huge impacts on people.

          1.2.3.2.3.5. Pro:
          Memories are often highly inaccurate [151]; frequently they do not reflect the reality of what happened so are not reliable sources for building policy and other influential societal changes.

        1.2.3.2.4. Pro:
        As storytelling is a powerful psychological tool [152] it can offer a wider array of benefits to scholars engaging with CRT than alternative forms of knowledge presentation.

          1.2.3.2.4.1. Pro:
          Storytelling can help to build empathy by releasing oxytocin [153]. CRT therefore can solicit more support for marginalised communities through this method.

            1.2.3.2.4.1.1. Pro:
            The identifiable victim effect [154], which stories tap into, is well-recognised in psychology. This can be leveraged to engender support and make readers more amenable to the central message.

            1.2.3.2.4.1.2. Con:
            Empathy [155] is not necessarily productive as it still operates within someone's moral system. If such a system is limited, so too are their uses of empathy.

            1.2.3.2.4.1.3. Pro:
            Words and stories can stimulate neural coupling [156], activating the same regions of the brain in the storyteller and listener. This builds empathy between the two individuals and may be very useful for bridging ideological divides.

          1.2.3.2.4.2. Pro:
          A major benefit of storytelling is the relief and emotional healing [157] following a profound or intense story; this may be particularly meaningful for CRT scholars who recount tales of racism. This catharsis is a useful healing moment.

          1.2.3.2.4.3. Con:
          Stories don't have to be truthful to have a profound psychological impact, with fake news as proof (p. 388 [158]). Given this, people may easily be led astray when storytelling activism is popularised.

        1.2.3.2.5. Pro:
        Racism is a phenomenological experience [159]. Storytelling is a more effective way to express this reality than other canonical methods of research.

          1.2.3.2.5.1. Pro:
          Storytelling serves as a useful springboard for later research on the universality of the experiences described.

            1.2.3.2.5.1.1. Con:
            Storytelling is primarily anecdotal evidence that does not serve to draw wider conclusions from the general population.

            1.2.3.2.5.1.2. Pro:
            By collating the shared experiences aggregated through storytelling, scientists can work to improve the standing of Black folks.

          1.2.3.2.5.2. Pro:
          The scientific method was created by white men [160] for use in white experiments and research. There are bound to be racial gaps that were not integrated because of the lack of diversity while the methods were being developed.

            1.2.3.2.5.2.1. Con:
            The Arab world dominated science for 500 years [161] prior to the European Renaissance; their advancements, especially the invention of the scientific method [162], were likely a catalyst [163] for the Renaissance itself.

            1.2.3.2.5.2.2. Pro:
            Using an alternative method for scientific discovery is a form of protest against methods that have been used for racist means.

              1.2.3.2.5.2.2.1. Pro:
              The "double helix [164]" and "bioassay [165]" methods are relevant, non-racist alternatives to the scientific method.

          1.2.3.2.5.3. Con:
          Canonical scientific methods are based upon principles of rationality [166] that are universal to humans and do not vary in accordance with racial categories. This is a better form of research than storytelling as it lacks subjectivity.

            1.2.3.2.5.3.1. Con:
