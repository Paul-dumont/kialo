Discussion Name:
Should Florida Pass HB 1 (Social Media Use for Minors)?

1. Thesis:
This mandates third-party age verification for minors on social media, bans all accounts for minors younger than 16, and requires platforms to disclose content and data policies, with penalties for non-compliance.

  1.1. Con:
  It is nearly impossible to accurately estimate age. Asking for it directly is just basically asking them to lie, AI estimates have proven to be horribly inaccurate(such as when popular online game Roblox implemented one). Only IDs are not even 100% foolproof as you can use someone else's or use/purchase already verified accounts and all it takes is one data breach for potentially millions of people's identities being stolen.

  1.2. Pro:
  Requires resources and reporting mechanisms for harmful behaviors.

    1.2.1. Con:
    Requiring resources for this mechanism isn't inherently a good thing, if anything its bad since the company has less resources that can go to other things like maintenance and expansion of their social media for eligible users and users outside of the influence of this bill.

  1.3. Con:
  This would not work, the big social media companies would rather not do this because it limits user traffic, it is far more likely that in order to avoid the policy that they will just lobby the government into either giving up the bill or stall it out indefinitely.

  1.4. Pro:
  Allows for civil penalties and legal action against non-compliant platforms.

  1.5. Con:
  May increase operational costs for social media companies.

    1.5.1. Pro:
    It also limits user traffic, meaning that on top of that, the social media company makes less money too which impacts those outside of this policy’s jurisdiction which is unfair to those people.

  1.6. Con:
  Could result in over-censorship or limit freedom of expression.

  1.7. Con:
  Might lead to privacy concerns over third-party involvement in age verification.

  1.8. Con:
  Enforcement could be challenged by interstate commerce and jurisdictional issues.

  1.9. Con:
  Potential for decreased user base for social media platforms.

  1.10. Pro:
  Social media has been proven in growing research to be significantly sticky for its behaviorally addicting mechanisms. This mandate reduces the use of social media among minors, thereby protecting them from engaging in negatively reinforcing addicting behaviors.

    1.10.1. Con:
    Children have always been addicted to talking to their peers.

  1.11. Pro:
  Pushes minors to spend more time doing things besides social media. Since social media is the major driver of smartphone hourly usage, this would lower their overall screen time and allow for them to spend time outside or engaging in irrefutably more beneficial activities.

    1.11.1. Con:
    This also allows for the potential that they may engage in worse activities(eg. getting involved in petty crime).

  1.12. Con:
  Lessens the ability minors have to find and communicate with friends, peers, and find community.

    1.12.1. Pro:
    Friendships gradually become more spread out, especially at the high school age. Using social media like Discord and, or Snapchat has become integral to maintaining connections, and planning in-person meetups.

    1.12.2. Pro:
    The internet has become the most primary source of information, and it will be so for a very long time. Restricting access to these because of “age” is not effective.

    1.12.3. Con:
    Social media lessons the ability for minors to actually engage in face-to-face communication, and more so, to be more effective body language readers. It can lead the person to communicate in unhealthy ways and effectively to say things online they would never say to someone's face.

      1.12.3.1. Con:
      The reverse is equally valid: Lack of exposure to written communication can decrease literacy & can let visual prejudices against marginalized groups go unchallenged.

    1.12.4. Con:
    social media has proven to increase depression and anxiety within teens this only results in difficulties when connecting in person with others. Is it worth it to trade face to face communication with online connections?

      1.12.4.1. Con:
      someone might have community over niche interests or identities not present in their physical locality

      1.12.4.2. Con:
      Lack of social interaction also causes depression, and with less and less people visiting their friends physically nowadays, online social media platforms have proven to be the most common way to maintain a connection with friends.

  1.13. Con:
  Infringement on Privacy and Autonomy: The bill proposes extensive parental control and monitoring, potentially infringing on minors' privacy and autonomy. Adolescents need to develop their own digital identities and learn to manage their online presence. Excessive oversight could undermine their ability to navigate and understand the digital world independently.

    1.13.1. Con:
    Children will still get experience with this, just later as adults with brains which are more developed

  1.14. Con:
  ‘Age’ is a policy standard that has proven to be quite ineffective ever since adopted. Early teens commit incredible crimes even without much access to the internet, and early teens also get more knowledgeable with/without much access to the internet. Age shouldn't be a metric.

    1.14.1. Con:
    Even still, age can still be used as a general measurement as kids of the same age even with varying personalities still have roughly the same maturity. Also pointing out crime happening in teens does not prove age is not a viable metric.

  1.15. Con:
  Since the most harmful mechanisms of social media are mostly kept unsolved, passing HB 1 will not be efficient while the mandates require strict regulations that can be abused.

    1.15.1. Pro:
    Addressing the advertising tracking mechanisms and databrokers is far more effective.

    1.15.2. Pro:
    Informing children about platforms like Kialo can have more positive impact.

  1.16. Con:
  When something disappears from people's lives, they tend to seek alternatives, which in this case are likely to be riskier and less moderated platforms or behaviors.

    1.16.1. Con:
    Enforcement could be done to these sites as well.

      1.16.1.1. Con:
      That would not be feasible since the most popular examples of these dangerous websites are under different jurisdiction, the most popular websites for pornography are under a central company that is based in Canada(outside the applicable zone of this bill). For unregulated discussion, 4chan is completely anonymous and even the British parliament previously failed to identify and sue its owner.

      1.16.1.2. Con:
      If your solution for people finding alternatives is to keep enforcing this bill on those other sites, the law would look less like a child protection act and more and more like a data harvest. The general public would not accept this, and the party that established this would be voted out.

  1.17. Pro:
  For many children, bullying ends when the school day ends. Social media can allow bullying to continue 24/7 which could have a significant impact on the mental health and safety of the child/minor.

    1.17.1. Con:
    Socialization also ends when the school day ends for many children. As much as social media can harm, it connects people and can become an escape from bullying. Allowing it but pushing restrictions or having parents check devices regularly is a less extreme compromise for both sides.

  1.18. Pro:
  Enhances age verification to protect minors.

  1.19. Con:
  The potential for data exploitation is too great. Almost all of these policies try and enforce the use of a state ID online to verify age, but this opens up these young people to data breaches, targeted advertising, and identity theft.

    1.19.1. Con:
    There are methods [1] of doing so which do not require storing user data centrally on a government server in a way that can be leaked. No modern implementation would have this security vulnerability.

      1.19.1.1. Con:
      The methods [2](as you can see, exact same source) that you claim to not have these security vulnerabilities only moves the problem, the article talks about using a third party provider to verify your age for you “securely” when in reality all this does is that now when people try to leak data they target this third party provider instead of the social media platform. Additionally coordinating and setting up all these different companies for this one policy would be a logistical nightmare.

  1.20. Pro:
  we have to be more careful to our future generations

    1.20.1. Con:
    This argument fails to prove that social media usage is bad, only that “we have to be careful with out future generations” which is very vague and abstract and doesn’t relate back to the original question at all

  1.21. Pro:
  Always good to reduce screen time

    1.21.1. Con:
    The main argument of screen time being bad is that it comes at the expense of your vision. However it has been scientifically proven that genetics play far more of a factor than screens ever could.

    1.21.2. Con:
    Not all screen time is bad, as platforms can also be educational, such as on YouTube you could watch a few videos and acquire a skill or knowledge(eg. many school subject videos are also on YouTube)

  1.22. Pro:
  Prohibits account creation by under-16s, reducing exposure to harmful content.

  1.23. Con:
  This wouldn't work in today's digital age because most of the newer generations have social media as young as when their in their single digits. The age regulation would not only be useless because of the era we are in, but because kids rely on social media to connect as that is what is taught and learned.

  1.24. Con:
  Platforms already disclose a lot of information, such as cookies, policies and agreements. These disclosures are intentionally designed to be a wall of text, hard to read and understand. Nobody reads those.

    1.24.1. Pro:
    This is true. For example, part of the privacy policy on Snapchat [3] essentially allows them to see your location every time you open Snapchat once you open the map(basically a map of current locations of yourself and others).

  1.25. Pro:
  can be so distracting to the students have a low attention span and are struggling with studies, so its more good to restrict them

    1.25.1. Con:
    That doesn’t speak for the majority of students, especially those in late middle and high school, most students still do just fine so you can’t make the claim it’s worth it to pass this bill just because a small percentage of students do poorly.

  1.26. Pro:
  students get in romantic relationships in such a less age where they 'know about themselves too and social media encourages them which is such kind of content which is an assault and harmful for them

  1.27. Pro:
  Mandates transparent policies on content and data usage.

  1.28. Con:
  This is pointless since it can easily be bypassed by using a VPN set to a location where this law is not in effect while registering and using social media.

    1.28.1. Con:
    One time where they forget to use a VPN means that their real location is leaked and the site can ban them and could potentially land them a criminal charge that would ruin their future.

      1.28.1.1. Con:
      VPNs can be set to auto activate continuously and the cost to track down, arrest, and prosecute them far exceeds the amount of harm they do by breaking this law so no one will come after them. This same reason is also why piracy almost never gets people arrested despite being explicitly against the law.
