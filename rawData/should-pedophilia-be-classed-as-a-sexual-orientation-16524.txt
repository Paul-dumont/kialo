Discussion Name:
Should pedophilia be classed as a sexual orientation?

1. Thesis:
Pedophilia should be classed as a sexual orientation.

  1.1. Con:
  Classing pedophilia as a mental disorder will ensure affected individuals receive appropriate treatment.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    There is documented evidence supporting the effectiveness of treatments for pedophilia. This shows that pedophilia is a curable mental disorder, rather than a sexual orientation.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      Treatment for pedophilia can only help pedophiles regulate and cope with their sexual impulses and urges, not eliminate the underlying attraction (p. 465 [1]), which makes it a sexual orientation.

        1.1.1.1.1. Con:
        Classing pedophilia as a mental disorder will ensure that relevant resources and interventions are classified as "mental health interventions," removing barriers to accessibility.

          1.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
          The therapeutic techniques used in preventive interventions provided to pedophiles are similar to those used with people with mental health conditions [2]. For accuracy, therefore, the condition should be classed as a mental disorder.

          1.1.1.1.1.2. Con:
          Among pedophiles, arguably the most significant barrier to accessibility is fear of exposure. Classing pedophilia as a mental health intervention will not absolve practitioners of their duty to report [3], meaning accessibility will still be poor.

            1.1.1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
            Reporting requirements can make therapy feel more like an interrogation [4], because therapists must ascertain the extent to which their client is a threat to children. This may increase their likelihood of dropping out of therapy.

            1.1.1.1.1.2.2. Pro:
            Practitioners are required to inform clients that certain disclosures may necessitate mandatory reporting [5]. In this way, therapy becomes yet another place where pedophiles cannot be their authentic selves, thereby jeopardizing recovery.

            1.1.1.1.1.2.3. Con:
            Confidentiality is limited not by the status of pedophilia as a mental disorder but by national laws. In countries with iron-clad confidentiality clauses [6], interventions have proven to be accessible and effective in preventing pedophilic acts.

          1.1.1.1.1.3. Pro:
          Online and offline [7] support groups are effective in preventing [8] child sexual abuse, but they are often targeted and shut down [9]. This is less likely to happen if these communities are reclassified as mental health support groups.

        1.1.1.1.2. Pro:
        Non-offending pedophiles believe that claims about being cured of their condition only serve to give them false hope [10] and that the emphasis should instead be on helping them manage their urges.

        1.1.1.1.3. Con:
        Limited evidence supporting the effectiveness of a cure for pedophilia is likely attributable to sampling bias resulting from reliance on prison inmates, who presumably differ from non-offenders and offenders who have not been convicted (p. 469 [11]).

      1.1.1.2. Pro:
      Classing pedophilia as a sexual orientation while attempting to treat the condition sends the problematic message that sexual orientation can be changed [12], which is harmful to the LGBTQ+ community. For this reason, it should be classed as a disorder.

      1.1.1.3. Pro:
      Medical treatments, including anti-libidinal drugs and castration (pp. 465-467 [13]), can effectively reduce the sex drive, which will inevitably eliminate pedophilic impulses.

        1.1.1.3.1. Pro:
        A 2022 literature review found that, though there is limited research on the topic, the available evidence supports the effectiveness of testosterone-lowering drugs [14] in managing pedophilia.

          1.1.1.3.1.1. Con:
          Medication is suitable only for a very small percentage of pedophiles, namely, those so preoccupied with their sexual urges [15] that they interfere with their normal functioning. Nine out of 10 pedophiles, therefore, need a different treatment.

          1.1.1.3.1.2. Pro:
          Testosterone is a hormone closely associated with the sex drive [16], including in pedophiles. Therefore, any method of reducing testosterone will likely reduce libido [17] and, consequently, pedophilic urges.

        1.1.1.3.2. Con:
        Pedophilia is characterized by not only sexual desire but also emotional attraction [18]. Therefore, even if sexual desire reduces, pedophiles may continue to be emotionally drawn to children.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    Pedophilic disorder is included in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5 [19]), published by the American Psychiatric Association.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      In the DSM-5, pedophilic disorder is listed as a paraphilic disorder, based on forensic considerations. Thus, those best informed on the condition—psychiatrists and forensic experts—believe that it is a disorder (p. 191-192 [20]).

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      Pedophilia has a high rate of comorbidity [21] with other mental disorders, including mood, personality, and paraphilic disorders, suggesting that these conditions may be rooted in a broader pattern of dysfunction.

        1.1.2.2.1. Pro:
        Pedophiles are more likely than their counterparts to have a mother with a psychiatric history (p. 463 [22]), implying that the condition may be one specific expression of a gene that predisposes one to develop mental disorders.

        1.1.2.2.2. Con:
        "Comorbidity" implies that pedophilia is a disorder, when in reality, they may simply be co-occurring [23] and attributable to a third variable. Pedophilia stigma, for example, may trigger the onset of these mental health conditions.

      1.1.2.3. Con:
      The DSM-5 describes pedophilia as a sexual orientation [24], implying that the condition is indeed a sexual orientation, albeit a disordered one.

        1.1.2.3.1. Con:
        The APA clarified that the use of the term "sexual orientation" was an error [25] and that the more appropriate term, "sexual interest," will be used in subsequent prints and electronic versions of the manual.

          1.1.2.3.1.1. Con:
          The APA decided to remove the term "sexual orientation" in response to public criticism [26]. This is bad science because they have allowed public opinion to inform scientific opinion, thereby preserving harmful myths about the condition.

        1.1.2.3.2. Pro:
        Many DSM-5 diagnoses pair value-neutral terms like "personality" with "disorder [27]" to characterize deviance in an otherwise-normal human attribute. Accordingly, pedophilia is best characterized as a disordered sexual orientation.

      1.1.2.4. Con:
      The DSM-5 distinguishes between pedophilia and pedophilic disorder [28]. The latter requires the presence of distress and dysfunction, meaning not all pedophiles have a disorder.

      1.1.2.5. Pro:
      The American Psychological Association [29] issued a statement clarifying their position on pedophilia, classing it as a mental disorder.

        1.1.2.5.1. Con:
        Not all psychologists reject the classification of pedophilia [30] as a sexual orientation. Indeed, some have testified accordingly in parliament [31], despite opposition from ideological counterparts.

        1.1.2.5.2. Pro:
        Some psychologists have coined terms such as "sexual preference disorder [32]" to include pedophilia, demonstrating that terms other than "sexual orientation" can be used to capture the nature of the condition.

    1.1.3. Con:
    The insanity plea will entitle pedophiles who commit sex crimes against children to more lenient sentencing [33] on the grounds that they have a mental disorder.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      Defendants can invoke the "irresistible impulse" defense [34], arguing that they had no control over their behavior. This can be used and misused by pedophiles to receive a "not guilty" verdict.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      The insanity plea is largely defunct in many jurisdictions [35], and when used, they are unlikely to be successful. The same applies to pedophiles [36] who invoke the insanity defense.

      1.1.3.3. Con:
      Neuroscientific findings suggest that brain trauma can significantly limit free will and, in some cases, manifest as acquired pedophilia [37]. In such cases, courts have rightly ruled that the defendants are not liable.

      1.1.3.4. Pro:
      Pedophiles whose insanity defense is accepted are admitted to psychiatric institutions and subsequently released. Because no one can guarantee [38] that they have been "cured," children will continue to be at risk.

  1.2. Con:
  Classing pedophilia as a sexual orientation will normalize something that is morally and legally reprehensible.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    "Sexual orientation" is a term that has typically been associated with the LGBTQ+ community. Associating this term with something commonly perceived as morally reprehensible could significantly derail the gay rights movement.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      Homosexuality and pedophilia have often been conflated [39] to the detriment of the LGBTQ+ community [40]. Grouping them under a common label makes it easy for homophobes [41] to legitimize this conflation.

      1.2.1.2. Pro:
      From a moral perspective, pedophilia is distinct because, unlike other sexual orientations, its expression is universally considered unethical, socially unacceptable, and harmful.

        1.2.1.2.1. Con:
        Sexual contact is not the only way human beings achieve sexual release. Non-offending pedophiles could, therefore, use alternative ways of sexual expression, such as masturbation, to ensure they do not harm a child.

        1.2.1.2.2. Con:
        About 50% of countries [42] allow child marriage, a figure much higher than the number of countries where gay marriage is legal [43]. Social, moral, and legal judgments, thus, can and do vary by sexual orientation and cultural context.

          1.2.1.2.2.1. Con:
          Child marriage is often arranged [44] based on social, economic, and political considerations [45], rather than on romantic or sexual attraction. Thus, social acceptance of child marriage cannot be generalized to pedophilia.

          1.2.1.2.2.2. Pro:
          Moral aversion to pedophilia is a recent development in modern societies, as demonstrated by the history, prevalence, and celebration of pedophilic acts in several cultures [46].

            1.2.1.2.2.2.1. Con:
            Ancient societies often treated children as "mini-adults [47]," exposing them to adult activities, including sexual activity. Improved scientific understanding of child development is, thus, responsible for modern norms condemning pedophilia.

            1.2.1.2.2.2.2. Pro:
            In ancient Rome and Greece, boys were entered into pedarastic relationships with older men, while girls were arranged to marry older men. These practices were considered a rite of passage into adulthood [48].

        1.2.1.2.3. Pro:
        Pedophilia is morally distinct from other sexual orientations because it refers to sexual attraction to someone who cannot consent. Classifying it as a sexual orientation may erase this distinction.

          1.2.1.2.3.1. Pro:
          Classing pedophilia as a sexual orientation could set a dangerous precedent supporting the reclassification of other sexual orientations characterized by a lack of consent, for example, zoophilia and necrophilia [49].

          1.2.1.2.3.2. Con:
          Attraction is not bound by the ethics of consent. For example, sexual fantasies [50] involving someone who does not reciprocate sexual interest are common, yet this is not considered immoral or illegal.

      1.2.1.3. Con:
      The scientific community depathologized homosexuality [51] amid widespread homophobia. Similarly, the status of pedophilia as a sexual orientation should be treated as an academic question, not a moral or sociopolitical issue.

      1.2.1.4. Con:
      Having experienced the injustice of alienation based on an attribute over which one has no control, the LGBTQ+ community should not be complicit in the vilification of a more marginalized sexual minority group [52].

    1.2.2. Con:
    The law informs society that child sexual abuse is a serious criminal offense. Therefore, preventing the normalization of pedophilia through moral policing is unnecessary, or worse, counterproductive.

      1.2.2.1. Pro:
      Moral policing bypasses the due course of law [53]. This is particularly harmful to innocent people who have been falsely accused of pedophilia or child sexual abuse.

        1.2.2.1.1. Pro:
        Moral policing is inherently dangerous [54] and has been weaponized [55] against innocent people. This is what happened to Bijan Ebrahimi, who was falsely called a pedophile and burned to death—an act that was later found to be racially motivated.

        1.2.2.1.2. Pro:
        Belief in the insufficiency of the law is likely a motivating factor behind the growth of vigilante groups that track down pedophiles and, in doing so, hinder rather than help [56] the police in tackling child abuse.

        1.2.2.1.3. Con:
        Convicted child sex offenders face a high likelihood of being killed by other prison inmates [57], proving that legal consequences are not considered sufficient justice, even by those with questionable judgement.

      1.2.2.2. Con:
      The age of consent varies significantly across cultures, going as low as 11 years [58]. In the absence of legal prohibition, socially enforced moral codes [59] are needed to protect children from what may otherwise be branded as "consensual" sexual activity.

      1.2.2.3. Pro:
      Child sexual abuse rates have been consistently declining for decades, despite recent calls to classify pedophilia as a sexual orientation (p. 4 [60]). Therefore, reclassification is unlikely to change this trend.

      1.2.2.4. Con:
      Due to legal loopholes, potential offenders may believe that they can get away with sex crimes [61]. In this regard, moral policing serves to warn them of the social ramifications of their actions, lowering their chances of harming a child.

        1.2.2.4.1. Con:
        Many offenders who have been abusing children for decades commit suicide [62] when finally faced with legal or social consequences. Thus, fear of neither the law nor exposure sufficiently deters them from abusing children.

    1.2.3. Pro:
    In jurisdictions where sexual orientation is a protected characteristic [63], classing pedophilia as such will entitle pedophiles to protections extended to vulnerable communities.

      1.2.3.1. Pro:
      Employers are justified in not wanting to hire pedophiles for roles that involve close proximity to children, yet such discernment would be tantamount to discrimination on the basis of sexual orientation [64].

      1.2.3.2. Con:
      Protections are not reserved exclusively for vulnerable communities. Criminal conviction status [65], for example, is also a protected characteristic in some jurisdictions, so the same benefits can be extended to pedophiles.

    1.2.4. Con:
    Morality and criminality apply only to voluntary actions, not involuntary reactions. Because pedophilia describes solely a reaction over which one has no control, it does not have moral or legal implications.

      1.2.4.1. Pro:
      Most pedophiles never offend (p. 5 [66]), and most child sex offenders are not pedophiles [67]. Focusing on the morality and criminality of pedophilia takes attention away from the real causes of child sexual abuse.

        1.2.4.1.1. Pro:
        Children are typically seen as vulnerable and helpless, making them an easy target for predatory [68] adults looking for an outlet for their sexual frustration.

        1.2.4.1.2. Pro:
        Alcohol and drug use has been implicated in increased risk for child abuse [69], including child sexual abuse perpetrated by parents.

        1.2.4.1.3. Con:
        The belief that sexual attraction to children is acceptable, so long as one does not act upon their urges, legitimizes the sexualization of children [70], which creates a conducive environment for child sexual abuse.

      1.2.4.2. Con:
      The public is not sympathetic toward non-offending pedophiles (p. 4 [71]). In a 2015 survey, about 50% of respondents agreed that non-offending pedophiles should be incarcerated, and more than 25% agreed that they would be better off dead (Table 5 [72]).

      1.2.4.3. Pro:
      Negative emotions like anger and jealousy are not considered inherently immoral or criminal. Only when a person acts on these urges in a way that causes harm to another person do morality and criminality become relevant.

      1.2.4.4. Con:
      Some jurisdictions require mandatory reporting of individuals [73] considered to be a threat to a child. Attraction is, therefore, sufficient to draw a person under the radar of law enforcement, even when this is expressed under a confidentiality clause.

  1.3. Pro:
  Classing pedophilia as a sexual orientation will reduce stigma and hatred faced by the community.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    Stigma remains a major barrier to research on pedophilia, which is necessary to better understand and manage the condition.

      1.3.1.1. Con:
      Researchers are incentivized to study unexplored phenomenon, because the novelty of their findings [74] would allow them to become pioneers in the field.

        1.3.1.1.1. Con:
        Medical experts and researchers fear linking their careers and names [75] to pedophilia, out of fear of suspicion that their interest in the topic may be rooted in their own attraction to children.

      1.3.1.2. Pro:
      Due to stigma, researchers may have limited access to pedophiles, resulting in samples that represent only a small segment of this population: convicted sex offenders.

        1.3.1.2.1. Con:
        Research methods such as surveys allow scientists to collect data anonymously, meaning respondents can self-identity as pedophiles, thereby making samples more representative [76].

        1.3.1.2.2. Pro:
        Only through a comparison of offending and non-offending pedophiles [77] will researchers be able to identify the factors that help the latter prevent themselves from harming children.

      1.3.1.3. Pro:
      Due to inadequate funding, pedophilia continues to be poorly understood [78], or the researcher is forced to pay out of pocket or seek alternatives such as crowdfunding.

        1.3.1.3.1. Pro:
        Crowdfunding pedophilia research is unlikely to be successful because people are hesitant to engage [79] with content on child sexual abuse. In the absence of stigma, crowdfunding would be more successful.

        1.3.1.3.2. Con:
        Child sexual abuse remains a major social problem, so governments are presumably incentivized to support [80] research on pedophilia. This counterbalances the negative impact of stigma.

          1.3.1.3.2.1. Pro:
          Significant government money is spent on the incarceration of child sex offenders [81], rather than on prevention. This shows that both funding and motivation are available and need to only be redirected toward pedophilia research.

          1.3.1.3.2.2. Con:
          Most child sexual abusers are not pedophiles [82]. Therefore, funding targeted toward preventing child sexual abuse may not necessarily translate into improved funding for pedophilia research.

          1.3.1.3.2.3. Con:
          Government funding decisions are often dictated by public opinion. Indeed, in 2007, Ontario withdrew funding [83] for a study that was considered "inappropriate" because it would compensate sex offenders for their participation in the study.

    1.3.2. Con:
    Stigma ought to be stamped out only when the stigmatized characteristic is value-neutral [84], for example, body weight. Because stigma serves to inform society that pedophilic acts are morally wrong, it is beneficial.

      1.3.2.1. Pro:
      The emergence of "radioactive stigma" against pedophilia has served to counteract the casual sexualization of children [85] that was common and accepted in earlier times.

      1.3.2.2. Pro:
      In general, criminal behavior is stigmatized [86]. This allows others to be warned about potential threats to self and property. Likewise, pedophilia stigma serves to protect one of the most vulnerable sections of society: children.

      1.3.2.3. Con:
      Stigma is often rooted in ignorance [87]. Likewise, stigma surrounding pedophilia is rooted in the conflation of attraction to children and child sexual abuse, meaning it serves to only promote misinformation.

    1.3.3. Con:
    Due to widespread stigma, reclassifying pedophilia as a sexual orientation will likely create outrage, exacerbating the harms faced by this community.

      1.3.3.1. Pro:
      The mere mention of pedophilia can elicit moral panic [88], derailing public discourse and policy-making. Reclassing pedophilia may, therefore, be highly provocative, leading to knee-jerk reactions that cause more harm to pedophiles.

      1.3.3.2. Con:
      Moral outrage will likely result from the erroneous belief [89] that classing pedophilia as a sexual orientation effectively condones child sexual abuse. Campaigns that dispel such misinformation will reduce stigma without sparking outrage.

    1.3.4. Pro:
    Non-offending pedophiles [90] should be able to live a life free of psychological and physical threats, as they have done nothing wrong.

      1.3.4.1. Pro:
      Non-offending pedophiles often develop mental health problems such as depression and suicidality [91], when they realize they are sexually attracted to children—something over which they have little to no control.

      1.3.4.2. Con:
      "Non-offending" is not an unchangeable attribute. Because of the enduring risk that a pedophile may commit their first sex crime, social barriers are necessary to keep pedophiles away from children.

        1.3.4.2.1. Pro:
        Analogically, environmental presence of alcohol is a trigger for recovering alcoholics [92]. Because children freely exist in society, exclusionary social norms serve as a way to protect children from potential predators.

        1.3.4.2.2. Con:
        One in three men are non-offending rapists [93], yet they are not socially isolated from women. Therefore, there is no reason why pedophilia should be treated differently, compared to other sex crimes.

        1.3.4.2.3. Con:
        Psychotherapy for pedophiles includes teaching them to practice non-judgmental acceptance [94] of their thoughts and feelings. A stigmatizing environment can make this difficult to practice.

    1.3.5. Pro:
    A hostile psychological climate increases the chances of a pedophile acting on their impulses. Reducing stigma and hatred is, therefore, a child abuse prevention strategy.

      1.3.5.1. Pro:
      Primary and secondary prevention interventions can help pedophiles abstain from offending. However, stigma adversely affects their availability, accessibility, and effectiveness (p. 9 [95]).

        1.3.5.1.1. Pro:
        Raising awareness about preventive resources and interventions for pedophiles requires public promotion, for example, through television. This would be easier if the public does not respond to pedophilia with derision and disgust.

        1.3.5.1.2. Con:
        Primary and secondary prevention interventions can be effectively delivered through the internet [96], thereby circumventing barriers such as social stigma, which impede uptake among pedophiles.

        1.3.5.1.3. Pro:
        Pedophilia stigma is so deeply entrenched that most mental health professionals are either not willing to work with pedophiles [97] or hold prejudicial beliefs that may harm rather than help them.

      1.3.5.2. Con:
      External factors cannot be blamed for individual behavior. Therefore, instead of blameshifting, the objective should be to help pedophiles regulate their impulses, regardless of external triggers.

        1.3.5.2.1. Con:
        Internalized stigma increases the likelihood of a pedophile offending (pp. 4-5 [98]). Because stigma is rooted in willful ignorance, social factors must also be considered relevant.

        1.3.5.2.2. Con:
        Legal sentencing takes into account extenuating, mitigating, and aggravating factors [99], thereby implying that culpability, even in child sex abuse cases [100], is contextually determined.

        1.3.5.2.3. Pro:
        Criminal behavior is often triggered by adverse life experiences. About one in three child abusers [101], for example, were victims of childhood abuse themselves, yet this does not absolve them of responsibility for their actions.

      1.3.5.3. Pro:
      Low self-esteem, stress [102], and desperation [103], which can be precipitated by psychosocial hostility and alienation, increase the chances of a pedophile offending.

  1.4. Pro:
  Pedophilia refers to an innate and enduring pattern of attraction, which is the definition of sexual orientation.

    1.4.1. Con:
    The age of onset of sexual orientation [104] is much lower than the age at which pedophiles become aware of their sexual interest in children. This implies that pedophilia is not innate, but shaped by the environment.

      1.4.1.1. Con:
      By definition, pedophilia must have an older age of onset, because it refers to age-discordant attraction to children. A child demonstrating sexual interest in another child would be age-concordant and indicative of normal sexual development [105].

        1.4.1.1.1. Pro:
        To be diagnosed as a pedophile, not only must a person meet minimum age requirements but they must also be significantly older [106] than the child about whom they have sexual fantasies. This is why most adolescents do not qualify as pedophiles.

        1.4.1.1.2. Con:
        Pedophilia has been conceptualized as the endurance of attraction to prepubescent children, originating in childhood, into adolescence and adulthood (p. 7 [107]). In contrast, in teleiophiles, such attraction ends when they reach adolescence.

      1.4.1.2. Con:
      Age of onset of pedophilia can be as low as 6 years, and lower ages of onset are associated with exclusive sexual attraction to children. This subtype of pedophilia [108] may qualify as a sexual orientation.

      1.4.1.3. Pro:
      Environmental triggers, including psychological trauma and brain injury, can lead to the development of pedophilia, suggesting that this condition is a caused by external factors [109], rather than a natural occurrence.

        1.4.1.3.1. Con:
        The neurodevelopmental deviances linked to pedophilia typically emerge in utero or during early childhood [110], which still means that the affected individual has no control over their urges. This makes pedophilia a sexual orientation.

        1.4.1.3.2. Con:
        There is a clinical distinction between acquired pedophilia and idiopathic pedophilia [111], and environmental triggers have been implicated only in the former. Idiopathic pedophilia may, therefore, still be classifiable as a sexual orientation.

        1.4.1.3.3. Pro:
        Rates of child abuse victimization, especially sexual abuse, are more pronounced among pedophiles, implying that early trauma involving a hyper-sexualized environment [112] may trigger the development of sexual interest in children.

          1.4.1.3.3.1. Pro:
          Many victims of child sexual abuse perpetrate sexual abuse against other children [113] while they are still children, underscoring the formative influence of childhood trauma on deviant sexuality in adulthood.

          1.4.1.3.3.2. Con:
          The percentage of child sex offenders who were themselves abused as children is often exaggerated [114]. For this reason, the notion of an "abused abuser" is considered a harmful myth that exacerbates the problem of child sexual abuse.

            1.4.1.3.3.2.1. Pro:
            Some child sex offenders falsely claim [115] to have been sexually abused as children, to garner sympathy from others, thereby minimizing their perceived culpability.

            1.4.1.3.3.2.2. Con:
            Debunking the belief that abused children grow up to become abusers does not negate the finding that many abused children grow up to develop pedophilia.

            1.4.1.3.3.2.3. Pro:
            The abused abuser myth exacerbates the trauma [116] experienced by child abuse victims and their family members, who may worry that they might grow up to become child sex offenders.

            1.4.1.3.3.2.4. Con:
            Though the size of the group difference may be small, it is still statistically significant [117]. Therefore, monitoring child abuse victims could be a helpful abuse prevention strategy.

    1.4.2. Con:
    Sexual orientation is defined in terms of attraction on the basis of gender and sex [118], not age. This is why pedophiles can be classified on the basis of their sexual orientation, for example, homosexual pedophiles [119].

      1.4.2.1. Pro:
      Pedophiles also vary on factors such as exclusivity, incestuousness, and externality of motivation (pp. 459-460 [120]). Classing age-based preference as a sexual orientation may warrant a similar status for these other preferences.

      1.4.2.2. Pro:
      Patterns of sexual offending among child offenders demonstrate consistency in the sex of the victim (pp. 1-2 [121]). Sexual orientation should be limited to the attribute of sex to keep these patterns distinct from age-based attraction.

      1.4.2.3. Con:
      Sexual orientation is not defined solely based on gender or sex. "Queer," for example, includes those with patterns of attraction that are not associated with gender or sex [122]. Pedophiles could technically, therefore, be considered queer.

    1.4.3. Pro:
    Pedophilia may represent an exaggerated evolutionary preference for younger mates (p. 263 [123]), who have a higher likelihood of reproductive success. This may explain why pedophiles are invariably men.

      1.4.3.1. Con:
      Pedophilia remains virtually unexplored [124] among women. The astronomical increase in child sexual abuse cases involving women indicates that prevalence rates [125] may be much higher, undermining the explanatory power of gender or sex.

      1.4.3.2. Con:
      Prepubescent children have not reached sexual maturity, while pubescent adolescents face a high risk [126] of pregnancy-related complications and mortality [127]. Thus, evolutionary pressures would discourage pedophilia.

      1.4.3.3. Pro:
      The term "pedophilia" is often erroneously used to refer to "hebephilia," which refers to sexual attraction to pubescent children [128] and occurs more commonly than attraction to sexually immature children.

        1.4.3.3.1. Pro:
        From a biological perspective, puberty, which marks sexual maturation, typically begins in adolescence and sometimes in childhood. For this reason, hebephelic attraction [129] is considered normal.

    1.4.4. Pro:
    Pedophilia has a biological basis, implying that affected individuals have limited control over the development and experience of their attraction to children.

      1.4.4.1. Pro:
      There are significant differences in the brain structure, function, and biochemistry [130] of pedophiles and non-pedophiles. This shows that they cannot control their bodily experience of attraction toward children.

        1.4.4.1.1. Pro:
        Braining imaging studies have found that pedophiles process the faces of children in the same way that adults process the faces of other adults, demonstrating a preference for sexual immaturity [131].

        1.4.4.1.2. Con:
        Brain structure, function, and biochemistry can differ based on factors such as mental health status [132] and criminality. These differences are indicative of deviance from normality, not of pedophilia being a sexual orientation.

      1.4.4.2. Pro:
      Phallometry, which is a test of sexual arousal in men [133], has shown that pedophiles demonstrate penile tumescence in response to stimuli involving children, in a manner comparable to what is observed in other sexual orientations.

        1.4.4.2.1. Con:
        Phallometry is a poor test of sexual arousal, let alone orientation. Not only can sexual arousal be suppressed [134] to fake results, but also phallometry may incorrectly classify other forms of arousal, such as anxiety, as sexual arousal (p. 444 [135]).

        1.4.4.2.2. Pro:
        Many pedophiles wish they were not attracted to children [136], yet phallometric tests have proven that they have not been able to modify their physiological urges. This lack of choice aligns pedophilia with the definition of sexual orientation.

      1.4.4.3. Con:
      There is no human behavior that does not have a genetic basis, as both nature and nature reciprocally shape behavior. In this regard, pedophilia is less heritable [137] than other patterns of human behavior, for example, mental disorders.
