Discussion Name:
Does the United States Need a Strong Third Political Party For Moderates?

1. Thesis:
The United States needs a strong political party for moderates.

  1.1. Con:
  If this would split the vote in Presidential elections among three major parties, a President could elected with the support of only a third of voters.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    The Constitution does not say the winner of the Presidential election must get the majority of the popular vote.

    1.1.2. Con:
    This has pretty much already happened under the current two-party system. When Bill Clinton ran for President the first time he won with 43% of the vote [1]. The conservative vote was split between the GOP and Ross Perot.

    1.1.3. Con:
    Having a smaller pool for victory would force more cooperation across the aisle, and possibly even require forming a coalition to be able to legislate and conduct the business of governing effectively.

    1.1.4. Pro:
    Under the First-Past-the-Post [2] sytem currently used, it is already possible to win an election with less than 50% of the vote. The more candidates, the lower that amount is likely to be.

  1.2. Pro:
  A moderate party would be more representative of the US population.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Democrats and Republicans polarize the nation.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      Political philosophy is not a binary system.

        1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
        Political identity is based on one's opinion on thousands of policy approaches.

          1.2.1.1.1.1. Pro:
          When voicing one's opinion on a particular policy, listeners routinely assume the speaker's party affiliation with little evidence. This mechanism promotes ignorance, stereotyping and lessens the quality and effectiveness of public discourse.

        1.2.1.1.2. Con:
        The two party stronghold tends to turn US politics into a 1D binary system.

          1.2.1.1.2.1. Pro:
          The two party system/stronghold is due to and reinforced by the First-Past-the-Post [3] voting system used in the US.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      False premise: our current polarisation is not a result of both parties moving away from the center. It is a result of the Republicans moving steadily to the right while until 2016 the Democrats stayed still or even drifted just slightly rightwards. For example, the ACA ("Obamacare") was based on a Republican plan from the 90s, similar to what Mitt Romney instituted as a Republican governor in Massachusetts. By 2009 the Republicans had abandoned such ideas, deriding them as socialist.

      1.2.1.3. Pro:
      A third party would represent the greater moderate majority, rather than requiring all to make clear cut, more extreme decisions.

      1.2.1.4. Pro:
      The US began as a nation that needed both ideology and pragmatism, now that our government, economy, population etc has had sustained growth and increased diversity; a new paradigm must exist. Both Republicans and Democrats are pushing each other to see who can be the most extreme while those pragmatic voices of each party are drown out. With ideology running both extremes, a third party is especially important now and moving forward.

      1.2.1.5. Pro:
      A third moderate party would create a way for moderate politics to exert power when the political environment becomes polarised. Instead of moderates being chained to the extremes reinforcing them even if they and their constituents don’t want to because of no viable alternative, a moderate party would allow these views to be powerful in their own right without the need for internal compromise in the party, thus making between-party compromise more likely.

      1.2.1.6. Con:
      A third party would divide the country further, and it would be possible for a party to win with even less percentage of voters, making an even smaller group of people decide the fate of the election.

        1.2.1.6.1. Con:
        The parties having to work together might actually lead into a percentage of the people supporting the bills.

    1.2.2. Pro:
    The Democratic and Republican party ideologies have drifted so far apart that many Americans cannot fully identify with either.

      1.2.2.1. Pro:
      Political identity is a spectrum, not two extremes.

        1.2.2.1.1. Pro:
        -> See 1.2.1.1.1.

        1.2.2.1.2. Pro:
        There are a variety of political science models depicting spectra [4] describing variety in political values. Most estimate a spectrum of two dimensions: civil liberties and economic liberties.

      1.2.2.2. Pro:
      From the perspective of a nation state with a lot of parties and different governments, micro/third parties allow the spreading of power to truly represent it's citizens. Whilst it is far less efficient than the two party rule, two party rule enables the party with the most power to essentially run the whole country for the term, whilst smaller parties must co-operate to gain majority votes in a parliament.

        1.2.2.2.1. Con:
        The U.S. congress has recently had its most ineffective years to date within the last administration.

      1.2.2.3. Pro:
      Some people agree with the Democrats on some issues, such as same sex marriage, while agreeing with the Republicans on other issues, such as abortion. Having a viable third party will enable these people to vote their conscience on all the issues that matter to them.

      1.2.2.4. Con:
      The Democratic and Republican parties are strikingly similar.  Small differences are emphasized by politicians for political purposes and by media outlets to generate interest.

        1.2.2.4.1. Pro:
        Both parties reject Medicare for All, despite 70% of Americans supporting it. Reuters Policy Poll [5]

        1.2.2.4.2. Pro:
        Game theory predicts that with a single-round majority election, the steady state consists of two parties very close to each other in terms of policy

        1.2.2.4.3. Con:
        The two parties have about as much in common as a chimpanzee has with a gorilla.

          1.2.2.4.3.1. Pro:
          Sure they both have the same ideals about the constitution and the three branches of government but they are radically different on other issues like gay rights.

  1.3. Pro:
  Having a third political party will force politicians to work together and compromise.

    1.3.1. Con:
    The presence of more political parties does not guarantee cooperation on the parts of these parties.

    1.3.2. Pro:
    If politicians in Congress belong to more than two parties, it is less likely that one party alone can supply the necessary majorities to make decisions.

    1.3.3. Con:
    A strong third party would only exacerbate these problems. With three major parties, it would be even less likely that agreement by all actors is reached.

      1.3.3.1. Con:
      The reason for slow progress is the two parties blocking each other and not making the majority.

        1.3.3.1.1. Pro:
        A third party would be able to switch sides to be able to create majorities.

  1.4. Pro:
  A third political party for moderates would improve the workings of the American political system.

    1.4.1. Pro:
    Americans would no longer have to vote for the "lesser of two evils."

      A 2 party democracy is a fallacy in the name of democracy. New ideologies, new kind of politics must be allowed to come up and exist, as new political parties. Only then it will be a true democracy.

      1.4.1.2. Con:
      It is quite possible that a third party may not be much better than the two main parties in the US.

        1.4.1.2.2. Con:

Regardless, diversity and variety are prerequisite to democratic efficacy because condensed choices over-generalize a broad spectrum of ideological possibility. By extension, even more than three parties would be even more democratically effective.

      1.4.1.3. Con:
      The populace would instead have to vote for the lesser of three evils.

      1.4.1.4. Con:
      Many people do not believe there are politicians who have their best interests and needs in mind, or do not agree with everything that politicians stand for. Therefore, they are unlikely to see a third candidate as extremely preferable.

      1.4.1.5. Con:
      The need to vote for the lesser of two evils is more a product of the voting system in the US than the lack of a strong moderate party.

      1.4.1.6. Con:
      The claim that Americans currently "have to vote for the 'lesser of two evils,'" is demonstrably false.  Americans do not have to vote at all.  Abstention is legal.

        1.4.1.6.1. Con:
        Yes,  while its legal to not vote, the massive amounts of people not voting has led to a little over a quarter of the population choosing who represents the whole.

        1.4.1.6.2. Con:
        Having to either begrudgingly decide, or have no impact whatsoever, is not an effective model for representation.

        1.4.1.6.3. Con:
        But by not voting you potentially allow the greater of two evils to gain power.

    1.4.2. Con:
    The existence of another political party does not guarantee that party will have any power and change the established two-party system.

    1.4.3. Pro:
    A third political party for moderates would allow greater means to challenge the dominance of the two main parties.

      1.4.3.1. Pro:

  1.5. Con:
  A third party is not necessary.

      1.5.1.1. Con:

Another choice may provide a fresh option for people who still consider the Libs and Greens to be borne of two party system reaction.

      1.5.1.2. Con:

    1.5.2. Pro:

The need for a new party is not in the middle, but to the Left.

        1.5.2.1.1. Con:

        1.5.2.1.2. Con:
        Liking something is not the same as wanting to vote for it. Many people may think something would be good but due to pessimism about politics won’t try to put it in place by voting for it, thus potentially explaining these poll results. It may not be a party problem but a voter problem.

The intent of the thesis was probably to suggest a 3rd party whose policies landed somewhere in the middle of our current 2 party system; thereby making it "moderate" by comparison to the 2 opposing ideological views currently being presented by democrats and republicans.
