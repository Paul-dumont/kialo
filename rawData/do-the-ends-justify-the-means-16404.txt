Discussion Name:
Do the ends justify the means?

1. Thesis:
The ends justify the means.

  1.1. Con:
  Good/bad is subjective, so the end can never absolutely justify the means.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    There is no universal moral code defining Good/Bad.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      Regardless of how morality is defined, there nevertheless are objectively correct and incorrect answers that correspond to whatever particular definition is being used at the time that that definition is being applied.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      While certainly subjective or socially driven (for the vast majority), there are certain universal norms or characteristics that can be found, such as not killing nor assaulting others.

      1.1.1.3. Pro:
      To compare good actions to bad actions, not only do acts need to be defined as good or evil, but they have to be ranked/score-able against each other.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    If two different groups/cultures can disagree on how bad the means are, or how good the ends are, it shows that the justification was not grounded on solid facts, and there is not a strong enough reason to have committed the bad acts as a means.

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      This confuses universality with objectivity. To say that The Holocaust was objectively bad is not to say that everyone can agree that it is bad. Objectivity is precisely about something being good or bad independent of opinion.

        1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
        To say that the Holocaust was objectively bad is to say that it would have been bad even if the Nazis would have won the second world war and killed or brainwashed anyone who disagreed so that everyone believed that The Holocaust was a good thing. It's irrelevant who agrees or disagrees.

    1.1.3. Con:
    Good and bad are not subjective.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      Something is always either good or bad in support of a specific goal. Something may not be absolutely good or bad or universally good or bad but this doesn't mean that there aren't objectively correct or incorrect ways of reaching a specific goal or moving away from something that one wants to avoid.

      1.1.3.2. Pro:
      All extrinsic goals are ultimately relative to our intrinsic values which we all share: to increase the happiness and decrease the unhappiness for ourselves, and others, who we care about.

    1.1.4. Con:
    The ends can justify the means within a group if they share a moral code defining what is good and what is bad, for example a sovereign country.

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      The end always justifies the means if the end is greater than the means ... so all this lower level societal stuff doesn't matter when the higher metaphysical level trumps it.

      1.1.4.2. Con:
      This assumes that all those within the country agree. Despite what may be legal or not, or broadly considered moral, individuals can differ in their own convictions.

    1.1.5. Con:
    If your intention is striving for the most possible good, it doesn't matter if the result wasn't good overall.

      1.1.5.1. Con:

        1.1.5.1.1. Pro:
        Ultimately intentions don't matter at all.

          1.1.5.1.1.1. Pro:

            1.1.5.1.1.1.1. Pro:

              1.1.5.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
              Because intentions are only practical realities they must, at most, merely be means to deeper, more metaphysical, ends.

          1.1.5.1.1.2. Pro:
          Malicious intentions are only worse because they *tend to* lead to worse results more often than innocent intentions do.

        1.1.5.3.2. Con:

          1.1.5.5.1.2. Pro:

  1.2. Pro:
  Within a legal context, the ends can justify the means.

    1.2.1. Con:
    Legality is not equivalent to morality.

    1.2.2. Pro:
    "Ends justify the means" was the underlying principle behind COVID-19 restrictions, such as mask mandates and stay-at-home orders, which would have otherwise been perceived as a violation of basic human rights.

    1.2.3. Con:
    The ends don't always justify the means in a legal context.

      1.2.3.1. Con:

      1.2.3.2. Pro:
      If a person assaults another to protect themselves this would be seen as self-defense because of the context within which it occurred. If another assaults someone without provocation this would be seen as illegal and punishment would follow.

    1.2.4. Pro:

            1.2.4.1.1.1.1. Con:

            1.2.4.1.2.1.1. Con:

              1.2.4.1.2.1.1.1. Con:

The defendant must have tried any possible alternative means (eg reporting to the police), have not led themselves into the situation of duress (eg joined a group that later blackmailed them), and that no more was done than was reasonably necessary for that purpose (they must use the least evil means available to them).

        1.2.4.5.1. Pro:

        1.2.4.5.2. Pro:
        According to the US State definition, self-defense can be used in 24 States through the Stand Your Ground Law, [26] 9 States use the Castle Doctrine [27], and 17 States use Duty to Retreat. ³ [28] and Shoot to incapacitate.

      1.3.3.2. Pro:

          -> See 1.1.

    1.4.3. Con:
    Governments should aspire toward a greater good, without depriving their citizens of what they already have.

      1.4.3.1. Pro:
      Law and order can be achieved through the use of coercion and brutal force, but this would come at the cost of individual and collective freedoms.

    1.4.4. Pro:
    In a society with no greater good it would be impossible for armed forces to shoot dead a terrorist, even if he/she was actively threatening civilians, as killing someone is morally wrong.

      1.4.4.1. Con:
      There is indeed a greater good. But this is a bad argument for it because harming an enemy when the harm is equal is no greater of a good than harming innocent people.

      1.4.4.2. Con:
      If the intention in killing the terrorist is to save the lives of others this is justifiable from the perspective on intention without the need for a greater good argument.

        1.4.4.2.1. Pro:
        The intention and reason of doing something is looking ahead to the future, which makes it an end result. In this case, killing a terrorist is to prevent a terrorist attack, which is an end. Ultimately, the intention, if it has anything to do with the future, is the "ends" that this debate revolves around

      1.4.4.3. Con:
      If you are narrowly looking just at the shooting, killing is a morally wrong action. However, looking broadly at the context of a terrorist act, the person may deserve to die. Therefore by looking at the wider picture, it's not a matter of a bad act achieving a greater good - It is simply a good act.

  1.5. Pro:
  According to several philosophical branches, the ends justify the means.

    1.5.1. Pro:
    According to holism [29] (where actions should be viewed as wholes), the ends could justify the means.

      1.5.1.1. Pro:
      There is nothing that exists to give meaning to an action other than what it does. Justification for means cannot come from anything other than end results.

        1.5.1.1.1. Pro:
        Instrumental values can only be justified by intrinsic values.

          1.5.1.1.1.1. Pro:
          Another word for an "instrumental value" is an "extrinsic value" and the extrinsic and the intrinsic depend on each other *by definition* ... so the parent claim is necessarily true.

        1.5.1.1.2. Con:
        Intention and context are unrelated to consequence but can change the morality of an act.

          1.5.1.1.2.1. Pro:
          If a person accidentally kills another this is seen as different morally to someone who deliberately does so because the intention and context are different.

          1.5.1.1.2.2. Pro:
          If a person kills another in self defence this is seen as different to someone who kills another in anger. It is a different context and different intention.

        1.5.1.1.3. Con:
        In isolation an end can never truly justify the means in all cases. In civilised society time and again we attempt to restrict means by which ends are acceptably accomplished e.g. murder and war crimes being prominent examples.

          1.5.1.1.3.1. Pro:
          Discussion about the eventuality of some event is a preferable method to discover all the motives at work.

            1.5.1.1.3.1.1. Pro:
            By using thought experiments approaching some event with multiple angles accounted for will result in actions that are more preferred.

          1.5.1.1.3.2. Con:
          Paralysis by choice [30] is a real phenomenon but in most cases action is a preferable means to reach finality. Which means that even if the wrong action was employed, still some resolutions is reached.

      1.5.1.2. Con:
      Whether actions can be conceptualized as being broken down into - reduced to - much smaller micro-actions or whether they are instead taken as larger wholes, has no effect on whether the consequences that justify them are ultimately good or bad.

      1.5.1.3. Con:
      According to reductionism [31], actions should be viewed as a collection of parts, therefore the ends couldn't justify the means.

        1.5.1.3.1. Con:
        Holism allows for certain methods to be forgivable in pursuit of a higher goal; reductionism picks away at small methodological problems that can interfere with a loftier higher goal.

          1.5.1.3.1.1. Con:
          Reductionism is merely that you can understand the whole by its parts and how they work together and there's absolutely nothing about that that can "interfere with loftier goals".

      1.5.1.4. Con:
      Breaking actions down into parts can make it easier to understand each individual action, as each may have its own reason.

    1.5.2. Pro:
    According to consequentialism [32], any action can only be justified by it's consequences.

      1.5.2.1. Con:
      -> See 1.1.6.1.

      1.5.2.2. Pro:
      Consequentialist ethics say that the moral worth of an action is determined by its potential consequence, not by whether it follows a set of written edicts or laws.

        1.5.2.2.1. Pro:
        This is a completely accurate definition of Consequentialism and Consequentialism is precisely the view that "The ends justifiy the means."

      1.5.2.3. Con:
      -> See 1.1.8.2.1.

      1.5.2.4. Pro:
      Any principle that compels us to act in a way that has horrible consequences is a bad principle.

  1.6. Con:
  The ends can only justify the means if the mean to end ratio is the highest possible in the given situation.

    1.6.1. Con:
    In an urgent decision there is not enough time to evaluate all the possible actions, and so taking the perceived best action is morally just.

      1.6.1.1. Pro:
      In an urgent decision there is not enough time to evaluate all the possible actions, and so taking the perceived best action is morally just.

      1.6.1.2. Con:
      In most non-theoretical decisions, there is enough time to evaluate the options. Meaningful decisions like the laws in a society are not justified by urgency.

    1.6.2. Pro:
    -> See 1.1.5.6.

    1.6.3. Pro:
    If you have one bullet, and are facing two terrorists, you should choose to kill the one that is threatening to kill the highest number of people. Choosing the other one is immoral, as is choosing none.

      1.6.3.1. Con:
      -> See 1.1.5.5.1.

      1.6.3.2. Con:
      Given you are the one under immediate threat, the "greater good" in this situation is to save yourself. In doing so you may need to disable both attackers.

      1.6.3.3. Con:
      Choosing to shoot either is an overall good action. Just because you did not choose the best course, does not mean your course was not good.

        1.6.3.3.1. Con:
        Reason does not support taking any path other than the single best option.

          1.6.3.3.1.1. Con:
          -> See 1.1.5.3.1.

        1.6.3.3.2. Pro:
        A good option is any option that is better than an indifferent option. And even an indifferent option is better than a bad option.

      1.6.3.4. Pro:
      If you are choosing to perform a bad action, it is your responsibility to choose the best action possible, otherwise you are not justified in aiming for the greatest possible good.

      1.6.3.5. Con:
      Choosing none is still valid, if you subscribe to the idea that the means cannot outweigh the ends; murder is a huge action to take, even if you believe it is for the right reasons.

        1.6.3.5.1. Con:
        If morals support the action, then morals cannot support one who chooses not to act.

    1.6.4. Con:
    It is impossible to verify, in full, what the end ratio would be. Even if it seems like a foregone conclusion, it's impossible to know.

      1.6.4.1. Pro:
      -> See 1.1.6.

      1.6.4.2. Con:
      We will never achieve absolute certainty over the end ratio. The most likely and most beneficial outcome must be pursued.

    1.6.5. Pro:
    In the situation of a murderer breaking into a family member's room, you might have a taser and a gun available to stop the murderer. Choosing the lethal gun would be immoral, because you could achieve the same ends with the non-lethal taser.
