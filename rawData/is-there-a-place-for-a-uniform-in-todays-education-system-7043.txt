Discussion Name:
Is there a place for a uniform in today's education system?

1. Thesis:
There is a place for a uniform [1] in today's education system.

  1.1. Pro:
  Uniforms advertise the school; when pupils wearing them behave well, it creates a positive association with the school which can have a number of benefits (p. 176-177 [2]).

    1.1.1. Con:
    It is not the students' role to advertise the school; given this, they should not be burdened with the cost and maintenance of uniforms.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      Public schools do not need advertising and as such school uniforms can't be justified.

        1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
        Public schools rely on public funds [3], rather than on profits. As there is no product to advertise, image does not matter.

        1.1.1.1.2. Con:
        Some nations rank schools using league tables [4]. Schools may feel under pressure to advertise so that they can attract a higher quality of students and improve their ranking.

        1.1.1.1.3. Con:
        There are alternatives to school uniforms that may be suitable for advertising such as the use of direct advertising, branded clothing and merchandise, or positive word of mouth coverage.

      1.1.1.2. Pro:
      Uniforms are rapidly becoming inaccessibly expensive [5] because of school branding; schools should not hurt families in order to advertise.

        1.1.1.2.1. Pro:
        In the UK, nearly one in six families [6] blamed school uniform costs for causing them to cut back on food and other basic expenditures. Branding can often make this worse.

        1.1.1.2.2. Con:
        Uniforms may be expensive based on the materials [7] used to make them. This quality is worth the high costs as it looks better.

        1.1.1.2.3. Pro:
        In some schools, uniform policies are so strict that they may be able to discourage low income students from applying there by increasing the cost [8] to parents.

      1.1.1.3. Con:
      As student behaviour can lead schools to be associated with negative or anti-social behaviours, positive actions should be drawn attention to by uniforms as a counter-balance.

        1.1.1.3.1. Con:
        It is not an individual's job to advertise the brand of their car when it has a company badge on. Including a logo incurs reputational risk that companies weigh off through subtle advertising.

        1.1.1.3.2. Con:
        It is strictly the individual's prerogative to advertise what they care to advertise - schools are illegitimate in trying to instrumentalise [9] their pupils.

        1.1.1.3.3. Pro:
        Uniforms conveniently allow a correlation of good behaviour with a particular school.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    There is a uniformity of appearance and neatness that benefits the school's reputation.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      It is more aesthetically pleasing to have students in uniform than to allow them to wear their own clothes.

        1.1.2.1.1. Con:
        Many school uniforms are unfashionable. This is particularly true in China [10] where they have a reputation of being ugly and poorly made.

        1.1.2.1.2. Con:
        It is likely that most schoolchildren do not think of school uniforms as aesthetically pleasing; their views should be counted when considering the value of the policy.

        1.1.2.1.3. Pro:
        Children, particularly teenagers, often enjoy rebelling [11] which can include dressing [12] in ways that are counter-cultural and push social norms. These are seldom aesthetically enjoyable.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      Championing uniforms on the basis of consistency in appearance assumes that all students wear uniforms in the same way. Yet, this is often not enforceable, as students customise [13] their uniforms to rebel.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        As students start wearing uniforms from a younger age [14], they may find themselves being more adherent to the rules as uniforms are normalised such that it does not occur to them to rebel.

        1.1.2.2.2. Pro:
        Uniforms provide the perfect conditions [15] for innovative and subtle stylisation; this could be difficult to clamp-down upon without parental dissent [16].

      1.1.2.3. Pro:
      When schools are perceived as respectable, and the pupils that go to them intelligent, workplaces may prefer to hire them.

        1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
        Schools may mimic universities [17] and give their students a reputational CV boost, which is especially beneficial in competitive job markets.

        1.1.2.3.2. Con:
        The level of respect a school can garner is not dependent solely on the way pupils dress but on historical factors as well as socioeconomic biases.

        1.1.2.3.3. Con:
        The level of respect schools earn through uniforms may diminish as more schools adopt them; such a competitive advantage [18] only works if it is unique.

      1.1.2.4. Con:
      Parents may buy their child's uniforms several sizes too big [19] so that they can grow into it and cut costs. This is unlikely to be aesthetically pleasing and defies uniformity.

        1.1.2.4.1. Pro:
        Buying a uniform that is too large may encourage parents to engage in sunk cost fallacies [20] - holding onto uniforms that are stained or torn to save money.

        1.1.2.4.2. Con:
        School uniforms are unlikely to remain too big forever; eventually the children wearing them will look neat. This may be a greater benefit than any gleaned by wearing their own clothes.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Uniforms make it easier to identify students as members of that school.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      Uniforms help identify students [21] at large events, in public places during trips, and when students are walking to and from school. This helps the school to take responsibility for the safety and welfare of their students.

        1.1.3.1.1. Con:
        Students old enough to go on school trips, or enter public areas with high teacher-student ratios, are likely able to keep themselves safe without the use of uniforms.

        1.1.3.1.2. Pro:
        School trips can often be stressful [22] for teachers - using uniforms may give some relief and make their jobs easier.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      By separating schools using uniforms, it becomes easier for schools to build rivalries and foster negative behaviours.

        1.1.3.2.1. Con:
        In combination with other interventions, uniforms change school culture and create a sense of belonging which can prevent gang activity (p. 5 [23]).

          1.1.3.2.1.1. Pro:
          Some schools with a high prevalence of gang activity and poor academic outcomes have experienced improvements by implementing a school uniform policy (p. 16 [24]).

          1.1.3.2.1.2. Con:
          Policies to alleviate gang activity exist outside of school uniforms and may be far more effective; both in their targeted nature and their impact (p. 7 [25]).

        1.1.3.2.2. Con:
        By distinguishing students, it becomes easier [26] for people in public to note anti-social behaviour in public spaces, limiting the extent of inter-school antipathy.

        1.1.3.2.3. Pro:
        Uniforms for religious schools may make students more vulnerable [27] to hate crimes.

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      Even after graduation an adult may identify with their school when seeing students wearing the uniform. This sense of belonging could bridge generations and increase community spirit.

        1.1.3.3.1. Pro:
        Over 80% [28] of teachers in the United States see uniforms as contributing to a happy school community, and even more believe that wearing a uniform helps students to 'fit in' with their peers.

        1.1.3.3.2. Con:
        Teachers and pupils seldom believe [29] that uniforms help to engender a sense of school or national spirit - this may limit any long term pride in the school.

        1.1.3.3.3. Pro:
        It is possible that wearing a uniform can encourage [30] positive social behaviours; these may be more likely to be directed towards the person's school given proximity and familiarity.

    1.1.4. Pro:
    Uniforms push students to truly have pride in their uniform, school identity and, consequently, behaviour.

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      Uniforms are commonly worn by groups of people working on a shared goal, such as sports teams and the military, presumably because they foster a shared sense of camaraderie. The same may be true for school students.

      1.1.4.2. Con:
      There are no known benefits to deriving pride from one's uniform. Therefore, instead of reinforcing external contingencies of pride, internal sources, such as skills and accomplishments, should be nurtured.

  1.2. Con:
  Uniforms kill creativity, dampening an essential part [31] of the human condition.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Young people are eager for more stimulus and interest; uniforms may stifle this desire by normalising a dull routine.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      Wearing the same outfit every day is boring and may encourage students to disconnect from school as a site of adventure.

        1.2.1.1.1. Con:
        School is not an appropriate forum for exciting outfits, as they may draw attention from lessons.

        1.2.1.1.2. Pro:
        Children often want to be unique and build autonomy [32]. They should be taught they can belong while being themselves; this could have more important benefits as it builds a sense of self [33] early.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      Given that the ability to conform is a necessary skill for development, by normalising governmentality with uniforms, students learn to adapt to and engage with social systems (p. 4 [34]).

        1.2.1.2.1. Con:
        A sense of belonging is likely to arise from the agreement and acceptance [35] of all parties involved, not through imposition by authority.

        1.2.1.2.2. Pro:
        School uniforms can help students feel more pride in their school - this may increase inclusion and cohesion (p. 18-19 [36]).

    1.2.2. Pro:
    Uniforms undermine [37] the freedom of expression of students.

      1.2.2.1. Pro:
      Clothing is a strictly personal matter that public institutions should not interfere in. This is especially true given what people wear seldom causes harm to others.

        1.2.2.1.1. Pro:
        States exist to maintain civil society and reduce violence [38]; anything more than this is an abuse of power. Given this, it is illegitimate for state institutions to suppress aesthetic choices, even for children.

        1.2.2.1.2. Con:
        Harm can be indirect and diffuse; if children's clothing choices limit their future ability to live full and happy lives then this is a harm that the state should not allow.

        1.2.2.1.3. Pro:
        People often prefer to cause harms through omission rather than directly (p. 166 [39]). Given that children may suffer directly from uniforms but future harms are nebulous, it is illegitimate to support uniforms.

      1.2.2.2. Con:
      School age children are often too young [40] to meaningfully access free speech; as such it is unimportant if uniforms undermine it.

        1.2.2.2.1. Con:
        Age is not a factor [41] that affects the right to free speech. As clothes are a form of expression [42] that bear symbols or words that have value to the wearer or others, they should not be restricted.

        1.2.2.2.2. Pro:
        For many, speech has an instrumental value [43] that facilitates truth and learning. Given that children's use of clothing does neither of these, it is not important.

        1.2.2.2.3. Pro:
        Children as young as 3 have an innate sense of gender identity [44]. As school uniforms are often gendered, requiring gender-diverse children to wear gendered clothing is an infringement of their right to expression.

      1.2.2.3. Pro:
      Without uniforms, students can experiment with their individuality [45]. They may then be better able to decide which facets of their identity are most important.

        1.2.2.3.1. Con:
        Students can still wear what they want outside of school. This may provide a controlled time for experimentation that is particularly rewarding for students exploring their identity.

        1.2.2.3.2. Pro:
        Uniforms are often worn in a student's teen years and remove a safe way for students to rebel. Given this, studetnts may be pushed [46] to riskier behaviours [47] to express themselves as they seek approval from their peers.

    1.2.3. Con:
    Uniforms may improve [48] learning, by way of increasing discipline. Improving someone's education can give them greater access to opportunities in the future wherein creativity can thrive.

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      There is little or no evidence [49] to suggest wearing a uniform improves learning, if we consider pupil performance metrics like exams. Therefore, uniforms are an unnecessary imposition.

        1.2.3.1.1. Pro:
        Students themselves do not believe that school uniforms contribute to their academic achievements (p. 4 [50]).

        1.2.3.1.2. Con:
        Uniforms can help children set boundaries between school and home; making the former a place for work and the latter for relaxation (p. 17 [51]).

      1.2.3.2. Pro:
      Uniforms can be highly useful [52] to students unsure of themselves in their teen years, offering them a way to fit in. This is more important than creativity.

        1.2.3.2.1. Con:
        Children need to understand freedom of expression [53] and diversity [54] from a young age, though this is limited by uniforms. Eschewing uniforms to practicing diversity early may decrease close-mindedness.

        1.2.3.2.2. Pro:
        Defining uniqueness through material objects is detrimental to society [55]. Uniforms may offer alternative avenues for personal development that are more beneficial for children.

      1.2.3.3. Pro:
      Other methods [56] of engendering creativity can be used in place of uniforms.

        1.2.3.3.1. Pro:
        In the Oulipan [57] method, restriction facilitates new forms of creativity. There is no reason for this not to apply to schools and uniforms.

        1.2.3.3.2. Con:
        Creative activities are often demeaned as frivolous and an unnecessary [58] part of education. With cuts to arts funding [59] occurring globally [60], individual dress may be one of the few remaining avenues for student experimentation.

    1.2.4. Con:
    Uniforms encourage consistency, discipline and respect, which are all skills which can benefit creativity in the long term.

      1.2.4.1. Pro:
      There are many methods by which individuals restrict the 'artistic freedom' of children, such as stopping them from painting on the walls, which benefits their restraint and discipline in the long term. This may lead to a better and more developed relationship [61] with creativity.

      1.2.4.2. Pro:
      Many theories of creative thought, such as design thinking [62], propose that some frameworks and restrictions can guide and enhance the creative process, uniforms are no different.

      1.2.4.3. Con:
      These skills can, at times, pressure individuals to conform and focus on material success, which is often in conflict with the creative process. As such, uniforms may discourage creativity.

  1.3. Pro:
  Everyone is equal when they wear uniforms.

    1.3.1. Con:
    Uniforms often enforce gender stereotypes in educational systems by requiring students to follow gender "norms [63]" in clothing.

      1.3.1.1. Con:
      Gendered segregation could be mitigated by offering a selection of clothing items for students to choose from.

        1.3.1.1.1. Con:
        If you have a selection of items for uniforms, it's not an uniform anymore.

        1.3.1.1.2. Con:
        Gender stereotypes might still be enforced even if there is a selection of clothing items to choose from. Social pressures will likely dissuade children from experimenting with alternative clothing options.

        1.3.1.1.3. Pro:
        School children have opted to break gender binaries and encouraged all people to consider wearing skirts - be it for practical [64] or personal [65] reasons. It is useful for schools to support this.

      1.3.1.2. Pro:
      By encouraging some students to wear skirts and others not to, schools limit behaviours [66] and socialise kids into gendered restrictions from a young age.

        1.3.1.2.1. Pro:
        Limiting clothing choice with physical education uniforms can have negative effects [67], particularly for people who prefer modest dressing, or worry about menstruation.

        1.3.1.2.2. Con:
        Gendered limitations around physical activity do not stem solely from wearing skirts but rather the stigmatisation [68] of girls sweating or being muscular. Not using school uniforms may not change this.

        1.3.1.2.3. Con:
        It is not clear that without uniforms students will not still adhere to gendered stereotypes of dress. Schools should not be seen as the sole arbiters of these norms.

      1.3.1.3. Pro:
      School uniforms are often monitored [69] in a way that encourages sexist double standards of dress and bodily surveillance.

        1.3.1.3.1. Pro:
        Casual wear dress codes are often used to aggressively police [70] girls. The use of uniform infractions takes that to an unpleasant extreme.

        1.3.1.3.2. Con:
        Consciousness raising [71] exercises [72] may help [73] teachers to be more aware of their biases; this may help stop misogynistic uniform monitoring, as well as remediating other forms of discrimination.

    1.3.2. Pro:
    Uniforms may even the playing field between high and low income students, reducing stigma tied to appearance (p. 15 [74]).

      1.3.2.1. Pro:
      Uniforms reduce the social stigma attached to not having the latest brand in schools that use attire as a class marker.

        1.3.2.1.1. Pro:
        The competitive element of choosing one's own clothing can lead to bullying and division in schools (p. 25 [75]). This can harm [76] students' education prospects.

          1.3.2.1.1.1. Con:
          Not all kids from less wealthy families are bullied; therefore, clothes may not be the determining variable (p. 289 [77]).

          1.3.2.1.1.2. Pro:
          Children who do not wear the latest brands are often bullied [78] by their peers.

        1.3.2.1.2. Con:
        Uniforms are often used as a status symbol [79], negating the intention to eliminate class differences in clothing.

          1.3.2.1.2.1. Pro:
          Low income students may be inclined to drop out of school earlier than their peers, due to symbolic exclusion [80].

        1.3.2.1.3. Pro:
        Kids from low-income families may not encounter bullying as often when wearing uniforms.

          1.3.2.1.3.1. Con:
          The UK [81] ranks worst for school bullying in Europe, despite widespread school uniforms.

          1.3.2.1.3.2. Pro:
          There is increasing evidence [82] that by strictly enforcing uniform policies, fewer incidences of bullying occur.

          1.3.2.1.3.3. Con:
          Uniforms were initially implemented to mark out low income [83] children as their wealthier peers did not have to wear uniforms to receive their education.

        1.3.2.1.4. Con:
        Even with uniforms, auxiliary items, like jewelry or shoes, can signal wealth and therefore social status.

          1.3.2.1.4.1. Con:
          For safety reasons, jewelry and other accessories are often not allowed [84] in many schools. This can easily be extended to more rigorously stop wealth signalling (p. 1 [85]).

      1.3.2.2. Con:
      Dress codes do not perfectly hide social status or other characteristics that can be targeted by bullies.

        1.3.2.2.1. Pro:
        Not all kids' families can afford the upkeep for high investment uniforms which may create subtle income distinctions between students.

        1.3.2.2.2. Con:
        Even if bullying is not absolutely reduced, with everyone dressed alike, there is a hope that kids will judge based on character and learn to appreciate one another outside of cosmetic assessments of worth.

        1.3.2.2.3. Pro:
        Uniforms may still signal in or out-group status through compliance to dress codes; adherent students may be bullied (p. 11 [86]).

      1.3.2.3. Con:
      It is better to focus policy making energy on reducing class disparities than introducing restrictive clothing mandates.

        1.3.2.3.1. Con:
        Policies that reduce class disparities and uniform implementation are not mutually exclusive.

        1.3.2.3.2. Pro:
        Uniforms may conceal class differences [87] such that they seem unimportant, and deprioritize making further changes. (p. 3 [88]).

      1.3.2.4. Pro:
      Many public schools also provide uniforms for virtually no cost. This removes the burden on parents to spend excessively on their child's attire.

        1.3.2.4.1. Con:
        School uniforms cost money for clothing that is unlikely to be used outside of school. This means that, in addition to clothing that children actually like, parents will need to buy uniforms.

          1.3.2.4.1.1. Pro:
          School uniforms include Physical Education (PE) kits which can cost [89] £70 for primary school children and between £111 and £140 for secondary school age children.

          1.3.2.4.1.2. Con:
          Children can get by with smaller wardrobes when they have school uniforms, as there is no need to wear a different outfit everyday. This reduces household clothing expenses.

          1.3.2.4.1.3. Con:
          Uniforms may reduce the costs of wear and tear on other clothing items as they are worn less often.

          1.3.2.4.1.4. Pro:
          Normal clothes have a lower cost per wear than uniforms as they can be worn more.

        1.3.2.4.2. Pro:
        In some nations, like England [90], schools can use funding to help [91] with uniform costs.

        1.3.2.4.3. Pro:
        Government schools in developing countries, like Pakistan, provide [92] uniforms for reduced costs.

    1.3.3. Con:
    Uniforms often benefit the majority group in a society, whilst penalising minorities.

      1.3.3.1. Pro:
      Uniforms may exclude students from minority religious backgrounds, either by ostracising them, or by denying them expression of their faith.

        1.3.3.1.1. Pro:
        Uniforms may make it easier to identify religious students within the school and contribute [93] to their exclusion.

        1.3.3.1.2. Con:
        Minority religious groups are often targeted [94], even outside of religious garb, as people use race as a proxy [95] for religion.

      1.3.3.2. Pro:
      Uniforms rules are often racialised [96] and target [97] minority groups.

        1.3.3.2.1. Con:
        Campaigns against natural hair discrimination in uniforms are becoming increasingly [98] more popular, reducing racism in uniform policies.

        1.3.3.2.2. Con:
        Schools with racially discriminatory uniform policies often exist [99] in profoundly racist societies - the issue isn't the uniform but those enforcing it.

        1.3.3.2.3. Pro:
        Where schools do not make provisions for religious dress, minority religious communities may feel alienated [100].

        1.3.3.2.4. Pro:
        Without uniforms, minority pupils may be able to choose between adhering to majoritarian norms and embracing their heritage. Such choice is preferable for those who could be discriminated against otherwise.

      1.3.3.3. Con:
      Uniforms may stop teachers from discriminating against their students, by removing cues that facilitate biased treatment.

        1.3.3.3.1. Con:
        People are not always treated equally. Enforcing uniforms may delay pupils' realization that the world is not fair and limit their tools [101] for managing this.

        1.3.3.3.2. Con:
        Teachers may discriminate against students that rebel against their uniform. This is no different than other forms of discrimination.

        1.3.3.3.3. Pro:
        Bullying and discrimination often have major long term impacts [102]; if it is inevitable in life, better to delay the onset of its occurrence so that students can have stronger educational outcomes (p. 37 [103]).

    1.3.4. Pro:
    Students should be able to participate in a competition for a school uniform and vote for the selected ones.

  1.4. Con:
  Requiring students to wear uniforms can create issues that are detrimental to, or distracting from, receiving a substantive education.

    1.4.1. Con:
    School uniforms make the decision of what to wear easier compared to the thousands of possible options that open up in their absence.

      1.4.1.1. Con:
      Choosing clothing is not difficult enough [104] to merit implementing uniform policies.

        1.4.1.1.1. Con:
        Pupils may be more distracted without uniforms, as they stress about wearing appropriate [105] yet expressive clothing.

        1.4.1.1.2. Pro:
        Schools can set out guidelines and heuristics for students outside of uniforms in order to streamline clothing choices if necessary.

        1.4.1.1.3. Pro:
        People learn from a young age what is and isn't acceptable to wear. Given this, it is unlikely that students will struggle particularly to select appropriate clothing for school without uniforms.

        1.4.1.1.4. Con:
        Young people are often deeply self conscious (p. 4 [106]). Even if uniform policies only marginally shift the mental load of planning outfits, this could help students focus more on school.

      1.4.1.2. Pro:
      Parents dressing younger children may have an easier time persuading them to put on a uniform than picking appropriate alternatives.

        1.4.1.2.1. Con:
        Children often have whims [107] that are incongruent with their needs; nonetheless, a full wardrobe offers more suitable options that make them happy than one uniform can.

        1.4.1.2.2. Pro:
        Morning routines are likely to be stressful enough [108] without fighting with a child about what they should wear to school; decreasing this stress for parents may be highly beneficial.

    1.4.2. Con:
    Wearing uniforms prepare children for future jobs which require a uniform. This is far more important than any short term issue that could arise.

      1.4.2.1. Pro:
      Many jobs have some form of dress code [109]. School uniforms can help [110] students get into the habit of dressing appropriately and correctly for these.

        1.4.2.1.1. Con:
        Uniforms might fail to prepare students to deal with a dress code because dress codes allow for variations whereas a uniform does not.

        1.4.2.1.2. Pro:
        Dressing well [111] at work may greatly help students’ careers. Knowing the ways that subtle mores can instill different standards [112] for people is essential.

      1.4.2.2. Con:
      The purpose of school is not merely [113] job preparation.

        1.4.2.2.1. Pro:
        School uniforms were historically used to normalise colonial thinking amongst pupils in the British empire (p. 85 [114]). This kind of indoctrination should not be encouraged.

        1.4.2.2.2. Con:
        Given the high percentage [115] of time people spend in work, it is vital that students have a good education that lets them find jobs they enjoy. This takes priority over other aspects of education.

      1.4.2.3. Con:
      Not every job requires a uniform.

        1.4.2.3.1. Pro:
        Students can learn about uniform requirements in the field, especially through internships, externships, and actually working.

        1.4.2.3.2. Con:
        Many successful business people [116] advocate for wearing a uniform in order to facilitate [117] productivity and commitment in the workplace.

    1.4.3. Pro:
    Uniforms may damage the health of those who wear them, reducing the choices available to them in the future.

      1.4.3.1. Pro:
      Uniforms may offer less coverage than other forms of clothing and as such expose kids to more UV radiation (p. 4 [118]).

        1.4.3.1.1. Con:
        Children should wear suncream [119] to school; exposure to UV is not a necessary consequence of wearing uniforms.

        1.4.3.1.2. Pro:
        Though sunglasses are supported by parents [120] as a useful part of uniforms, these are seldom adopted. This restriction leaves children unduly exposed to UV rays.

      1.4.3.2. Con:
      Should school uniforms damage children's health they should be improved instead of abolished, given the other benefits [121] provided.

        1.4.3.2.1. Pro:
        Non-uniform clothes that children wear may be no better for their health. In their standardisation, uniforms are the best way of ensuring some benefit.

        1.4.3.2.2. Con:
        Insofar as school uniform policies may be long-term and unresponsive to new science, the faster responses from parents may lead to better health outcomes.

      1.4.3.3. Pro:
      There may be long term ramifications from wearing a school uniform, particularly with regards to comfort in and around exercising.

        1.4.3.3.1. Pro:
        Girls often become less active when they wear school uniforms, potentially limiting [122] the amount of exercise they do.

        1.4.3.3.2. Pro:
        Larger children may feel particularly self-conscious [123] in their school uniforms during activities like PE, and as such not participate as much.

        1.4.3.3.3. Con:
        School uniforms are only worn for a limited time each week; even when looked at cumulatively, this time is unlikely to make a significant impact on children's health.

    1.4.4. Pro:
    Students wearing uniforms incorrectly may be carded [124] and reprimanded by school staff and/or faculty (p. 25 [125]). Yet this runs counter to the purpose of school as they should be educated, not monitored for adherence to uniform policies.

      1.4.4.1. Con:
      In schools with high disciplinary standards, students may be used to being reprimanded; adding clothing infractions to the list will not significantly change school culture or educational standards.

        1.4.4.1.1. Pro:
        Students learn about societal norms by coming to appreciate their school uniform and the rules around it. This can complement a communitarian school culture and support social learning.

        1.4.4.1.2. Con:
        A positive and supportive environment [126] is necessary to spark creativity and facilitate learning. Uniforms should not be encouraged as they undermine this support, even in strict environments.

      1.4.4.2. Pro:
      Children deserve a space to learn freely and without constant monitoring before they enter the workforce. Uniforms remove this avenue.

        1.4.4.2.1. Pro:
        By allowing teachers the discretion to reprimand pupils because of their uniform, favouritism may arise. Choosing teacher's pets can often be negative [127] and should not be facilitated.

        1.4.4.2.2. Pro:
        Students may feel persecuted [128] and presume that they are being unfairly attacked when reprimanded for their uniform, even if a teacher is even handed in their approach (p. 51 [129]).

        1.4.4.2.3. Con:
        Teachers must always enforce safety minimums, some of which will include regulating student clothing. Should there be no uniform policy, this is likely to be subjective and stir dissent.

      1.4.4.3. Pro:
      Students are often punished [130] in schools for not wearing clean uniforms, either by their teachers or peers (p. 29 [131]).

        1.4.4.3.1. Con:
        It is likely that schools would punish wearing unclean clothing regardless of the type, as they attempt to enforce a minimum standard of appropriate appearance.
