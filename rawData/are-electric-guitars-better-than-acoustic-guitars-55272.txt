Discussion Name:
Are electric guitars better than acoustic guitars?

1. Thesis:
Electric guitars are better than acoustic guitars.

  1.1. Pro:
  Everything that can be done with an acoustic guitar can be done with an electric guitar, but not everything that can be done with an electric guitar can be done with an acoustic guitar.

    1.1.1. Con:
    Different types of acoustic guitars produce unique sounds that cannot be replicated on an electric guitar.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      A Spanish guitar [1] has a unique tone that cannot be generated using an electric guitar.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    Acoustic guitars have significantly fewer (accessible) frets [2] than electric guitars, thereby reducing the number of octaves one can play.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      The design of the acoustic guitar - how the neck joins the body [3] - can significantly limit one's reach to higher notes on the fretboard.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      S-cut guitars [4] allow the player to access higher notes on the fretboard.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    With a few adjustments, an electric guitar can be played like a bass [5], which is fundamentally an electric instrument.

      1.1.3.1. Con:
      The same adjustments can be made to make an acoustic guitar sound like an acoustic bass.

    1.1.4. Pro:
    Electric guitars can produce a wide range of sounds and effects.

    1.1.5. Con:
    The hollow wooden body of the guitar allows one to play percussive guitar [6], but this is not possible on an electric guitar.

      1.1.5.1. Con:
      One can generate percussive sounds on the electric guitar in the same way that bassists slap bass [7].

    1.1.6. Pro:
    Electric guitar amplifiers allow one to use distortion [8], which significantly expands the genres of music that one can play, notably rock and metal.

      1.1.6.1. Pro:
      Norman Greenbaum played Spirit in the Sky [9] on an electric guitar, which allowed him to create a very unique fuzzy tone that is forever inextricably linked to this number.

      1.1.6.2. Con:
      It is possible to use distortion with acoustic guitars [10].

    1.1.7. Pro:
    The tremolo arm in electric guitars allows players to create a wide range of fun and interesting [11] sound effects.

    1.1.8. Pro:
    Lead guitar solos [12] are typically played on electric guitars.

      1.1.8.1. Con:
      Just because lead solos are typically played on the electric guitar does not mean [13] that they cannot or should not be played on the acoustic guitar.

    1.1.9. Con:
    Acoustic guitars have a richer, more natural tone that is created by their hollow wooden body [14]. In comparison, electric guitars sound "artificial."

      1.1.9.1. Pro:
      Acoustic guitars can be played like a ukulele [15], which is typically used as an acoustic instrument.

      1.1.9.2. Con:
      The soundhole in the acoustic guitar is a practical liability.

        1.1.9.2.1. Pro:
        Guitar picks often fall [16] into the guitar hole, but this is not the case with electric guitars.

          1.1.9.2.1.1. Con:
          Removing the guitar pick from the soundhole [17] is not difficult. The guitar just has to be held in such a way that the soundhole faces the ground and shaken vigorously until the pick falls out.

        1.1.9.2.2. Pro:
        The hollow wooden body of the acoustic guitar can collect dust and anything else that can fall into it - ranging from a mummified mouse to pet feces [18]. An electric guitar has no such hole.

          1.1.9.2.2.1. Pro:
          The guitar hole can be difficult to clean [19]. One would need to remove the strings and maneuver their hand awkwardly; even then, the entire area cannot be cleaned.

          1.1.9.2.2.2. Con:
          Soundhole covers [20] prevent dust from falling into the sound hole.

          1.1.9.2.2.3. Con:
          Guitars should ideally be stored in guitar cases [21]. This protects their soundholes from collecting dust and other garbage.

        1.1.9.2.3. Con:
        There are acoustic guitars that do not have [22] a soundhole.

      1.1.9.3. Con:
      By altering amplifier and tone settings [23], the player can create sounds that are very similar to those emitted by an acoustic guitar.

      1.1.9.4. Pro:
      Acoustic guitars sound better [24] as they age.

      1.1.9.5. Con:
      The tone knobs [25] in the electric guitar allow guitarists to create a wider range of tones than what an acoustic guitar would allow.

  1.2. Pro:
  Electric guitars have the "cool [26]" factor that acoustic guitars lack.

    1.2.1. Con:
    Those who play the acoustic guitar may not be interested in appearing "cool." They may instead prefer to play acoustic guitars, which look classy and elegant.

    1.2.2. Con:
    Just because something is cool doesn't mean it is sensible. The electric guitar is one of the most "smashed" instruments in history; though this looks cool, it is wasteful [27].

    1.2.3. Pro:
    Among songs that include only electric or acoustic guitar work (i.e., not both acoustic and electric), the most iconic numbers [28] - as determined by science - were played on the electric guitar.

      1.2.3.1. Con:
      Yesterday [29] by The Beatles is played on an acoustic guitar.

      1.2.3.2. Pro:
      Mark Knopfler originally played "Sultans of Swing [30]" on his National Steel guitar, but he felt that the song came alive only when he played it on his newly bought Strat.

      1.2.3.3. Pro:
      Kurt Cobain played Smells Like Teen Spirit [31] on an electric guitar.

      1.2.3.4. Pro:
      Jeff Buckley played Hallelujah [32] on an electric guitar.

    1.2.4. Pro:
    Headbanging with an acoustic guitar would look ridiculous.

      1.2.4.1. Pro:
      Some of the most popular headbanging songs [33] were played on an electric guitar.

      1.2.4.2. Con:
      Headbanging - which is almost exclusive to electric guitars - is bad for health.

        1.2.4.2.1. Pro:
        Vigorous headbanging can endanger life by causing brain bleed [34].

        1.2.4.2.2. Pro:
        Headbanging to high-tempo songs [35] (> 150 bpm) increases the risk of head and neck injuries.

        1.2.4.2.3. Con:
        People typically headbang to metal music, which has analgesic effects [36] on people suffering from migraines.

        1.2.4.2.4. Con:
        When head banging properly (by using the entire body rather than just the head), the risk of injuries is reduced significantly, and very few people headbang enough for it to pose a serious risk (at least throughout the week).

    1.2.5. Con:
    Whether a guitar looks cool depends on who is playing it, not on the instrument.

      1.2.5.1. Con:
      Cross-culturally, women are more attracted [37] to men with guitars. They don't even have to play them; they just need to hold them for the effect to kick into action.

        1.2.5.1.1. Pro:
        In the study, women were more likely to give the confederate their number when he was carrying a guitar bag [38] (31%) than when he was carrying a gym bag (9%). This proves that, in the world of attraction, women prefer musical talent over physical prowess.

          1.2.5.1.1.1. Pro:
          Being able to play a guitar implicitly informs women that the man is likely to be sensitive and romantic [39] - traits highly desirable in a romantic partner.

          1.2.5.1.1.2. Con:
          Physical prowess is visible, but musical talent is not. Therefore, whereas the women could have easily determined how physically fit the man was by just looking at him, they may have overestimated his ability to play the guitar well.

            1.2.5.1.1.2.1. Pro:
            If the confederate had a non-gym body, he would have been perceived as a liar, thereby reducing his chances of getting a phone number.

        1.2.5.1.2. Con:
        This effect may not be specific to guitars. Any musical instrument may elicit a similar reaction.

        1.2.5.1.3. Con:
        The article describing the study conducted in France was retracted over concerns about ethics and methodology [40].

        1.2.5.1.4. Con:
        The confederate in the study conducted in France used an acoustic guitar [41] (p. 3).

          1.2.5.1.4.1. Con:
          The confederate was carrying a guitar case. It is difficult for people - especially non-players - to discern whether a musician is a carrying an acoustic guitar or an electric guitar just by looking at their case.

        1.2.5.1.5. Con:
        The confederate in the study conducted in France was the man who was rated most attractive [42] in a prior study (p. 3 [43]). Thus, this effect may not generalize to unattractive men.

      1.2.5.2. Pro:
      In one of his most iconic stage photographs, Kurt Cobain is playing an acoustic guitar [44].

  1.3. Con:
  Electric guitars are bad for health.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    One can experience electric shocks [45] from an electric guitar, if improperly connected to the amplifier.

      1.3.1.1. Pro:
      Famous electric guitar players have been electrocuted and rendered unconscious [46], and some have even died.

    1.3.2. Pro:
    California Proposition 65 [47] requires guitar manufacturers to add a "cancer hazard" warning tag to their guitars.

      1.3.2.1. Pro:
      Electric guitars are carcinogenic and increase the risk of prostate cancer [48] in men, who hold the guitar near their pelvic region (p. 130).

        1.3.2.1.1. Con:
        The cited article merely proposes a hypothesis. Neither this article nor any other publication offers empirical support [49] for this hypothesis.

        1.3.2.1.2. Pro:
        The authors of the cited article have noted that guitarists were over-represented to a "remarkable [50]" extent in obituaries in the New York Times (p. 130).

          1.3.2.1.2.1. Con:
          The scientists should have quantified the data to back up their claims. In the absence of such evidence, the article can be regarded as disingenuous junk science.

      1.3.2.2. Con:
      Harmful chemicals are used during the manufacturing of guitars [51], but they are no longer harmful once the product reaches the hands of the consumer.

    1.3.3. Pro:
    Electric guitars are essential to rock music, which is inextricably tied to substance use.

      1.3.3.1. Pro:
      Alcohol and drugs [52] are "synonymous" with certain genres of music like rock n' roll.

      1.3.3.2. Pro:
      Many rock (n' roll) songs are centered around alcohol and drug use [53].

      1.3.3.3. Con:
      Substance use is not alien to acoustic guitarists.

        1.3.3.3.1. Pro:
        Reggae music - which can be played on the acoustic guitar [54] - is closely linked to Rastafarianism [55], which promotes marijuana use.

      1.3.3.4. Pro:
      Drug addiction is common among rock guitarists [56], many of whom have died from drug (over)use.

      1.3.3.5. Con:
      People consume drugs because it enhances their enjoyment of music [57]. Therefore, one does not need to play an instrument (let alone an electric guitar) to want to consume drugs for enhanced music enjoyment.

    1.3.4. Pro:
    -> See 1.2.4.2.

    1.3.5. Con:
    Almost all of the threats that electric guitars pose to health can be mitigated or eliminated entirely by taking a few precautions [58].

    1.3.6. Pro:
    Playing the guitar [59] confers physical and mental health benefits [60]. By choosing the acoustic guitar over the electric guitar, one can circumvent all of the health risks and capitalize on all of the health benefits of guitar playing.

    1.3.7. Con:
    Both electric and acoustic [61] guitarists are at risk for carpal tunnel syndrome. However, the higher action of acoustic guitars can increase this risk, because of which those with this condition should prefer electric guitars.

    1.3.8. Pro:
    The electric guitar cable is a tripping hazard [62] for guitarists and everyone in their vicinity.

  1.4. Con:
  Electric guitars are more expensive [63] than acoustic guitars.

    1.4.1. Con:
    The higher cost of the electric guitar is offset by the unique advantages it has over the acoustic guitar.

      1.4.1.1. Pro:
      Electric guitars [64] have a more robust build, because of which they require less care and caution than an electric guitar. This offsets the increased cost.

    1.4.2. Con:
    Good-quality acoustic guitars [65] cost more than basic electric guitars.

      1.4.2.1. Con:
      Higher quality is correlated with a higher rate for any product. When quality is held constant, an electric guitar is still more expensive than an acoustic guitar.

    1.4.3. Pro:
    As an electric guitarist, with the vast amounts of kit and equipment you can buy, I know they can be expensively addictive!

    1.4.4. Pro:
    Electric guitars need electricity, which costs money.

      1.4.4.1. Con:
      The amount of electricity [66] consumed by an electric guitar is very less - significantly lesser than what would be consumed by standard household appliances.

      1.4.4.2. Pro:
      The cable connecting the electric guitar to the socket limits free movement, so the player cannot play the instrument wherever they want.

        1.4.4.2.1. Con:
        Guitarists can use wireless transmitters [67] to play their instrument without being "tied down."

        1.4.4.2.2. Con:
        Guitar cables can comfortably be as long as 20 to 30 feet [68]. This gives the guitarist a lot of space to move around quite freely.

        1.4.4.2.3. Pro:
        Filming outdoors would be easier with acoustic guitars.

          1.4.4.2.3.1. Pro:
          In The Sound of Music, Julie Andrews sang "Do Re Mi [69]" while playing her guitar with a scenic mountain background. This would not have been possible with an electric guitar.

          1.4.4.2.3.2. Pro:
          Playing outside (e.g., in the park) is more complicated with an electric guitar than with an acoustic guitar, as one would need to find an electric socket.

        1.4.4.2.4. Pro:
        -> See 1.3.8.

      1.4.4.3. Con:
      Electric guitars can be played without electricity. The only difference would be a lack of sound amplification [70], which is not essential for practicing the instrument.

    1.4.5. Pro:
    Electric guitars require many accessories (e.g., amplifier), some of which are essential to play the instrument.

      1.4.5.1. Pro:
      Picks or plectrums are more essential to electric guitarists [71] than to acoustic guitarists.

        1.4.5.1.1. Pro:
        Lead guitar requires the use of a pick or plectrum [72].

        1.4.5.1.2. Pro:
        Electric guitars require thicker picks [73], which tend to be more expensive.

        1.4.5.1.3. Con:
        Many famous electric guitarists [74] do not use a pick.

        1.4.5.1.4. Con:
        Guitarists applying more complex techniques like fingerstyle guitar [75] and two-handed tapping [76] cannot use picks or plectrums while playing the guitar.

          1.4.5.1.4.1. Con:
          Kazuki Isogai [77] uses a plectrum to play individual notes and switches to no-plectrum fingerstyle guitar playing, when required.

          1.4.5.1.4.2. Con:
          While playing "I'm on fire" by Bruce Springsteen, Nathaniel Murphy [78] uses a plectrum to play fingerstyle guitar and apply two-handed tapping.

    1.4.6. Pro:
    Because acoustic guitar makeup is simpler, home fixes are often sufficient. In contrast, electric guitars often require expert intervention.

  1.5. Con:
  Electric guitars can be annoying to neighbors, as they tend to be louder and perceived as unpleasant [79], particularly when compared to acoustic guitars.

    1.5.1. Pro:
    On average, guitarists practice for about an hour a day [80]. This can be a very long period of unpleasantness for listeners.

    1.5.2. Con:
    There are cheap and effective ways to sound proof [81] one's room.

    1.5.3. Con:
    There are several indoor (e.g., kitchen appliances) and outdoor (e.g., construction work) sources of cacophonous noises that neighbors put up with only a daily basis, so the more musical sound of an electric guitar pales in comparison.

    1.5.4. Con:
    Neighbors should learn to tolerate musicians practicing their electric guitars, because, without them, musical talent will decline.

      1.5.4.1. Pro:
      Every famous musician who created popular numbers was once a beginner. If their neighbors had complained about their playing, history might tell a different story about these musicians.

    1.5.5. Pro:
    Music genres typically associated with the electric guitar - rock and heavy metal - can be particularly annoying to listeners, because of their harsh, abrasive sounds and aggressive lyrics. [82]

      1.5.5.1. Con:
      Metal music can have a cathartic and calming effect [83].

        1.5.5.1.1. Con:
        Metal has a positive emotional effect only on listeners who like this music genre [84]. It is unlikely that all neighbors will like heavy metal music or feel like listening to it at the same times the guitarist decides to practice.

    1.5.6. Con:
    The electric guitar can be played with earphones plugged into the amplifier.

      1.5.6.1. Pro:
      The electric guitar can also be played without any amplification at all, which makes it significantly quieter than an acoustic.

      1.5.6.2. Pro:
      When played with earphones plugged in, the electric guitar would be quieter than an acoustic guitar.

      1.5.6.3. Con:
      Distortion is associated with high volume [85]. Playing distortion while plugged in with earphones can damage one's ear drum [86].

    1.5.7. Pro:
    To perfect a song, guitarists need to play the same song over and over again. This can be annoying to listeners, particularly those who do not enjoy the type of music being played.

  1.6. Pro:
  Electric guitars are significantly easier to play [87].

    1.6.1. Pro:
    Electric guitars are thinner than acoustic guitars; this makes it easier for players to hold the fretboard and strum without strain. Acoustic guitars are bulkier and require the player to stretch their arms more.

      1.6.1.1. Pro:
      The thinness of the electric guitar makes it possible for the player to hold the instrument closer to their body. This facilitates bonding between the player and the guitar.

      1.6.1.2. Con:
      Electric guitars are heavier [88], which means that one either has to play for short durations or risk injuring their shoulders.

        1.6.1.2.1. Con:
        The guitarist can play the guitar while remaining seated, thereby reducing strain on the shoulder.

          1.6.1.2.1.1. Con:
          Sitting and playing the acoustic guitar does not make the guitarist look "cool" to an audience.

            1.6.1.2.1.1.1. Con:
            Some of the most talented guitarists on YouTube, like Kazuki Isogai [89] and Kfir Ochaion [90], almost exclusively play the guitar while seated.

          1.6.1.2.1.2. Con:
          If the guitarist practices the guitar while remaining seated, they would be unprepared for the kind of shoulder pain that they are likely to experience when performing standing up in front of a live audience.

            1.6.1.2.1.2.1. Con:
            Not everyone plays the guitar to play in front of a live audience. For many, playing the guitar is just a hobby.

        1.6.1.2.2. Pro:
        In general, heavier guitars [91] sound better than lighter guitars. As such, as people upgrade to better and heavier guitars with time, they will be increasing the strain on their shoulders and back.

      1.6.1.3. Pro:
      No other instrument is held so close to the body like the guitar. In this regard, the thinner electric guitar beats the acoustic guitar in terms of proximity to the body.

    1.6.2. Con:
    It is easier to change the strings on an acoustic guitar [92] than on an electric guitar [93].

    1.6.3. Pro:
    Bending strings [94] takes more effort on the acoustic guitar than on the electric guitar.

      1.6.3.1. Pro:
      Acoustic guitars have higher action [95], which makes them more difficult to play.

    1.6.4. Con:
    Electric guitars hide mistakes [96], making it difficult for the player to identify and rectify mistakes.

      1.6.4.1. Pro:
      Distortion is effective [97] at covering up mistakes while playing.

    1.6.5. Con:
    Beginners are often encouraged to learn the acoustic guitar [98] rather than the electric guitar.

      1.6.5.1. Con:
      Building finger callouses is more painful on the acoustic guitar than on the electric guitar. This can demotivate beginners [99].
