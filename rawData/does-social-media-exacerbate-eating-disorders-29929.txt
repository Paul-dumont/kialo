Discussion Name:
Does Social Media Exacerbate Eating Disorders?

1. Thesis:
Social media exacerbates eating disorders.

  1.1. Pro:
  The content on social media creates unrealistic body standards.

    1.1.1. Con:
    The large volume of content on social media helps to highlight the diversity of body types that exist.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      If people are exposed to a wide variety of body types being depicted in positive or glamourised ways, it becomes harder to believe there is only one type of body which can be viewed as beautiful.

      1.1.1.2. Pro:
      Plus-size bloggers like GabiFresh [1] and Nicolette Mason [2], and curvy models like Iskira Lawrence [3] have massive audiences.

    1.1.2. Con:
    People are becoming increasingly aware that the content they are consuming on social media is distorted or manipulated in particular ways. This means they are less likely to take it at face value as being real life.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      The media is increasingly exposing the 'secrets' of producing the perfect social media post [4].

        1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
        More and more users are beginning to call out [5] celebrities and influencers on social media for publicising edited and unrealistic images of themselves.

          1.1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
          'Celeb Face [6]' is an instagram account dedicated to calling out celebrity images that have been photoshopped.

          1.1.2.1.1.2. Con:
          Celebrities can now block [7] comments and users, which makes calling them out more difficult, especially when it is done by less popular accounts.

          1.1.2.1.1.3. Pro:
          Body-positive activist Jameela Jamil's iWeigh campaign [8] has gained millions of followers. It encourages women not to weigh themselves in kilograms or pounds, but in the things that are important to them, such as relationships, family, or career.

            1.1.2.1.1.3.1. Pro:
            Jameela Jamil regularly calls out [9] other celebrities on social media for pushing weight loss products when the results are clearly a result of other methods (personal trainers, airbrushing, etc).

              1.1.2.1.1.3.1.1. Pro:
              Jameela Jamil has frequently criticised [10] the Kardashians for promoting harmful weight loss products.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      Eating disorders are a form of mental illness and, as such, the brains of sufferers do not always accept the usual laws of logic [11]. So, while an individual may be aware images are edited and unrealistic, the reality of its origins may not register when the brain is processing it.

      1.1.2.3. Pro:
      Influencers posting on Instagram [12] are required to follow local laws and regulations, such as the ASA (Advertising Standards Authority) guidelines [13], when posting content so it’s clear to users when a celebrity is promoting something they have been paid for.

        1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
        According to the Competition and Markets Authority [14], if influencers do not label their posts properly, followers may be led to believe that an endorsement represents the star’s own view, rather than a paid-for promotion.They are then more likely to place trust in that product, as they think it has been recommended by someone they admire. They might not do so, however, if it was made clear that the brands featured have paid, or in some other way rewarded, the celebrity in return for endorsement.

          1.1.2.3.1.1. Con:
          Research suggests [15] that disclosing when content is sponsored has very little effect on an influencer’s engagement. Sponsored content is engaged with equally in comparison to non-sponsored content.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    A range of editing programs and apps [16] are available for any social media user to edit their own photographic content.

    1.1.4. Con:
    Eating disorders are a form of mental illness [17] and not of extreme vanity.

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      Research has shown the brains of those who suffer eating disorders function and react differently [18] to those who have not.

    1.1.5. Pro:
    There is no way of knowing whether or not a photo has been photoshopped to make the individual look more stereotypically beautiful.

      1.1.5.1. Con:
      There are ways to analyse photos to be able to tell whether editing has occurred.

        1.1.5.1.1. Pro:
        Some websites, such as fotoforensics.com [19] can analyse an image to determine whether it has been digitally enhanced or edited.

        1.1.5.1.2. Pro:
        Discerning social media users can look for signs [20] of editing [21] in an image.

        1.1.5.1.3. Con:
        -> See 1.1.2.2.

      1.1.5.2. Pro:
      These harms are exacerbated when users can post comments stating that it is a natural photo and that they've used no filters or photoshop, when there is no way of knowing if they are telling the truth.

    1.1.6. Pro:
    Social media influencers often use professional photographers, lighting, and equipment to take their pictures, however they are passed off as candid and natural.

      1.1.6.1. Pro:
      Unnatural representations of the female body in this way are used to promote and sell [22] seemingly 'miracle' weight loss solutions by sponsoring the celebrities and influencers to endorse a product.

        1.1.6.1.1. Con:
        Many social media influencers are actively walking away [23] from such promotions once they realise the damage they have the potential to cause.

        1.1.6.1.2. Con:
        Buying 'miracle' dieting aids that are advertised and promoted online can potentially be extremely dangerous [24].

      1.1.6.2. Con:
      -> See 1.1.2.1.1.

      1.1.6.3. Con:
      Some influencers are turning away from the traditional instagram aesthetic [25] in order to appear more accessible to their followers.

  1.2. Pro:
  Social media sites facilitate the promotion of disordered eating.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Pro-anorexia (‘pro-ana’) and pro-bulimia ('pro-mia’) websites [26] promote the illness as a lifestyle and encourage extreme thinness through web pages filled unrealistically thin bodies.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      Research shows that although there are more anorexia-related videos on Youtube that are informative in nature, pro-anorexia videos are viewed more often and favored three times more than purely informative ones (p.2 [27]).

      1.2.1.2. Pro:
      'Pro-ana [28]' groups can propagate unchecked on many social media platforms.

        1.2.1.2.1. Con:
        In some places, such as France [29], new legislation means that platforms found to be supporting, allowing, or otherwise encouraging the proliferation of pro-ana groups and websites can be fined.

      1.2.1.3. Pro:
      Jacqueline Head from BBC News (2007 [30]) reports that "joining one 'pro-ana' group can lead you to five more, and so on, opening up a world that, while posing as a means of support, more often tends to glamorise and advocate illnesses that can cause infertility, heart disease and death."

    1.2.2. Pro:
    The majority [31] of social media users are 'millennials [32]', the same age group that are most at risk [33] of developing an eating disorder.

    1.2.3. Pro:
    Suggested hashtags [34] promoting poor eating habits and encouraging weight loss can appear on people's social media feeds.

      1.2.3.1. Pro:
      This can lead to more conversations among friends about weight and weight loss, which can have a negative impact on one's body image.

        1.2.3.1.1. Pro:
        Recent research has shown that conversations with peers about appearance play a significant role in affecting body image (p. 27 [35]).

          1.2.3.1.1.1. Pro:
          A 1999 study of adolescent girls found that the frequency in which friends talked about dieting significantly predicted body image concern and dietary restraint (p. 5 [36]).

          1.2.3.1.1.2. Pro:
          In a 2011 study, appearance conversations with friends emerged as a significant predictor of body dissatisfaction, and this effect was mediated by internalisation processes (p. 5 [37]).

    1.2.4. Pro:
    The photo related features of social media websites reinforce or worsen body image issues.

      1.2.4.1. Pro:
      Researchers have found that it is not the total time on Facebook that leads to negative self-image, but it is the time that is spent on viewing photos that results in negative views. They suggest that teenage girls with high thin ideal internalization and body dissatisfaction are more likely to interact with photo related Facebook features, which will reinforce or worsen the existing body image issues (p. 11 [38]).

      1.2.4.2. Pro:
      A researcher at the University of Edinburgh in Scotland compared [39] two big social networks and concluded that text-heavy Reddit has a clear advantage over photo-led Instagram. Reddit tends to be more supportive and foster a sense of common purpose as it is based around interest communities, not photos.

        1.2.4.2.1. Pro:
        Reddit has an entire forum [40] dedicated to anonymous discussion of eating disorders, providing a safe space for sufferers to open up as well as ask for advice and help.

      1.2.4.3. Con:
      The photo related features of social media websites also have benefits.

        1.2.4.3.1. Pro:
        Image-based platforms such as Instagram have been shown to confer a significant decrease in self-reported loneliness [41] to users, whereas text-based platforms such as Twitter do not.

          1.2.4.3.1.1. Con:
          Image-based platforms, along with other forms of social media platform, are negatively affecting [42] real-life, face-to-face interactions.

      1.2.4.4. Pro:
      A systematic review of 20 papers [43] published in 2016 found that photo-based activities, like scrolling through Instagram or posting pictures of yourself, were a particular problem when it came to negative thoughts about your body.

      1.2.4.5. Pro:
      In a study [44], 88 percent of women said they compare themselves to images in the media, with half saying the comparison is unfavorable. 65 percent of men said they compare themselves to images in the media and, of that, 37 percent said the comparison is unfavorable.

    1.2.5. Pro:
    “Clean eating” is promoted by [45] some food bloggers, which can have a negative impact on vulnerable young people.

      1.2.5.1. Pro:
      Pioneers of the ‘healthy eating movement’ havea powerful social media presence [46], particularly on Instagram, reaching and influencing hundreds of thousands of people, despite often having no formal training in health sciences or nutrition.

        1.2.5.1.1. Pro:
        Although often not based on scientific evidence, individuals are encouraged [47] by such bloggers to cut out various food groups from their diets, potentially leading to an unbalanced diet and deficiencies.

          1.2.5.1.1.1. Pro:
          Whilst a legitimate medical problem for a small proportion of the population, perceived gluten intolerance - encouraged by food bloggers and the media - has lead to an unhealthy obsession [48] with a gluten-free diet amongst many non-coeliac sufferers.

          1.2.5.1.1.2. Pro:
          Nutritionists have warned that Instagram vegan diets [49] are risking malnutrition amongst millennials.

          1.2.5.1.1.3. Pro:
          Only 4% [50] of food and nutrition tweets come from qualified dieticians.

        1.2.5.1.2. Con:
        There is a wealth of factual and legitimate information on nutrition on the internet, a long with guidance [51] on how to be discerning about the 'facts' one comes across.

      1.2.5.2. Pro:
      “Clean eating” dietary trend may encourage psychological problems [52] around food, and in some cases, lead to eating disorders such as anorexia nervosa (AN) or orthorexia nervosa (ON).

        1.2.5.2.1. Pro:
        Orthorexia nervosa [53] is a psychological condition. Experts have described it as a “fixation with righteous eating”.

        1.2.5.2.2. Pro:
        According to a dietitian at the British Dietetic Association [54], a fixation with eating healthily had been a noticeable route into eating disorders for vulnerable individuals in the past couple of years. She identified social media and the rise of healthy food trends and blogs as key drivers of the trend.

        1.2.5.2.3. Pro:
        Research [55] shows that the healthy eating community on Instagram has a high prevalence of orthorexia [56] symptoms, with higher Instagram use being linked to increased symptoms. The prevalence of orthorexia nervosa among the study population was 49%, which is significantly higher than the general population (<1%).

      1.2.5.3. Con:
      Clean eating is a recommended means of improving health [57]. That some celebrities misrepresent what it entails does not negate the impact of a healthy "clean" diet.

    1.2.6. Pro:
    Algorithms suggest content to users based on what they have viewed/liked before. This can result in individuals who view some weight loss tips being bombarded [58] with content about extreme dieting and pro-ana content. This could push them towards disordered eating.

      1.2.6.1. Pro:
      Because algorithms use commonalitiesrather than human rationale [59] and intuition to differentiate between content, even users who frequently - yet solely - view body positivity and healthy eating content may be bombarded with inappropriate posts containing, for example, unhealthy dieting methods and which push 'miracle' slimming products. All of the above could be triggering for a suffering or recovering individual, despite their best efforts to avoid such content.

    1.2.7. Con:
    Social media sites, such as Instagram and Facebook [60], are now clamping down on harmful and pro-anorexia content.

      1.2.7.1. Con:
      Even though such groups contravene the rules of these sites, there is often still a short time delay [61] between their inception and their deletion by the platforms moderators or algorithms.

      1.2.7.2. Con:
      Some third party moderators [62] are circumventing social media platforms' codes of conduct and allowing such groups to remain.

      1.2.7.3. Pro:
      New plans mean social media bosses could be held liable [63] for harmful content on their platforms.

      1.2.7.4. Pro:
      Tumblr has banned [64] blogs that promote or glorifies self-harm, like eating disorders.

      1.2.7.5. Con:
      Social media sites have been unable to effectively remove [65] pro-eating disorder content, as it is easy for individuals to get around blocks on content by using slightly different hashtags that are not blocked.

        1.2.7.5.1. Pro:
        Experts report that such controlling measures actually exacerbate the problem [66] by polarising the vulnerable pro-ED [67] community.

        1.2.7.5.2. Pro:
        For example, the hashtag #thinspiration was quickly replaced with #thynesperation following a ban on the former.

        1.2.7.5.3. Pro:
        According to research by a Georgia Tech team [68], banning terms like 'thigh gap' and 'thinspiration' only made the network's pro-anorexia problem worse. Young people searching for them worked around the ban by creating variants. Some of the terms had even further reach than those they were designed to replace, spreading to other sites such as Tumblr and Twitter.

      1.2.7.6. Con:
      Despite implementing policies to try and remove pro-ED content, studies [69] have found that there is still a significant amount of harmful content on social media sites.

        1.2.7.6.1. Pro:
        Social media filtering algorithms cannot effectively discern [70] between positive and negative or potentially damaging content.

      1.2.7.7. Pro:
      Facebook has increased efforts to curb misleading health content and has recently announced [71] that Facebook’s AI will place the posts attempting to sell products or services based on health-related claim and including sensational health claims lower in News Feed. This includes posts promoting a medication or pill claiming to help you lose weight.

        1.2.7.7.1. Con:
        Placing such posts lower in the News Feed does not mean they are banned and won't be accessed by people. Facebook still allows weight loss products and plans ads for people aged 18 years or older (5.13 [72]).

      1.2.7.8. Pro:
      -> See 1.1.2.3.

      1.2.7.9. Pro:
      According to Facebook and Instagram's advertising policies [73], adverts cannot feature ‘before-and-after images’ that portray unlikely results. They also cannot imply negative or self-conscious perceptions in order to promote diet, weight loss or other health related products.

    1.2.8. Con:
    Social media is taking proactive steps to reach out to individuals suffering from eating disorders.

      1.2.8.1. Pro:
      If you search for pro-ED terms, Instagram flags you [74] with a warning: “Can we help? Posts with words or tags you’re searching for often encourage behavior that can cause harm and even lead to death. If you’re going through something difficult, we’d like to help.” The platform offers you the option to “get support” or “see posts anyway”.

      1.2.8.2. Pro:
      If someone searches for “eating disorders” on Tumblr, a popular blogging network, they will see a callout note [75] that asks them if everything is okay.

      1.2.8.3. Pro:
      If a person searches for posts related to eating disorders on Instagram, they will be given the option to learn more about eating disorders and get support. Users are navigated to the Privacy and Safety center that has information [76] on how to spot signs of an eating disorder, how to help sufferers and what organizations to contact for further assistance.

    1.2.9. Pro:
    Celebrities frequently advertise weight loss products on social media, like teas, shakes, appetite-suppressing lollipops [77] and diet pills.

      1.2.9.1. Con:
      -> See 1.1.2.1.1.3.1.

      1.2.9.2. Pro:
      The NHS has warned that celebrity influencers could be damaging [78] children's health.

        1.2.9.2.1. Pro:
        Research [79] has found that exposure to social media leads to problems in adolescence, and that the link between the two is more noticeable in girls than in boys.

        1.2.9.2.2. Pro:
        Research shows that children develop strong opinions on body image much earlier [80] than most parents think.

        1.2.9.2.3. Pro:
        Children don’t always understand [81] that the pictures on Instagram are heavily photoshopped.

        1.2.9.2.4. Pro:
        NHS England’s [82] national medical director has urged social media companies to ban “irresponsible and unsafe” adverts for these health products of dubious value [83]. He thinks that the risks of quick-fix weight loss outweigh the benefits, and advertising these products without a health warning poses a risk to the physical and mental well-being of followers who use them.

      1.2.9.3. Con:
      Celebrities have been used to popularize diet culture sans social media.

        1.2.9.3.1. Pro:
        Jane Fonda’s [84] 1982 work-out video sold 17 million copies, helping ignite VCR sales.

      1.2.9.4. Con:
      Some celebrities - such as Holly Willoughby [85] - have had their images and personas used without permission to falsely promote unhealthy dieting products.

      1.2.9.5. Pro:
      Do to their high number of followers, these celebrities can command fees of between $1,000 to $250,000 per post [86]. This sort of money can tempt celebrities to endorse risky products they haven’t researched.

  1.3. Con:
  Social media can be used to promote body positivity.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    Both influencers and recovery institutions can use social media [87] to reach those suffering from eating disorders.

    1.3.2. Pro:
    Hashtags such as #bodypositivity [88] encourage users to post pictures in which they feel confident and happy in themselves.

      1.3.2.1. Pro:
      This movement aims to subvert the stereotypical idea of what 'beautiful' is by encouraging women of all body types to post pictures of them feeling happy and confident.

        1.3.2.1.1. Pro:
        -> See 1.1.1.2.

      1.3.2.2. Con:
      These hashtags can be misused [89] by users (largely thin, white women: those who conform to traditional western beauty ideals) to promote their own brand of #fitspo [90].

      1.3.2.3. Pro:
      A study [91] showed that women who viewed self-compassion quotes showed greater body satisfaction, body appreciation, self-compassion, and reduced negative mood compared to women who viewed neutral images.

      1.3.2.4. Pro:
      The term ‘Body Positivity’ started to explode [92] in 2017, with over 4.3 million hashtags of #bodypositive, and 1.36 million of #bodypositivity on Instagram alone.

      1.3.2.5. Pro:
      A study [93] has revealed that viewing social media content aimed at challenging mainstream beauty ideals can boost body confidence among young women.

      1.3.2.6. Con:
      Hashtags can become little more than buzzwords [94] that encumber the growth of the debate surrounding issues such as eating disorders.

    1.3.3. Pro:
    Body positive influencers [95] can talk about their own experiences with learning to love their body which can help to inspire others.

      1.3.3.1. Pro:
      Some influencers and models [96] are leading the body positive movement on social media, encouraging people (women especially) to love their bodies the way they are and reject traditional beauty standards.

      1.3.3.2. Pro:
      An Instagram [97] study involved showing 195 young women either body-positive content from popular accounts, photos showing thin women in bikinis or fitness gear, or neutral images of nature. The researchers found that exposing women to #bodypositive Instagram content appeared to boost their satisfaction with their own bodies.

        1.3.3.2.1. Con:
        The same study [98] found that women who’d seen the body-positive photos still ended up objectifying themselves.

    1.3.4. Pro:
    Social media can push pop culture to be more accepting of the body-positive movement.

      1.3.4.1. Pro:
      Tess Holliday, a body-positive activist's career was launched [99] when she founded the #EffYourBodyStandards hastag. She has since become Self magazine's first-ever digital cover star [100] and has signed with London-based modeling agency MiLK Management.

      1.3.4.2. Pro:
      Lane Bryant, a plus-sized retailer, owes [101] much of its newfound popularity to social media.

      1.3.4.3. Pro:
      Social media creates pressure [102] on businesses to be more socially responsible and to rethink value creation [103] as a long-term investment in society.

      1.3.4.4. Pro:
      Social media has popularized [104] the fitness industry so much that it has infiltrated most commercial brands and industries today.

    1.3.5. Pro:
    -> See 1.1.2.1.1.3.

    1.3.6. Con:
    Individuals who are prone to eating disorders often seek out content which reinforces their views rather than counters them. If pro-eating disorder and body positive content exists on social media at the same time, individuals suffering with the disorder are likely to mainly consume content which exacerbates their illness.

    1.3.7. Pro:
    Unlike traditional media where editors have an incentive to pick stereotypically beautiful women in order to sell or promote products, there is no oversight on who can post content on social media. This makes social media a unique outlet for expressing body positive sentiments and allowing non-stereotypical ideas of beauty to gain attention.

      1.3.7.1. Pro:
      With so many people [105] (Instagram influencers and “regular” folk alike) going viral every day for embracing—and celebrating—their body’s flaws, social media allows for the amplification of such positive content in a way that would not be possible in real life.

      1.3.7.2. Pro:
      Social media is often credited [106] with starting and facilitating the body positive movement.

        1.3.7.2.1. Pro:
        Tess Holliday [107] has used social media for her body-positive campaigns.

        1.3.7.2.2. Pro:
        -> See 1.1.2.1.1.3.

        1.3.7.2.3. Pro:
        Model and body-positive activist Ashley Graham [108] used social media as a way to reach out to women and build her profile after years of being rejected by mainstream modelling agencies.

      1.3.7.3. Con:
      The images which receive the most attention on social media, and which are therefore most widely shared, are likely to conform to popular stereotypes about what is beautiful and attractive.

    1.3.8. Con:
    The language of body positivity can easily be appropriated to reinforce traditional beauty standards. A lot of content which claims to be body positive is in fact counter-productive.

      1.3.8.1. Pro:
      Successful social media influencers often appropriate [109] the language of body positivity, despite being entirely conventionally attractive. Thus a lot of content which claims to be body-positive in fact reinforces traditional beauty standards.

        1.3.8.1.1. Pro:
        Because body positivity is a popular trend, conventionally attractive celebrities or influencers have an incentive to use the language of body positivity, even though their own bodies are already deemed attractive.

      1.3.8.2. Pro:
      Clothing and fashion brands have an incentive to add body-positive language to their advertising in order to sell more clothes. However, they also have an incentive to use conventionally attractive models. The result is brands using stereotypically beautiful models, with one or two stretch marks [110], thus enabling brands to claim that they are body positive while changing nothing about how they market products.

        1.3.8.2.1. Con:
        Some brands are turning away from traditional influencers to push their products and choosing, rather, to partner with lesser known [111] social media users who seem more trustworthy and authentic than their more famous counterparts. This tendency towards more relatable individuals - whilst market driven - may have the effect of promoting a less extreme and manipulated version of what body positivity means.

        1.3.8.2.2. Pro:
        Even if a clothing or fashion brand uses body-positive language, it is very unlikely to hire substantially overweight or disabled models. Most models are also Caucasian [112]. Thus the brand's body-positive language ends up being applied primarily to white, thin, able-bodied men and women.

          1.3.8.2.2.1. Con:
          Lately, a growing number of brands are beginning to hire more diverse models, including plus-sized people [113] and people with disabilities [114].

      1.3.8.3. Pro:
      If the language of body positivity is coupled with images of conventionally attractive people, this implies that only conventionally attractive people can be positive about their bodies.

        1.3.8.3.1. Pro:
        Many believe the body positivity movement has been co-opted, now alienates the people it was designed to encourage, and promotes the idea that one has to be a certain size to be considered 'acceptably fat [115]'.

  1.4. Con:
  Social media is a necessary avenue to finding peer support in recovery.

    1.4.1. Con:
    The line between groups which "support" a sufferer by helping them cope with their illness, and groups which "support" a sufferer by encouraging unhealthy behaviour, can be blurred.

      1.4.1.1. Pro:
      -> See 1.2.1.3.

    1.4.2. Pro:
    Social media can aid recovery for individuals with eating disorders by creating a support network who encourage developing a 'recovery identity'. [116]

      1.4.2.1. Pro:
      Reading about other individuals experiences can provide [117] reassurance and encouragement for individuals who often feel like they are going through the experience alone.

      1.4.2.2. Pro:
      Eating Disorder Hope [118] is a large online community that provides Eating Disorder sufferers, their friends, families, and treatment organizations with educational information and assistance.

    1.4.3. Con:
    According to a Pew Research Center survey [119], only 5% of teenagers said that social media helps in getting support from others.

    1.4.4. Pro:
    Individuals can express their feelings on a variety of social media platforms, even if they're not communicating with someone directly. Studies [120] show that putting your feelings into words can reduce their intensity.

      1.4.4.1. Pro:
      A study [121] of Twitter users indicated that when people are shown emotionally evocative images, their distress or negative emotions are significantly reduced by the mere act of putting their feelings into words.

    1.4.5. Con:
    A study [122] found that the likelihood of recovery from eating disorders for individuals primarily using Tumblr for support to be less than the likelihood of recovery for individuals using more conventional outlets.

    1.4.6. Pro:
    A sharing of stories on social media can help individuals identify the symptoms [123] of an eating disorder in their own life.

    1.4.7. Pro:
    Recovery communities can encourage [124] others to seek professional help and treatment for their disorder by speaking about how beneficial they found it to be for their own recovery.

    1.4.8. Con:
    Encountering pro-ED content of any kind - or even just images promoting traditional beauty standards - on social media can have the power to knock someone in recovery's confidence and disrupt their progress.

      1.4.8.1. Pro:
      One woman, Joanna Kay [125], was far along the road to recovery from her eating disorder until she came across a wealth of pro-ED content on sites like Instagram, Facebook, and Tumblr. Being exposed to this content resulted in her recovery halting and her disorder reappearing.

    1.4.9. Pro:
    Many people might not have close friends or family with whom they can discuss their illnesses. Reaching out to others online may be their only source of support and encouragement.

      1.4.9.1. Pro:
      Loneliness has been linked [126] to eating disorders.

        1.4.9.1.1. Pro:
        For many people struggling with binge eating disorder, compulsive over-eating, or bulimia, food is described [127] as their “main friend or comfort.”

        1.4.9.1.2. Pro:
        A study [128] has shown that loneliness contributes to and fuels eating disorder symptoms.

      1.4.9.2. Pro:
      Many people believe that only women can suffer from eating disorders, and so male suffers may be even more likely to remain silent [129]. Being able to ask questions and find help anonymously on social media may be the only support structure for men who feel they cannot talk about their experiences elsewhere.

        1.4.9.2.1. Pro:
        Eating disorders amongst men have been stigmatised [130] to a greater extent than amongst women.

    1.4.10. Pro:
    Social media can allow people to voice their problems and seek advice anonymously, which can be useful to those who are embarrassed or confused by their illness.

      1.4.10.1. Pro:
      This may be especially true for men [131], who are less likely to come forward and seek help for eating disorders due to social stigma.

      1.4.10.2. Pro:
      Researchers [132] have found that people with eating disorders hold low trust beliefs in others and show an unwillingness to disclose personal information to close others, notably about eating.

    1.4.11. Con:
    Recovering from mental illness often requires identifying and challenging harmful assumptions and beliefs. Other sufferers (who often make similar assumptions and have similar beliefs) may be poorly placed to help with this.

      1.4.11.1. Pro:
