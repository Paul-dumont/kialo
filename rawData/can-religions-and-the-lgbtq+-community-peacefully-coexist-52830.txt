Discussion Name:
Can Religions and the LGBTQ+ Community Peacefully Coexist?

1. Thesis:
Religions and the LGBTQ+ community can peacefully coexist.

  1.1. Con:
  Religious communities and the LGBT community tend to be very different from one another.

    1.1.1. Con:
    Communities with opposing beliefs can avoid interacting with one another to prevent conflict.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      Avoiding one another does not amount to coexistence in any meaningful sense.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      Conflict can still occur where aspects of identiy intersect.

    1.1.2. Con:
    The religious community is very different (i.e., heteronormative) from the LGBTQ+ community because they have excluded these individuals throughout history. The differences are neither natural nor essential to their identities.

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      There are many examples of Hindu mythological figures [1] that are part of the LGBTQ+ community.

        1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
        Iravan is the god of transgender people. He is highly regarded as honest, and he fulfills his duty [2]. He is the son of Arjuna, a great warrior.

        1.1.2.1.2. Pro:
        Shikhandi [3], a great Hindu warrior in the Mahabharata, is transgender, and the uniqueness and holiness of his sexuality helps the good defeat the evil in the Mahabharata.

        1.1.2.1.3. Pro:
        Agni (the god of Fire), Varuna (the god of water), Mitra (the god of the deep water), and Soma (the god of the moon) are gay [4].

        1.1.2.1.4. Pro:
        Ardhanarishvara [5] is androgynous.

        1.1.2.1.5. Pro:
        Arjuna got a curse that he would become a girl for a year. While everyone was stunned, Indra, the king of the gods, told Arjuna that it was nothing to be ashamed of and that his transgenderness [6] would help him and his brothers immensely.

        1.1.2.1.6. Pro:
        The Gay & Lesbian Vaishnava Association (GALVA) emphasises [7] the gender fluidity of Hindu deities, and states that “everything in this world is a reflection of the original subtle and spiritual reality.”

        1.1.2.1.7. Con:
        Even if there are many Hindu mythologcial figures, they are still only representative of the position adopted by a single religion.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      Nearly half [8] of US LGBTQ+ adults are religious.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        Religion hurts LGBTQ+ people as they can suffer from internalized homophobia. In fact, such internal ideological conflicts can increase their risk for psychological problems, including suicide [9].

      1.1.2.3. Pro:
      The religious community is not a homogeneous group. There are many different religions, many sects within each religion, and individual differences in beliefs within these subgroups. Thus, there is no essential homogeneity that threaten peaceful coexistence with LGBT+ people.

        1.1.2.3.1. Con:
        Even if religion does not manifest through a homogeneous group, religious groups share many characteristics.

          1.1.2.3.1.1. Con:
          Said shared characteristics are not universal across the board, including their ideas of what constitutes sexuality and gender identity.

    1.1.3. Pro:
    Religions such as Islam condemn homosexuality, which acts as a major barrier for coexistence.

    1.1.4. Con:
    Diverging aspects of identity, whether religious or sexual orientation, does not need to determine how individuals treat each other.

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      Individuals should treat others with respect, even if they are of a different religious or sexual orientation.

      1.1.4.2. Con:
      If diverging identities have key tenants that are opposed to one another, these differences may inevitably impact how people treat one another.

        1.1.4.2.1. Pro:
        If we can educate individuals to treat others with respect, reguardless of any external factors, it will balance out their differences.

      1.1.4.3. Pro:
      Basic human rights [10] apply to all people, regardless of their identity. All individuals should uphold these virtues with regard to their treatment of all other individuals.

      1.1.4.4. Pro:
      Groups with different beliefs can learn from each other.

        1.1.4.4.1. Pro:
        Members of the LGBTQ+ community could benefit from the moral and spiritual teachings of religions.

          1.1.4.4.1.1. Con:
          Religious people teaching about morality and spirituality does not imply that they follow them. Therefore, the LGBTQ+ community would be better off improving their spiritual life on their own or with help from non-religious advisors.

      1.1.4.5. Pro:
      LGBTQ+ people and religious people are always going to exist at the same time, so they should start trying to live in peace with each other. They do not have to agree with one another, but they should stop hating each other.

    1.1.5. Pro:
    Many LGBTQ+ individuals feel conflicted about their religious and sexual identities. This suggests there is incompatibility between both identities.

      1.1.5.1. Con:
      Most LGBT individuals do not experience conflict between their religious and sexual identities [11].

      1.1.5.2. Pro:
      Having to integrate religious beliefs and LGBT identities can lead to identity conflict in young people [12] and render them vulnerable to many mental heath problems, including suicide.

        1.1.5.2.1. Con:
        Individuals can learn to accept, reconcile and integrate conflicting parts of their identity.

      1.1.5.3. Con:
      Feeling conflictual about one's religious and sexual identity does not indicate that the sources of that conflict is derived from religious teachings or scripture.

        1.1.5.3.1. Pro:
        One may feel conflict about their sexual and religious identities for fear of social rejection.

    1.1.6. Con:
    Many people are both memers of the religious community, and members of the LGBT community. This suggests the positions represented by the two communities, can be reconciled.

      1.1.6.1. Pro:
      Many people of faith live in such a way that what they would affirm as their beliefs are different from how they live their lives. Similarly, LGBTQIA+ people may feel comfortable living with this dissonance, making it possible for both communities to co-exist.

      1.1.6.2. Pro:
      There are religious gay people and closeted religious people. The two communities trying to peacefully coexist would help these individuals resolve the conflicts created by such ideological polarization.

    1.1.7. Pro:
    Religion, gender identity or sexual orientation are profoundly important aspects of self-identity. Opposing views with regard to identity is therefore likely to produce conflict.

      1.1.7.1. Pro:
      Conflict is generally most intense around those areas deemed to be especially signficant.

      1.1.7.2. Con:
      Differences in aspects of personal identity does not equate to those differences being oppositional to one another.

  1.2. Pro:
  Religious groups accept the LGBTQ+ community.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Most religions encourage association with non-believers or those they consider "sinners", instead of prohibiting it.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      Christianity encourages evangelism [13] to spread the word of God, including missionaries who travel and live amongst non-believer populations.

        1.2.1.1.1. Con:
        Missionaries might associate with anyone, but historically their purpose was not always peaceful for example the forced conversions of the British empire [14].

        1.2.1.1.2. Con:
        To evangelize a population is not the same as to co-exist with them if the evangelizers are actively trying to change the population, in this case LGBTQ+ people, into being something else, in this case, e.g., either straight or celibates who deny their sexuality. To evangelize gay people without trying to change them could be co-existence.

      1.2.1.2. Pro:
      Muslims are allowed to [15] live in a non-Muslim country and marry non-Muslims [16], as long as they have the freedom to worship their religion.

        1.2.1.2.1. Con:
        Relatively few Muslims count non-Muslims among their close friends, and, in most countries, few are comfortable [17] with the idea of their son or daughter marrying outside the faith.

      1.2.1.3. Pro:
      Jesus explicitly encouraged contact with sinners [18] and the pariahs of society. He saw individuals not just their labels, and passages in the Bible where Jesus ate with sinners shows that he looked beyond culture to people’s hearts.

        1.2.1.3.1. Pro:
        Jesus was criticized for spending time with the outcasts and “socially unacceptable” people [19], calling Him a “friend of sinners.”

        1.2.1.3.2. Pro:
        In Matthew 9:10–13 [20], Jesus was ridiculed by the religious leaders for his associations. He answers them by saying, “I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners”.

      1.2.1.4. Pro:
      Pro-gay Christians may believe that, even if it is wrong, they have no right to judge, as they may have done far worse.

    1.2.2. Pro:
    A growing number of organized religious groups [21] have welcomed LGBTQ people.

      1.2.2.1. Pro:
      Large majorities [22] of every major religious group favor nondiscrimination laws that protect LGBTQ+ people.

      1.2.2.2. Pro:
      Religions originating from India including Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism tend to have no strong stance against LGBTQ+ actions.

        1.2.2.2.1. Pro:
        India has had a tradition of transgender acceptance for at least 4000 years, with ancient religious Hindu texts such as the Vedas [23] often referring to people of a third gender (often seen as an ancient parallel to lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender and intersex identities) known as Hijra [24].

        1.2.2.2.2. Pro:
        Hindu groups are historically not unified [25] regarding the issue of homosexuality, each one having a distinct doctrinal view. This allows each group the religious freedom to peacefully coexist with LGBTQ+ groups.

          1.2.2.2.2.1. Con:
          If there is no unified position on homosexuality, this provides scope for opposition to homosexuality.

        1.2.2.2.3. Con:
        Some Hindu communities tend to be unwelcoming of LGBTQ+ people [26].

        1.2.2.2.4. Pro:
        The Hindu American Foundation in its policy brief on Hindus and Homosexuality, claims that Hinduism does not provide a reason to reject LGBTQ individuals [27].

        1.2.2.2.5. Con:
        Not having a concrete position on LGBTQ+ issued does not equate to acceptance of the LGBTQ+ community.

          1.2.2.2.5.1. Con:
          In the absence of religious guidance, any position adopted on LGBTQ+ issues is a product of individual thought and not of religion.

      1.2.2.3. Pro:
      Pagan religions are generally more accepting of and followed by LGBTQ+ persons than the general population.

        1.2.2.3.1. Pro:
        A 2003 survey found 28.3% of American neopagans identified as gay, lesbian, or bisexual [28], much higher than the 2.3% of the general population [29] in 2012.

      1.2.2.4. Pro:
      Satanism is accepting of all sexual orientations [30].

        1.2.2.4.1. Con:
        Satanism is not a religion but merely a counter-cultural movement against religious institutions.

          1.2.2.4.1.1. Con:
          The US government [31] considers the Satanist group "The Satanic Temple" as a religion.

          1.2.2.4.1.2. Con:
          Satanists [32] consider their beliefs a religion.

          1.2.2.4.1.3. Con:
          Satanism has a recognisable set of beliefs and values.

            1.2.2.4.1.3.1. Pro:
            The Satanic temple outlines the fundamental tenets [33] of the organisation.

        1.2.2.4.2. Con:
        Satanism is too far outside the "normal" belief systems of other religions to be applicable when debating whether religion as a whole is accepting of LGBTQ+ persons.

          1.2.2.4.2.1. Con:
          Satanism may be more "normal" than other religions, if its beliefs are more consistent with the values of modern democracy (e.g., equality, liberty). In this regard, traditional religions are regressive and should be de-emphasized.

            1.2.2.4.2.1.1. Pro:
            Modern Satanist branches include some modern liberal stances such as pro-choice [34], freedom of speech, and sex-positivity [35].

          1.2.2.4.2.2. Pro:
          The major modern Satanist religion, The Satanic Temple, was founded as a joke/political satire [36], and uses the rhetoric of religious devil-worship not to actually honor any supernatural being — its members are explicitly secular — but to call attention to potential loopholes in religious freedom laws designed to benefit the Christian right.

        1.2.2.4.3. Pro:
        Satanists are "united in their shared values of sexual liberation and viewing sexuality as a form of authentic self-expression [37]".

        1.2.2.4.4. Pro:
        According to the co-founder of the Satanic Temple, they will fight for the equal rights of LGBTQ+ people [38].

        1.2.2.4.5. Pro:
        Many Satanists are members of the LGBTQ+ community.

          1.2.2.4.5.1. Pro:
          Nearly half of all Satanists identify as LGTBQ [39]+.

          1.2.2.4.5.2. Pro:
          Many LBTQ+ people felt disowned from traditional religious groups [40] and became Satanists seeking greater acceptance.

      1.2.2.5. Pro:
      Most religious people are similarly or more accepting of homosexuality than the general world population (which is 52% [41] according to a Pew survey of 32 countries).

        1.2.2.5.1. Pro:
        Around 54% [42] of Christians believe homosexuality should be accepted.

        1.2.2.5.2. Pro:
        Around 71% [43] of Hindus believe homosexuality should be accepted.

        1.2.2.5.3. Pro:
        81% [44] of Jews believe homosexuality should be accepted.

        1.2.2.5.4. Con:
        Homophobia is stronger in developing nations than in developed nations [45]. Therefore, average population-levels of homophobia are higher at the global level than at the national level in developed countries.

      1.2.2.6. Con:
      Religious groups oppress LGBTQ+ people.

        1.2.2.6.1. Pro:
        Religious groups often harass LGBTQ+ community.

          1.2.2.6.1.1. Pro:
          LGBTQ+ students face more harassment at Christian colleges [46].

          1.2.2.6.1.2. Con:
          -> See 1.1.2.3.

          1.2.2.6.1.3. Con:
          Harrassment is the result of individual choice and not due to the group the individual is a member of.

            1.2.2.6.1.3.1. Con:
            Individual choices are still impacted by the groups and belief systems that individuals subscribe to.

        1.2.2.6.2. Pro:
        Religion has been a major contributing factor to the oppression of LGBTQ+ people [47] worldwide.

          1.2.2.6.2.1. Con:
          -> See 1.2.2.1.

          1.2.2.6.2.2. Con:
          Neither the religious organizations nor their congregations get to decide who has sinned against God and how God feels about the LGBT community. This is the fundamental problem that the LGBT community has contended with and continues to contend with throughout history.

          1.2.2.6.2.3. Con:
          Religion doesn't promote any hate against the LGBTQ+ community, but their community does. The LGBTQ+ people are frowned upon by reverends that still have a 20th-century mindset (who are, by the way, a vast majority).

        1.2.2.6.3. Pro:
        Religious groups have used the LGBTQ+ community as a common enemy to galvanize their followers.

          1.2.2.6.3.1. Pro:
          Many anti-LGBTQ+ groups have religious links [48].

          1.2.2.6.3.2. Con:
          There are many LGBT-affirming religious groups [49].

          1.2.2.6.3.3. Con:
          Many religious people support the LGBT community or are also part of the LGBT community themselves.

          1.2.2.6.3.4. Pro:
          Religious people and LGBT individuals are not two separate groups. It is the dogmatic organizations and power structures that require an adversary that are the issues. The destruction of organized religious bodies would result in more tolerance generally.

        1.2.2.6.4. Con:
        Acceptance of the LGBTQ+ community by religious groups varies widely from location to location [50].

          1.2.2.6.4.1. Pro:
          There are religious communities that don't support the LGBTQ+ community, but there are also many mainline religious communities that do [51].

        1.2.2.6.5. Con:
        Oppression is the "unjust or cruel exercise of authority or power [52]." As such, "religious groups" in general are not in power, nor have any more authority than any random person with no religious affiliation.

        1.2.2.6.6. Con:
        The problem is LGBTQ+ community [53] tries to push their views onto children without respecting their parents' religious views [54].

          1.2.2.6.6.1. Con:
          To that same end, there is a belief that religion should be taught in schools [55] as a requirement. That ignores the desires of non-religious parents not wanting their kids to be forced to learn religious beliefs.

      1.2.2.7. Con:
      No matter how much one may like a person, so long as they think the other person is hellbound because of their sexual orientation, they cannot in any meaningful way peacefully co-exist.

    1.2.3. Con:
    Many religious groups are merely tolerant of the LGBTQ+ community, rather than accepting [56] of them.

    1.2.4. Con:
    Many religious groups outwardly denounce the LGBTQ+ community.

    1.2.5. Con:
    Some religions have so much internal conflict about LGBTQ+ issues that it seems unlikely they will be able to peacefully coexist more widely

  1.3. Pro:
  Religious teachings and scripture suggest that the LGBTQ+ community should be respected.

    1.3.1. Pro:
    The Bible says that God does not discriminate [57] between people on the basis of their social identities. Thus, Christians have an obligation to treat all people in the same way.

      1.3.1.1. Con:
      The Bible does not say that it is acceptable for people to sin just because it is a part of who they are [58].

        1.3.1.1.1. Con:
        When it says God does not discriminate, it means God doesn't discriminate with how he judges that person. However, according to Christianity, some go to heaven and others go to hell, which could be seen as showing bias.

        1.3.1.1.2. Con:
        Parts of the bible also have other prohibitions, such as not planting two different crops side by side (Leviticus 19:19 [59]), not eating anything that does not have scales and fins (Leviticus 11:9 [60]), and not wearing two different kinds of fabric (Deuteronomy 22:11 [61]).

        1.3.1.1.3. Con:
        Other sections of the bible include pre marital sex (Mark 10:17-19)and divorce (Leviticus 18:20). We should not selectively follow some verses, but ignore others..

        1.3.1.1.4. Pro:
        Leviticus 20:13 [62] says, "If a man has sexual relations with a man as one does with a woman, both of them have done what is detestable. They are to be put to death; their blood will be on their own heads."

          1.3.1.1.4.1. Con:
          If we read this in context of the rest of Leviticus 18 [63], which is talking about incest being disallowed, and consider the Hebrew translation which does not fit with male-male sex [64], it makes more sense that Leviticus 18 and 20 calls incest an abomination, not homosexual sex [65].

            1.3.1.1.4.1.1. Pro:
            The primary concern of chapter 18 is precisely male–female incest: with mother, stepmother, aunt, mother-in-law, sister-in-law, daughter-in-law, etc. A supplementary clause in Lev. 18:22 proscribing male–male incest would make perfectly good sense.

          1.3.1.1.4.2. Con:
          Levicitus 18 and 20 can also be interpreted as prohibiting homosexual intercourse involving a married man [66].

        1.3.1.1.5. Pro:
        Leviticus 18:22 [67] says, "Do not have sexual relations with a man as one does with a woman; that is detestable."

          1.3.1.1.5.1. Pro:
          If using the Priest for Equality translation this also includes lesbian relations [68]: “Do not lie with a person of the same-sex in the same way as you would lie with a person of the opposite sex; it is detestable.”

          1.3.1.1.5.2. Con:
          -> See 1.3.1.1.4.1.

          1.3.1.1.5.3. Con:
          -> See 1.3.1.1.4.2.

    1.3.4. Con:
    Religious scripture and teachings often provide judgements about the way society ought to be. Some of these teachings oppose those promoted by the LGBTQ+ community.

      1.3.4.1. Pro:
      The Abrahamic religions of Judaism, Christianity and Islam, have traditionally forbidden sodomy [69], believing and teaching that such behavior is sinful.

        1.3.4.1.1. Con:
        Many denominations within these religions are accepting of homosexuality and inclusive of homosexual people [70], such as the United Church of Christ and the Metropolitan Community Church.

        1.3.4.1.2. Con:
        Reform Judaism incorporates lesbian and gay rabbis and same-sex marriage [71] liturgies, while Reconstructionist Judaism and Conservative Judaism in the US allows for lesbian and gay rabbis and same-sex unions.

        1.3.4.1.3. Pro:
        All of the major Islamic schools disapprove of homosexuality [72].

        1.3.4.1.4. Con:
        -> See discussion #18187: The Bible does not support the conclusion that homosexuality is a sin against God.

      1.3.4.2. Con:
      Religious scripture should not be taken literally.

        1.3.4.2.1. Pro:
        Religions could be interpreted in a way that is in line with the LGBTQ+ community.

          1.3.4.2.1.1. Pro:
          Different demographics have produced different interpretations of the same religion.

        1.3.4.2.2. Con:
        Many religious people believe their holy book is the literal word of God and thus prefer a literal interpretation of the holy book.

          1.3.4.2.2.1. Pro:
          Most Christian fundamentalists emphasize biblical literalism. [73]

          1.3.4.2.2.2. Pro:
          Many Muslims believe the Quran is the literal word of Allah communicated to his messenger [74].

        1.3.4.2.3. Pro:
        It's not possible to take religious scripture literally due to the frequent use of imprecise words [75]. Making sense of these words demands some form of interpretation.

          1.3.4.2.3.1. Pro:
          Much of religious scripture is translated into different languages so that they are accessible to more people. Whenever reading a translated text, the interpretations of the individual tasked with conducting the trasnlation are imposed on the reader.

        1.3.4.2.4. Pro:
        In the Bible, there are many stories which are contradictory [76]. Literal interpretations do not allow for such contradictions.

      1.3.4.3. Con:
      Religious scripture encompasses an enormous body of literature.

      1.3.4.4. Pro:
      Religion is very important [77] in many people's lives. So, if a religion prohibits followers from associating with LGBTQ+ people, then they must abide.

        1.3.4.4.1. Pro:
        A global survey conducted by the Pew Research Center found that approximately 50% [78] of the respondents considered religion to be a necessary antecedent to morality.

        1.3.4.4.2. Con:
        Religions already accommodate closeted LGBTQ+ people. By targeting those more open about who they are, religions punish honesty and authenticity in people.

        1.3.4.4.3. Con:
        Sexuality and gender have nothing to do with religion. If LGBT+ people want to be a part of a religion, they should.

        1.3.4.4.4. Con:
        Religion has no authoritative value because anyone could create a religion, for example, by recording their observations, experiences, and reflections.

          1.3.4.4.4.1. Pro:
          Although religion provides historical and cosmological insights, it is dynamic and might change over time.

        1.3.4.4.5. Con:
        Religion is very important to many LGBTQ+ people [79]. If they can reconcile the underlying ideological disparities, so can their cis and straight counterparts.

          1.3.4.4.5.1. Con:
          "Cis straight counterparts" cannot be expected to reconcile something that is irreconcilable. It is possible for religious LGBTQ+ persons to be functioning outside of what's permitted by their religion.

            1.3.4.4.5.1.1. Con:
            Individuals like Matthew Vine [80] are openly homosexual and have accepted that God loves all.

            1.3.4.4.5.1.2. Con:
            The authority to determine whether personal identity is reconcilable with religious beliefs does not unequivocally lie with cis straight people.

              1.3.4.4.5.1.2.1. Con:
              The authority lies in the texts of the religion and whatever human authorities it appoints (such as the pope). What's permitted by the religion is a matter of objective fact - which LGBTQ+ people wrongfully believe is reconcilable with the LGBTQ+ worldview.

                1.3.4.4.5.1.2.1.1. Con:
                Much of what was originally permitted or required by religion (e.g., stoning offenders to death) is considered human rights violations in modern society. Religion cannot, therefore, be considered to be the basis for objective facts.

        1.3.4.4.6. Con:
        Religion can be interpreted in different ways and lead to different conclusions.

        1.3.4.4.7. Con:
        Just because religions practice something does not mean their religion demands that. Racial segregation [81], for example, was practiced in the Church even though the Bible never required such a practice.

          1.3.4.4.7.1. Con:
          While racial enmity/discrimination is discouraged in the Bible (Gal. 3:28), homosexuality is absolutely not allowed within Christianity (Lev. 18:22, 20:13, Rom. 1:26-27, 1 Cor. 6:9-11, 1 Tim. 1:8-11, Mt. 19:4-6)

    1.3.5. Con:
    In the case of religions where there is no central governing body, there is no definitive guidance [82] surrounding the religious position on issues of interest to the LGBTQ+ community.

      1.3.5.1. Con:
      This does not preclude that a majority of those who follow a certain faith come to a shared stance vis-a-vis the LGBTQ+ community.

      1.3.5.2. Pro:
      It all depends on how much the religious organization/practitioners manage to influence the government and so impose their beliefs on non-religious people.

    1.3.6. Pro:
    Most religions have a central teaching of loving and accepting everyone, meaning there shouldn’t be any conflict; if there is, then it is perhaps outside of the religion.

  1.4. Pro:
  Religious people and LGBTQ+ people have already demonstrated their ability to peacefully coexist and cooperate with one another.

    1.4.1. Pro:
    The American "Melting Pot" of different communities coming together was one of the main drivers for the US's economic success [83], showing how diverse groups coming together can be beneficial overall.

      1.4.1.1. Con:
      Even if the coming together of diverse groups would be beneficial, this does not mean that the groups can be persuaded to do so.

    1.4.2. Pro:
    Religious people and LGBTQ+ people have encountered each other in place that require cooperation such as in the workplace.

      1.4.2.1. Con:
      Cooperation and peaceful coexistence in restricted and particular contexts does not mean that will be extended to wider contexts.

    1.4.3. Con:
    There is little constructive dialogue between the religious community and the LGBTQ+ community.

      1.4.3.1. Pro:
      LGBTQ people have created the notion that they are a protected class, so even legitimate concerns about their behaviors is regarded as homophobic or transphobic [84]. This makes discourse between the LGBTQ community and any other community impossible.

      1.4.3.2. Con:
      Through social media and other forms of digital communication, those with oppositional views have more means than ever to commmunicate with one another.

        1.4.3.2.1. Con:
        Although social media may give more opportunity for communication between those with opposing views, it does not guarnatee any form of meaningful dialogue.

          1.4.3.2.1.1. Pro:
          Exposure to opposing views on social media can increase political polarization [85].

        1.4.3.2.2. Pro:
        Social media provides a means to communicate with a greater number of people from different geographical locations.

        1.4.3.2.3. Con:
        Social media algorithms tend to creae echo chambers [86] that limit exposure to opposing viewpoints.

        1.4.3.2.4. Con:
        Social media platforms have the power to censor viewpoints. [87] Therefore it can reduce exposure to diverging viewpoints.

      1.4.3.3. Pro:
      People often avoid discussing controversial topics for fear of harming their social relationships [88].

    1.4.4. Con:
    There have been instances of violence and conflict [89] between religious communities and the LGBTQ+ community.

    1.4.5. Pro:
    [Common values] Both most religions and the LGBTQ+ community value peace [90] and will always try to understand each other’s viewpoints.
