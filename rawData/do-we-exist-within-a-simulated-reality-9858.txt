      1.5.9.1. Con:
      All sequences of expressible states are by definition computable, which is what a simulated reality would consist of. Undecidable problems are not relevant to the possibility of simulated realities.

  1.6. Pro:
  If it is possible and it would eventually happen at least once, then the probability we live inside one is near 100%.

    1.6.1. Pro:
    The probability we will eventually live in a simulation is extremely high.

    1.6.2. Con:
    The proportion of physical realities capable of supporting ancestor realities might be very small.

      1.6.2.1. Con:
      Actually, that just means there's infinitely more base realities from which simulated realities can proliferate. It does not change the ratio.

        1.6.2.1.1. Con:
        No, as stated, simulation might not be possible in most physical realities. To elaborate: only a very tiny fraction may allow for structures so complex that they can simulate. Simulated universes would then be in the minority.

          1.6.2.1.1.1. Con:
          We are essentially moving the goal posts. We are saying there might be a huge number of realities in which either:
          A. The Laws of Nature are so different that the probability of Civilizations capable of producing Simulations is approximately zero. e.g. Higher incidence of Life Exterminating events
          B. The Laws of Nature are so different from our own reality that it is impossible to simulate.
          
          In either event, the conclusion remains unchallenged.
          
          As a percentage of "habitable" realities.

    1.6.3. Con:
    Certain authorities believe it unlikely/impossible.

      1.6.3.1. Pro:
      Nick Bostrom, the originator of the simulation hypothesis, thinks that there's < 50% likelihood that we live in a simulation: Nick Bostrum on simulation likelihood [54]

        1.6.3.1.1. Pro:
        -> See 1.2.8.1.

      1.6.3.2. Con:
      Rich Terrile, a scientist at Nasa’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory claimed, “Quite frankly, if we are not living in a simulation, it is an extraordinarily unlikely circumstance,”

      1.6.3.3. Con:
      Astrophysicist Neil deGrasse Tyson, said that he thinks the likelihood of the universe being a simulation "may be very high."

      1.6.3.4. Con:
      Elon Musk Famously stated the odds of this reality being R0 as "billion to one"

      1.6.3.5. Con:
      An Argument from Authority is a logical fallacy.

    1.6.4. Con:
    It's possible that not enough time has passed for the number of simulated intelligent entities to vastly outnumber intelligent entities in the base reality.

      1.6.4.1. Con:
      It took 3 billion years for us to emerge from chemistry, 600 million from microbes, 200 000 from primitive hominids, 10 000 from hunter gatherers, 250 from agriculture, 70 from heavy industry. The information age has only been going on for 50 - 70 years. Once it starts, the emergence of intelligent machines will progress essentially instantaneously compared to earlier developments

      1.6.4.2. Pro:
      A simulation necessarily runs slower than the base system by a large factor, and this slowdown is compounded in each nested simulation. A simulation can be thought of as a Turing Machine [55] computing a simulation of a Turing Machine. But it takes N base machine instructions for each simulated instruction. Hence, the inevitable slowdown.

        1.6.4.2.1. Con:
        -> See 1.5.3.2.2.

        1.6.4.2.2. Con:
        The time scale in the simulation need not to be correlated with the time scale of the parent world. We already have simulation games that run many millenaries in a few hours of playing. For example Sid Meier's Civilization game.

          1.6.4.2.2.1. Pro:
          Inside those simulated millenaries there could be other nested simulations that run even faster.

          1.6.4.2.2.2. Con:
          An acceleration of the time scale is accompanied by a simplification of the simulation complexity. So the simulations with low complexity may not be able to create sub levels of simulated universes.

            1.6.4.2.2.2.1. Con:
            Whilst a simplified reality should require fewer resources, it does not follow that accelerating the timescale of a simulation necessitates simplification of the simulation.

          1.6.4.2.2.3. Con:
          -> See 1.5.3.2.2.1.2.

          1.6.4.2.2.4. Pro:
          Once reality is abstracted to information, the time step between events becomes a function of the resources made available to process that information.

            1.6.4.2.2.4.1. Pro:
            Events only need to appear to be synchronized on a linear timescale within the game.

      1.6.4.3. Con:
      -> See 1.6.4.2.2.

    1.6.5. Con:
    One cannot speak of probabilities if one is unaware of the probability space.

      1.6.5.1. Con:
      We at least have ourselves as a data point. You can infer a surprising amount [56] from one data point.

      1.6.5.2. Con:
      We are unaware of how probability works in the parent reality; we are only assuming it is similar to what we know.

        1.6.5.2.1. Pro:
        -> See 1.1.4.3.1.

    1.6.6. Pro:
    There are infinite ways to simulate a reality, and only one way for a reality to be unsimulated. Probability therefore gives as close to a guarantee as can exist that we are in simulation.

      1.6.6.1. Pro:
      The simulation could have different logic from the real world. Because there is no limit to potential options when we cannot determine any to be impossible, there is truly an infinite number of ways to be in simulation.

      1.6.6.2. Con:
      -> See 1.1.4.3.1.

      1.6.6.3. Con:
      There is an infinite number of possible worlds for an unsimulated world just as there is an infinite number of possible worlds.

    1.6.7. Pro:
    The Rate of Improvement of our simulated realities in context to the age of civilizations and the universe heavily implies a simulation can and would be created indistinguishable from reality. This follows that we most likely already exist within one.

      1.6.7.1. Pro:
      A simulated reality does not have to be indistinguishable from actual reality to those 'playing the game' when they are 'outside the game'. For instance 'sleep' (potentially the exit-points between turns in the SR game') and the 'black, signal-free night sky' (Olbers' Paradox [57] and the failure of SETI to find any sign of intelligent life [58]) might appear very silly to an SR player in their equivalent of real life but be essential for SR game-play.

        1.6.7.1.1. Pro:
        In the film Inception [59], a character carries a little top with him. If the top keeps spinning endlessly, that means he is in a dream [60]. If it stops and falls over, that means he is back in reality. An SR game might contain "obvious" absurdities to remind players of the boundaries between fiction and reality

    1.6.8. Pro:
    Any increase in the number of simulated realities would decrease the likelihood of any single entity existing in the original or "base" reality.

      1.6.8.1. Pro:
      That includes all ancestor simulations, i.e. simulated realities within simulated realities, within simulated realities, etc. This increases the likelihood of any reality to be a simulated one.

    1.6.9. Con:
    This could be the 'base reality'. We could be living in a reality in which posthumans have not yet developed. Our experiences could be real and one day our decendents may consider it fun to pretend to be us.
