Discussion Name:
Capitalism v Socialism

1. Thesis:
Capitalism is better than Socialism.

  1.1. Con:
  Socialism is “Morally superior”

    1.1.1. Con:
    Socialism has a morally highly questionable track record.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      Stalinism, which produced millions of deaths, famine, and war throughout the 1940s and 1950s, is built on a Socialist foundation [1].

    1.1.2. Pro:
    Socialism makes society more equal and is therefore fairer than capitalism.

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      Capitalism encourages equality of opportunity, which is fairer than a system that encourages equality of outcome.

        1.1.2.1.1. Con:
        Not everyone has the same opportunities - those who start with more money inherently have more opportunities.

    1.1.3. Con:
    Morality cannot be legislated. A lack of morality can be punished. An excess of morality might be rewarded, but no laws can, do, or will exist that can mandate a bad person be good.

  1.2. Con:
  Capitalism is an easily-gamed system by the ultra-wealthy.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Socialism, by its very nature, should not have an "ultra-wealthy" class of people.

    1.2.2. Pro:
    Under Capitalism, if one has money, one has the power to manipulate the system to benefit oneself to make more money, etc. In a self-perpetuating cycle.

      1.2.2.1. Pro:
      Lobbying in the United States describes paid activity in which special interest groups [2] hire well-connected professional advocates, often lawyers, to argue for specific legislation in decision-making bodies such as the United States Congress [3]. These laws can be (and, often, are) used to make it easier for the special interest groups (frequently large, rich corporations) to make more money.

    1.2.3. Con:
    Some extreme forms of Socialist governments, like some Authoritarian Communist regimes, are also easily-gamed.

  1.3. Con:
  Socialism promotes equality

    1.3.1. Con:
    Equality on its own is not necessarily beneficial if it undermines other goals, for example general prosperity and welfare.

    1.3.2. Con:
    Socialism promotes equality of outcome, not equality of effort

      1.3.2.1. Con:
      Equality of opportunity is better and preferable equality of outcome since it incentivizes hard work.

      1.3.2.2. Pro:
      Socialism wants the fast food worker to be valued equally as the research scientist. If this were true, then they could swap roles and produce the same results as the other.

    1.3.3. Pro:
    All socialist schools of thought share the belief in human equality [4].

    1.3.4. Pro:
    Socialists hold that economic organisation should be based on common ownership [5] of assets.

    1.3.5. Pro:
    Socialism promotes a more equal distribution of wealth and incom [6]e within society.

      1.3.5.1. Pro:
      Socialism is based onpublic ownership of the means of production [7] and decisions are made by the government to cover the needs of individuals.

      1.3.5.2. Con:
      Socialism promotes a more equal distribution in theory [8], but in the practice, it hasn't successfully reached its goal.

  1.4. Pro:
  Socialism has its roots in the idea that people should receive based on their needs. This is unethical.

    1.4.1. Con:
    In a family, members do not recieve food based upon their contribution to the wealth of the family, but rather they receive it based upon their needs. If this is done in our families, it cannot be unethical to do so in our governments as well.

  1.5. Con:
  Socialism promotes a more environmentally stable future

    1.5.1. Con:
    Capitalism does not have to be bad [9] for the environment.

      1.5.1.1. Con:
      Capitalism priorities the pursuit of profit. Thus, the environment is only ever a concern so far as it aligns with this primary aim.

      1.5.1.2. Con:
      Yes, it does. Capitalism requires constant economic growth to remain stable. Without economic growth, there can be no return on investments. But there cannot be continued economic growth without continued environmental destruction.

    1.5.2. Pro:
    Socialism attempts to deliver on this environmentally stable future by having the state run all businesses

    1.5.3. Pro:
    Pursuit of accumulation of profit in capitalism generates waste [10].

      1.5.3.1. Pro:
      Capitalism has been criticized for a practice of planned obsolescence [11].

  1.6. Pro:
  Capitalism is not beholden to any one social group. This makes it the most fair system.

    1.6.1. Con:
    Socialism is also not beholden to any one social group. So it is equally fair.

      1.6.1.1. Con:
      Socialism always favor the ruling class and castigates the "worker" as being nothing more than an "important cog in the machine of liberation from the shackles of oppression." Yet ruling class remains the ruling class and never was one worker freed.

    1.6.2. Con:
    Capitalism is beholden to a social group. They are called the rich.

    1.6.3. Con:
    In capitalism, inequality [12] increases because firms retain profits at the expense of workers.

    1.6.4. Con:
    Capitalism is not the fairest system because it exploits the workers [13].

  1.7. Pro:
  Winners Under Capitalism Deliver Value

    1.7.1. Con:
    Not every human is capable of delivering value. Those people should not suffer as a result.

    1.7.2. Con:
    This is not the case. In fact, the fundamental mechanism of capitalism is the institution of capital gains, which can accrue on real estate, on the stock market, on business ownership, etc. Capital gains are entirely passive income. You don't have to deliver any value whatsoever to receive them. Many landlords are a good example of this: They don't actually build the houses; they just control them. As a result of that, they get to profit.

      1.7.2.1. Con:
      Capital gains in real estate (capital appreciation) don't pay the bills until the property is sold, similar to how you don't make profit owning a stock (without dividends) until you sell the stock. Landlords ARE delivering value by providing an affordable place for people to live, handling repairs, and keeping the home up to date.

        1.7.2.1.1. Con:
        Landlords don't generally provide any of that. Instead, they pay other people (construction workers, technicians, etc.) to provide it. Even if they do some of the work themselves, the value of that work is far less than what the tenant pays for the right to live in the property. We know this is the case, because home owners never choose to pay someone to manage the same maintenance work as a landlord, or if they do, they pay them far less than what tenants pay in rent.

  1.8. Pro:
  The market place of ideas in a capitalist society means that good ideas are of value.

    1.8.1. Pro:
    Capitalism causes a highly competitive market [14] in which only the top ideas are of value.

  1.9. Pro:
  The market place of ideas in socialist society are strictly regimented. Certain ideas get you ostracized.

  1.10. Con:
  Socialists are only socialists to the degree they practice socialistic ideologies, which limits the scope of attention any socialist can give to novel and unique ideas.

  1.11. Pro:
  Capitalism promotes choice and freedom

    1.11.1. Con:
    Too much choice can result in making people more unhappy [15] than if they only had one or two options.

    1.11.2. Con:
    Capitalism only promotes choice and freedom in a consumerist sense. You can choose between 10 types of flour, but you are still not free from your forced need to work to survive. True freedom would be to abolish unnecessary Labour for workers so as to increase their spare time, and thus their freedom to pursue more important goals in life than making money to buy bare necessities.

      1.11.2.1. Con:
      You don't need to work to survive under Capitalism. You can acquire cash-flowing real estate to cover all of your expenses. This idea is known as financial freedom. Another example could be living off of stock dividends.

        1.11.2.1.1. Con:
        Not everyone has enough money to afford real estate (or other investment opportunities) - those people still need to work to survive.

    1.11.3. Con:
    Socialists [16] hold that freedom occurs when individuals are unhindered by constraints of material scarcity and coercive institutions.

      1.11.3.1. Pro:
      Marx [17] held that reduction of labour time would grant individuals the opportunity to pursue the development of their true individuality and creativity.

      1.11.3.2. Con:
      Capitalism is responsible for more resources and less coercive institutions than socialism

        1.11.3.2.1. Pro:
        "Simon finally challenged Ehrlich to a wager on resource depletion [18]" that would show that capitalism in realm of exhaustible resources would allow the price to become cheaper and more accessible. Which is what was observed. "Cox and Alm proposed a new methodology to evaluate Simon’s thesis" applying the same thing to the resource of labor and found better labor wages (p.3) [19] and lower prices for goods (p.11)

        1.11.3.2.2. Pro:
        Socialism intuitively puts demands on the public market in addition to providing goods and services, institutions must also adhere to and require adherence to socialist demands and edicts.

    1.11.4. Pro:
    Capitalism, in an expression of a person's freedom, promotes the ownership of private property [20]; this allows people to self determine their own destiny. Socialism, on the other hand, seeks to remove private ownership.

      1.11.4.1. Con:
      Only the most extreme forms of Socialism favor complete removal of private ownership.

    1.11.5. Con:
    Under capitalism [21], critical economic decisions that impact the lives of people are made by a handful of elites.

    1.11.6. Con:
    Marxists argue that in conditions of economic inequality [22], the purchase of labour cannot occur under free conditions.

    1.11.7. Pro:
    Capitalism incentivizes innovation [23] and diversity through competition. Therefore customers have the freedom to choose what they want to consume.

  1.12. Pro:
  Capitalism is Consistent With the Human Condition

    1.12.1. Con:
    The human condition, by itself, is not necessarily positive or worth preserving.

      1.12.1.1. Pro:
      The human condition has, throughout its history, included poverty, suffering, and death.

    1.12.2. Con:
    Capitalism is only a few hundred years old. If it were consistent with the human condition, then it would have existed for as long as humanity had, and we would probably also see it in other related species.
