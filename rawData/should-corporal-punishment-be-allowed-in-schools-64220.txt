Discussion Name:
Should corporal punishment be allowed in schools?

1. Thesis:
Corporal punishment should be allowed in schools.

  1.1. Con:
  Corporal punishment can negatively affect the well-being and educational outcomes [1] of students.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    The possibility of facing corporal punishment can instill fear in a child, which may subsequently lead to adverse educational outcomes.

      1.1.1.1. Pro:
      Being singled out for punishment can lead to peer stigmatization, harming a student's self-esteem and school performance [2].

        1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
        Students might develop an aversion to the school environment, leading to increased absenteeism and even dropping out [3] altogether.

          1.1.1.1.1.1. Con:
          A stricter approach to schooling might boost attendance [4], a key factor in educational achievement.

          1.1.1.1.1.2. Pro:
          A stricter approach to attendance typically hinders [5] student success.

        1.1.1.1.2. Con:
        Only disruptive students are typically subjected to corporal punishment; thus, reducing their social capital through stigma and shame might benefit the overall class learning environment [6].

        1.1.1.1.3. Pro:
        Corporal punishment is often delivered publicly, which can be undignifying.

      1.1.1.2. Con:
      Conversely, the threat of punishment might motivate a child to work harder and achieve higher scores to avoid such discipline.

        1.1.1.2.1. Con:
        Physical pain and the stress that comes with the fear of corporal punishment can interfere with a student's ability to concentrate [7], process information, and retain knowledge.

        1.1.1.2.2. Pro:
        The potential for punishment can lead to heightened awareness and focus on tasks that need to be completed, as the child may prioritize these tasks to avoid disciplinary action.

        1.1.1.2.3. Pro:
        When the consequences of not meeting certain standards are clear and consistently enforced, it can provide a structured environment where children understand what is expected of them, potentially leading to better performance.

        1.1.1.2.4. Con:
        Corporal punishment can lead students to associate teachers with potential harm, hindering the formation of productive student-teacher relationships [8] and deterring children from asking questions essential for learning difficult concepts.

    1.1.2. Con:
    There are various factors more critical than corporal punishment that influence educational outcomes.

      1.1.2.1. Pro:
      The quality of teachers and the teaching strategies [9] they employ are the most important factors in helping students achieve positive outcomes.

      1.1.2.2. Pro:
      Students typically perform better in schools with superior facilities [10].

      1.1.2.3. Con:
      For children who suffer from anxiety or lack confidence, the presence of corporal punishment might be the predominant factor hindering their academic performance.

    1.1.3. Con:
    Corporal punishment can push students to take academic matters more seriously when there are physical costs to failing to deliver academic expectations.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      For students with no intrinsic motivation to take school seriously, corporal punishment can force them to put in more of an effort.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      If students do not have a personal understanding of the relevance of their education, they are likely to merely show enough academic success, through memorising information, for example, so as to avoid punishment rather than actually learning.

    1.1.4. Pro:
    Corporal punishment can result in long-term physical injury or disability.

      1.1.4.1. Pro:
      There have been numerous cases [11] of excessive use of violence in the application of corporal punishment.

        1.1.4.1.1. Pro:
        The widespread awareness of these cases makes schools culpable for future occurrences if they occur.

          1.1.4.1.1.1. Con:
          In the absence of legislation prohibiting corporal punishment or imposing consequences, it's unlikely that a school would bear significant culpability.

        1.1.4.1.2. Con:
        These examples can be instructive on what not to do in order to avoid unexpected consequences.

          1.1.4.1.2.1. Con:
          Treating instances of extreme violence against children as mere learning opportunities doesn't negate the irreversible harm inflicted on the child.

            1.1.4.1.2.1.1. Pro:
            Accidents during punishments can result in fractures, sprains, and sores [12] that could have a lasting impact on the student's health and lifestyle.

          1.1.4.1.2.2. Pro:
          Potential consequences, such as suspension, for offending staff may deter other staff members from exhibiting similar behavior.

      1.1.4.2. Con:
      The most common method of administering corporal punishment, namely paddling [13], is unlikely to cause significant injury.

        1.1.4.2.1. Pro:
        The practice of paddling in over 15 US states [14] suggests that it is generally not deemed overly harmful.

        1.1.4.2.2. Con:
        Paddling reportedly leaves marks and can cause bruising [15].

        1.1.4.2.3. Con:
        Scars [16] from flogging and beating, even if they are minor, can still have a long-term psychological impact on individuals.

    1.1.5. Pro:
    Corporal punishment can have negative emotional and psychological impacts.

      1.1.5.1. Con:
      Corporal punishment uses negative reinforcement [17], which can have positive impacts on the psychology of students showing deviant behaviours.

        1.1.5.1.1. Pro:
        Negative reinforcement is straightforward and often easy for children to understand; if they engage in undesirable behavior, there will be consequences.

        1.1.5.1.2. Con:
        Negative reinforcement might stop undesirable behavior in the short term, but it does not necessarily teach children the underlying reasons why certain behaviors are wrong.

      1.1.5.2. Pro:
      Corporal punishment can normalise the use of violence for children.

        1.1.5.2.1. Pro:
        As children are in their formative stages [18], their perceptions are influenced by these interactions and may grow to have the wrong views about the use of violence.

        1.1.5.2.2. Pro:
        Experiencing corporal punishment might desensitise children to violence. [19]

          1.1.5.2.2.1. Pro:
          The acceptability of violence particularly harms girls, as it raises the likelihood of them tolerating domestic violence [20] in the future.

          1.1.5.2.2.2. Con:
          There are worse depictions of violence [21] in video games and pop culture; thus, observing corporal punishment isn't meaningfully different.

        1.1.5.2.3. Con:
        Corporal punishment does not propose violence as a solution to all challenges but merely as a means of enforcing discipline within the school context.

          1.1.5.2.3.1. Con:
          When children observe adults using violence to enforce discipline, it is reasonable to suggest they may see it as a viable tool for resolving conflicts (pp. 1-2 [22]).

          1.1.5.2.3.2. Pro:
          Similarly, children being taught about wars does not teach them that killing is accepted in all contexts.

    1.1.6. Con:
    Problems associated with corporal punishment are often associated with how it is applied rather than the practice itself.

      1.1.6.1. Con:
      The problem with corporal punishment also lies with the concept itself, and how it gives teachers the responsibility to inflict pain on students who are not their wards.

        1.1.6.1.1. Con:
        Teachers are generally considered responsible for classroom discipline and, therefore, hold a position of power [23], a perception that remains unchanged with or without corporal punishment.

        1.1.6.1.2. Pro:
        Revering teachers and granting them excessive power [24] through corporal punishment might shift their focus away from children's learning outcomes to pursuing unchallenged personal teaching methods.

      1.1.6.2. Pro:
      In schools, there is often oversight and regulation [25] to ensure that punishments are not excessive.

        1.1.6.2.1. Pro:
        Social services and police are always available to intervene when corporal punishment veers into physical abuse.

        1.1.6.2.2. Pro:
        Some schools, such as public schools in Louisiana [26], have specific rules on the extent and method of corporal punishment allowed in schools.

          1.1.6.2.2.1. Pro:
          For example, flogging can offer no long-term damage if done in moderation.

          1.1.6.2.2.2. Con:
          Conversely, some states only instruct teachers to use a reasonable amount of force [27], which is highly subjective.

        1.1.6.2.3. Con:
        The line between appropriate punishment and abuse can be blurry or subjective, and therefore regulations are likely to be inadequate.

  1.2. Con:
  Corporal punishment is a violation of the rights and dignity of students.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    There is a global consensus against the legality of corporal punishment.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      Article 19 of the UNCRC [28] ensures every child's right to protection and mandates member states to implement measures safeguarding children from all types of violence.

        1.2.1.1.1. Pro:
        Since its adoption by the UN in 1989, 196 countries [29] have ratified the UNCRC.

        1.2.1.1.2. Con:
        The UNCRC is a broad treaty designed to safeguard children's rights. While nations have prioritized universally agreed-upon issues [30] like banning child labor and mandating child care, corporal punishment hasn't received the same attention.

        1.2.1.1.3. Pro:
        Over 60 countries [31] have banned corporal punishment without exception.

      1.2.1.2. Con:
      There are various jurisdictions worldwide that don't criminalize corporal punishment, indicating a lack of global consensus on the issue.

        1.2.1.2.1. Con:
        135 countries [32] worldwide have passed laws prohibiting corporal punishment in schools.

        1.2.1.2.2. Pro:
        The UK has resisted global appeals [33] to criminalize smacking children.

        1.2.1.2.3. Con:
        Outlier states cannot be viewed as representing a deviation from international consensus, which is often derived from global entities like the UN [34].

        1.2.1.2.4. Pro:
        The United States Supreme Court, which is a global legal pacesetter, ruled that [35] corporal punishment is an acceptable form of punishment.

      1.2.1.3. Pro:
      The UN's Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) [36] specifically highlight the elimination of violence against children, including corporal punishment, as a measure of progress for member countries.

        1.2.1.3.1. Pro:
        The UN's SDGs have led to the establishment of global organizations dedicated to ending violence against children worldwide (p. 2 [37]).

        1.2.1.3.2. Con:
        The UN's SDGs are not legally binding [38] for any country.

    1.2.2. Pro:
    Corporal punishment constitutes assault and an infringement on the bodily autonomy of children.

      1.2.2.1. Con:
      A child's bodily autonomy is already considered to be limited.

        1.2.2.1.1. Pro:
        Parents make decisions about a child's future and what goes into their bodies [39], such as vaccines, not the children themselves.

        1.2.2.1.2. Con:
        Teachers, unlike parents, who have legal authority and state-mandated responsibility over children, usually administer corporal punishment without exercising such authority.

      1.2.2.2. Pro:
      Children are typically unable to give informed consent to corporal punishment, making any physical discipline an imposition on their autonomy.

        1.2.2.2.1. Pro:
        Children typically have no avenue to appeal against the administration of corporal punishment, leaving them helpless even if they gain knowledge and understanding about it.

        1.2.2.2.2. Con:
        Schools can establish processes that involve children and parents, allowing them to give consent.

    1.2.3. Con:
    Students and their guardians cede these rights when they agree to join a school and abide by its rules.

      1.2.3.1. Pro:
      School admissions forms often include disciplinary agreements that are likely to include punishments.

        1.2.3.1.1. Con:
        Even if schools list the types of punishments they use, it's unlikely these agreements can specify the severity of corporal punishment, which is determined on the spot by the administering teacher.

      1.2.3.2. Con:
      Students have no real choice when signing such agreements, as the alternative is not to attend school.

        1.2.3.2.1. Pro:
        Attending school is often compulsory [40], and therefore the choice not to attend school is, in many cases, not a real option.

    1.2.4. Con:
    Corporal punishment in school merely mirrors the operation of the criminal justice system. [41]

      1.2.4.1. Con:
      Criminal justice systems in many jurisdictions worldwide have been ineffective in reforming people's behavior.

        1.2.4.1.1. Pro:
        Punishing children as we do adults in the criminal justice system can create mental health issues in them, just as it does in many incarcerated adults, leading to higher chances of re-offending [42].

        1.2.4.1.2. Con:
        The system serves to protect society [43] by incarcerating dangerous criminals; similarly, corporal punishment can shield children from bullies and similar offenders.

      1.2.4.2. Con:
      It is immoral to subject children to standards used to regulate or punish adult behaviors.

        1.2.4.2.1. Pro:
        Children are undergoing cognitive, emotional, and social development [44], so applying adult standards overlooks their distinct understanding and emotions.

        1.2.4.2.2. Pro:
        Children may not fully understand [45] right from wrong, so punishing them by adult standards can be unjust due to their limited moral comprehension.

        1.2.4.2.3. Con:
        Holding children to certain adult standards might prepare them for the real world, teaching them about consequences and responsibility from a young age.

        1.2.4.2.4. Con:
        In many jurisdictions, children as young as 16 [46] are allowed to vote; hence, holding them accountable to adult standards isn't radically different.

      1.2.4.3. Pro:
      The existence of correctional facilities for minors indicates that society recognizes some forms of accountability, such as corporal punishment, as valid.

        1.2.4.3.1. Pro:
        Children, like adults, can harm others, making it necessary to have forms of accountability that include aspects of retribution [47].

        1.2.4.3.2. Con:
        Correctional facilities for minors often cause irreparable harm [48] and should not be considered ideal models.

  1.3. Con:
  Corporal punishment teaches the principle that the physically-strong are permitted to attack the physically-weak; explaining how bullying is perpetuated in the wider society.

  1.4. Con:
  Corporal punishment means that students so punished become a disruptive burden on the students whom do not require punishment because of the time wasted on punishments.

  1.5. Con:
  Corporal punishment is a distraction for the better teachers whom would much rather focus on actual education than on wasting time punishing those whom do not wish to be in school. Better to expel them than resort to physical violence.

  1.6. Con:
  Corporal punishment does not promote GOOD behaviour it merely promotes OBEDIENT behaviour. Obedient people are less educable because they learn not to think for themselves nor to express themselves freely.

  1.7. Pro:
  Corporal punishment promotes good behaviour in students.

    1.7.1. Con:
    There are better alternatives for ensuring discipline and compliance in schools.

      1.7.1.1. Pro:
      Detention has largely replaced corporal punishment [49] in many schools and has shown more success.

        1.7.1.1.1. Con:
        Detention requires extra labour and costs, and is therefore not accessible to all schools.

        1.7.1.1.2. Pro:
        Detention is less disruptive than corporal punishment or suspension, keeping students in school and preserving valuable learning time.

      1.7.1.2. Pro:
      Other disciplinary methods, such as counseling [50] and positive reinforcement [51], are known to be effective.

        1.7.1.2.1. Pro:
        Counseling is more effective because it engages and resolves the root causes of student misbehaviour.

        1.7.1.2.2. Con:
        Positive reinforcement can be more costly [52], as it involves providing rewards.

      1.7.1.3. Con:
      Corporal punishment can be beneficial if it is reserved as an option for students who are unresponsive to other methods.

        1.7.1.3.1. Pro:
        Some students can be disruptive or threaten more vulnerable peers, necessitating discipline.

          1.7.1.3.1.1. Con:
          Since corporal punishment might make students more violent in the long term [53], this violence is merely transferred to other people beyond the school system.

          1.7.1.3.1.2. Pro:
          By deterring rebellion, corporal punishment can prevent the emotional harm that can come to vulnerable students from rebellious actions such as bullying.

        1.7.1.3.2. Pro:
        Corporal punishment offers a strong and arguably different deterrent that ensures that students obey school rules.

          1.7.1.3.2.1. Pro:
          Humans, by instinct [54], avoid pain. Therefore, the infliction of pain through corporal punishment offers a universally effective deterrent.

        1.7.1.3.3. Con:
        Students who are generally unresponsive to other forms of punishment might be so as a result of psychological disorders [55]. Beating them for a condition that is no fault of theirs is wrong.

          1.7.1.3.3.1. Pro:
          Using physical discipline on such students could provide a short-term, superficial solution to their challenges, which would only allow them to gain roots and become more of a challenge [56] when they emerge out of school and no longer face the threat of corporal punishment.

          1.7.1.3.3.2. Con:
          Some children who are unresponsive to other forms of punishment may be so due to their background and personality type, rather than any disorder.

    1.7.2. Pro:
    Corporal punishment benefits teachers, as it eases their burden by helping them maintain order in schools.

      1.7.2.1. Pro:
      Teachers have limited time and cannot allocate a significant portion to disciplining their class using alternative methods.

        1.7.2.1.1. Pro:
        The use of corporal punishment for classroom discipline allows teachers to dedicate more time to teaching, as the simple threat of physical punishment can compel students to comport themselves without active teacher intervention.

        1.7.2.1.2. Con:
        Administering corporal punishment might be more disruptive or time-consuming than simply assigning detention to a child, as punishments such as flogging could result in classroom chaos, crying, and the need to rush students to an infirmary in case of any injuries.

      1.7.2.2. Con:
      The practice of delivering physical punishment can negatively influence the behaviour of teachers who execute it.

        1.7.2.2.1. Pro:
        Teachers might become overly reliant on corporal punishment, neglecting the development of more positive teaching and disciplinary strategies.

        1.7.2.2.2. Pro:
        Teachers might replicate acts of physical punishment at home due to its normalization in schools.

        1.7.2.2.3. Con:
        Teachers, as trained professionals [57], are expected to adhere to ethical guidelines and professional standards, including managing their behavior and emotions in disciplinary situations.

        1.7.2.2.4. Con:
        Teachers inclined to use physical punishment may already have behavioral traits that align with such practices, suggesting corporal punishment might not change their behavior but rather resonate with existing tendencies.

      1.7.2.3. Con:
      Practising corporal punishment can expose teachers and other school authorities to significant legal risks.

        1.7.2.3.1. Pro:
        Accidents during corporal punishments can make teachers liable for lawsuits [58] from parents.

        1.7.2.3.2. Pro:
        Schools can be sued for assault [59] if they use corporal punishment without parental permission.

          1.7.2.3.2.1. Pro:
          Multiple suits from displeased parents could result in significant financial losses for the school.

        1.7.2.3.3. Con:
        Schools that allow corporal punishment often have means of limiting their legal risks, one of which is having a witness present. [60]

      1.7.2.4. Pro:
      The deterrence provided by corporal punishment results in student compliance and discipline, which provides a conducive environment for teachers to operate.

        1.7.2.4.1. Pro:
        The fear of being punished would prevent students from disrupting classes and disobeying rules, which would make the teachers' work more difficult.

        1.7.2.4.2. Con:
        The fear of being punished as well might prevent students from asking questions in class, which might affect their performance, which is also a performance indicator for teachers.

    1.7.3. Con:
    There is little or no evidence to support the belief that corporal punishment is effective.

      1.7.3.1. Pro:
      Reliance on penalties as a mode of control generally is not effective.

        1.7.3.1.1. Pro:
        For instance, traffic fines are largely ineffective [61] at deterring wealthy people from committing violations.

        1.7.3.1.2. Con:
        Gradually increasing penalties, as opposed to fixed ones, have been found to be relatively effective [62].

        1.7.3.1.3. Con:
        Penalties are often easier to administer on a large scale compared to other methods that may cost the institution.

      1.7.3.2. Con:
      There has been an increase in behavioral issues [63] in schools where corporal punishment has been abolished.

        1.7.3.2.1. Pro:
        Many teachers [64] in the UK support reinstating corporal punishment, citing its effectiveness in controlling student behavior.

        1.7.3.2.2. Con:
        The increase in behavioral issues is perceived subjectively [65] rather than being captured through meaningful data, and it is compared with similar data from when corporal punishment was utilized.

      1.7.3.3. Pro:
      There is a larger body of evidence [66] backing the negative impacts of corporal punishment on children.

        1.7.3.3.1. Pro:
        The World Health Organization (WHO) reports that corporal punishment escalates behavioral problems [67] in children over time.

    1.7.4. Pro:
    Corporal punishment can be a quick and effective way to manage disruptive behavior.

      1.7.4.1. Con:
      The process and outcomes of corporal punishment might be time-wasting.

        1.7.4.1.1. Con:
        Corporal punishment offers an immediate response to indiscipline, saving time compared to other methods that may take longer to yield results.

        1.7.4.1.2. Pro:
        Accidents that occur during corporal punishment might result in the need for first aid or medical attention, which would take away from the students' learning time.

      1.7.4.2. Pro:
      Younger students might not be developed enough to respond to other forms of punishment.

        1.7.4.2.1. Con:
        It is unlikely that students will be able to focus fully in classes as a result of the pain and/or crying from the punishment.

        1.7.4.2.2. Pro:
        Corporal punishment works for ages because it works via an instinctual aversion to pain. [68]

    1.7.5. Con:
    The desire for corporal punishment is designed to conceal the many failings of formal schooling; chiefly, that many students see school as irrelevant to their lives and so won't follow school rules.

  1.8. Pro:
  Corporal punishment should be an option for families and societies that support it.

    1.8.1. Con:
    Corporal punishment is often executed with bias.

      1.8.1.1. Con:
      Any biases in execution are a reflection of societal prejudices and not intrinsic to the punishment.

        1.8.1.1.1. Con:
        Racial disparities, while present in society, manifest violently [69] in the realm of corporal punishment.

          1.8.1.1.1.1. Pro:
          In Mississipi, it was observed [70] that black students were beaten more frequently and more intensely.

            1.8.1.1.1.1.1. Pro:
            Even among black students, it was observed that darker-skinned students were beaten more [71] than their lighter-skinned peers.

          1.8.1.1.1.2. Con:
          Racist teachers might choose other means to punish black students if corporal punishment is not allowed.

        1.8.1.1.2. Pro:
        For example, since racial bias exists virtually in every facet of life [72], the presence of racial bias in punishment cannot be the reason why it is abolished, as doing so would not solve the larger problem.

        1.8.1.1.3. Con:
        Even if racial bias is neither exclusive nor intrinsic to corporal punishment, it does not change the fact that this practice further perpetuates society's racist norms towards children.

      1.8.1.2. Pro:
      Corporal punishment can often have a gendered nature [73] for those who support it.

        1.8.1.2.1. Pro:
        Corporal punishment is often inflicted exclusively on male students [74], while female students are either given lesser punishments or are fully exempt.

          1.8.1.2.1.1. Pro:
          Research [75] from South America and Africa indicates that male teachers often direct corporal punishment more towards male students.

          1.8.1.2.1.2. Con:
          To claim that abuse and use of corporal punishment do not occur in girls' schools trivializes the experiences [76] of young girls.

        1.8.1.2.2. Con:
        It can be ensured that corporal punishment is applied regardless of gender, rather than abolishing the practice altogether.

        1.8.1.2.3. Pro:
        The impact and experience of corporal punishment on girls differ from that of boys and can perpetuate detrimental gender dynamics in society.

          1.8.1.2.3.1. Pro:
          Corporal punishment is often used to regulate girls' social and sexual behavior [77], whereas this is not typically the motive with boys.

          1.8.1.2.3.2. Pro:
          Corporal punishment of girls by male staff reinforces the notion that women should be meek and submissive [78].

          1.8.1.2.3.3. Con:
          If patriarchal gender norms are prevalent in society, they will likely persist in the classroom even without corporal punishment.

        1.8.1.2.4. Con:
        Corporal punishment can exist in same-sex schools without such bias.

    1.8.2. Pro:
    Corporal punishment is an integral part of some religions [79]. As such, the beliefs of families of such faiths should be accepted in schools by allowing corporal punishment.

      1.8.2.1. Pro:
      In the Judeo-Christian faith, there are scriptures [80] that are believed to support corporal punishment.

      1.8.2.2. Pro:
      The Quran prescribes [81] corporal punishments for crimes such as theft, which occur in schools.

      1.8.2.3. Con:
      In a secular state, religious prescriptions need not be applied in public spaces such as schools, where people of all faiths attend.

    1.8.3. Pro:
    Where a society accepts corporal punishment, there is collective consent, which makes it acceptable.

      1.8.3.1. Pro:
      Malaysia, Singapore, Nigeria, Ghana, Mali, Senegal, and Liberia have this collective consent.

      1.8.3.2. Con:
      This type of consent usually does not involve the students themselves.

        1.8.3.2.1. Pro:
        Students are unlikely to consent to being beaten, either beforehand or in the face of punishment.

        1.8.3.2.2. Con:
        Surveys have found that when given a choice, most students prefer [82] corporal punishment and show an understanding of its value.

        1.8.3.2.3. Con:
        The students do not need consent since the thing being taught has already been socially defined with its mythos and where the ethos of the parents and teachers enforce it.

      1.8.3.3. Pro:
      This is no different from national laws on punishment, which are similarly created and justified by the social contract. [83]

    1.8.4. Pro:
    An opt-in or opt-out policy can be made for families that support this practice.

      1.8.4.1. Con:
      Opt-in and opt-out mechanisms don't offer a meaningful choice to families.

        1.8.4.1.1. Con:
        Families in Mississippi [84] are allowed to opt their children into corporal punishment, which many have used [85], proving it provides a real and actionable choice for families.

        1.8.4.1.2. Pro:
        Children whose parents choose not to opt into corporal punishment are often sent home or suspended [86], which is inconvenient for parents working long hours.

      1.8.4.2. Pro:
      Parents hold the primary decision-making power in raising their children; thus, an opt-in and opt-out mechanism acknowledges this right.

        1.8.4.2.1. Pro:
        The right of parents to make decisions is recognized when they get to choose for their child regarding life-altering medical procedures [87].

        1.8.4.2.2. Con:
        When it comes to corporal punishment, parents don't have control over when, how hard, or for what misdeed a teacher might strike their child.

        1.8.4.2.3. Con:
        In jurisdictions where corporal punishment has been banned [88], parents have no decision-making power in this context.

      1.8.4.3. Con:
      It would still not be acceptable to allow corporal punishment only for some students or some schools, as it would create a two-tiered disciplinary system in schools.

        1.8.4.3.1. Pro:
        Some children who opt for corporal punishment might feel the need to adjust their behavior more than others. This mirrors how racial minorities might second-guess [89] actions that majority members readily engage in.

        1.8.4.3.2. Con:
        The alternative, mandating a ban on corporal punishment in all schools and all students, would infringe on the autonomy of private schools to prevent them and parents who consent to this from applying corporal punishment.

      1.8.4.4. Pro:
      Families that opt in likely use corporal punishment at home already. As such, the application of corporal punishment in schools would offer no unique consequence.

        1.8.4.4.1. Con:
        Children facing corporal punishment from both parents and teachers experience more physical pain than from parents alone.

        1.8.4.4.2. Pro:
        Consistent discipline methods across home and school can provide children with a more uniform message about the consequences of engaging in harmful behavior.

    1.8.5. Con:
    Corporal punishment is an outmoded practice [90] and has no place in modern society.

      1.8.5.1. Con:
      The increase in student indiscipline after disallowing corporal punishment provides a reason for returning to the practice.

        1.8.5.1.1. Pro:
        There has been a marked rise [91] in student misbehaviour in schools around the world.

        1.8.5.1.2. Pro:
