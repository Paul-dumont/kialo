Discussion Name:
Should all religions be banned on a global scale?

1. Thesis:
All religions should be banned on a global scale.

  1.1. Con:
  It is not practically feasible to ban religion.

    1.1.1. Pro:
    Prohibiting religion requires the oppression of its adherents, which is challenging to execute.

      1.1.1.1. Con:
      Enforcement of religion is likely to result in greater state-led repression.

        1.1.1.1.1. Con:
        North Korea's ban on religious expression has resulted in the state's overt persecution [1] of religious people.

        1.1.1.1.2. Pro:
        Sudan's strict interpretation of Islamic law [2] has displaced Sudan's religious and sexual minorities.

        1.1.1.1.3. Pro:
        Uzbekistan's enforced adherence [3] to Islam resulted in the persecution of its protestant denominations.

        1.1.1.1.4. Con:
        Banning religion is not the same as the state enforcing a singular religion.

      1.1.1.2. Pro:
      Past efforts to prohibit religion have had detrimental effects on adherents and proven ineffective.

        1.1.1.2.1. Con:
        Albania's suppression of religion was neither [4] unsuccessful nor catastrophic.

        1.1.1.2.2. Con:
        Some governments have successfully restricted certain aspects of religious expression [5] without resorting to violence or persecution.

        1.1.1.2.3. Pro:
        Banning religion has failed [6] throughout history because belief is more powerful than oppression.

          1.1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
          Early Muslims persevered in holding onto their beliefs despite facing oppression from the Quraysh of Makkah [7] and enduring eventual exile.

          1.1.1.2.3.2. Con:
          Due to governmental bans, newer generations are likely to eventually abandon religion since its preservation becomes unattainable.

        1.1.1.2.4. Pro:
        Numerous governments have resorted to violent means in their efforts to prohibit [8] religious practices.

          1.1.1.2.4.1. Con:
          Governments willing to resort to violence to prohibit religion were likely tyrannical in the first place.

          1.1.1.2.4.2. Pro:
          In their attempts to ban the Uighur religious identity, the Chinese government has detained millions [9] in internment camps.

    1.1.2. Pro:
    Prohibiting religion may lead to its practice being driven underground and conducted secretly.

      1.1.2.1. Con:
      All bans are likely to push activities underground. However, this is not a good enough reason not to ban a harmful act or institution.

        1.1.2.1.1. Pro:
        The ban on underage sex work [10] and illicit drugs sales [11] in some parts of the world pushed the profession underground [12]. Underground, these acts occur to a lesser degree.

        1.1.2.1.2. Con:
        Many activities become more dangerous underground because of the absence of regulation.

      1.1.2.2. Con:
      Through efficient enforcement, the prohibition can also eradicate underground religious practices.

        1.1.2.2.1. Con:
        It would be difficult to root out all underground religious activities without severe invasions of privacy and freedom.

        1.1.2.2.2. Pro:
        Efficient enforcement can include monitoring [13] of public and private spaces as well as online activity, making it more difficult for religious groups to operate undetected.

      1.1.2.3. Pro:
      The absence of established religion may lead to the proliferation of informal and potentially harmful faith groups.

        1.1.2.3.1. Pro:
        Informal religious cults tend to be rife with nefarious acts and violence [14].

          1.1.2.3.1.1. Pro:
          Some underground religious cults have been known to lead their members to commit mass suicide. [15]

          1.1.2.3.1.2. Pro:
          Some underground religious cults have practiced slavery [16].

          1.1.2.3.1.3. Pro:
          The China Baiyang Sigui Lingbao Holy Path is a cult that tortured a member to death. [17]

          1.1.2.3.1.4. Con:
          The effectiveness of established religions in preventing harmful behaviors is not necessarily superior to that of underground cults.

          1.1.2.3.1.5. Con:
          There is a tendency [18] to only focus on cults that have engaged in nefarious acts rather than considering those that have not.

        1.1.2.3.2. Con:
        A prohibition on religion would also impact informal faith-based communities.

          1.1.2.3.2.1. Con:
          Cults may continue to exist even after a religious ban, as they have prior experience practicing religion clandestinely.

          1.1.2.3.2.2. Pro:
          Informal faith groups frequently engage in recruitment from within mainstream religions [19].

        1.1.2.3.3. Con:
        The media's negative depiction [20] of cults makes it unlikely for religious individuals to turn to cults as an alternative in the event of a religious ban.

      1.1.2.4. Pro:
      Many deeply religious people would simply ignore a ban and continue to practice regardless of the legal consequences.

        1.1.2.4.1. Con:
        Driving religion into secrecy would lead to a significant decrease in religious practices, which represents an improvement over the current state.

        1.1.2.4.2. Con:
        People are likely to comply [21] with a ban on religion that imposes severe consequences.

        1.1.2.4.3. Pro:
        Throughout history, there have been numerous instances of individuals practicing their religion covertly [22] following its following its legal prohibition.

        1.1.2.4.4. Pro:
        Given that religion pertains to matters of belief, it would be challenging for society to prohibit it without the ability to accurately ascertain an individual's thoughts.

    1.1.3. Con:
    Not exposing people to religious beliefs can result in a greater likelihood of supporting a prohibition on religion, thus making it more feasible.

      1.1.3.1. Pro:
      The popularity of secularism [23] illustrates society’s willingness to accept a ban on religion.

        1.1.3.1.1. Pro:
        There are 96 countries [24] around the world that are considered secular.

          1.1.3.1.1.1. Con:
          While many countries are officially secular, they operate as multi-religious [25] states.

          1.1.3.1.1.2. Pro:
          When countries bind themselves to a secular status, they provide a legal basis for the enforcement of secularity.

        1.1.3.1.2. Con:
        Non-religious people may oppose the ban alongside religious groups.

          1.1.3.1.2.1. Pro:
          People may be motivated to care about the plight of others due to societal norms [26], regardless of their personal involvement.

            1.1.3.1.2.1.1. Pro:
            Non-religious people are likely to have religious friends and relatives with whom they would sympathise in light of a religious ban.

            1.1.3.1.2.1.2. Con:
            Individual differences in values, beliefs, and experiences can affect empathy and concern for others, indicating that societal norms alone may not be sufficient to motivate care for the plight of others (p. 2) [27].

          1.1.3.1.2.2. Con:
          Many non-religious people do, in fact, espouse negative feelings [28] towards religion and are, therefore, less likely to oppose the ban on religion.

      1.1.3.2. Con:
      As religion significantly influences people's perspectives, prohibiting all religions is unfeasible since it would necessitate erasing the worldview of numerous individuals.

        1.1.3.2.1. Pro:
        Religion is deeply rooted in society's cultural traditions, values, and history; banning it will negatively impact society.

          1.1.3.2.1.1. Pro:
          A ban on religion, which would include prohibiting religious texts such as the Bible and Qur'an, is likely to eradicate significant historical artifacts.

          1.1.3.2.1.2. Con:
          The disappearance of religions such as the cults of Râ, Zeus, and Odin [29] demonstrates that religion can be eradicated without necessarily affecting culture, as they still exist in historical records and cultural references.

        1.1.3.2.2. Con:
        Introducing a ban on religion in stages could offer distinct substitutes, like interest groups and hobbies [30], that can assist in shaping an individual's perspective.

      1.1.3.3. Pro:
      As the non-religious population continues to grow [31], a ban on religion may become more feasible since a decreasing number of individuals would oppose it.

        1.1.3.3.1. Pro:
        The decline in religiosity [32] means that religious people will be less successful in attempts to prevent the ban.

        1.1.3.3.2. Con:
        The religious population is likely to grow faster than the non-religious population.

          1.1.3.3.2.1. Pro:
          Religious people in general tend to have more children [33] than atheists, so the growth in their population is likely to outpace that of atheists.

            1.1.3.3.2.1.1. Pro:
            Muslim immigrants in Europe have more children [34] compared to their European counterparts.

            1.1.3.3.2.1.2. Pro:
            Most religious populations oppose [35] abortion; therefore, they are less likely to avoid unwanted pregnancies.

            1.1.3.3.2.1.3. Con:
            There is no guarantee that the children of religious parents will also grow up to be similarly religious.

          1.1.3.3.2.2. Pro:
          Religious people are more likely [36] to spread their views than non-religious people.

          1.1.3.3.2.3. Con:
          Presently, over 1 billion [37] people identify as nonreligious.

            1.1.3.3.2.3.1. Con:
            The projected percentage [38] of unaffiliated individuals in the global population for 2060 indicates a minimal increase in the number of atheists but a decrease in their proportion from 16% to 13%.

            1.1.3.3.2.3.2. Pro:
            Atheism has spread rapidly [39] over time.

          1.1.3.3.2.4. Con:
          The UK has more atheists and agnostics [40] than Christians, and almost 75% of young people do not identify with a religion.

    1.1.4. Con:
    The principle of banning harmful ideologies is crucial, regardless of the practical challenges it brings.

      1.1.4.1. Con:
      The philosophy that it is important to try even with a low chance of success ignores opportunity cost [41].

      1.1.4.2. Pro:
      Even if a ban is unsuccessful in eliminating religion, it would most likely result in fewer adherents, which would still have positive effects.

  1.2. Con:
  Religion is beneficial to people on an individual level.

    1.2.1. Pro:
    Religion provides clarity, hope, and a sense of continuity in changing and uncertain times.

      1.2.1.1. Pro:
      Religion often inspires people to improve their lives.

        Religion teaches people to be content [42] with what they have and to be thankful to God so that they can be rewarded in the afterlife.

          1.2.1.1.1.1. Pro:
          Being content holds people back from actively seeking change or fighting oppression.

            1.2.1.1.1.1.1. Con:
            In situations that cannot be changed, being content for religious reasons offers people peace and comfort.

              Religious support is an essential part of palliative care [43] for terminally ill patients.

              1.2.1.1.1.1.1.2. Pro:
              Where the state condemns a person to death [44], faith can provide peace and an opportunity for redemption.

              1.2.1.1.1.1.1.3. Con:
              It is often difficult to know whether a difficult situation can be changed. People previously diagnosed with terminal illnesses have recovered [45], and people on death row have received pardons [46].

            1.2.1.1.1.1.2. Pro:
            Religion was used to condition slaves [47] to not pursue liberation.

          1.2.1.1.1.2. Con:
          Religion also leads people to believe they are entitled to great things, as promised by their God.

              1.2.1.1.1.2.1.2. Con:

              1.2.1.1.1.2.1.3. Pro:

          Religion offers a simpler explanation [57] of the universe compared to science or philosophy.

          1.2.1.2.2.1. Pro:
          Religions are essentially theories of everything [59] that offer members a holistic understanding of the world.

          1.2.1.2.2.2. Con:
          While religion offers useful insights, it often attempts to hold a monopoly on the truth [60], which prevents further exploration.

        1.2.1.4.1. Con:

        1.2.1.4.2. Pro:
